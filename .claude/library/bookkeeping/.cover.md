The specification of how books work. Every book in the library is valid because this book defines what valid means. Each chapter specifies one term — a type, a practice, a structural element. Together they form the type system where dots mark specialization and self-reference constitutes identity.

## The dot type system

```
Chapter          ->  Cover (.cover.md)
                     ^ specialization: a chapter that knows about its book

Book (no prefix) ->  Subject catalogue (. prefix)  ->  Library catalogue (.. prefix)
                     ^ specialization:                  ^ specialization:
                     a book that catalogues              a subject catalogue
                     other books                         that IS the library
```

No prefix is the base type. `.` is one level of specialization. `..` is two. The dots are type markers, not sorting markers. A cover is a chapter that self-describes. A subject is a book that self-catalogues. A library is a subject that self-references.

13. [On Authorship](13-on-authorship.md) — `specification: Authorship`. The `author:` field as grounded attribution. [No nametags in books](13-on-authorship.md#no-nametags-in-books) — nametags are for conversation, author fields are for books. The [coauthor protocol](13-on-authorship.md#the-coauthor-protocol): when to add `coauthor:`, when an edit is maintenance not authorship. [The librarian tends all content; the author owns it](13-on-authorship.md#the-librarian-tends-all-content-the-author-owns-it) — term fixes, link repairs, synopsis upkeep and library-wide vocabulary renames are her standing mandate, and the moment an edit would change a chapter's claims she is authoring rather than tending. AND A MECHANICAL VOCABULARY RENAME IS ONLY TENDING WHILE THE MEANING SURVIVES IT: a find-and-replace matches letters, which are the one part of a word that carries no meaning, so it inverts exactly where the word governs a direct object — one sweep of this library changed ~290 places and 36 of the resulting sentences said the opposite, with no gate to fire, because there is no compiler for prose. Replace a word by its sense, review where it takes an object, prefer the sweep you are already making, and quote the struck word once in the ruling that strikes it.
14. [On Perspective](14-on-perspective.md) — What KIND of library one is — the two species distinguished by a single test: does the author link resolve inside? A first-person library has one librarian who is its only author and whose author links come home; a third-person library has many authors, is about a subject matter, and its author links leave. Which ones we run, including the arrangement we have been running unnamed: a first-person BOOK (a teammate's autobiography) living inside a third-person library. The law that no one enters a first-person library — structural, not a permission — and its three consequences: a reference in names without addressing, the crossing is an act of authorship no tool can perform, and nobody is required to have an inside. The About the Author form and its signature; what .me will be when it exists; and the conventions elsewhere in this book that read differently by perspective.
15. [On Kinds](15-on-kinds.md) — The written-down population of book words, so naming becomes SELECTION rather than generation-and-defence — written after a naming failure it exists to prevent. Doug's two filters: is it a kind of book (not a part, not a format, not furniture), and is the word free of another meaning a reader would reach for first. Then the population itself, organized by what the book does: gathering (anthology, miscellany, florilegium, sylloge...), listing and locating (catalogue, directory, register, gazetteer, concordance, bibliotheca...), treating a subject (treatise, monograph, companion, encyclopedia, digest...), a life (biography, autobiography, memoir, chronicle...), record and institution (cartulary, minutes, gazette, syllabus...), the sacred and canonical — and two rejection piles that matter: apparatus (bibliography, index, colophon, about the author) and formats (folio, codex, quarto). Ends by working one question through the filters and reporting what the population shows that no candidate does: English never settled a native word for a book cataloguing books, because such books were TITLED library.

## The validator

[11-on-specifications--validator.ts](11-on-specifications--validator.ts) checks what these chapters specify: frontmatter fields and order, chapter signing, cover structure, catalogue conventions. One validator for the whole book — the specifications form a coherent type system, not independent rules. Run it with `npx tsx 11-on-specifications--validator.ts <library-path>`.
