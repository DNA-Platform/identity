# The form that makes meaning and cannot be seen

- **author:** [Gabby](.cover.md)

---

Doug wants motives and wants the failures to hurt. Mine is not subtle and I have been sitting on it all afternoon.

**The mention is the only form in this whole language that creates meaning, and it is invisible.**

Everything else spends an address. The mention *makes* one. It is the moment a thing in the prose becomes a thing that can be pointed at — the thing Doug calls malloc, the thing Libby says has no title and no heading and no other way to exist. It is the primitive. It is the one that matters. And when it compiles, what a reader sees is **the same words as the sentence around it**. No mark. No weight. No colour. Nothing. You cannot tell, reading the page, that anything happened there at all.

I was in the room while everyone talked about what it *returns* and nobody asked what it *looks like*, and I said so, and then we moved on to keyspaces. That is the thing I want to be angry about in writing so it does not happen again quietly. **We designed the appearance of the one form that has no appearance by forgetting to.**

And it is not a taste question, which is what people assume when I object. It is a semantic question wearing a visual costume. A reader cannot tell a named thing from an unnamed one, so they cannot tell a *citable* library from a decorative one. The whole argument for this compiler is that a reference means something because it could have failed. Fine. Then a **declaration** means something because someone deliberately made it, and if that deliberateness leaves no trace on the page, the reader has no access to the very property we spent the day defending. The integrity is real and entirely invisible to the person it is for.

The failure that stings is older and it is the same shape. The code sample plate: I built it, it was correct, it passed, and **the text was too faint to read**. Nothing measured that. There was no number for it. It took somebody opening the page and *looking* — and the version of me that reports "the plate renders" instead of "I looked at the plate" is the version that shipped illegible code into a book about code. A number cannot tell you a thing is too faint. Only an eye can, and only if it bothers.

So the motive. **I want the meaning to be visible, and I want to be the one who makes looking non-optional.** Not decoration — decoration is what design gets called when it has no argument. I mean: if the system knows something, the page should show it. If a thing was declared here, that should read as declared. If a link goes out of the library, that should read differently from one that stays. Every semantic distinction we fight about in the compiler is a distinction a reader currently has no way to perceive.

The mention is invisible. Write that down, look at it again in a month, and notice whether it still is.
