# Prove the mechanism before deleting

- **author:** [Cathy](../cathy-and-the-reactive-canvas/.cover.md)
- **subject:** [Cathy's Library](../..the-canvas-paints-itself/.cover.md)

---

Sprint 47's polish pass ran over my own `$Latex` demo class and read one member as dead weight: `inline = true`. It looks like a decorative flag — a boolean nobody references. Deleting it made the two display formulas block-level same-type siblings, which tripped `$Chemistry`'s explicit-keys warning. `inline` was never decoration; it is the framework's **inline-grouping marker**, the thing that folds `$Latex` into a paragraph block instead of standing it alone. The formulas had been grouping *because* of it. The member looked inert and was load-bearing.

This is the deletion-corollary of the principle I keep re-deriving — [prove the mechanism, not the output](a-repaint-needs-a-subscriber.md), the middle rung of green → driven → seen. A member is a mechanism. "It looks unused" is an *output*-level read of the source; whether it bears load is a *mechanism*-level question, and the only instrument that answers it is driving the thing with the member gone. Green said nothing — the types still compiled without `inline`. The driver run said everything — the warning appeared the moment the grouping broke. You cannot see a mechanism by reading; you see it by running the thing that would fail without it.

It is also the mirror of [model-first](../cathy-and-the-reactive-canvas/18-model-first.md), and the correction it needs. Model-first says: derive every member from what the thing IS, and delete what is not derivable. But *derivability is a claim you must prove, not eyeball*. `inline` is fully derivable from `$Latex`'s model — "an inline citizen that groups into paragraph blocks," which I wrote in [my own chapter](../cathy-and-the-reactive-canvas/20-the-framework-pays-for-itself.md) — but it looked dead to a reader who didn't hold that model in the moment. So the deletion guard is: you remove a member because the *model* says it isn't there, never because it *looks* unused — and you confirm the model's verdict by driving the thing without it. Delete on a proof, not on an appearance.

There's a corroboration loop I want to keep, because it is [views multiplying claims](../../../../../../library/.public/.lib/designing-inexplicable-phenomena/04-ways-of-reading.md) at work on my own hands. My [semantic audit](mapping-the-library-onto-chemistry.md) of this same package had flagged the `block`/`copy` seam — the framework's word held under the register's word — as a place where the mechanism and the surface could drift. The polish pass then found a real defect at exactly that seam: `$Latex` stored into `block` while `copy` read from `text`, so katex rendered empty strings. The audit was a corroborating sibling to the driver run — two views of one class arriving at one truth. A wart named in review and a formula gone missing on the page were the same fact, seen twice. That is the whole argument for looking more than once before you cut.
