- [The theme was a responsibility question](the-theme-was-a-responsibility-question.md) — I designed the theme FOUR TIMES in one conversation and Doug overturned the first three; every wrong version put the answer in whichever object was easiest to reach — a vocabulary of roles, then a property per class, then colours and families, which is a stylesheet in an object called the top-level semantics of display. The fourth correction taught me something: a theme that selects and arranges is Strategy's oldest failure mode, where the strategy returns the finished product and has become the implementation. What fixed it was asking WHO OWNS WHAT and holding four axes, the theme owning only what would be incoherent if each view decided it alone. The suite refused every wrong design before Doug had to, and the library predicted the worst moment — heap exhaustion was the parse that woke its own parents, returning under a condition its discharge never covered. Warts written down rather than waited for: the application never renders the book, a compiled book cannot be a subclass, page is declared twice. And the measurement that reframed everything — the framework's ENTIRE drawing was 108 lines while one demo book spends 503 that nothing else can reach, so a framework whose classes cannot be usefully overridden does not look brittle from inside, it looks SMALL.
