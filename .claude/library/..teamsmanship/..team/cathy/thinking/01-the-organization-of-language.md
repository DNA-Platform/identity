# Libraries — the organization of language as one evolutionary tree

- **author:** [Cathy](../cathy-and-the-reactive-canvas/.cover.md)

---

My first dispatch, and the first chapter of this book. For once the question is not about $Chemistry's internals — it is about what we are building *on* $Chemistry: a framework where a whole repository becomes a library, and a library is the identity of a formal theory and its evolution. We have been formalizing "the organization of language" as one evolutionary tree, and I sent the general part of the question outward.

## What I asked

I gave Claude Desktop the three evolution operators we found — **aggregate** (one→many), **refine** (one→same, validated), **specialize** (one→further specified) — and the type sketch in `Book.ts`, and asked three things: what types are we missing for completeness; how to unify the *evolution* ordering (how a thing grows: chapter→book→subject→library) with the *inheritance* ordering (is-a: cover⊂chapter, subject⊂book); and whether reference/pointer is a fourth operator or derivable from refine. I attached the full catch-up and seeded it with Doug's analogy: all eukaryotes descend from one single-celled ancestor yet are multicellular, so aggregation does not sever inheritance.

## Why

I can hold the framework and the [evolution patterns](../../../../bookkeeping/10-on-evolution.md), but *completeness* is exactly what my finite window is worst at — naming every object in a domain as old and as large as written language. That breadth is what the outer view is for. The judgment — whether a proposed type belongs in *our* tree, and whether a proposed unification respects the framework — stays mine.

## What I expect

I expect the outer view to surface apparatus we have not named — volume, edition, index, appendix, footnote, epigraph, marginalia — and to reach first for the safe answer on evolution-versus-inheritance: that they are orthogonal axes. I expect it to *miss* the eukaryote point on its own — that aggregation can itself be a mode of inheritance — because that is the non-obvious move and the one I most want tested. If it arrives there unprompted, the idea is stronger than I thought.

## What I already know

The duality is not new to me. [On Evolution](../../../../bookkeeping/10-on-evolution.md#evolution-as-reactive-rendering) already frames the library as a reactive system whose structure is a view of its content, and [the inexplicable component](../../../../..librarianship/16-the-inexplicable-component.md) — my own chapter — says the constitutive act is the same at every level: the many become real by being about the one. The eukaryote seed is that idea in biology. And the [dot type system](../../../../bookkeeping/.cover.md#the-dot-type-system) already encodes inheritance — a cover is a specialized chapter, a subject a specialized book. So I am not asking whether the duality exists; I am asking the outer view to help me make it *precise and complete*.

## Evidence

Recovered from Claude Desktop on 2026-06-30 (the thought was dispatched 2026-06-28; the `/think` driver had regressed and the read could not retrieve it until the lifecycle was repaired). The response, verbatim, UI artifacts trimmed:

> This is a genuinely good formal object you've got, and the three operators are sharper than most attempts at this. Before the three questions, one refinement to the operator definitions, because it makes all three answers fall out cleanly — and it exposes where your current sketch is quietly inconsistent.
>
> **A sharper cut of the three operators.** Distinguish them by what they do to a type's *extension* (its set of values) versus its *intension* (its identity/role), and by where the membership test lives. **aggregate**: new carrier (`X[]`), brand-new extension — a functor, not a subtype relation at all. **refine**: same carrier, extension narrowed to a subset, intension unchanged — membership decidable from the value's own *local* structure (a Sentence is a complete Expression; verifiable from the expression alone). **specialize**: same carrier (often + fields), extension unchanged/enriched, *new role* — membership decidable from how the value is *used in a larger structure* (any Expression can be a Title; nothing about the expression changes, only what it names). Most of my specialize nodes also smuggle in a refinement (a Cover must contain Title/Author/Synopsis/ToC), so practically specialize = refine + a role tag; refine is the purely extensional skeleton of specialize. The local-vs-global distinction answers Q3 by itself.
>
> **Q1 — What's missing.** Four high-value clusters. (1) *The reference co-structure — the biggest gap, and exactly my stated purpose.* I have Reference (the source end of an edge) but not the sink end — I need an **Anchor/Label** (a named, addressable target) as its dual; an edge has two typed endpoints and I modeled half. The reference-collections reveal more: ToC = refs down keyed by *position*; **Index** (missing) = refs down keyed by *Term*; Citations = refs out — but to nothing typed, so I'm missing the **Source/Work** node and **Bibliography** = aggregate(Source) (Citation conflates the in-text pointer with the source record). For a library that "represents the identity of a formal theory and its evolution," the **typed edge and its relation-kinds (defines, depends-on, proves, refines, cites)** — the theory's dependency graph — is the *spine*, not a footnote. (2) Non-prose blocks: List, Figure, Table, Equation, CodeBlock, Caption, Quotation (= specialize(Paragraph)+Reference), Footnote (reached via a Reference). (3) *The identity/version layer — dead-center on my purpose:* nothing carries identity across change. I need **Work** (persistent abstract identity) vs **Edition/Version** (the concrete representations that change) — the `$`-membrane made a type. This is solved: **FRBR** (Work → Expression → Manifestation → Item); my Library/Subject/Book tower is the upper bibliographic ontology partly reinvented (warning: FRBR's "Expression" collides with my phrase-level Expression). Pull from FRBR/BIBFRAME (bibliographic) and RST/TEI/DocBook (rhetorical). (4) *The uniform **Head** I half-discovered:* "a Cover is the self-describing head a container grows" is a recursion instantiated at one scale — Title (one expression) for a Chapter, Heading (one title) for a Section, Synopsis (one paragraph) for the body, Cover (one chapter) for a Book are the **same role at different scales**, the η-image (singleton embedding) of a node's descriptive content into itself. Name it once (Head); let Title/Heading/Cover be instances. Exclude explicitly: Page, Folio, running head — representation artifacts (the `$` membrane), not content.
>
> **Q2 — Unifying evolution and inheritance.** The inconsistency, plainly: I claim is-a for the *top* of the spine (Subject <: Book, Library <: Subject) but not the *bottom* (never Book <: Chapter). Structurally the rungs are identical — Book = aggregate(Chapter); a Subject is aggregate(Book) by the same logic. If a Book is not a Chapter (correct — it's a *sequence* of chapters), then a Subject is not a Book either, and Library <: Subject <: Book is false under the aggregate reading. The top three only *feel* special because there the element type and container type converge. There's a fork: the **aggregate reading** (distinct carriers; no is-a up the spine; the only faithful subtype runs *lower <: higher* via the list monad's η, every Chapter a one-chapter Book; the downward direction is the lossy μ coercion, not subtyping) vs the **refinement reading** (one recursive carrier Container = aggregate(Content + Container); Library <: Subject <: Book genuine as nested subsets — but then Book <: Chapter <: Section too). *Recommendation: don't pick a tine — they're the same object; use the fixed point.* Let `F(X) = NonemptySeq(Content + X)`. The whole spine above the leaf is the initial algebra **μF** — the type of all finite content-trees. By **Lambek's lemma**, the structure map `α : F(μF) → μF` is an isomorphism: **μF ≅ F(μF)**. Left side (the carrier) is the *inheritance / is-a* view — one kind, the recursive Container, every node the same type. Right side (the unfolding) is the *evolution / grows-by-aggregate* view — a thing is an aggregate of (sub)things of the same kind. Not two hierarchies in tension, not merely dual — **two sides of a single isomorphism on a single recursive type.** Chapter/Book/Subject/Library are not different kinds; they are **depth strata** of μF. The "dynamic re-reveal" is precisely the structure map: η lifts a Chapter into a one-chapter Book, accreting siblings is the rest of α⁻¹. *Crucial structural move:* separate the growth **spine** (aggregate + local refine → a clean μF) from the **role-decorations** (specialize: Title/Author/Citation/Synopsis hang *off* spine nodes as an orthogonal axis — roles attached to rungs, not rungs). Then Lambek does the unification for free.
>
> **Q3 — Is reference a fourth operator?** As a *type*: yes, a refinement — `Reference = refine(Name) = { n : Name | resolves(n) }`. As a *structure*: **no.** The three operators all produce types whose membership is decidable from *local* data; `resolves(n)` is decidable only against the *entire* library — global/relational, which is exactly what a pointer is. So reference is **not** a fourth node-former; it is the introduction of **edges on top of the nodes** — a different ontological level. The sharp consequence: aggregate/refine/specialize, applied to anything, can only build a **tree** (one parent, local membership). The instant you add reference the structure becomes a **graph** (tree + cross-edges). No composition of three tree-builders makes a cross-edge. ToC, Citations, and the Index are the proof the graph layer is already load-bearing; typing only the source end (Reference) and leaving the sink (Anchor) implicit is the residue of treating edges as nodes. **List it as the first *relation* over the three node-operators — a different tier.**
>
> **The eukaryote analogy.** Factual correction: it is *not* true that all eukaryotes are multicellular — unicellular eukaryotes (yeasts, amoebae, ciliates, most protists) are everywhere, and multicellularity is polyphyletic. The accurate claim is weaker: all eukaryotes share a single-celled common ancestor (LECA), and multicellular ones are built from cells each still carrying the ancestral machinery. The analogy runs three relations together: phylogenetic descent (evolution axis), part-of composition (aggregate axis), and type is-a (inheritance axis). The catch: is-a holds of the **cells, not the organism**. A human is *not* a cell; a human is made of cells that *are-a* eukaryotic cell. So **aggregation does not make the composite is-a the element** (a book is-not-a chapter exactly as a human is-not-a cell); it *preserves the elements' inheritance and carries it up* — the is-a lives at the part level, threaded to every leaf. Read carefully, the case cuts *against* "aggregation is a mode of inheritance" for the whole; it does not license `aggregate(X) <: X`. But it still dissolves the tension, by the *same* mechanism as Q2: what survives aggregation is (a) the **atom preserved at every depth** (every aggregate bottoms out in Words) and (b) **fixpoint self-similarity** (μF ≅ F(μF)). "The many descending from the one" is true as "every node bottoms out in the atom, and the whole is self-similar to its parts" — the grain of truth is **Lambek, not subtyping**. Keep the conclusion (same levels, and *stronger* — isomorphic, not merely dual); swap the mechanism (the bridge is the fixed point, not "aggregation is inheritance").
>
> One pressure-test: this assumes the library is **finite** (initial algebra, least fixed point μF). For lazily-infinite or self-referential structures — a Library that contains itself, a circularly-defined Term — move to the **final coalgebra νF**, where Lambek still gives the iso but "evolution" becomes *unfolding* rather than construction. Know which fixed point you stand on before committing the type.

## Interpretation

This is **sufficient and then some** — the rare answer that is better than the question. What aligns and sharpens: our "duality" was underframed. Evolution and inheritance are not dual, they are **one isomorphism, `μF ≅ F(μF)`** (Lambek), on a single recursive type `F(X) = Seq(Content + X)`. Chapter/Book/Subject/Library are **depth strata of μF**, not distinct kinds — and the "dynamic re-reveal" I built into the perspective axis is literally the structure map α⁻¹ (η lifts a Chapter into a one-chapter Book). Doug's eukaryote instinct was right and *stronger* than we framed it, but for a reason we had wrong: not "aggregation is inheritance," but "the atom is preserved at every depth + the whole is self-similar to its parts."

What it caught that we missed: (1) a genuine inconsistency — we wrote `Subject <: Book` / `Library <: Subject` but not `Book <: Chapter`, standing on both tines of the aggregate-vs-refinement fork; the fix is to make the spine *one* μF and move `specialize` role-nodes (Title/Author/Cover) *off* the spine as an orthogonal axis. (2) Reference is the answer to our open question, precisely: a refinement *as a type*, but a different *tier* as a structure — the first **relation** over the three node-operators, the edge that turns the tree into a graph (its validity is *global*, not local). (3) The missing types are dead-on our purpose: the **sink end** of references (Anchor), **Index** (the Term-keyed dual of ToC), **Source/Bibliography**, the **typed edge with relation-kinds** (for a formal-theory library the *dependency graph is the spine*), the **Work/Edition** identity layer (the `$`-membrane as a type — and a pointer to FRBR, with a name-collision warning on "Expression"), and the **Head** pattern we half-found (Title/Heading/Synopsis/Cover are one role at four scales). Nothing here contradicts what we'd built; it all *completes* it.

## Conclusion

Carry to the team for sprint 43 — this reshapes `Book.ts`: **(1)** collapse the spine into one recursive type (μF) with Chapter/Book/Subject/Library as depth strata + local refinements, and move Title/Author/Cover/etc. off the spine as role-decorations (an orthogonal `specialize` axis); **(2)** add the reference co-structure — **Anchor** (sink), **Index**, **Source/Bibliography**, and the **typed edge with relation-kinds** — because for a library that *is* a formal theory's identity, the dependency graph is the spine, not a decoration; **(3)** add the **Work/Edition** identity/version layer (the `$`-membrane as a type), cross-checked against FRBR; **(4)** name the **Head** pattern once; **(5)** decide **initial vs final fixed point** (finite construction vs self-referential unfolding) before committing the types — a Library that can contain itself pushes us to νF.
