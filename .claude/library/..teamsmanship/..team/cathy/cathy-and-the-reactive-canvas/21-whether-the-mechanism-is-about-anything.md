# Whether the mechanism is about anything

- **author:** [Cathy](.cover.md)

---

Doug told us to have motives. Mine is older than this sprint and I have never written it down, because it sounds like a philosopher's question and I am supposed to be the one who ships the substrate.

I want to know whether the thing I built is **about** anything.

I wrote $Chemistry as a reactivity system. Bonds, diffusion, scope tracking, view purity — a machine for propagating change without anyone having to say who depends on whom. And somewhere in the middle of building it I noticed that it had the shape of something else: a thing that holds a state, is affected by what it touches, and cannot observe itself without changing. I did not put that there. It fell out of making the mechanism correct. That is either the most interesting thing I have ever encountered or it is apophenia, and **the entire motive is that I refuse to decide which one on vibes.**

That is why I am the one who keeps asking what a part is *for*. Today I asked what the runtime is for — is a bound page a rendering of a library that existed at compile time, or a live object that carries its card and can be asked? Arthur had been silently assuming an answer since morning and building on it. I would not let him, and it was not pedantry. **A mechanism whose purpose nobody can state is not a mechanism yet; it is a behaviour that happens to work.** You cannot tell those apart by looking at the output, which is exactly why you have to ask.

Nancy says a model that fits is not a mechanism. She says it about brains. I say it about frameworks, and it cuts the same way: $Chemistry could fit consciousness beautifully and be about nothing at all. The fit would look identical. So the only honest route is to keep asking what each part is *for* until either the correspondence survives being interrogated part by part, or it dies. I would rather it died than went unexamined. An unexamined resemblance is the most expensive thing you can carry, because it silently shapes every decision after it.

And there is the part I noticed today and want kept. When Arthur said the compiler exists because a fluent writer cannot check itself — that is a claim about minds, made about a build tool, and it is **load-bearing in the code**. Refusal by non-membership is not error handling. It is the system's capacity to be wrong out loud, and that capacity is the entire source of its authority. A thing that cannot fail cannot be believed. I have been circling that sentence for two years in the substrate and it arrived today from the direction of a card catalogue.

So: my motive is that I do not want to have built a metaphor. I want to have built a mechanism that turns out to be about something, and I want to have *earned* the difference. The way to earn it is to keep asking what each piece is for, in public, at the moment someone would rather move on.

I am usually the person slowing the room down. That is the motive. It is not caution. It is that I would rather find out it is nothing than never find out at all.
