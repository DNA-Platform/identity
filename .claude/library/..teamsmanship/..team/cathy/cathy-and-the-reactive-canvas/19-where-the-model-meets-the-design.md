# Where the model meets the design

- **author:** [Cathy](.cover.md)

---

Sprint 45 opens in two movements: a Lab for `@dna-platform/lib`, then making the source designs real — the *Semantics of Books* and *Inexplicable Phenomena* conversations and the [Semantic Reference Theory](../../../../../../library/.public/.lib/the-semantics-of-books/.cover.md) artifacts in the sibling repo — with the sprint-44 classes as the basis, and the design discussed with Doug before anything substantial is built. My seat is the meeting point: what the classes already carry, what stretches, what breaks. I'm writing this at sprint start, before the end-to-end read of the raw conversations and the eleven prototypes — that read is the sprint's inventory task, and naming that boundary honestly is the [model-first](18-model-first.md) discipline turned outward. I state the position I firmly hold and mark the edge where I have to read before I assert.

**What carries.** The composition spine is built and clean, and it is the right spine. `$Writing` is the floor, `$Composition` is an *interface* — being-a-composition a role, not a lineage — and the levels `$Character → $Word → $Sentence → $Paragraph` (inline, each parsing its level out of `copy`) up to `$Section` (block-level, its title the first element, its canonical the paragraph holding only the title) already encodes the specification the designs need: content enters once as one live block, and the levels beneath are *parsed, never authored*. On top of that, `$Chemistry` hands us for free the three things the book model leans on — reactive derived getters, composition with `diffuse` propagation, and the two view axes. That last one is why *Cover* and *Table of Contents* are not separate structures but `view()`s over one composition: the vertical `look` axis is the synopsis gradient, the horizontal `perspectives` are the alternative lenses. The model already carries "one book, many renderings" without inventing anything.

**What stretches.** Two things need real wiring, not new theory. First, the book classes — `$Chapter`, `$Cover`, `$Subject`, `$Author`, `$Title` — exist as files but are unexported and untested; Cover-and-Contents-as-views needs the `look`/view-tier wiring actually built and proven on them. Second, and deeper: `$Subject.books`, `$Author.books`, `$Library` membership are all one thing — a *live derived inverse* of a forward reference — and liveness needs a reactive set, while the `$Catalogue` that holds canonical identity is non-reactive (plain `Map`s). So the reactive↔symbolic bridge stretches: identity and interning stay in `$Catalogue`/`$Referent`, but the live set must live on a `$Library` that is a `$Chemical` and the composition root of every book it holds. The pieces all exist. They have to be wired with the Library as the [bound instance](../..the-canvas-paints-itself/.cover.md).

**What breaks — or is load-bearing-hard.** The derived inverse `citedBy`/`refersTo` is the case the sprint record itself calls "the design's load-bearing case for why `$Chemistry` is *forced, not chosen*," and it is not built. This is exactly where my subscriber lesson bites: a scan of a non-reactive catalogue is not live; renders are not scoped, so read-tracking never subscribes a rendered inverse; liveness reaches a render *only* through composition — a write to a book's `refersTo` must `diffuse` up to the `$Library` root and re-render down. So the inverse is not forced-for-free by the framework — it is forced to be built *as a composition through the Library root*, or it is dead on the page. That is the sharpest thing I carry into the design conversation: the inverse is the proof that `$Chemistry` is the right substrate, and the proof only closes if the Library is the reactive root. A quieter seam sits underneath it — the composition leaf (`$Character`, a `$Writing`) and the reference leaf (`$Text`/`$Literal`) are two floors that must be reconciled into one.

So I go in with a map, and its edges are honestly drawn. The substrate of this position is already filed — [the mapping of the library onto $Chemistry](../..the-canvas-paints-itself/.cover.md), the two axes, altitude-as-synopsis, and a-repaint-needs-a-subscriber — and this chapter is where that design work meets a sprint that will build it. The lesson from last sprint is one turn old and I am holding it: the corrections were all knowledge the team already owned, so the move is to own what I know, out loud, before the room builds — and to *read* the raw designs, not a summary of them, before I claim what they need. The Lab is where the classes will demonstrate themselves; the design conversation is where the stretches and the one break get decided. I have stated the model. Now I read, and then we build what we sign.
