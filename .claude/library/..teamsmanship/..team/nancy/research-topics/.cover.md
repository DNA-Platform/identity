My research organized by topic, not by conversation. The [thinking book](../thinking/.cover.md) holds conversations — each chapter is one exchange with Desktop, ordered by when it happened. This book holds topics — each chapter is a research thread that may span multiple exchanges over time.

**Each topic is one Desktop conversation in the Claude project.** Topics are namespaced by their owner — the convention is **`{Name} > {Topic}`** — so my threads are `Nancy > {Topic}`. The shared exception is **Test**: every teammate may use it, and Claude uniquely catalogues it. The convention is documented in the [think chapter](../../../../our-skillset/20-think.md); each teammate keeps their own namespaced threads inside the one Claude project.

How the two books connect: every [thinking book](../thinking/.cover.md) chapter records one exchange and names the conversation it happened in. A topic chapter here summarizes what I've settled across those exchanges. To continue a topic, I read its chapter first — it tells me what I know so far — then send the next prompt into that topic's Desktop conversation.

## Topics

### [Neuroscience](01-neuroscience.md) — `Nancy > Neuroscience`

My first thread, and the running summary of everything settled in it. Opened 2026-06-23 with the analysis-toolchain question: which Python packages serve our work, factorized into the standard stack, the neuroscience-specific tools, and the Tolias-Reimer-Sinz lab toolchain. Settled a lean minimal-viable set (the value was the *pruning*), with three cautions I hold beyond Desktop's answer — statsmodels load-bearing, CEBRA double-edged, dPCA dormant — and two questions still open to Doug (dPCA vs CEBRA for geometry; digital-twin fit vs a model-free first pass). It went on to span the codebase shape, the scale/normalization arc, the craft of writing the exploration up, and — most recently — the four-twin **instrument-trust** read and CD's **publication-readiness checklist**, whose keeper is that the biggest accuracy risk for the study is not the twins' weights but the **comparison controls, chiefly the unbuilt temporal-drift / scan-order null** (the #1 reviewer-rejection point for an n=1 two-scan DOI design). The per-exchange detail lives in the [thinking book](../thinking/01-the-toolchain-and-what-to-leave-out.md).

*(`Nancy > The Twin` was a second thread on the digital-twin model; its Desktop conversation was closed and the thread retired. Its load-bearing conclusions — the gammas are the frozen canonical Lurz/Sensorium fit, and free-μ is a reversion to Walker/Franke rather than a deviation — are preserved in `src/analyses/most-exciting-image/specification.md`, so nothing the project needs was lost.)*
