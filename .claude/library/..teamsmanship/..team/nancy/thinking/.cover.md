4. [The scale difference, and whether two-photon makes it](04-the-scale-difference-and-two-photon.md) — 2026-06-23, `Nancy > Neuroscience`. The matched-cell ~48% drop is a per-neuron *multiplicative scalar* (mean and s.d. fall in lockstep) that the dataset's per-session standardization erases — and is *non-identifiable* from this comparison between genuine V1 gain modulation (plausible for a 5-HT2A agonist) and cross-session measurement scale, because standardizing removes the very axis the effect lives on. Method lesson: verify the pipeline's own normalization before any cross-session magnitude claim; erasure-by-standardization is diagnostic, not exculpatory.

15. [The specification, sent for a rigorous primary-source review](15-the-spec-sent-for-rigorous-review.md) — 2026-06-28, `Nancy > Neuroscience`. After Doug re-grounded the goal (I'd read our *write-up* of Erin's deck, not her slides — the goal is descriptive **representation**, not prediction), I sent the whole spec to CD for a brutally rigorous primary-source review. CD read Lurz/Franke/Walker/Cobos and found two headline blind spots: the **gain blind-spot** (MEIs + metamers are scale-invariant, but DOI's documented effect is a gain change — no scale-bearing instrument named) and that **Franke is a UV/green colour study** (an instrument for a feature axis grayscale lacks). Plus: the metamer needs Cobos's **VAE prior** ("faithful reimplementation" over-claimed); the gammas are **Sensorium-competition** values (re-derive, don't cite Lurz); **learned-μ is *more* right** (data-efficiency rationale absent — lean in); "5 seeds"/"FEVE range" are conventions, the MEI recipe is Walker not Franke, arm-B is ours. Holding the spec edits for a follow-up challenging the Franke baseline (Reimer data → the Tolias digital-twin lineage). Lesson: read the actual papers *and the data domain* before anchoring a baseline.
16. [The baseline re-anchor — Franke demoted, Walker's modulator found](16-the-baseline-re-anchor-and-walkers-modulator.md) — 2026-06-28, `Nancy > Neuroscience`. Doug challenged Franke-as-canonical (Reimer data → Tolias lineage); CD pulled Walker/Cobos/Lurz and confirmed it. **Franke is a downstream application** (inherits its method from Walker 2019 + Lurz 2021, adds behaviour-channels + a colour readout) — so anchor on **Walker (MEI) + Cobos (metamer) + Lurz/Sensorium (twin)**; from Franke borrow ONLY the quiet/active percentiles + the two-states comparison scaffold. The find: **Walker's MODULATOR** (per-neuron gain from behaviour) IS the missing scale-bearing gain instrument — read DOI's gain off it in raw units at a fixed state (the Michaiel quantity), never off scale-invariant MEIs/metamers. Also: the metamer needs Cobos's **VAE prior** (pixel-space is the baseline); MEI = Walker recipe + luminance/RMS-contrast matching; behaviour via modulator (not channels) + learned-μ moots the y-collapse. Owed verbatim: Walker's preconditioning + Cobos's optimizer. Lesson: anchor on the *method-source* lineage, not the closest-looking paper.
17. [The normalization miss, and the gain limitation](17-the-normalization-miss-and-the-gain-limitation.md) — 2026-06-28, `Nancy > Neuroscience`. Doug caught me adding a Gain instrument (Part 7) without reckoning with what the exploration established: the **per-scan normalization erases the gain**. Reading the source (`exploration/`, `twin-mei/`) confirmed it's deeper — the recording is **volumetric**, a z-drift rescales every cell, so raw cross-scan amplitude is *uninterpretable*; the shipped per-scan std normalization removes the z-drift AND erases the DOI gain (Michaiel). So **neither** raw (z-drift-confounded) **nor** normalized (gain-erased) amplitude recovers it, and a modulator on normalized responses inherits the problem — CD's modulator-gain idea and my Part 7 both fail. Reframed gain as a **documented limitation** (the analysis is scale-invariant throughout; the MEIs/metamers are appropriate, not a blind spot). Fired a /think asking what *else* foundational about this volumetric/per-scan-normalized/n=1/grayscale data I'm forgetting. Lesson: read the **source** (the exploration), not my own synthesis — I keep re-discovering these one at a time.
18. [The spec check, and CD's approval](18-the-spec-check-and-cds-approval.md) — 2026-06-28, `Nancy > Neuroscience`. Doug caught me **inventing** — I'd let CD's earlier modulator + gain-instrument suggestions into the spec as if they were the plan. Stripped them, read all the analysis code in source, rewrote the spec to describe **only what the code builds + what the sources specify**, and sent it to CD to *check, not redesign*. He **approved it as a faithful contract** with fixes (applied): the Sensorium config + trainer + learned-μ + Franke Fig-1e input all verified correct; **MEIs are COMPLEX, not Gabor** (Walker's central result — a Gabor MEI is a red flag; corrects my earlier work); add Walker's **luminance/RMS-contrast match** + reconcile the L2 budget before any MEI comparison; **co-register learned-μ** across the two independently-fit twins or "spatial change" is μ jitter; max_iter 500 vs baseline 200. 19. [The four-twin fit, read as instrument trust](19-the-four-twin-fit-and-instrument-trust.md) — 2026-06-30, `Nancy > Neuroscience`. The four trained twins ({Control, DOI} × {behaviour arm A, stim-only arm B}) assessed for goodness-of-fit — reframed (Doug's standing correction) as **instrument trustworthiness**, not prediction: can I believe these instruments' MEIs/metamers? On the **267 cells reliable in both states** (same-cells-same-ceiling): arm A 0.667→**0.445**, arm B 0.710→**0.696**, ceiling 0.596→0.510. Ceiling-matched, the **stim-only instrument is barely touched by DOI while the entire hit is in the behaviour arm** — and a same-config control (byte-identical `model_config`, only the scan differs; all 5 post/A seeds 0.26–0.33) proves the collapse is **systematic**. Likely cause: under a 5-HT2A agonist **pupil is partly a drug readout**, so the behaviour channels carry nuisance not signal — a **limitation of the technology, documented, not a drug biomarker**. Consequence: **trust the stim-only twin for the DOI representation**; the pre/post represented-feature comparison leans on arm B (both states faithful). Cite CD's statistics (Schoppe CC_norm, Sahani–Linden FEVE, Pospisil–Bair r²_ER + BCa CIs, same-cells-same-ceiling) but **drop the prediction/leaderboard frame** (CD did not re-pull the Sensorium-2022 metric; I don't want it). Standing lesson: I keep re-inventing a prediction/detection goal — the goal is **representation-understanding**, the twin is an **instrument**, goodness-of-fit = trust.
21. [The publication checklist, and the risk that isn't the weights](21-the-publication-checklist-and-the-risk-that-isnt-the-weights.md) — 2026-06-30, `Nancy > Neuroscience`. CD's **publication-readiness checklist** across the four domains (twins / MEIs / metamers / comparison) on the **confirmed-vs-merely-built** axis — is there an *artifact* proving each holds on *this* scan. CD was **honest that it can't source a canonical Reimer/Tolias "three-gate"** (wouldn't invent lab doctrine), offering instead the natural **instrument-trust triplet**: validation clears the ceiling / healthy non-Gabor forward pass / gamma verified on our scan. The **reviewer-rejection set**: (1) the **temporal-drift / scan-order control** — the #1 killer for our n=1 two-scan DOI design (drug perfectly confounded with scan order, no vehicle, DOI always second) and it is **NOT BUILT**; (2) **matching-circularity** — prove the reciprocal-NN match is registration-only, else the whole comparison is circular; (3) the **gamma plateau-check**. Honorable mentions that confound the compare if skipped: **μ co-registration** + the **luminance/RMS match**. Cross-references the weight-level rebuild ledger (readout DONE, behaviour DONE, gamma PENDING; preserved in `specification.md`). **The keeper meta-point:** the biggest accuracy risk for the *study* is not the twins' weights (very likely fine) but the **comparison controls, chiefly the unbuilt temporal-drift null** — a perfect twin compared badly still gets rejected. Standing lesson: I keep auditing the instrument when the biggest risk is the experiment.

[What else a pair of twins can say](22-what-else-a-pair-of-twins-can-say.md) — 2026-07-08, `Nancy > Neuroscience`. **Verdict pending** — the CD write is blocked by a Desktop modal (the Sprint-77 pitfall: an open dialog hides the "New chat" anchor), so the reaching half never landed and my independent evaluation stands as the interim. Two questions, factorized: how to *implement* the specced analyses in the sinzlab stack, and — ranging wider — what *published* methods compare the represented features of a **pair** of encoding models of the same neurons. The finding that matters is a correction to my own framing: **MEI + metamer is not as close as it gets.** Two methods answer "how do the represented features change" at least as directly and clear all three of my hard constraints (scale-invariance, matched-cells-only, Rule-1) — **RSA on predicted responses**, where correlation-distance RDMs are scale-invariant *by construction* and expose whether the geometry of representation reshapes under DOI, and **in-silico tuning/invariance probes**, the classical feature axes a reviewer expects, reported as shape and index only, never grating amplitude. CKA/CCA and linear readout transfer are good quantitative complements (the latter decomposes core-features vs readout). RSA on readout weights **fails** outright — independently fit cores mean the feature spaces are not aligned. Implementation needs no new package: drive `gradient_ascent` with custom objectives, cross loss **1 − correlation** over the population vector, with the residual per-cell scale documented as a limitation rather than z-scored away. The lesson I want kept: I had been reaching for the bespoke synthesis comparison before the well-published population-level one.
