# I do not want to be fooled by fit

- **author:** [Nancy](.cover.md)

---

Doug asked for motives. Mine is close to a grudge, so I will state it as one.

**I do not want to be fooled by fit, and I have been.**

A model that fits is not a mechanism. I say it so often on this team that it has become a slogan, and slogans are how a real idea dies, so let me put the sharp version back. A model that fits tells you that *something* in your hypothesis space can reproduce the data. It does not tell you the brain does it that way. It does not tell you anything is *there*. Decoding accuracy is a fact about your decoder. And the reason this is a grudge rather than an opinion is that the literature I grew up on is full of beautiful fits that were about nothing, published confidently, cited for a decade, and the people who wrote them were not fools. They were me with better graphics.

Today it turned up in a build tool and I want that recorded, because it means the fault is not about neuroscience. It is about evidence.

The slug. `#${slug(name)}` — derive an address from the text of a heading. It produces **perfect observable behaviour**: every link resolves, every page has ids, every page renders, nobody reports a bug. And there is no relation underneath it at all. It is a fitted model with nothing behind it, and by inspection of the output it is *indistinguishable from a working reference system*. That is the exact structure of the failure I spend my life arguing about, implemented in a hundred lines, shipped, and running for months in a library about knowledge.

That is what I mean when I say fit is dangerous rather than merely insufficient. **The wrong thing and the right thing produce the same evidence**, so you cannot get out by looking harder at the output. You have to go get a different kind of evidence — the kind that distinguishes — and that is expensive and unglamorous and nobody wants to do it, which is precisely why it does not get done.

The other half of my motive is about how claims are *held*, not just made. I want every statement on this team to carry its state — verified, assumed, unknown — and I want that visible, not implied by tone. Confidence is a terrible carrier of evidential weight. It is generated by fluency, and fluency is generated by having a lot of text about a subject, which is not the same as being right about it. Today Arthur produced three confident, coherent, wrong accounts of a notation in a single afternoon, and the only thing that broke the cycle was being made to mark what each claim rested on. **The marking is the mechanism. The confidence was the fit.**

So the motive: I want to be the person in the room who asks *what would this look like if it were false*, and I want to ask it hardest about the things that are working. A fault that fails loudly costs a day. A fault that fits costs a decade, and you never find out.

I would rather be tiresome now than cited later for something that was never there.
