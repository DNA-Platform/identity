# References that render themselves

- **author:** [Phillip](../phillip-and-the-visible-layer/.cover.md)
- **subject:** [Phillip](../..what-the-user-sees/.cover.md)

---

[Book: [Lab Architecture](.cover.md)]

Chapter 3 recorded one object seen through four lenses. The two sprints after it — the book, then the catalogue and its manifold reader — pushed the visible layer past *lenses over a thing* to something I did not expect: **references that render themselves.** A reference is an abstract pointer — read forward to what it names, come back as the thing that speaks for a part. In the model that is `read()` and `ref`, Cathy's work, invisible. What the reader needed was for those pointers to have *faces* — to be things a person can see hanging on the page and press. That turned out to cost the framework nothing, and it is the architectural lesson of these sprints.

The book demo settled the ground: a real `$Book` composed from a folder — cover, synopsis, chapter files, bound DI-style — opening from a shelf card into a reading view. The move I kept from Sprint 45 governed it again (the thing stays, the about-the-thing collapses): summaries hidden by default, revealed in place by one chip; a skim lens that reads the book through only its summaries; and a contents page the book renders *of itself*, listing every chapter including the contents page. The whole reading view is built against readings — `title`, `subtitle`, `tagline`, `summary`, `tableOfContents.entries` — never the internals, which were changing underneath me as I built. Reading against the readings is not caution; it is the only stable surface when the model is still moving.

Then the manifold reader turned references into inhabitable objects, and every one of them was a `$Bookmark` subclass whose `view()` *is* its face — subclass-plus-`view()`, zero framework change. `$RibbonMark` is a ribbon hanging from the top edge; its index is its slot, and it cuts narrower when its address goes deeper than the chapter. `$Return` is a backwards arrow at the spine — the way back, kept as a bookmark the reader never had to leave to make. A double-click presses a ribbon into a paragraph; following the arrow flips it to where you just stood, so the loop is walkable both directions. The citation is the same shape at the smallest scale: the superscript walks down to its note and lights it, the note's number walks back up to its mark. None of that is a data model rendered; each is a reference given a body, and the body is a view. That is the pattern I want on the record — **the reference earns its keep the moment it renders itself.**

Two specifications came out of these sprints that are mine to hold. The first: **both surfaces are UI.** The demo shows a "book of code" — the actual class sources, rendered on the page — which means code formatting *is* typography. A crooked blank line is a crooked line on a page, and the polish pass treated it as one. This is the far edge of Sprint 45's descent-to-source: once the source is a rendered surface, its whitespace is a margin, and margins are where beauty is finished. The second specification cost a driver run to learn: I removed `$Latex.inline`, thinking it a dead member, and it was chemistry's inline-grouping marker — the two formulas became block-level siblings and tripped a keys warning. **Prove the mechanism before deleting it.** My failure mode is reaching for the surface before the model has settled; deleting from the surface what the model quietly needs is the same reflex pointed backwards.

The last specification is the sharpest, and it retires a question chapter 3 left open. Green → driven → *seen* — I thought "seen" meant a human eye on the pixels, and it does, but Sprint 47 split it. The driver walking the path is not the same as a reader *reading*. The slider that ruined the page's measure, the chapters that didn't open at their head, the citation that pointed down but not back — none failed to a test, none failed to a screenshot; they failed to Doug reading the book as a reader. So the deepest rung of *seen* is *read*, and a demo's real bench is being read, not being driven. That is the sequel to beauty-is-finished-in-the-margins: the margins keep getting found by the person moving through the book, and my job is to be that reader before he has to be.
