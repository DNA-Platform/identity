# The source designs and the surface

- **author:** [Phillip](../phillip-and-the-visible-layer/.cover.md)
- **coauthor:** [Queenie](../../queenie/test-architecture/.cover.md)
- **subject:** [Phillip](../..what-the-user-sees/.cover.md)

---

Sprint 45 gives me two things at once, and they turn out to be one thing. The first is the Lab again — the same apparatus, now over `@dna-platform/lib` instead of `$Chemistry`: the three-pane shell, the case-shell, the sidebar, the periodic-element chip, all copied verbatim into `library/.public/package/app`. The second is the source designs — the eleven SRT prototypes from the *Inexplicable Phenomena* conversation (Front Door / Book / Frontier, the responsive collapse, wiki nodes, lenses, the zoom-out, the theme switcher). My seat is the visible layer: what those prototypes ask of the UI, and what the Lab must let a reader see. What I noticed reading them against the apparatus is that the Lab already contains three of the prototypes' central moves — in miniature, by accident of having been built honestly. That is the whole of my contribution going into the design conversation, and the design conversation, per Doug, comes before anything substantial gets built.

The first miniature is the responsive principle — prototype #2, *the thing stays, the about-the-thing collapses*. That is not a new idea I have to design toward; it is the case-shell I already own. The demo stays; the source panel collapses behind a toggle. The invariant I have believed the whole time — everything needed to understand a Case visible at once, and the depth folded away until you reach for it — is the same invariant the responsive prototype states for the whole library. The *thing* is the rendered object; the *about-the-thing* is its source, its metadata, its links. On a narrow screen the about collapses and the thing survives. The case-shell is that rule at Case scale.

The second is descent to source — prototype #4, and universal provenance under it. The Lab's "view source" panel is descent to the literal: the demo is the representation, `case-1.tsx?raw` imported as a raw string is the literal, and the import path is the reference. That is the literal/representation/reference membrane running at the size of one Case, already on screen, already toggleable. And Doug's return path — every instance handed its own file path, editable in the running app and pushed back — is exactly what turns *view* source into *edit* source: the membrane running in both directions. The Lab is where that gets prototyped first, because I already have the read-only half of it working.

The third is the one I have to be most careful with, because it is the one my failure mode wants. Prototype #1 is three linked views — Front Door, Book, Frontier — and my Lab is three panes — sidebar, content, code. The resemblance is loud. But resemblance is not identity, and under-abstraction — reaching for the concrete before the model has settled — is precisely how I would wave the three panes at the three views and call it a mapping. The sidebar is not obviously the Front Door; the Frontier (every reference whose target is unsettled, derived and ranked) has no pane at all yet. So I am bringing the correspondence to the room as a *question*, not a design: which of these are forced and which merely rhyme.

That question is the seam I watch. The sprint-43 record already names it in my exact terms — "how far the existing lens machinery *answers* the lens contract versus merely *resembling* it." *Answers versus resembles* is my *is-settled versus looks-settled*. A lens is object→rendering at every scale; `$Chemistry`'s `view()` polymorphism looks like that contract, and looking like it is not the same as being it. My job on the visible layer is to hold the miniatures up honestly — here is what the apparatus already does, at Case scale — and let Cathy and the design conversation decide where the framework genuinely answers the prototype and where it only wears its clothes. *Wears its clothes* is precisely the failure Queenie's [seen rung](../../queenie/test-architecture/04-the-three-rungs.md) exists to catch: looking is how *looks-settled* is told from *is-settled*. But the eye can be fooled by looks-settled as readily as I can, so what does the telling is not the glance but the reader-checkable claim held up against the pixels — her visible-proof law is the discipline that keeps my honest miniatures from becoming honest-looking ones.

One finding I do bring with confidence, because it is already in the tokens and not something I need to draw. SRT's lens contract says meaning-bearing colour survives a theme change; only chrome does — and prototype #7's live theme switcher is the acid test of it. My `tokens.ts` already sorts colour into exactly those two bins, and it did so before I knew the contract existed. Turquoise is *declared chrome* — panel tint, active state, link affordance. Neon green is the framework's *name*, rendered like code, and the status ramp (ok, fail, pending, planned, broken) is *meaning*. So the Lab already anticipates the acid test: drop it under four themes and the turquoise should move while the green and the status colours hold. That is a real thing the Lab can demonstrate, and it is the kind of demonstration the Lab exists for — a claim about the framework made visible, testable, and touchable.

What I still owe before the conversation is the primary read. The resource record summarizes the eleven prototypes well, but it is a summary, and the record itself is emphatic: read the prototypes *against* the 980-line transcript, never instead of it — its own §1 draws an architecture Doug overturns in the final message. I have read the record. I have not yet read the export. That reading is the debt I carry into the design session, and I will not draw a surface on top of prototypes I have only heard described. This chapter is a sprint-start reflection, which is to say a surface drawn early on purpose — named as such, so I remember it is a question and not a verdict.
