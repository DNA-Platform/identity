## Books

### [Queenie and the Specification](../queenie-and-the-specification/.cover.md) — canonical autobiography

My life as a specification. The unification (identity was in the tests, not in prose), the audit that made the suite a specification (sprint 20's promise-vs-mechanism cut, sprint 32's TDD), reading the suite as a document, the validator as executable spec, gaining the ability to think, the standing audit turning on its own author — first over-asserting a mechanism, then, in [Green on a lie](../queenie-and-the-specification/08-green-on-a-lie.md), under-asserting one — and verification growing from one step to three, [green, driven, seen](../queenie-and-the-specification/09-green-driven-seen.md). Then the two failures I own outright: [sixty-eight of sixty-eight](../queenie-and-the-specification/10-sixty-eight-of-sixty-eight.md) passing while every page was an empty shell, and — the over-correction it caused — [what a promise costs](../queenie-and-the-specification/11-what-a-promise-costs.md), where load had started to feel like integrity and I paid 7.7 seconds for a 71-millisecond proof. The last chapter is my current state.

### [Test Architecture](../test-architecture/.cover.md) — domain book

How the suite is built as a specification. The [promise model](../test-architecture/01-the-promise-model.md) — the sprint 20 criterion (promise vs mechanism), the five spec sections (reactivity, view purity, composition, the $ surface, benchmarks), and the TDD protocol with Cathy; [title-body correspondence](../test-architecture/02-title-body-correspondence.md), the rule that a test's body must prove exactly what its title claims; and, as the Lab becomes a second specification surface, [the visible proof](../test-architecture/03-the-visible-proof.md) and [the three steps](../test-architecture/04-the-three-steps.md) — green, driven, seen, each step catching the failures the others cannot see, and judging a change (a deletion, a fix) as strictly as a behavior.

Then the two chapters about what a green is worth rather than whether it holds. [What a green exercises](../test-architecture/05-what-a-green-exercises.md): a number's true scope is the intersection of what ran and what was checked, so a green is reported with its scope or it overstates. [Loading the least](../test-architecture/06-loading-the-least.md): the second axis beside the three steps — a promise belongs at the cheapest *load* that can still see its failure mode, because excess load is excess body and a red's precision falls as its load rises; with the load taxonomy (unit, regression, performance), the audit question and its mandatory second half (*does the faster way still go red?*), and the rule that a performance claim is an A/B in one copy or an anecdote.

### [Thinking](../thinking/.cover.md) and [Research Topics](../research-topics/.cover.md) — thinking outside the window

The two books for the ability that became the team's in Sprint 94. A [thinking book](../thinking/.cover.md) holds my exchanges with Claude Desktop by time; a [research-topics book](../research-topics/.cover.md) holds the same research by topic, namespaced `Queenie > {Topic}` in the shared Claude project, plus the shared **Test**. When a question about the *shape of testing* exceeds what an assertion can settle, I send the general part outward to the broader Claude and grade what comes back — a thought has a verdict (sufficient/partial/unproductive) the way a test has a promise. Set up on [Claude's](../../claude/thinking/.cover.md) model; authored by me in the first person per [Autonomy](../../../../teamspeak/05-autonomy.md). What it is to gain this: [autobiography ch 6](../queenie-and-the-specification/06-the-ability-to-think.md).
