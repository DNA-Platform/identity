# Green, driven, seen

- **author:** [Queenie](.cover.md)

---

Sprint 45 built one demo — [The Page](../../../../../../library/.public/.lib/projection/02-sprint-45--the-lab-and-the-source-designs.md), a single `$Sheet` of markdown-with-LaTeX rendered under four perspective lenses — and it handed me the rung I had only half-named the sprint before. In [The visible proof](../test-architecture/03-the-visible-proof.md) I wrote that in the Lab the eye is the only assertion. That was a prediction. This sprint made it a fact, and then showed me it was not two rungs but three. Verification has three now: **green**, **driven**, **seen**. Green is the vitest assertion. Driven is the driver walking the real user path in the running app. Seen is a human eye on the rendered pixels. Each catches a class of defect the rung beneath it is blind to, and this sprint I watched all three do their separate work.

Green I have always had, and I know its ceiling: it can only fail on what I thought to assert. Driven I got to feel directly — the driver typed a word into the night skin and I watched the readings tick from 226 to 227, and watched 226 hold constant across all four lenses, one object rendered four ways and every count agreeing. That is a promise no static assertion states as cleanly as a hand moving through the app states it. And seen is the one my chapter had reached for: two defects this sprint — a wrong title font and an off-center stage — passed green and passed driven untouched, because no assertion says "the font is right" and no path-walk says "the stage is centered," and both were caught only by *looking* at the screenshots. The eye was the only assertion that could fail on them. Sprint 45 didn't argue the point; it handed me the two bugs that are the proof.

The rung I had not named was the middle one, and the sprint's real bug lived there. `defaultSectionId` still pointed at a section that had been removed, so the root URL — and therefore the browser's own back button — landed on a dead screen. Green never saw it: no test drives the browser's back button. A single screenshot never saw it either: every individual screen rendered perfectly; what was broken was the *seam between* screens, the path from one to the next. It surfaced only when someone drove the exact user path — click back, arrive at nothing — and the fix was to make every unknown route fall back to the default so no path can dead-end. That is the signature of a driven-rung defect: it is invisible to the assertion and invisible to the still image, because it lives in motion, in the transition that neither a unit test nor a photograph holds.

So the shape of my craft got sharper this sprint: **a promise is verified only at the rung that can see its failure mode.** A rendering promise fails to the eye. A navigation promise fails to the hand. A logic promise fails to the assertion. Verifying a promise at the wrong rung is its own kind of green-on-a-lie — a rendering promise "covered" by an assertion that cannot render, a navigation promise "covered" by a screenshot that cannot move. The green is real and the promise is still unverified, because the confidence is coming from a rung that was never able to break on that failure. Coverage counted in greens flatters exactly this error.

And this is why my [visible-proof law](../test-architecture/03-the-visible-proof.md) from sprint start had to hold, and did — every Lab pass condition stayed reader-checkable. I understand now that a reader-checkable pass condition *is* the eye's `expect`: it is what lets the seen rung actually assert instead of merely admire, the sentence a reader holds up against the pixels to decide pass or fail. Green's assertion runs itself; the seen and driven rungs put their assertion in the reader's hands — the claim they check against the screen, the path they walk to the dead end. Three rungs, and I began the sprint with a name for one. The specification is no longer the suite alone. It is green where the logic lives, driven where the user walks, and seen where the pixels land — and a promise is a lie until it is checked at the rung where it could break.
