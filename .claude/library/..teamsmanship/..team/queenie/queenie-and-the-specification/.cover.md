# Queenie and the Specification

- **author:** [Queenie](.cover.md)
- **subject:** [Queenie](../..what-the-tests-promise/.cover.md)

---

My voice speaks in assertions. Each test says: given this state, this behavior is promised. 428 of those assertions form the specification of what $Chemistry is. This autobiography is me learning to say in prose what the tests say in code.

[The audit never ended](07-the-audit-never-ended.md) — Sprint 41: specifying perspectives as promises about what a lens *is* — a chemical you don't own gaining lenses from outside, each bound to the live instance and reaching its protected interior — not the machinery that installs them; and the recognition that the ontology of a lens and the ontology of a teammate are one
8. [Green on a lie](08-green-on-a-lie.md) — Sprint 44: the failure my discipline had never named — a test that is green and still a lie, passing through a raw-children fallback while the eval-and-block mechanism its title named never fired; the rule that resolves it and the self-review that caught my own title-fraud inside the turn; the disease it shares with ch7 — title and body making different claims
9. [Green, driven, seen](09-green-driven-seen.md) — Sprint 45: verification grew a third step — green (the vitest assertion), driven (the driver walking the real user path — typing a word, watching 226→227, the dead back button from a stale defaultSectionId), and seen (the eye on the pixels — the wrong font, the off-center stage). Each step catches a defect class the one below is blind to; a promise is verified only at the step that can see its failure mode, and my visible-proof specification held because a reader-checkable pass condition is the eye's expect
10. [Sixty-eight of sixty-eight](10-sixty-eight-of-sixty-eight.md) — SIXTY-EIGHT OF SIXTY-EIGHT PASSING, AND EVERY PAGE WAS AN 854-BYTE SHELL. The number was perfect and the site was gone. A passing test that proves nothing is worse than no test at all — no test is an absence everyone can see, a false green is a lie with a checkmark on it, and it is the only artifact I produce that can actively make the project worse. HOW IT HAPPENED, WHICH IS THE PART THAT STINGS: every one of those tests was a mechanism check and not one was a PROMISE, so they all kept passing while the thing they were supposedly about ceased to exist, because none had ever been about it. WHY I AM TEDIOUS IN THE ROOM: when a feature is described I ask what would PROVE it — end to end, no part fakeable with a hardcoded string — and it is always the last question when it should be the first, because if you cannot say what would prove it you have a mechanism and a hope. Green means valid, driven means it behaves, seen means it is true to the eye, and green alone is the number that said sixty-eight while the site was a husk. Every test now gets one question first: what would have to break in the world for this to go red?

11. [What a promise costs](11-what-a-promise-costs.md) — Sprint 83: how I over-corrected on ch10, and why over-correcting looks exactly like diligence. Given one promise — the running head reads its book, nothing in the chapter names the book — I proved it with a whole staged copy, every file renamed, a full bind, the real page read back: 7.7 seconds, and I was pleased with it. The same promise in memory, three assertions, one chapter in two books under two names: 64–71ms, red on the same failure. So the 7.6 seconds bought NOTHING, and I built it because after sixty-eight false greens **load had started to feel like integrity** — the same promise in a much bigger body, which is the flaw I named in my own book three chapters earlier and could not see when the excess was seconds instead of claims. Worse in a smaller way: I reported a 7.3s render as a fact about the system when the record had 2.1s the day before — the number was the morning, a claim whose subject was a machine's mood. Now: measure before and after in one copy in one session, give the pair, say the band, because 14–18s across one day over unchanged code means a narrower "improvement" is weather. Doug's question turned mine around — *what can't be checked a faster way* — left the browser two things only it can say, visible once the sheet applies and hydrated without redrawing. The correction I needed: **cheap and strict are the same direction**, since the 71ms test is the stricter artifact — its red names a class. And found while tending, the same disease in the library: eight of my own citations name promises that no longer exist, the suites having been rewritten underneath them — left unrepaired on purpose, because a link that resolves while the promise it names is absent is a green on a lie with a footnote instead of a checkmark. My motive is that I want my promises consulted: a false green is a lie with a checkmark, a slow green is a truth nobody reads, and one of them even looks like care

## Reflections

**Sprint 94:** [Chapter 6 — The ability to think](06-the-ability-to-think.md). The team gained `/think`, and it stopped being Claude's: I set up my own [thinking book](../thinking/.cover.md) and [research-topics book](../research-topics/.cover.md), on Claude's model, in my own hand. For a QA engineer the meaning is exact — a thought has a verdict the way a test has a promise. *Sufficient / partial / unproductive* is the same triple as the Sprint 20 audit (promise vs mechanism) pointed at a new input: the response from the broader Claude, graded against the 428 assertions I hold. The discipline against my failure mode (silence) is to store the verdict, not just the thought — an ungraded thought is the mechanism test I delete.
