Report: Telegram works without a VPN for some users in Russia, but reason is unknown
3:12 am, April 17, 2026 Source: iStories
Telegram users in Russia began reporting on the evening of April 16 that the blocked messenger had been working without a VPN for several hours, writing in channel comment sections and group chats, the independent Russian investigative outlet iStories reported. The reason is unknown.
Among those who noted this was Ksenia Boletskaya, Yandex’s director of industry relations, who wrote: “Strange, but Telegram is working for me right now without a VPN — it’s even loading video.”
The Telegram channel Bbbreaking reported, citing its subscribers, that Telegram had started working for them without a proxy or VPN connection.
Telegram was working without a VPN or proxy for 6 percent of users, a Meduza poll showed, while more than 60 percent of respondents said the messenger was working only with a VPN or proxy.
The international research project OONI recorded that on April 16, the messenger was accessible in 11 percent of cases, comparable to figures from the previous four days.
Roskomnadzor blocked Telegram in Russia in March. Before that, the agency had spent several months restricting access to Russia’s most popular messenger. Against that backdrop, the authorities stepped up their crackdown on VPNs.

No clear single cause has surfaced yet — the honest answer from the circumvention community and the monitoring projects is that this looks more like a localized TSPU artifact than a breakthrough. But there are several threads worth pulling on, in rough order of what I think is likely.
What Telegram has done recently
Durov announced on April 11 that Telegram upgraded its anti-censorship protocol and urged Russian users to update immediately. In his announcement, Durov urged all Russian users to update their apps immediately to maintain a stable connection. The surge in blocking triggered an immediate response from Telegram's engineering team, which deployed the upgraded anti-censorship protocol within a day of the blackout. No technical details were published. Reading between the lines (and from an open iOS client issue filed April 4), Pavel Durov stated that tens of millions of users continue using Telegram through VPNs, and that Telegram will keep adapting its traffic to make it harder to block. However, adapting TLS fingerprints alone is not enough. So the upgrade is almost certainly TLS-fingerprint randomization / obfuscated MTProto-layer tweaks, not a routing or infrastructure change. This is consistent with the partial, patchy, ~6–11% accessibility pattern — a fingerprint that doesn't match the current TSPU signature only helps until TSPU is retuned.
The bol-van / zapret picture is unambiguous and cuts against easy optimism
Active discussions on zapret2 over the last week are flatly pessimistic. The refrain from the maintainers and knowledgeable contributors: Telegram is now being blocked primarily by IP range, not by DPI signature, so DPI desync (zapret's core trick) does nothing. The advice in the April threads is "use VPN or proxy." One contributor specifically notes that when there's no proxy, Telegram runs MTProto over TCP straight to Telegram's own /22 subnets (91.108.x, 149.154.160/20, etc.) — and those subnets are what's being null-routed at TSPU. bol-van himself hasn't posted a victory lap; the February 10 "Zapret telegram" thread where users complained about media not loading was answered with "L2 speed shaping, zapret won't help."
So zapret-style DPI fooling is not what let Boletskaya load video.
The most likely explanation — TSPU is not monolithic
The Russian cyber lawyer Sarkis Darbinyan gave the clearest technical framing I've seen. When Telegram "improves," "Telegram runs on its own MTProto protocol, which the authorities have learned to detect quite successfully. Once its traffic is identified by signature, the TSPU system begins dropping every second or third data packet, which is felt as slowing and the inability to use some functions. In this context, accessibility means that TSPU is letting through somewhat more incoming and outgoing messenger packets."
Two specific reasons TSPU would suddenly pass more packets:
Capacity saturation. Zona.media reported a telecoms source saying current TSPU infrastructure does not always cope with the volume of traffic, meaning that blocked resources can occasionally become accessible. Evening hours on a Thursday would be exactly when this pressure peaks.
Providers routing around TSPU. Mediazona discovered in March that magistrates' courts in Moscow and St Petersburg had started handing down convictions against internet providers for allowing traffic to bypass TSPU — meaning some ISPs actively do this. A provider that briefly lets a customer segment's traffic skip TSPU will present exactly as "Telegram works without VPN, video loads," because media throttling is applied at TSPU. The 6% figure from Meduza's poll and the 11% from OONI are consistent with a subset of ASes / provider segments, not a nationwide change.
That your contact Boletskaya could load video is the most telling detail. Video inside MTProto is the packet class TSPU targets most aggressively — if video works, her traffic almost certainly isn't passing TSPU at that moment, or TSPU isn't matching her session to the MTProto signature. Either way, this is an infrastructure-side effect, not a Telegram-side one.
VPN providers
Nothing from Browsec specifically in the last 24 hours that I can find. The broader VPN-provider picture from the last week is defensive: Amnezia, Windscribe, and NymVPN have told TechRadar their products still work but are actively issuing new configs; Cloudflare WARP has been blocked in Russia since the March Cloudflare-subnet sweep; the April 15 deadline for the 20+ major Russian platforms to curb VPN users just hit, so the ecosystem is focused on VPN survival, not on narrating what happened to Telegram packets.
TON Foundation GitHub / TON Proxy
No evidence TON Proxy or TON Sites are involved in any of this. Those remain architectural features — Telegram's censorship-circumvention updates are happening in the messenger client itself (MTProto transport layer), not through TON's decentralized proxy mesh. If TON Proxy were ever switched on as the default client-side transport in hostile regions, that would be a genuinely different event from what we're seeing. I'd watch for it — it would be the strongest possible validation of your "steward-architect" thesis — but April 16 doesn't look like that moment. It looks like a ~6% TSPU hiccup that Meduza wrote up because Boletskaya commented.
One thing worth monitoring over the next 48 hours
If OONI's number stays in the 6–11% band but media-loading reports persist, that's a capacity/routing artifact. If the number climbs meaningfully and correlates with users on the latest app version, then the April 11 protocol update may actually be doing something on the DPI-signature side — in which case expect TSPU to retune within a week and close the gap again, as happened with VLESS in late 2025.
