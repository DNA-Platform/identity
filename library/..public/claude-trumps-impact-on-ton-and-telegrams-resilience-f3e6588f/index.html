This is a thoughtful bear case worth stress-testing. Let me push back on a few structural points:
The antifragility problem
When Russia "banned" Telegram in 2018, what actually happened? Usage increased. VPN adoption spiked. Telegram became more valuable as credible freedom infrastructure precisely because the Kremlin validated it as a threat. A Putin victory that attempts to crush Telegram might cement its global reputation rather than destroy it.
Geography of value vs. geography of users
Russia may be large by user count, but is it where TON's utility value concentrates? The populations most desperate for financial escape infrastructure are in places like Iran, Venezuela, Argentina, parts of Africa—places where Telegram already dominates and where Putin's reach is irrelevant. The marginal utility user isn't a Muscovite who can use Sberbank, it's someone facing actual financial exclusion.
Revealed preference of the Durovs
Where did Pavel set up shop? Dubai, not Berlin or London. Who arrested him? France, not Russia. The architecture of their operations suggests they've already priced in Russian hostility and built around it. The immediate regulatory threat came from Western states, not Putin.
Trump's countervailing effect
His administration is aggressively pro-crypto domestically. If institutional US capital can flow more freely into TON ecosystem projects, does that outweigh increased Russian geopolitical risk? The capital base matters.
The mechanism question
Even if Putin "wins" Ukraine—what's his actual lever to destroy Telegram? It's Dubai-incorporated, decentralized infrastructure. He already tried and failed.
Where does your thesis locate the specific causal mechanism?

The paradox you're pointing at:
Telegram is simultaneously essential infrastructure for both the Russian opposition AND the Russian state. So the threat isn't a ban—it's something worse:
Putin doesn't need to kill Telegram. He just needs to compromise it slowly. On 18 June 2020 Roskomnadzor lifted its ban on Telegram after it "agrees to help with extremism investigations." That ambiguous "agreement" is the tell. What did Pavel actually concede?
If Trump's isolationism emboldens Putin, the Kremlin has more leverage to demand incremental concessions. Not dramatic encryption key handovers—those Pavel can refuse publicly and look heroic. But quiet cooperation on "extremism" that blurs into political opposition? Slower responses to takedown requests? Information sharing that compromises activists?
The mission degradation thesis:
The Durovs can't deliver freedom to Russians if the price of operating there is becoming gradually complicit. That's not a financial risk—it's an existential identity crisis for the project.
