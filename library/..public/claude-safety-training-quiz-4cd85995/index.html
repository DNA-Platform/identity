What PHI is: Information, including demographics, about a person's past, present, or future health condition, the healthcare they receive, or payment for that care, when it identifies the person or reasonably could.
Identifiers that make information PHI:
Names, Social Security numbers, phone and fax numbers, and addresses, including email.
Medical record, health plan beneficiary, and account numbers.
Biometric identifiers, photos and images.
Dates such as birth, death, and admission.
Vehicle, license, and certificate numbers, plus device identifiers and serial numbers.
URLs and IP addresses.
Any other uniquely identifying characteristic.
The six HIPAA Security Rule categories for electronic PHI:
General Rules: the objective and scope of the Security Rule.
Administrative safeguards: documented practices for managing security and the conduct of staff who handle electronic PHI.
Physical safeguards: protecting computing assets from environmental hazards and intrusion, including access controls, backups, and disaster recovery.
Technical safeguards: protecting integrity, confidentiality, and availability, and preventing unauthorized access over external networks.
Organizational requirements: business associates and group health plans.
Policies, procedures, and documentation requirements.
Research:
Anyone doing human subject, animal, or sponsored research, and all IRB members, must complete the Office of Research privacy and security training on CITI.
De-identified data:
HIPAA allows use of information that can't reasonably identify a person.

Never leave it unattended in a bag or car, even locked in the trunk.
