Not a dumb question at all — you're asking whether you can arbitrage between decentralized compute markets. The logic being: rent cheap GPU time on Akash, point it at Cocoon, collect TON.
The honest answer is: theoretically interesting, probably not practically profitable, and there's one hard technical blocker.
The blocker is the TEE requirement. Cocoon doesn't just need GPU compute — it specifically needs GPUs running inside Intel TDX trusted execution environments with attestation. That's specialized hardware configuration, not just any GPU you can rent. Akash's provider network is general-purpose — most nodes are not TEE-configured, and you'd have no way to verify attestation on rented hardware even if they were. Cocoon's whole privacy guarantee depends on hardware-level verification that the enclave is genuine. You can't fake that on rented infrastructure.
So the arbitrage path is blocked at the hardware layer, not the economics layer.
What could work conceptually is the reverse direction — if you owned TEE-capable hardware, you could rent itout on Akash or similar when Cocoon demand is low, and redirect it to Cocoon when TON yield is higher. The hardware is the asset, and you're optimizing which network pays best at any given moment. That's actually how sophisticated node operators in DePIN networks think about yield.
But renting someone else's compute to farm Cocoon — the TEE requirement closes that door.
