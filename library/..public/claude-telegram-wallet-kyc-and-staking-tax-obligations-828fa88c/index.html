This is actually a genuinely unsettled question, and your specific situation with tsTON makes it even more interesting.
The IRS position under Revenue Ruling 2023-14 is that staking rewards are taxable as income when received — not just when sold. So under the standard interpretation, if you stake and receive new tokens as rewards, that's income at fair market value the moment you get them, and then selling later creates a separate capital gains event on top of that.
However — your situation is different from vanilla staking. With Tonstakers' liquid staking model, you didn't receive discrete reward tokens dripping into your wallet. You deposited TON, got tsTON back, and the tsTON just gradually appreciates in value relative to TON. You haven't "received" anything new since January 5. The rewards are embedded in the exchange rate.
This creates a real ambiguity. There's a reasonable argument that:
You received tsTON on Jan 5 (taxable event — income equal to the fair value of tsTON received, which was roughly equal to the TON deposited, so negligible income)
Everything since then is unrealized appreciation of the tsTON token
You'd owe capital gains only when you unstake or swap tsTON back to TON
That said, the IRS hasn't specifically addressed liquid staking tokens, and aggressive interpretations could treat the accruing value as constructive receipt of income. It's a gray area that tax practitioners are actively debating.
Practically speaking, the most defensible position is probably to treat the unstaking event as the realization — but I'm not a tax advisor, and given your situation in Georgia this may be more of an academic question anyway.
