The part worth writing up is the inescapability argument, not the possibility argument. Those are different claims with very different burdens, and you named both in one sentence.
Possibility means: aboutness could be an ontological primitive, here's what an ontology containing it would look like. That's a positive metaphysical claim. It needs an account of what primitive aboutness is, and it invites every "this collapses into functionalism/information theory" objection. That's Part II, and it's expensive.
Inescapability is cheap and lands harder: neither party to the debate can state their own position without deploying aboutness. Chalmers can't specify what the zombie is missing — your line 1177 argument, that a complete physical ontology articulates absence via presence, so an absence with no describable presence is a subtraction that can't be stated inside the ontology doing the stating. And Dennett can't say the phenomenal terms fail to refer without helping himself to reference. Failure-to-refer is an aboutness predicate. That's your "Multiple Drafts contains almost no photons" observation turned into an argument instead of a joke.
So the writeup is symmetric, short, and says almost nothing metaphysical: the physical-incompleteness reading of the hard problem is conducted in an ontology that already admits aboutness, by both sides. Which is the modest register you keep insisting on.
Where it has to do real work: Dennett's reply is already built. The intentional idiom is instrumentally indispensable and ontologically non-committal, and indispensability arguments have a long history of being refused on exactly those grounds. So the piece either blocks that move or explicitly concedes it and narrows to "the debate cannot be conducted from within physical closure," which is a claim about discourse rather than about the world. I'd argue the hallucination case is your blocker (the "as" relation survives the missing referent), but that's a choice you should make before I draft anything, because it determines whether this is three pages or fifteen.
Structurally it isn't Part I or Part II. It's the hinge — what licenses the move to Part II without asserting Part II's conclusion.
