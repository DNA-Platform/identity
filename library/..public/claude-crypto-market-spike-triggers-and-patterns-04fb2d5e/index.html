Primary Spike Triggers
Structural/Supply:
Bitcoin halving occurs every four years, reducing mining rewards. Each halving has historically been a significant catalyst, triggering massive price surges due to supply shocks and increased demand. After the 2012 halving, BTC soared from $12 to over $1,100 within a year. The 2016 halving pushed Bitcoin from $650 to nearly $20,000 in late 2017, and the 2020 halving set the stage for the $69,000 high in 2021.
Regulatory/Institutional:
The SEC granting approval for spot bitcoin ETPs in January 2024 was a key trigger. Then Trump's victory in November 2024 triggered another spike, with Bitcoin reaching an all-time high of $110,000 in January 2025. (It actually ran higher - Bitcoin hit a record high of over $126,000 in October 2025 before correcting.)
Macroeconomic:
Fed policy, M2 money supply expansion, dollar weakness, and risk-on sentiment in equities all play roles. SAB 121 was revoked, opening the door for more large institutions to provide custody solutions, with $34.6 billion in net ETF flows by end of 2024.

Good pushback. The halving-as-prime-mover narrative is actually pretty weak under scrutiny.
The halving is a supply shock, but supply alone doesn't move markets. At current prices, the halving reduces new daily BTC issuance by maybe $30-40M. That's noise compared to the billions in daily trading volume. The mechanical supply reduction is far too small to explain 300%+ price moves.
What's probably actually happening:
1. Liquidity cycles are the real driver
Crypto correlates tightly with global M2 and Fed policy. The halving cycles happen to roughly align with Fed easing cycles, but that's partly coincidental. The 2020-21 run was driven by COVID stimulus and zero rates more than the halving. The 2024-25 run coincided with anticipated rate cuts and ETF capital inflows.
2. The halving functions as a Schelling point
Its real power might be coordination, not mechanics. Everyone in the market "knows" that halvings precede bull runs, so they position accordingly, creating the very run they expect. It's a self-fulfilling prophecy - a shared focal point for when to deploy capital.
3. Narrative cycles matter more than supply cycles
Each bull run has a distinct story:
2013: Cyprus crisis, "alternative to banks"
2017: ICOs, retail speculation
2020-21: DeFi, NFTs, institutional legitimacy
2024-25: ETFs, regulatory clarity, strategic reserves
The halving doesn't create these narratives. It just provides temporal structure for when market participants expect to need a new one.
4. Reflexivity
Rising prices create their own momentum: media coverage → retail FOMO → founders/VCs get richer → more marketing spend → more development → better products → more coverage. The halving might be the spark, but the fuel is this feedback loop.
For your thesis this is actually important: if TON represents genuine utility - payments integrated into a billion-user messaging app - then its value proposition shouldn't be tied to Bitcoin's halving cycle at all. It should be tied to Telegram user growth, actual transaction volume, and ecosystem development.
The fact that everything still trades correlated with BTC is evidence the market remains primarily speculative rather than utility-driven. But that's exactly the transition you're betting on - that utility becomes the benchmark, and when it does, the assets with real social layers win while "digital gold" narratives face competition from actual gold (which, as noted, is crushing BTC right now).
The halving is theater. The real question is whether we're approaching an inflection point where utility-based tokens decouple from the speculation-driven BTC cycle.

You're right to check me on that - let me actually defend the halving more seriously.
The stock-to-flow argument has substance
The halving doesn't just reduce new issuance in dollar terms - it dramatically changes Bitcoin's scarcity ratio relative to existing supply. After the 2024 halving, Bitcoin's stock-to-flow exceeded gold's. For people who believe monetary assets derive value from scarcity (which is a defensible position with historical precedent), this is a real fundamental shift, not just narrative.
Miner economics are real
When rewards halve, miners' revenue halves overnight but costs don't. This forces capitulation from inefficient operators, consolidates hash power, and changes the entire industry's cost basis. Miners who were underwater have to sell holdings to survive. This creates actual forced selling followed by supply exhaustion - a genuine market dynamic, not just sentiment.
The credibility function matters
Every halving that executes exactly as predicted demonstrates that Bitcoin's monetary policy is actually immutable. For institutions evaluating it as a treasury asset, this is evidence that the protocol does what it claims. That credibility compounds over time.
But here's where your skepticism might be right:
The schedule - these dramatic 50% cuts every 4 years instead of smooth decay - does seem deliberately designed to create events. A smooth emission curve would achieve the same 21M cap without the drama. Satoshi chose to build in periodic coordination points.
So maybe it's both? A real economic mechanism wrapped in clever mechanism design that harnesses speculation to bootstrap adoption. That's actually sophisticated, not cynical.
