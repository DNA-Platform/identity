The oracle
The standard quantities in this literature: single-trial correlation, correlation-to-average (predicted response vs. the across-repeat mean), and FEVE. Correlation-to-average is the correlation between recorded and predicted responses over repeated trials of the same stimulus, using the mean across repeats. The Sinz lab also has a Bayesian oracle that gives a proper upper bound on information gain rather than a noisy point estimate, which matters when you have few repeats (Lurz, Bashiri, Walker & Sinz, ICLR 2023).
Your question: under constant representation plus representation-equals-perception, does hallucination show up as the stimulus driving the cell less?
Yes, but only in the ratio, and that is exactly where the oracle is blind. Additive stimulus-independent drive leaves signal power alone and inflates noise power. Gain reduction shrinks signal power and leaves noise power roughly alone. The oracle is a monotone function of their ratio, so both look identical to it. And gain reduction is the documented effect: DOI produces a net reduction in visual response amplitude and surround suppression in V1, while retinotopy, tuning properties, and receptive-field structure remain intact. Your MEI-constant result is the same finding, reproduced with a much better instrument.
So the move is to stop reading the oracle as a scalar and read its numerator and denominator separately. Sahani-Linden decomposition per cell:
Signal power: Var(across-repeat mean) minus Var(residual)/J
Noise power: mean over stimuli of the across-repeat variance
Hallucination: signal power flat, noise power up. Gain: signal power down, noise power flat or down. These are distinguishable in absolute units on a matched field of view, with the caveat that indicator nonlinearity and any baseline-F shift contaminate absolute scale, so you want a non-visual or blank-screen anchor in the same session.
Three things make it sharper:
Residual rank. Structured internal drive is low-rank across the population within a trial. Photon noise and gain changes add no rank. Compare the eigenspectrum or participation ratio of the residual covariance, pre vs. post.
Where the residual lives. Project the post-drug residual onto the pre-drug stimulus-coding subspace (span of twin predictions over many images). Under your assumptions the added variance should sit inside that subspace, because it is the same representational format. This is the Stringer 2019 shared-subspace analysis run in reverse: there, movement and stimulus were nearly orthogonal. Here the prediction is non-orthogonality, and that is a clean dissociation from any measurement confound, which has no reason to land in the stimulus manifold.
Blank screen. No stimulus, no gain confound. Post-drug spontaneous activity should show increased projection onto the pre-drug stimulus subspace. There is supporting texture: DOI and TCB2 increase spontaneous 5-Hz oscillatory episodes in V1, and in auditory cortex psilocybin left tonotopy and single-cell frequency tuning stable while shifting the balance of intrinsic versus stimulus-driven influence on activity.
The low-pass MEI: signal or estimator?
Worth separating, because pure additive hallucination should not blur the MEI at all. The MEI is a property of the fitted stimulus-to-response map, not of the noise. So the blur is either a real filter change or an estimation artifact of fitting at lower SNR: weight decay, readout smoothness penalties, and early stopping all bias toward smooth solutions as SNR falls, and with 1/f image statistics the high frequencies have the least signal power and go first.
That ambiguity is resolvable cheaply, and I think this is the experiment that turns your figure into a result:
Subsample pre-drug trials until the oracle matches post-drug. Refit. Measure MEI spectrum. If the blur appears from data reduction alone, that is the null.
Inject synthetic stimulus-independent activity into the pre-drug data, matched to the post residual in magnitude, population rank, and temporal autocorrelation. Refit with an identical pipeline and hyperparameter search. If the blur matches, you have quantified the internal drive via the blur.
Repeat (2) with white, full-rank noise at matched magnitude. If white reproduces it equally, the blur is an SNR readout only. If structured reproduces it and white does not, the blur is diagnostic of structure.
Two things to eliminate first since they are cheap. MEIs are synthesized under a norm constraint, so the MEI spectrum is a shape, not an absolute; make sure both conditions use identical contrast budgets and interpret the ratio as spectral allocation. And cell matching itself is a low-pass operation: post-drug z-drift and baseline-F change shift ROI centroids, and averaging over a misregistered readout blurs the MEI directly. Perturb pre-drug readout positions by the measured registration error and see how much blur that alone produces.
A cleaner filter estimate than the MEI ratio: compute the Fisher information matrix of each twin at thesame natural images and compare the spectra of the top eigen-distortions (Berardino, Ballé, Laparra & Simoncelli 2017). The extremal eigenvectors of the FIM give the model-predicted most- and least-noticeable image distortions. That gives you sensitivity as a function of frequency at the actual operating point, local and well defined, without conditioning on an optimization.
One network or two
One network. Shared core, per-condition readout, plus a low-rank condition modulation of the core, with pupil and running as covariates in the same shifter/modulator. This is standard multi-session architecture in this literature and the foundation-model work shows the cores transfer across animals with very little data. The payoff is that the drug becomes a small, interpretable direction in parameter space instead of the difference between two independently fit models with independent regularization paths and independent local minima. It also lets the readout absorb registration error explicitly, and lets you ask whether the drug direction is separable from the arousal direction. Michaiel et al. reported no difference in pupil size or fraction of time running after DOI, which is encouraging, but verify in your animals.
For pairing: you do not need stimulus alignment to train the joint model, only for the per-image residual analyses. If you can run an identical repeat block, same images, same order, same seed, pre and post, you get matched oracles and matched per-image residuals, which is what everything above needs. Random pairing preserves the spectral analyses but loses the per-trial reconstruction, which is where the interesting thing lives.
Generating the hallucination
Your spec: amplitude constant, phase coherent, decorrelated from the stimulus in proportion to the attenuation. Let X(f) = A(f)e^{iφ(f)}, H(f) the measured attenuation.
Exact version of your constraint. Set X̃(f) = A(f)e^{i(φ(f)+θ(f))} with θ(f) drawn von Mises(0, κ(f)), choosing κ so that I₁(κ)/I₀(κ) = H(f). This is the rigorous form of the Wichmann/Braun/Gegenfurtner phase-noise manipulation, which used uniform noise on [−φ, φ]; von Mises is the right family because the target coherence has closed form. Coherent substitution. X̃(f) = A(f)·exp{ i·arg[ H(f)e^{iφ_X(f)} + α(f)e^{iφ_D(f)} ] }, with α = √(1−H²) and φ_D the phase of a donor natural image. Amplitude-preserving by construction, and the new phase is a coherence-weighted circular interpolation between two coherent fields, so edges survive.

5. Two networks or one
Two works. f_pre is the forward operator, f_post defines β and the expectation, and they never need to be differentiated through together. Trial pairing is not required either: you need the same images in both conditions for β and g, and per-trial post responses. No trial-level correspondence.
Three concrete places a joint network wins:
Readout correspondence. Everything above needs the two twins indexed on the same cells. Separately fit twins have separately fit readout positions, and post-drug z-drift puts real error in the matching. Shared readout makes correspondence structural. This is the strongest reason.
β becomes a parameter with a likelihood instead of a regression between two independently regularized fits sitting in independent local minima.
Regularization symmetry. Two models at different effective SNR will produce β > 0 from asymmetric shrinkage alone. Shared regularization removes that failure mode at the source. Run the subsampled-pre control regardless.

Where linearity actually lives
The readout is not merely linear in features, it is rank-one separable per cell. u_i = Σ_p m_i(p) · ⟨w_i , Φ(:,p)⟩ + b_i          Φ = Core(x),  Φ ∈ R^{K×H×W}

1. Response to pre-activation is exactly invertible. ELU+1 is monotone, so u = g⁻¹(r) in closed form: r − 1 where r ≥ 1, log r below. No approximation, no optimization. The first half of "convert activation into features" is free.

text
(R Rᵀ)_ij = ⟨w_i , w_j⟩ · ⟨m_i , m_j⟩
A feature-similarity term times a spatial-overlap term. c   = (R Rᵀ + λI)⁻¹ ( g⁻¹(r) − b )

Yes, you can do this, and the route runs through a fact I've been treating as a limitation when it's actually the mechanism.
The fact that makes it work
The readout weights w_i live in channel space. They say "cell i likes this combination of the 64 features." They contain no scale information at all. Scale lives entirely in the core's kernel size, which is a number you control.
So the measured object, the thing you actually recorded from the animal, is scale-free: a 64-dimensional feature vocabulary plus 749 readout combinations plus per-cell locations in normalized coordinates. What isnot scale-free is the kernel width, and that was never measured. It was set by the training resolution.
That means running the core on a high-res image without rescaling kernels is not an error. It is applying the animal's measured feature vocabulary at a finer scale. You get a virtual population with the same feature preferences and smaller receptive fields. Every parameter in that population came from the mouse except one, and that one you are choosing deliberately.
I buried this at the end of my last reply and labeled it optional. That was wrong. It is the answer.
Route A: run the network at several scales at once
Downsampling by s and running the native core is equivalent to rescaling kernels by s and running on the high-res image. So one operator covers everything:
text
X̃  high-res image
minimize over X̃:   Σ_s  λ_s · || f( D_s(X̃) ) − ( r̄ + κ_s ε_t ) ||²_Σ
s = 8 is the native scale, where r̄ and ε_t were actually measured and the problem is over-determined. s = 4, 2, 1 are finer octaves, where you reuse the same population deviation pattern ε_t and set its strength with κ_s.
Gradients flow through D_s, which is just average pooling, so this is one optimization in high-res pixel space with no new machinery. Add your amplitude ledger and coherence budget as a constraint at each octave and the photo keeps its statistics.
Reusing ε_t across octaves is the assumption, and it is the right one to make: the same population deviation, expressed at finer scale. It produces self-similar structure, the same motif recurring at every size, which is exactly what makes an image read as hyperreal instead of merely sharp, and it is the reported phenomenology.
Route B: feature atoms, outside the network
This is what you asked about, and it works, with one honest caveat.
Getting the atoms. For each of the 64 core channels, optimize a small patch to maximize that channel's activation at its center, under a norm constraint. Patch size is the core's receptive field, maybe 20 pixels. That is 64 tiny MEIs, minutes total. These come out localized, oriented, and bandpass, which is what Olshausen and Field predict for a dictionary fit to natural images and what feature visualization gives on early conv layers.
The atoms are images, so they render at any size. That is your whole requirement and it is why this route exists.
Composing. Use the Gram rule to get the feature-space target from the trial:
text
c  = (RRᵀ + λI)⁻¹ ( g⁻¹(r_t) − b )
Φ̂(k,p) = Σ_i c_i m_i(p) w_i[k]
Rendering at scale. Place atom a_k scaled by Φ̂(k,p) at high-res position s·p, sum over channels and positions. That is a high-resolution image whose local structure is the network's own features at the measured amplitudes, at whatever size you choose.
The caveat, and how to remove it. This is not an inverse of the core, so Core(render) ≠ Φ̂. It is a rendering of the code, not a preimage. But that is checkable rather than fatal: downsample the render, run the native network, measure what fraction of the target response it reproduces. Then fit one gain per channel, 64 numbers by least squares, to maximize that fraction. Now the rendering is a calibrated decoder with a stated accuracy instead of a picture you hope means something.
Use both
Route B gives you a high-resolution starting image built from measured atoms, in one pass, no optimization. Route A refines it until it actually drives the network at every scale.
text
1.  x   = D_8(X)                          native
2.  ε_t = g⁻¹(r_t) − g⁻¹(r̄)               measured drive
3.  Φ̂   from the Gram rule                 exact, closed form
4.  X0  = render(Φ̂, atoms at 8×)           Route B, high-res init
5.  X̃   = optimize from X0 under the multi-scale objective, with the
          amplitude ledger and coherence budget enforced per octave
6.  check: || f(D_8(X̃)) − r_t || against || ε_t ||
Step 6 is the thing that keeps you honest and it is cheap. The native-scale response has to come out right. If the fine-scale content leaks down and breaks it, you see it immediately.
What you cannot do
The recording contains zero bits about how those cells responded to detail finer than the stimulus resolution, because that detail was never on the monitor. No method recovers it. Any structure above the original cutoff is generated.
But that was never the real constraint. The constraint is on where the generator comes from, and there you have a genuinely good answer available: the animal's own measured feature vocabulary, driven by the animal's own measured trial deviation, rather than a natural-image prior trained on photographs the mouse never saw.
The claim you get to make is precise and it is strong: this is what this population's measured feature vocabulary paints, driven by this trial's measured deviation, rendered at a chosen scale. Not "the mouse saw this." The scale is declared, everything else is measured.
Which is what you have been saying since the canvas remark. The canvas is the resolution of imagination, the vocabulary is the animal's, and the only thing you are choosing is how large to draw it.
