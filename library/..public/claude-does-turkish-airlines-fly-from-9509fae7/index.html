Here's what I found on all your questions:
Turkish Airlines: Antalya to Tbilisi
Turkish Airlines does fly the Antalya–Tbilisi route, but it doesn't operate nonstop flights. It offers connecting flights (typically through Istanbul), with around 289 connecting flights per week. For nonstop service on this route, Pegasus and SunExpress are the carriers — Pegasus offers about 7 nonstop flights per week, and SunExpress has 3 per week. The flight is only about 2–2.5 hours direct.
Pegasus and Turkish Airlines: No ownership relationship
They are completely independent companies. Pegasus Airlines is owned by the Turkish private equity firm Esas Holding AS, which was acquired in 2005 by the Sabancı family. Turkish Airlines is majority-owned by the Turkish government (through the Turkey Wealth Fund). They are competitors — Pegasus is Turkey's leading low-cost carrier while Turkish Airlines is the full-service flag carrier. Neither owns any part of the other. Pegasus does have codeshare agreements with several airlines, but the relationship with Turkish is purely that they're both Turkish-based carriers competing on similar routes.
Pegasus baggage for a move
This is where it gets interesting for your situation. Most Pegasus flights (including Turkey to Georgia) use the "Weight Concept" rather than a piece concept. This means you get a total weight allowance and can split it across as many bags as you like, as long as each individual piece stays under 32 kg. The standard checked dimension limit is 158 cm linear (length + width + height).
The system works by purchasing extra kilograms online in advance. Buying online in advance could cost anywhere from €25 to €50 for 20 kg on a European route, while paying at the airport could run €10–15 per kg. You can keep adding weight in increments (5 kg, 10 kg, 20 kg chunks).
However, here's the catch for a move: I couldn't find a published hard cap on the number of individual pieces — it's all weight-based. The practical limit is whatever total kilos you purchase, split across bags each under 32 kg. If you recall being able to check 8–10 items, that would be consistent with buying, say, 200+ kg of allowance and splitting it across many bags. You'd want to call the Pegasus call center directly (they're 24/7) to confirm the maximum number of pieces they'll accept at check-in for a single passenger, because even with unlimited purchased weight, there may be practical limits on how many individual items they'll process.
For the Antalya→Tbilisi move, my suggestion: Since you're looking at a family of four travelers, buying significant extra baggage across multiple tickets on Pegasus could let you move a lot of boxes at once on that direct nonstop flight. Also worth noting that Pegasus has a cargo service (Pegasus Cargo) — for a move, it might be worth checking whether shipping some boxes as cargo is more cost-effective than excess baggage fees. The call center number from Turkey is 0888 228 12 12.
