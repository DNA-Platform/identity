Classically, it effectively is XOR. A ⊕ ¬A is also a tautology, and it is equivalent to two separate claims joined together:
Excluded middle: A ∨ ¬A (at least one holds)
Non-contradiction: ¬(A ∧ ¬A) (at most one holds)
Since non-contradiction already rules out the "both" case, inclusive OR loses nothing, and stating LEM with ∨ just isolates the "at least one" half.
The split earns its keep because the two halves can fail independently, and that is exactly where the major non-classical logics diverge:
Intuitionistic logic keeps non-contradiction but drops excluded middle. You can't assert A ∨ ¬A without a proof of one side.
Paraconsistent logics like Priest's LP keep excluded middle but drop non-contradiction. Some A can be both true and false without everything exploding.
Classical logic keeps both, which together give you the XOR.
If LEM were stated as XOR, it would bundle two independent commitments into one axiom, and you couldn't express those logics by removing one.
A smaller, practical reason is that ∨ is the standard primitive in most axiomatizations. ⊕ is usually defined from ∨, ∧, and ¬, so it's more natural to state the axioms in primitives.

You're pointing at a real gap, and which side of it you're on depends on whether "or" is read as a statement about truth or about proof.
Your reading (provability): "G or ¬G" means ⊢G or ⊢¬G. Gödel shows that fails for a consistent, sufficiently strong theory, since neither G nor ¬G is provable. So read this way, incompleteness is precisely a failure of excluded middle, at the level of provability, and of XOR too, since neither side holds.
The classical reading (object language): PA still proves the single formula G ∨ ¬G, because it's an instance of the LEM schema. What fails is the disjunction property: proving A ∨ B doesn't let you prove A or prove B. Classical theories lack that property, and incompleteness is a vivid demonstration. In the standard model of arithmetic, G is in fact true; the theory just can't show it.
So the two claims are:
⊢ (G ∨ ¬G): holds
(⊢G) ∨ (⊢¬G): fails
This gap is essentially the intuitionist's complaint. Under the BHK interpretation, asserting A ∨ B means having a proof of one disjunct, so an unprovable, unrefutable G is exactly a case where they'd refuse A ∨ ¬A. Intuitionistic theories like Heyting arithmetic have the disjunction property, which is the formal version of your intuition that a disjunction should carry its witness.
Classical logic keeps LEM by decoupling truth from provability. Incompleteness is the price of that decoupling: truth outruns proof.

ωGap(⌜φ⌝) := ∀n Prov(⌜φ(n̄)⌝) ∧ ¬Prov(⌜∀x φ(x)⌝)
It is a well-formed Π₂ formula. In the standard model it says exactly what you want: every instance is derivable, the universal is not.
Applied to the natural witness
Let φ(x) = "x is not a derivation of 0=1." Then ∀x φ(x) is Con, which is G.
First conjunct: PA proves ∀n Prov(⌜φ(n̄)⌝) outright. If n isn't a derivation of 0=1, PA can verify that by checking. If it is, then 0=1 is derivable, so everything is, including φ(n). Either way the instance is derivable, and PA can carry out that case split internally.
Second conjunct: ¬Prov(⌜Con⌝). This is the whole content.
Putting these together, PA proves ωGap(⌜φ⌝) ↔ Con ↔ G.
What that means in your world
The ω-gap property of the system's own non-contradiction sentence is that non-contradiction. The gap between "all instances" and "the universal" isn't a separate phenomenon from LNC_S; it is LNC_S, seen from the quantifier side.
