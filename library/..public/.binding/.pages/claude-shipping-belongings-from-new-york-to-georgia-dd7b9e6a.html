Option 2: LCL ocean freight via a US freight forwarder (cheapest for your volume).
LCL = Less-than-Container Load, where you share a 40-foot container with other shippers and pay only for your cubic meters. You don't need to be a business to ship overseas household goods with a US freight forwarder; AMID Logistics explicitly serves "ordinary shippers looking to send relatively large items from the USA" alongside commercial customers.
How it works for your shipment:
Get an instant online quote at oceanfreightusa.com (also internationalshippingusa.com — same company, AMID Logistics, FMC-licensed).
They pick up your boxes on Long Island OR you drop them at the nearest CFS (Container Freight Station) at the Port of NY/NJ.
Goes by sea to Poti or Batumi (Georgia's Black Sea ports), then by truck to Tbilisi.
Transit time: typically 5–8 weeks port-to-port plus inland.
Likely cost structure for 1.5 CBM: ocean freight ~$300–600, US origin charges ~$200–400, Georgian destination/handling charges ~$300–600, plus 18% VAT on declared CIF value. Total freight side: roughly$800–$1,600. Cheaper than USA2Georgia, but you're more on your own.
The catch: with LCL there's no door delivery included — your cargo is shipped to a warehouse in the destination city, and the ocean carrier's overseas agent guides you through customs release and pickup. You'd need to arrange Tbilisi-side pickup yourself or pay the destination agent extra. Given you're already in Tbilisi with local resources, this is workable. You'll also need a Georgian Tax ID to clear customs — fast to get at any RS Service Centre.
What to verify: get an actual quote with the destination charges spelled out (this is where freight forwarders sometimes hide cost). Local destination charges for personal-effects LCL typically run $250–$600 or higher, but you want yours in writing.
Option 3: Seven Seas Worldwide — sometimes used for moves like yours, but verify Georgia coverage.
They have a New York depot, deliver empty boxes to your home, collect filled, and they're explicitly individual-focused (expats, students, returning travelers). Their US depots are in Los Angeles, New York and San Francisco, and they help around 40,000 people a year transport personal effects door-to-door.
Two reasons I'm putting them third, not first: (1) I haven't been able to confirm they actually serve Georgia — Tbilisi/Georgia doesn't appear on their main destination lists, which feature Australia, UK, Europe, etc. (2) Their Yelp aggregate is poor and complaints often center on destination-side delivery problems, exactly your weak link being far from the US.
Worth getting an instant quote from their site to compare, but assume Georgia may not be in their network. If it isn't, skip them.
What I'd actually do in your position.
Get quotes from both USA2Georgia and AMID Logistics in parallel this week. Decision criteria:
If the price difference is small (~$300 or less), pay the premium for USA2Georgia because they manage the Georgian customs side, which is the part that goes wrong.
If LCL comes in materially cheaper and the destination agent in Georgia is a real entity you can call before booking, take LCL — you have the Tbilisi infrastructure to handle pickup.
Either way: don't combine with airline check-in. Pick one channel for the bulk and only carry what you actually need on hand at arrival.
Three things to handle before any provider can quote accurately:
Detailed valued inventory list. Georgian customs requires the value of every item — for new items, what you paid; for used items with resale value (electronics), eBay prices; for items without resale value (used socks, documents), a symbolic $1. If your valuations seem about right, customs typically doesn't open most boxes; if you try to lowball, they may revalue everything against you.
Weigh and measure 2–3 representative bins. This lets every quote come back accurate instead of "from $X."
Ask each provider, in writing, the exact question that bit you with Turkish Cargo: "I am an individual moving personal household effects, not a business. The shipper and consignee are both me, the same name, no company involvement. Can you accept this booking, and if so, please confirm in your reply." If they hedge, move on.
One last note worth flagging: household goods and personal effects are treated as commercial cargo for Georgian customs purposes and subject to import duties, fees, and VAT — there's no automatic "moving household" exemption like the US has for returning citizens. The 18% VAT is real and standard. Budget for it on top of shipping.
