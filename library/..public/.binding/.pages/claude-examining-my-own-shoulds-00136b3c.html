HOW RUSSIAN CUSTODY COURTS VIEW BLOCKED COMMUNICATION:
The parent without custody is normally entitled to contact with the child. The custodial parent must abide by any respective court judgment and cannot prevent the other parent's right of access to the child.
The non-custodial parent has the right to get involved in the upbringing of the child and to communicate with him/her.
The parent residing apart from the child shall have the right to communicate with the child. The parent with whom the child resides should not interfere with communication of the child with the other parent, if such communication does not harm the physical and mental health of the child.
The critical clause is: IF such communication does not harm the child. But proving harm requires evidence.
The only situation mentioned under Russian law is "creating an obstacle to communication with the second parent." The fine is up to several thousand Rubles.

Only around 3 percent of domestic violence cases make it to court. Official studies suggest that only around 10 percent of survivors of domestic violence in Russia report incidents to the police.
In such cases, the injured party bears the burden of gathering all evidence necessary for prosecution and must pay all costs of the prosecution.
