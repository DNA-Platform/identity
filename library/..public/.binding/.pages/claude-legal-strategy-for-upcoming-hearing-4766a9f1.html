Your notes reveal a really important strategic tension that I think deserves careful thought before the Feb 5 hearing.
The core conflict I'm seeing:
The lawyers are preparing to fight the Russian case comprehensively - interim custody, demonstrating Georgia stability, engaging Georgian guardianship authorities. This is good lawyering for winning in Russian court.
But your instinct seems to be: we may not win in Russian court regardless of how well we prepare, and revealing Georgia burns our one safe location. If Max gets a favorable ruling and knows exactly where you are, what then?
The specific tension points:
Interim custody application (2.2) - This essentially requires proving you have a suitable residence in Georgia. You can't get interim custody "without specifying an address" and simultaneously engage Georgian guardianship authorities to inspect the apartment. Those two things are in direct conflict.
The narrative about Georgia - The lawyers want to tell the court Ana is "temporarily" in Georgia. But once that's on the record, it's on the record. And "temporary" may invite questions about return.
Georgian guardianship engagement - Once Georgian authorities are involved and producing documents for Russian courts, you've created a trail.
Questions worth discussing:
What's the actual enforcement mechanism if Max gets custody and you're in Georgia? Russia isn't in Hague with Georgia, correct?
Is the Russian case outcome actually determinative, or is it primarily evidence-building for Georgian humanitarian status?
Can you partially engage - submit some materials but decline interim custody?
What's your current thinking on the hierarchy of priorities here?
