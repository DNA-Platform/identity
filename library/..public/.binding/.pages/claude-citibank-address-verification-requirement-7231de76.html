Short version: yes, this could be a real problem for you — but the danger isn't the one the letter is pointing at. The letter wants you to "fix" your address. For someone living abroad, fixing it the obvious way (entering your Georgian address) is the move most likely to get the account closed.
A few things, separated by what I can and can't verify.
Is the letter legitimate? The underlying requirement is genuine — US banks are required under Customer Identification Program rules to keep a physical residential street address on file rather than a PO box or mail-forwarding service, so the premise checks out. The number printed in it, 1-888-248-4226, does appear to be a real Citi phone support line. So the letter is plausibly real. But I can't confirm from the text alone that this specific notice actually came from Citi and sits on your account, and scam letters copy this format exactly — sometimes even using real phone numbers. So don't act on the letter itself: don't call the number printed on it, don't click anything. Log into the Citi app or call the number on the back of your card and ask them to confirm the notice and the wire restriction are genuine.
The actual risk — this is the part that matters. Citi's standard US retail accounts generally require a US residential address, and Citi has a documented history of closing accounts that show a foreign one. In 2019 it closed accounts of holders with overseas addresses, citing local regulatory requirements. There's at least one firsthand account of someone having all their Citi accounts closed once a foreign address surfaced, with Citi refusing to reopen even after the customer offered a US address — requiring a fresh application and credit check. More broadly, using a foreign address as your primary banking address commonly triggers freezes or closures at US banks. Foreign-resident banking at Citi is routed through its International Personal Bank division, which is aimed at high-net-worth clients with high minimums.
So you're in a genuine bind, and it's worth seeing clearly before touching anything. Leave the mail-handling address, and the letter says the account gets blocked in 30 days — plus you already have an outgoing-wire restriction now. Replace it with your Georgian address, and there's real risk Citi closes the account outright because it doesn't serve foreign-resident retail customers.
What I'd verify before doing anything — and to be clear, I'm not a financial advisor, and the country-specific piece I genuinely can't confirm for you:
Confirm the notice and wire hold are real, through the app or card-back number, not the letter.
On that call, ask directly what happens if you put a Georgia (country) address on the account — whether that account type can even hold a foreign address, or whether it forces a move to International Personal Bank or closure. Citi's country list isn't public and I can't verify whether Georgia is on it.
Since you've been wiring to Georgia, ask specifically what's needed to lift the wire restriction, and whether keeping a US address (a trusted family member's, with the usual compliance caveats) preserves the account versus a foreign one triggering closure.
