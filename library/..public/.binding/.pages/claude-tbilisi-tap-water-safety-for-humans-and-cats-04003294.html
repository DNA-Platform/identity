Based on the search results, here's what you need to know about Tbilisi tap water:
For humans: The water quality itself is officially safe and meets WHO standards. Georgian Water and Power states that tap water complies fully with WHO recommendations, and the water is tested hourly against 53 parameters.
However, there's a significant caveat: the issue is often the aging pipes, not the water itself. Many travelers report stomach issues after drinking Tbilisi tap water, though experiences vary widely. Some people drink it without problems, while others get sick. The mineral content can be high due to aging pipework in many buildings, which is why tap water doesn't agree with everyone.
Practical recommendation for you: Start cautiously. Given Ana's giardia situation with the cats and your temporary accommodation status, I'd suggest using bottled water initially. You can test small amounts of tap water to see how you both tolerate it, but given the variability in building plumbing, bottled water (which is cheap and readily available) is the safer choice during your stay.
For cats: Cats are generally more sensitive to water quality changes than humans. Given that your cats are already dealing with giardia, I would strongly recommend using bottled water for them rather than tap water. Their compromised digestive systems make them more vulnerable to any bacterial content or mineral variations in the water, and you don't want to complicate their recovery.
The water itself may be treated properly, but the pipe infrastructure variations make it risky for cats with existing GI issues. Bottled water is inexpensive in Georgia (around $0.50-1.50 for 3-5 liters), so it's worth the peace of mind.
