Here's the breakdown for Tbilisi apartment hunting:
Primary Websites (Georgian Portals)
ss.ge (home.ss.ge) is considered the largest and most comprehensive. Most expats recommend starting here.myhome.ge is the other major portal with similar listings. The interfaces on both have English versions. You'll probably find the same properties on both, so focusing on one is fine.
korter.ge is another option with a clean English interface and listings from agents.
Strategy for Your Situation
Since you need proximity to a specific school, the approach is:
Identify the neighborhood first - once you know which school, determine what districts/metro stations are within reasonable commute. Tbilisi traffic can be challenging, so walking distance or one metro stop is ideal for kids.
Search by district - all the major portals let you filter by district (Saburtalo, Vake, Vera, etc.). This narrows results dramatically.
Call listings directly - most listings have agent phone numbers. Agent fees are paid by landlords, not tenants, so there's no cost to you for using them.
Finding Agents (English-Speaking)
Tbilisi Home (tbilisihome.ge) specifically serves expats and has an American expat on staff named Jeff who's been there 6+ years. They offer relocation support beyond just finding apartments.
Citizen Tbilisi (citizentbilisi.com) is a family-run operation since 2013 that specializes in long-term expat rentals.
When calling agents from ss.ge or myhome.ge, ask immediately if they speak English. Many don't, but as expat traffic has increased, more do. Having Ana on calls could help significantly since many agents speak Russian.
Facebook/Telegram
Groups like "Expats in Tbilisi" and "Flats For Friends Tbilisi" have listings, but be cautious of scams. Never pay without a proper contract.
For a 2-3 Month Stay
Airbnb monthly rentals are actually quite competitive in Tbilisi and offer more flexibility and protection than some direct rentals. This might be a good initial option while you find the right longer-term place.

Other Neighborhoods for School Access
Happy School (Sairme campus) is near Medical University metro on the M2 line. So any neighborhood along that line gives direct metro access:
Along M2 line (easy commute):
Saburtalo (where you are now) - direct, walkable
Vera - 1-2 metro stops, more central, bohemian vibe, slightly pricier
Didube/Station Square area - cheaper, less English-speaking, rougher
Vazha-Pshavela area (further out on M2) - more affordable, more residential
Adjacent but no metro:
Vake - upscale, leafy, family-friendly, but you'd need bus/taxi to school. Traffic is bad.
Is Saburtalo "Russian"?
Not specifically Russian, but Russian is very functional there. Every time I have an interaction with a Georgian, whether it's at a shop or pharmacy etc... I always ask first which is easier for you to communicate in English or Russian. Eight times out of 10 they will always say Russian.
Saburtalo is popular among international students and expats. The Medical University nearby has many foreign students. After 2022, a large wave of Russian/Belarusian/Ukrainian relocators came to Tbilisi, and many settled in Saburtalo because of the metro access and reasonable prices.
So Ana would have no language problems in Saburtalo - Russian works fine for daily life. It's not a "Russian enclave" but rather a mixed expat/student/local neighborhood where Russian is commonly understood.
For the kids' social integration at Happy School (which is a Russian-language school), being in Saburtalo means they'd likely have classmates living nearby too.
