For 2026, per the SSA's official Red Book, the Substantial Gainful Activity (SGA) limits are $1,690 per month for non-blind disability recipients and $2,830 per month for those who are statutorily blind.
Two related thresholds worth knowing if it's SSDI specifically:
Trial Work Period (TWP) trigger: $1,210/month in 2026 — any month you earn above this counts toward your 9-month TWP, during which there's no earnings cap on benefits.
Extended Period of Eligibility (after TWP): SGA limits ($1,690 / $2,830) apply on a month-by-month basis for 36 months.
If it's SSI rather than SSDI, the rules are different — SSI is needs-based and reduces payments based on countable income formulas rather than a clean cutoff, with a 2026 federal benefit rate of $994/individual.
These are gross earnings before taxes, though IRWE (impairment-related work expenses) and subsidies can be deducted before comparison to the threshold.

Right — the test is applied monthly, not averaged annually. So:
$1,690 × 12 = $20,280/year is the most you can earn while staying at or below the cap every single month.
You can't average — earning $3,000 one month and $400 another would still trigger an SGA-level month, and after your Trial Work Period and Extended Period of Eligibility, that month's benefit can be lost.
Bonuses, retroactive pay, and commissions get attributed to the month they're for, which can accidentally push a month over.
The only window where the monthly cap doesn't apply is the 9-month Trial Work Period — during those months you can earn unlimited amounts and keep full benefits. After that, every month is evaluated against the $1,690 threshold independently.

Yes — $1,210/month in 2026 triggers a Trial Work Period (TWP) month.
Here's how it works:
Any month you earn over $1,210 (gross), that month gets counted as one of your 9 TWP months.
You get 9 TWP months total within a rolling 60-month window.
During those 9 months, there's no earnings cap — you can earn any amount and still get your full SSDI check.
Once you've used all 9, the SGA test ($1,690/month) kicks in for benefit decisions.
So the two numbers do different jobs:
$1,210 = "this month counts as a trial work month" (but you still get paid in full)
$1,690 = "this month's earnings disqualify you from a benefit" (after TWP is used up)
Earning between $1,210 and $1,690 is a weird zone — it burns a TWP month without actually being SGA-level work. Some people deliberately stay under $1,210 to preserve TWP months for later.

Yes, exactly. $1,200/month ($14,400/year) is the practical "safe" ceiling if you want to earn indefinitely without ever burning a TWP month or risking benefits.
To be precise: the TWP threshold is $1,210, so technically you can go up to $1,209.99. At $1,200/month you have a small buffer for things like a slightly larger paycheck due to a 5-Friday month or a small bonus.
The hierarchy:
≤ $1,209/month → invisible to SSA's work tests, benefits fully safe forever
$1,210–$1,689/month → burns a TWP month, but full benefits still paid
≥ $1,690/month → SGA-level; safe during TWP, risky after
So if your goal is "earn the most I can without ever putting benefits at risk or using up trial months," $1,200/month is the right mental anchor.
