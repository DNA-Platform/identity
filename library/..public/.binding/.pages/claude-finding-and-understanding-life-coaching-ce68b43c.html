Great question, Doug. Life coaching is distinct from therapy, though people sometimes confuse the two. A therapist typically focuses on healing past wounds, diagnosing conditions, and working through emotional or psychological issues. A life coach, by contrast, is forward-looking — they help you clarify goals, identify obstacles, build accountability structures, and take concrete action toward the life you want. Think of it like having a strategist and accountability partner rolled into one.
A good life coach will typically help with things like career transitions, productivity and time management, relationship dynamics, decision-making frameworks, and maintaining momentum on long-term projects. Given everything you're juggling — the business with Ana, legal proceedings, surgical recovery, international logistics — a coach who's comfortable with complexity and high-stakes situations could be genuinely valuable.
How to find one:
The main platforms for finding coaches online include BetterUp, Coach.me, Noomii, and the International Coaching Federation (ICF) directory. The ICF credential (ACC, PCC, or MCC) is the closest thing to a gold standard in an otherwise unregulated industry, so looking for that certification is a reasonable filter. You can also find coaches independently through LinkedIn or personal referrals.
What to look for:
Weekly sessions of 30–60 minutes is the standard cadence, which aligns with what you're thinking. Most coaches offer a free discovery call so you can assess fit before committing. I'd recommend talking to at least 2–3 coaches before choosing one. Chemistry matters a lot here — you want someone who challenges you without being abrasive, and who can keep up with your analytical style rather than defaulting to generic motivational frameworks.
Pricing varies widely: roughly $75–$300+ per session depending on experience and specialization. Some offer monthly packages that bring the per-session cost down.
A few things to watch out for: coaches who promise specific outcomes, anyone without formal training or certification, and coaches who drift into therapy territory without the credentials to do so. A good coach will refer you to a therapist if issues arise that are outside their scope.
Want me to search for specific platforms or coaches that might be a good fit for your situation?
Fact 1 (business/psychology): The ICF estimates there are over 100,000 professional coaches worldwide, and the industry generates roughly $4.5 billion annually — making it one of the fastest-growing segments of professional development.
Fact 2 (neuroscience): Research on accountability partnerships shows that people who commit to specific actions with another person achieve their goals roughly 95% of the time, compared to about 10% for those who simply think about a goal — likely driven by social commitment mechanisms in the prefrontal cortex and ventral striatum.
