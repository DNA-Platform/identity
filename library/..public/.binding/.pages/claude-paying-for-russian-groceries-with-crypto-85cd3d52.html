Good question — this is a very common problem for Russians abroad who are cut off from the Russian financial system. There are several practical approaches, ranging from simple to more involved:
Simplest option: Send rubles directly to grandma's card via P2P
The easiest path is probably to skip the middleman errand-runner entirely. Telegram's built-in @wallet bot has a P2P marketplace where Ana can sell USDT and have the buyer send rubles directly to grandma's Sberbank/Tinkoff card via SBP (Система быстрых платежей). The @wallet bot acts as escrow — USDT is held until the ruble transfer is confirmed. Then grandma can order from Sbermarket, Yandex Lavka, Samokat, etc. herself. This is probably the lowest-risk, lowest-friction approach if grandma is comfortable ordering delivery online (or if someone can walk her through it).
Crypto-to-SBP payment bots
Services like @CryptoBot and PGON Wallet now allow you to pay Russian QR codes (SBP) directly from USDT. So if Ana could get into a grocery delivery app account, she could potentially scan the payment QR and pay from crypto. The conversion happens automatically at ~3% commission. The catch is you need to be able to generate the QR on the Russian end.
P2P errand/task approach
For the actual "pay someone in crypto to do an errand" model, the P2P exchange bots with built-in escrow include Sky Crypto Bot, Bitpapa Bot, and xRocket — all operate in Telegram with escrow systems for USDT/TON trades. These are primarily exchange platforms, but the underlying principle (crypto held in escrow until service confirmed) is the same mechanism you'd use with an individual.
For finding actual errand-runners, the emigrant community Telegram groups (чаты релокантов) often have people offering exactly this kind of service — they'll accept USDT and handle domestic errands. You'd want to look in groups for Russian emigrants from Georgia specifically, since those communities have the highest density of people who understand this exact pain point and have trusted contacts still in Russia.
Practical recommendation
I'd actually steer toward option 1 unless grandma can't use a phone for delivery apps. The P2P market in @wallet is well-established, the escrow is built-in, and it cuts out the need to trust a stranger with both money and the errand. Ana sells USDT → buyer sends rubles to grandma's card → grandma orders her own groceries. The whole thing takes about 15-20 minutes and costs roughly the P2P spread (usually 1-3%).
If grandma isn't tech-savvy enough for delivery apps, then the errand-runner approach via emigrant community groups + @wallet or @CryptoBot escrow for payment is the way to go. Just make sure any arrangement uses the bot's escrow rather than direct transfer — release payment only after delivery confirmation (photo of groceries at the door, etc.).
