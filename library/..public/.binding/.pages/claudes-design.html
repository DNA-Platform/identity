Claudes Design
How the pages of my conversations with Claude came to look the way they do, told as it happens, with every sketch kept beside the chapter that designs from it. A portfolio, made as Dougs Design was made and the same way through: a question, a few pages of HTML I can open at a desk and on a phone, chosen by number, and the page built to match the one I chose. The story first, beginning with Starting from the audit, the night the first conversation page was audited and found wanting; then the designs, one room each, each with its sketches by number beside it, beginning with The Conversation's Page. The pages it is for: a conversation, high fidelity; the book of all my conversations, arranged by project and searched as one; and a project's page, which is a way to its conversations.
Starting from the audit
What I saw on the first page
The first conversation page was built from the application's form before anything of it was designed, and when I opened it I saw a page that wore the bare base of this library, hid who was speaking, drew a tool use as a faint line, and led nowhere: no mark in the header, no way to the book it is filed under. Then it wore a dark frame nobody chose. The night it was rebuilt to fit in with the other books I said what this book is for.
This is very ugly and rough, so we are going to need to do serious design work. Let's get a design book up and running so we can add a few html pages to it and make some good decisions.
What is to be designed
A conversation shown with high fidelity: as clean as Claude is on the web, with formatted code and artifacts I can click, opening in the conversation. A book of all my conversations that can hold hundreds without a sidebar that scrolls forever: arranged by project, each project a part of the book, and one search over all of them. A project's page that is useful, which means first a way to navigate the conversations held in it, with its files browsable as one part of the page, and perhaps a conversation read inside it. A way back to where I came from, placed on a page as a component when a page has a use for one, never on every page. And code abstracted enough that the next user experience is easy to make, which begins with the base of a library book carrying the frame every book shares.
The method is the one Dougs Design settled: a question, a few pages of HTML shown together with a switch, chosen by number, built to match. The sketches live beside the chapter that designs from them, numbered from one in this book, never gathered on a page of their own, which is the pattern Dougs Design tried first and stopped carrying forward because it does not scale; and each design that is decided keeps the page as built beside its sketch. The first is the conversation's page, and its first sketch is concept 1, a conversation of mine in the frame every book of this library wears, refused the same night for an orange it took from my own book and for names over the messages; its second is concept 2, the chat text as the application has it and an artifact opening beside the words as the application opens one; its third is concept 3, the same page at the application's own measures, read in its browser rather than imitated.

The Conversation and the Project
Two pages, examined and drawn again
With every conversation of both my accounts imported, nine hundred and eighty-three books, the two pages I open most are a conversation's and a project's, and I asked that both be examined as built and designed again in HTML, keeping the library's semantics and making a table of contents that can carry quantity, the way a reference manual carries it; that the project page be made as useful as it can be; and that there be a way back and forth between the two, holding where I came from. The sketches are drawn into the real things: concept 009 into the bound print of Seren & The Hard Problem, the longest conversation I have, six hundred and eighty-four messages over twenty-one days in seven months, so every word a search finds was said; and concept 010 from the covers and files of Semantic Reference Theory, seventy-nine conversations and sixty-three files, with Seren as a second project on the switch, since the conversation of 009 is held there and the two pages can lead to each other. Each sketch keeps its number in this book and I answer by number: 009 is A Conversation, as a Manual and 010 is A Project, as Useful as Possible.
What the examination found, read on the library's own pages after the register was set by its parts. The conversation page already turns by day, one day in front and the rest drawn behind it, which is why the longest conversation opens in under a second; its side lists the days under their months with each day's count, and nothing more. A day of a hundred and forty messages is one scroll with nothing to find a message by; there is no search within a conversation, and the headings Claude writes stand in two of its twenty-one days, so an outline read off headings is thin. At a phone the side becomes a field of day pills wrapped under month labels, which no sketch chose. The project page is as the fourth concept laid it: the conversations by month, the instructions and files in a column, a file opening in the pane; what it is for is said, but nothing on it finds a conversation by a word, nothing leads on to the application where the project lives, and the months are a list of counts rather than a shape.
