The honest answer for Harry Potter specifically: probably not reliably, and it's worth checking before you count on it.
Here's how it actually works. On Prime Video, audio and subtitle tracks are set per title and vary by region, not by the service as a whole. So "does Prime have Russian dubs" has no single answer — each film has its own track list. The Harry Potter films are Warner Bros titles, and on Prime they're almost always rent/buy (not included with your Prime subscription), and the bundled audio tracks tend to be limited to the storefront's main languages. From Georgia, your storefront likely won't carry a Russian dub for them, and combining "Russian audio + English subtitles" on the same Warner title is the kind of mix that's frequently missing even when each track exists separately.
The reliable way to verify, before committing: open the Harry Potter title page on Prime, and look at the "Audio languages" and "Subtitles" lists shown on the detail page. If Russian isn't listed there, it won't appear during playback. During playback you'd tap the speech-bubble/CC icon to switch tracks, but it only offers what's listed.
For your actual goal — kids watching in Russian, you reading English subtitles — Prime is a weak bet for Harry Potter. A few stronger options worth knowing:
Russian streaming services have the official Russian-language Harry Potter dubs as their default and often carry English subtitle tracks too — these are the natural home for that exact audio/subtitle combination. Kinopoisk and Okko are the main ones, though access and payment from Georgia can be fiddly.
