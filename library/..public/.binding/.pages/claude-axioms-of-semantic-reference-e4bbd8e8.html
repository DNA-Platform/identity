In the statement:

The way this is written, r is existentially quantified (bound by E[r]), while x, y, and t are free variables.
Standard Interpretation
In first-order logic, free variables are typically interpreted with an implicit universal quantifier at the outermost scope. So this statement would be read as:
srt
E[r]: A[x,y,t]: x =t> y =|> x =r> y
Meaning: "There exists a single r such that for all x, y, and t, if x =t> y then x =r> y"

srt

Why does logic interpret free variables as universally quantified at the outermost scope?
This is a convention in formal logic, and there are good reasons for it:
Sentences should have definite truth values: A formula with free variables isn't really a proposition - it can't be true or false without specifying what those variables refer to. By implicitly universally quantifying at the outermost scope, we make it a complete statement.

Historical convention: This comes from the tradition where formulas represent "schemas" or "patterns" that should hold for any substitution of the free variables.
Prenex normal form: There's a standard form where all quantifiers come at the front. Making free variables into outermost universal quantifiers aligns with this.

The Five Axioms of Semantic Reference Theory v1
The Five Axioms of Semantic Reference Theory at version 1
The Five Axioms of Semantic Reference Theory
Axiom 1: The Root of Universality
srt
E[r]:A[x,y,t]: x =t> y =|> x =r> y
There exists a universal relationship r such that all relationship types inherit from it.
Syntactic Definition
srt
def(x => y): x =r> y
Untyped reference: x relates to y through the universal relationship.
Axiom 2: The Root of Specification
srt
x => y =|> E[s]: x =s> y =|> x =t> y =|> x' =s> y' =|> x' =t> y'
Every related pair has a most specific relationship that implies all their other relationships.
Syntactic Definition
text
def((x,y)): the unique s satisfying Axiom 2 for the pair (x,y)
Most specific relationship: the unique relationship that characterizes how x and y relate.
Axiom 3: The Root of Directionality
srt
A{s,t}: x =s> y =|> x =t> y =||> x =t> y !=|> x =s> y
Distinct relationships cannot mutually imply each other - the inheritance hierarchy is directed.
Axiom 4: The Root of Canonicity
srt
x =y> y =|> (x,y) == y | E[y']: (x,y') == y' & y' =y> y
Representation is local - to represent y, you must be directly connected to y or through exactly one intermediary. Representatives are canonical.
Syntactic Definitions
srt
def(x > y): (x,y) == y
def(x -> y): x == y | x =y> y
Direct inheritance (x > y): the most specific relationship IS y itself. General inheritance (x -> y): either identity or representation through the chain structure.
Axiom 5: The Root of Representation
srt
(y,x) == y =|> (x,y) == x
If y is a direct representative of x, then x is a direct representation of y - but not conversely. Symbolization and representation are asymmetric.
Summary
These five axioms establish:
Universality - A common ancestor for all relationships
Specification - Unique most specific relationships for each pair
Directionality - No cycles in the inheritance hierarchy
Canonicity - Local, chain-like structure of representation
Representation - Asymmetry between symbols and their representatives
Together they form the foundation of Semantic Reference Theory, building from universal structure down to the specific nature of symbolic reference.
