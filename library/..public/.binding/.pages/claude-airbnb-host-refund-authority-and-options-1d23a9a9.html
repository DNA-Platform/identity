Hosts do have direct power to refund — it's not all locked behind Airbnb.
A host can issue a refund to a guest directly through the Airbnb platform, either during the stay or after, using the "Send or request money" / resolution tools tied to the reservation. The host chooses the amount (partial or full), and Airbnb processes the actual money movement back to the guest's payment method. So thedecision is the host's, but the transaction still flows through Airbnb's system rather than host-to-guest cash.
The two main paths:
Host-initiated refund — the host voluntarily refunds some or all of the payout, which is what happens when a host agrees a stay was bad and wants to make it right. This is the host's call.
Airbnb-mediated refund — if the guest files a complaint under AirCover / the rebooking-and-refund policy and the host won't refund, Airbnb can step in and decide, sometimes refunding the guest out of the host's payout regardless of the host's wishes.
So both are true depending on the situation: a host can choose to give money back, and Airbnb can also force a refund if a guest escalates a valid complaint. A host who wants to refund is never blocked from doing so.
One caveat worth checking against current terms, since Airbnb adjusts its refund and AirCover policies periodically — want me to pull the latest on a specific scenario (e.g. guest already checked out, or a cleanliness/misrepresentation claim)?
