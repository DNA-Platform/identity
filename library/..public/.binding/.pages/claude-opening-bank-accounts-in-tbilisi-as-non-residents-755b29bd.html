The answer here is unambiguous: Credo Bank is the go-to for non-residents in Tbilisi, and crucially, they've built two dedicated Expat Branches specifically for this. Here's the full picture:
Why Credo Bank
Credo Bank has dedicated Expat Service Centers with banking products specifically designed for foreign citizens living in Georgia. To open an account, you only need a passport and a Georgian SIM card — the account opens within roughly 2 days, with staff who speak Russian and English. It's the most foreigner-friendly option and has documented cases of accounts being opened with passport only.
Your nearest Expat Branch: 16 Ilia Chavchavadze Avenue
This is the purpose-built expat location, about a 10-minute walk from Central Park. The Tamarashvili 17 branch in Saburtalo proper is physically closer to home but is a standard branch — you want the dedicated expat branch for a smoother experience, especially given the crutch situation.
What to bring
Both passports (foreign)
Georgian SIM cards (mandatory for account registration — get these before you go if you haven't)
Your address in Tbilisi (rental agreement or even just the address is fine)
Documentation of income source (bank statement, contracts — for the KYC form)
45 GEL for the annual Expat Package
Critical fee to know
As of May 2025, Credo introduced a 30 GEL/month "Cross-Border Fee" that applies if you haven't made any card transactions within Georgia for 3 consecutive months. Since you'll be living there and using the cards locally, this won't be an issue — but it's worth knowing if you open an account and then leave for an extended period.
Hours: Mon–Fri 10:00–17:30 | Sat 10:15–12:15 | Closed Sunday

Email to pre-register (optional): account@credo.ge — you can submit a filled KYC Excel file with passport scans to start the process before visiting in person.
One watch-out from reviews: some staff have been known to push unnecessary add-ons like card insurance or business account packages. None of those are required — the core Expat Package at 45 GEL/year covers what you need. Just decline anything extra confidently.
Also worth noting: you already have Ziraat Bank Georgia flagged in your notes as a passport-only option for a GEL debit card. Credo is the stronger all-around choice (multicurrency IBAN, better app, expat infrastructure), but Ziraat remains a valid backup if there are any unexpected issues.
