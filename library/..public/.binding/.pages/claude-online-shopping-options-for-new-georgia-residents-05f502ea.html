Your intuition is essentially right, though it's less a deliberate gatekeeping policy and more a consequence of underdeveloped payment infrastructure. Online card payments are still somewhat limited in Georgia, especially for foreign cards. The reason Glovo and Wolt work is that they're international platforms running their own global payment stack — they're not routing through Georgian processors. Georgian e-commerce sites like alta.ge typically run through local payment gateways (TBC Pay, BOG Pay) which are built around Georgian cards and don't have reliable foreign card support.
The practical fix is cash on delivery, which is extremely common on Georgian e-commerce sites and sidesteps the whole problem. Most of those sites I mentioned — alta.ge in particular — offer it as a checkout option. You pay the courier in GEL when the item arrives. Given that you're in Saburtalo and delivery within Tbilisi is typically same or next day, this is genuinely the path of least resistance here.
The longer-term unlock is opening a Georgian bank account (TBC or Bank of Georgia are the main two). Both allow foreigners to open accounts with just a passport — you don't need a Georgian personal number for the basic account. Once you have a Georgian Visa/Mastercard debit card, the entire local e-commerce ecosystem opens up normally. It's worth doing anyway for paying Georgian utilities, subscriptions, and anything else that's awkward with foreign cards.
