Now the GRAM/USDT relationship. The honest structure is that USDT flow doesn't buy GRAM mechanically — the coupling runs through four channels of very different strengths, and one of them is exactly the floor your intuition is pointing at.
Channel 1: Fee flow (weak, and shrinking by policy). Every USDT transfer is a jetton transfer paying gas in GRAM, of which TON burns half. But do the arithmetic: at the new fixed fee (~0.0004 GRAM ≈ $0.0005), even a billion transfers a day generates ~$500K/day of fee flow — trivial against a $4B market cap, and Durov's stated endpoint is zero fees for most transactions. This matters because it's the deliberate inversion of Tron, the canonical existence proof for your question: Tron carries $60B+ of USDT and monetizes the flow through billions a year in fees burned against TRX. TON is severing that exact link on purpose. So whoever tells you "USDT volume = GRAM fee demand" is describing Tron, not TON.
Channel 2: The security floor (this is your bound). This is the precise sense in which a trillion-dollar USDT float can't coexist with GRAM imploding. Proof-of-stake security is economic: the cost to censor or reorg the chain is the cost of acquiring a controlling share of stake, which is (staked GRAM) × (price). The credible USDT float is bounded by some multiple of that slashable value — carry $50B of stablecoins on $500M of stake and the attack is profitable, so either the float flees or the chain gets broken. Formally: P ≥ credible_float / (k × S). Note carefully how this operates — it's an equilibrium-selection constraint, not buy pressure. It doesn't push GRAM up as USDT grows; it rules out the state where USDT stays while GRAM collapses. Your instinct is correct as a floor, not as a pump. Telegram staking as largest validator is them personally underwriting this floor.
Channel 3: Collateral and liquidity lockup (moderate, mechanical). USDT on-chain needs venues: USDT/GRAM is the base pair on the DEXes, so deeper stablecoin activity locks GRAM in LP positions, and lending markets use GRAM as collateral against USDT borrow. This one does scale roughly with USDT TVL and does remove float.
Channel 4: Platform cash flows (the actual value driver). With fees at zero, GRAM's discountable value rests on Telegram-layer flows denominated in it — ad revenue share, Stars settlement, commerce, and now gas sponsorship itself: "zero-fee" means someone pays the validators, and the natural sponsor is Telegram, converting network security into a customer-acquisition cost recovered through the platform economy. GRAM becomes a claim on Telegram's commerce layer, with USDT as the payments payload riding on top.
Notice this is your own convenience-yield-migration principle applied internally: the payment function accrues to USDT, and GRAM's value migrates to the reserve/security/platform role — the same division of labor Bitcoin and Tether arrived at, engineered deliberately inside one ecosystem. So the precise answer: USDT volume gives GRAM a floor (channel 2), a moderate lockup (channel 3), near-zero direct flow (channel 1), and its upside runs entirely through whether Telegram's GRAM-denominated economy monetizes the traffic (channel 4).

Activity per se doesn't change this: a validator processing a billion free transactions earns the same subsidy as one processing ten. Where it becomes real money: if zero-fee is implemented as sponsorship — Telegram paying for the gas its billion users consume — then validator revenue acquires an external, fiat-backed component for the first time, sized to activity.
