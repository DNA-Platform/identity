Yes! These are two important rules — and you're right, both deserve their own slots in the notebook. Let me teach them properly first, then I'll update the artifact.
Rule 1: Time expressions need prepositions
After starting / beginning / from + a time word, English almost always requires a preposition. The preposition depends on what kind of time it is:
Time typePrepositionExample
Monthsinstarting in February, in 2026
Yearsinbeginning in 2019
Seasonsinstarting in spring
Specific datesonstarting on March 1st
Days of the weekonstarting on Monday
Specific timesatstarting at 9 AM
Emphasizing the starting pointfromfrom February onwards
The one exception: relative time adverbs (tomorrow, today, yesterday, next week, last month) take no preposition because they're already adverbs:
✓ starting tomorrow
✓ starting next week
✗ starting in tomorrow
So the rule of thumb: if the time word is a name of something (February, Monday, 9 AM, 2026), you need a preposition. If it's a relative pointer (today, next week), you don't.
Rule 2: After "worth," use -ing (the gerund family)
Certain English words are always followed by the -ing form, never the infinitive. Worth is one. So is a whole family of others you should learn together because they break the same way for Russian speakers:
Word✓ Correct✗ Wrong
worthIt's worth trying~~worth to try~~
enjoyI enjoy reading~~enjoy to read~~
avoidShe avoids being late~~avoids to be~~
finishWe finished eating~~finished to eat~~
mindDo you mind waiting?~~mind to wait~~
suggestI suggest going early~~suggest to go~~ ← this is already in your notebook!
considerConsider taking the bus~~consider to take~~
keepKeep practicing~~keep to practice~~
spend (time)I spent two hours reading~~spent to read~~
Why this is hard for Russian speakers: Russian uses the infinitive in most of these constructions:
стоит попробовать → literally "worth to try" → but English needs "worth trying"
я предлагаю пойти → literally "I suggest to go" → but English needs "suggest going"
Bonus rule that follows from this: After any preposition, use -ing:
good at painting (not "at to paint")
interested in learning (not "in to learn")
before leaving (not "before to leave")
instead of waiting (not "instead of to wait")
So whenever you're about to say "to + verb" after a preposition or after one of those words above, switch to-ing.
Connection to what's already in your notebook
The suggest pattern you already have is actually one specific case of Rule 2. So when I update the notebook, I'll add a cross-reference — "suggest" is part of the -ing family, not a lonely exception.
