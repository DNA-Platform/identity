There are several well-researched approaches, depending on whether you're looking to reduce self-criticism or criticism of others:
For self-criticism:
Kristin Neff's Self-Compassion program is probably the most empirically validated. It teaches three core skills: self-kindness (treating yourself as you'd treat a friend), common humanity (recognizing suffering and imperfection are universal), and mindfulness (observing difficult emotions without over-identifying with them). Her website has free guided exercises.
Compassion-Focused Therapy (CFT), developed by Paul Gilbert, specifically targets the "inner critic" by training people to activate their self-soothing system rather than their threat system. It's particularly effective for people whose self-criticism has a harsh, attacking quality.
ACT (Acceptance and Commitment Therapy) approaches it differently - rather than trying to change critical thoughts, you learn to defuse from them, noticing "I'm having the thought that I'm incompetent" rather than being fused with "I'm incompetent."
For criticism of others:
Nonviolent Communication (Marshall Rosenberg) reframes judgments as unmet needs - recognizing that criticism often masks underlying needs or values.
Gottman's research on relationships identifies criticism as one of the "Four Horsemen" that predict relationship breakdown, and teaches replacing it with "gentle startup" - expressing needs without attacking character.
Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction can reduce overall reactivity, which tends to decrease judgmental responses.
What's the context - self-criticism, interpersonal dynamics, or something else?
