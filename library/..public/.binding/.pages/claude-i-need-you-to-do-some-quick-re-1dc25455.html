Here's what the numbers translate to:
The #4 guard: US guard numbers aren't the standard reference here — post-Soviet barbershops work in millimeters. A #4 guard leaves hair 1/2 inch, which is 13 mm (barbers here will round to 12 or 13 mm — standard attachment sets in the region run 3, 6, 9, 12 mm, so "12 мм" is the most recognizable ask). Don't just say "number four" — attachment numbering varies by clipper set, so the number alone is ambiguous. Millimeters are unambiguous.
1.5 inches = 3.8 cm — say "4 сантиметра" / "4 სმ" and you're within rounding.
Something to show the barber, in Georgian:
გვერდებზე და უკან — მანქანით, 13 მმ. წინ და ზემოთ — მაკრატლით, დაახლოებით 4 სმ სიგრძე დამიტოვეთ. (Sides and back — clippers, 13 mm. Front and top — scissors, leave about 4 cm.)
One caveat: the Georgian is my construction, not a phrase verified against native barbershop usage — the mm/cm numbers will carry it regardless. Also worth knowing: many Tbilisi barbers speak Russian (several barbershops explicitly staff Russian-speaking masters), so the Russian version works as a backup:
Бока и затылок — машинкой, 13 мм. Спереди и сверху — ножницами, оставить примерно 4 см.
