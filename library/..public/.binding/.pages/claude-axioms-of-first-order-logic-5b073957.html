Claude: 1. "If P then (if Q then P)" This first axiom essentially says that if you know something is true (P), then it stays true regardless of what else you might learn (Q). It's like saying "if knowing A lets you deduce that B leads to C, then if A leads to B, A must also lead to C."
Claude: 3. "If (not Q then not P) then (if (not Q then P) then Q)" This is a form of proof by contradiction. It says that if not-Q implies not-P, and not-Q also implies P, then Q must be true. Why?
