The name Rothschild is pronounced differently depending on the language:
English (most common in the US/UK): "ROTHS-child" — rhymes with "moths-child," with the "th" as in "cloth."
Original German: "ROHT-shilt" — the "sch" makes a "sh" sound, and the final "d" is devoiced to a "t." The "o" is long, like in "road." The name literally means "red shield" (rotes Schild), referring to the red shield that hung on the family's house in the Frankfurt Jewish ghetto.
French (since a major branch settled in Paris): "ROT-sheeld" — with a French "r" (guttural/uvular) and a longer final vowel.
So the native German pronunciation is closest to "Roht-shilt" — quite different from the anglicized version most people use.
Fact 1: The Rothschild family's Frankfurt house, marked by the red shield (zum roten Schild), was in the Judengasse — one of Europe's earliest Jewish ghettos, established in 1462 and home to a remarkably dense community that produced several influential banking dynasties beyond just the Rothschilds.
Fact 2: German final-consonant devoicing (Auslautverhärtung) — the rule that turns the "d" in Rothschild into a "t" sound — is a systematic phonological process shared across several Germanic languages, including Dutch and Russian, where voiced stops become voiceless at the end of a syllable.
