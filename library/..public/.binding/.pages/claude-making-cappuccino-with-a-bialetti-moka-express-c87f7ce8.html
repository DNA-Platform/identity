How does a bialetti express work for espresso
Claude
08:02 UTC, 5 February 2026
A Bialetti Moka Express uses a simple three-chamber design driven by steam pressure.
The bottom chamber holds water. Above it sits a funnel-shaped filter basket where you pack finely ground coffee. The upper chamber, with a rubber gasket and filter plate, screws on top.
When you place it on a stovetop, the water heats and generates steam pressure (about 1–2 bar). That pressure forces the hot water up through the coffee grounds in the filter basket. The brewed coffee then travels up through a central tube and collects in the upper chamber. You'll hear a gurgling/hissing sound when most of the water has been pushed through — that's your signal it's done.
A few practical notes: fill water to just below the safety valve, use medium-fine grounds (slightly coarser than true espresso), don't tamp the grounds (just level them off), and keep the heat at medium to avoid burning. It produces a strong, concentrated brew — not technically espresso since it operates at much lower pressure than a machine (9 bar), but it's rich and full-bodied in a similar spirit.
