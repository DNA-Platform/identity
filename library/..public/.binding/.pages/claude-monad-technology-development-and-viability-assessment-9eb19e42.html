The MONAD_NINE hard fork in March 2026 activated the Osaka EVM upgrade and added a reserve balance precompile, so they're tracking Ethereum's evolution rather than forking off. Validator set sits around 200, globally distributed — credible but materially less decentralized than Ethereum, which is the standing tradeoff for the throughput claim.
Ecosystem: the harder question, and where the genesis thesis is currently failing the empirical test. Solana is essentially trying to manufacture an OS-level position from scratch because it has no application-layer position to inherit one from.
TON doesn't have this problem because Telegram already won this fight years ago. Telegram is an approved app on every iOS and Android device. Identity is your Telegram account, which is already established and already carries social graph + verification. The signing surface is the Telegram UI, which is already on the phone. Yes, and I'd go further: your framing isn't just defensible, it's nearly tautological once you accept the premise. Money is a social technology. It only exists because there's a network of people who agree to accept it, and its utility scales with the density of that acceptance. A cryptocurrency that justifies itself solely through token-denominated trading of other tokens is a closed loop with no exogenous demand — every dollar of "utility" ultimately comes from the next entrant betting that someone after them will pay more. That's the structural definition of a Ponzi, regardless of whether the participants are running fraud or are genuinely confused about where value is supposed to originate. For a cryptocurrency to have non-Ponzi utility, the chain it lives on has to be a substrate where economic interactions between identifiable participants actually happen for non-speculative reasons. Which means: identity, communication, discovery, and exchange between counterparties — i.e., a social network in the structural sense, even if it doesn't look like Facebook.
This is the reframe that I think clarifies the whole landscape. Pavel Durov was arrested in France in August 2024 on charges related to Telegram's content moderation policies, held briefly, and is currently restricted from leaving France pending the legal process. That arrest dramatically raised Telegram's profile in Western policy discourse and made the platform legible as a strategic actor in a way it previously wasn't. If anyone at Meta was paying attention, the Durov arrest was the moment when Telegram crossed from "messaging app we don't worry about" to "platform that Western governments are actively trying to compel into compliance." That doesn't make Meta a competitor, but it does suggest the regulatory environment is starting to treat Telegram as an entity worth disciplining — and the moments when governments start trying to discipline platforms are typically the moments when those platforms have become structurally important. So the more accurate version of the answer is probably: Meta isn't worried about Telegram on any axis they currently measure, but Telegram has been quietly becoming the kind of asset Meta couldn't build for political reasons, and the people who could have flagged this internally have already left. That's not a comfortable position; it's just one that's invisible from inside Meta's existing strategic frame.
