Good news: Georgia is built for exactly this kind of trip. Three directions out of Tbilisi give you very different flavors of "remote with a view," and the real decision is how much driving you want to trade for how much drama. Here they are on a map first so you can see them relative to home:

Here's how I'd think about the three directions, easiest to most dramatic:
Kakheti (east, ~2 hr, easy roads) — the relaxed wine-country option. This is the gentlest drive and probably the best fit for "low-key with a view that doesn't require an ordeal to reach." It's pastoral rather than true wilderness — rolling vineyards backed by the Caucasus across the Alazani Valley — but genuinely beautiful and very calm. Two solid, verified stays: Hotel BelleVue in the hilltop town of Sighnaghi (4.8 rating, the balcony view over the valley is the whole point), and Chateau Mosmieri near Telavi, a winery hotel set away from any town — guests specifically describe it as peaceful and isolated, which matches your "remote with a view" brief on an easy road. Sighnaghi has a walkable old town; Mosmieri is more "see no one but the vineyard."
Borjomi (southwest, ~2.5 hr, mostly good highway) — the forest option. Borjomi sits at the edge of Borjomi-Kharagauli National Park, one of the largest protected forest areas in Europe, so this is your closest "actual wilderness adjacent" without a hard drive. Crowne Plaza Borjomi is the reliable comfortable base (4.6, forest and mountain views, spa, right by the central park). Bakuriani, a bit higher up, is the more alpine/forest-cabin feel if you want quieter.
Kazbegi (north, ~3 hr) — the showstopper, with a catch. The view of Mt Kazbek and Gergeti Trinity Church is arguably the most spectacular in the country, and Rooms Hotel Kazbegi is the famous place to take it in. The honest catch: the Georgian Military Highway is winding, gets heavy truck traffic, and reviewers consistently warn the drive is slow and tiring (and it's weather-prone). This is the one trip where I'd strongly lean toward hiring a driver rather than self-driving — more on that below.
A few practical notes, with the verified-vs-not flagged clearly:
On car vs. "something easier": hiring a driver in Georgia is cheap, common, and very normal — a round-trip transfer or a driver-for-the-weekend is an easy alternative to renting, and it neatly solves the Kazbegi road problem and means neither of you has to deal with mountain driving. I haven't priced a specific driver for you, so treat that as "the standard option exists," not a quoted rate.
On season: late May into June is a good window for all three — mountains are accessible, Kakheti is warm, Borjomi forest is pleasant. Not verified for your exact dates, but no seasonal closures apply to these three right now the way they would for the truly high passes (Tusheti, for instance, isn't reliably open yet).
On Airbnb specifically: the places above are real hotels/guesthouses I could verify, not Airbnb listings — Airbnb's inventory isn't something I can search directly. If you want the remote-cabin-with-a-view Airbnb feel, the best search terms are the villages themselves: Sighnaghi or Kvemo Magaro (Kakheti), Bakuriani or Tsemi (Borjomi area), and Gergeti, Sno, or Juta (Kazbegi — Juta in particular is a tiny end-of-the-road alpine valley that's about as "wilderness" as it gets, though more rugged).

Your instinct is right for exactly one kind of spot — but the place you'd actually base in isn't that kind, so the worry mostly dissolves. The key is being deliberate about it.
Here's the thing: "Kazbegi" as a base means the town of Stepantsminda, and that's not wilderness — it's a real, decent-sized town that happens to sit in a dramatic mountain bowl. You get the Mt Kazbek view from town or just above it without being stranded. It has several grocery stores including a Spar, plus a Nikora and a Magniti a bit further down the main highway with better prices, and the local Ska Market on the main street, a popular bakery next to Ska selling fresh shoti bread, khachapuri and lobiani, and a PSP pharmacy near the square, with a few bars and restaurants along the main street. All walkable. Travelers specifically contrast it with genuinely remote regions like Tusheti precisely because here you don't have to haul in all your own food and supplies.
The bigger point: on a trip like this in Georgia you mostly don't grocery-shop at all. The guesthouse model means your hosts cook for you — breakfast is typically included, dinner usually available for a small charge, homemade khinkali, khachapuri, grilled meat, often their own wine and chacha. So "where do we get food" is usually answered by "the place we're staying," not a shop run.
Even the view-first options work this way. Rooms Kazbegi sits up on the hill with its own restaurant and a big view terrace — and there's a grocery store in the village just below it. The best-view guesthouses are up the hillside east of the square or just north of it, still a short walk or cheap 4x4 ride from the shops.
Where your instinct fully applies: the truly remote spots like Juta — a village at the end of a dirt track, meant to be reached by 4x4, a base for treks. Stay up there and you really would depend on the guesthouse for every meal, so you'd treat it that way on purpose.
So the move for a relaxed, car-free trip: base in or just above Stepantsminda — view plus walkable food and restaurants — and reserve Juta-type isolation for one deliberate "wilderness night" if you want it, where the guesthouse feeds you. A car with high clearance is preferable, but a 4WD isn't mandatory — people do drive sedans like a Prius all the way in dry conditions, though in rain or snow an SUV is a must. Makes sense — Kazbegi's the most dramatic but also the fussiest logistically. Stepping back, here are three areas that fit "secluded stay, view out the window, happy to drive" better, each a different flavor. All three have a good supply of standalone houses and cottages on Airbnb, and all are a manageable drive from Tbilisi.
1. Kakheti wine country — Sighnaghi & the Alazani Valley. Roughly 2 hours east on good roads (the gentlest drive of the three). Hillside houses looking out over endless vineyards to the Caucasus — pastoral rather than true wilderness, but genuinely beautiful, very low-key, and the warmest of the three right now. The towns are tiny, so you can sit on the edge of one with a big view and still have wine, food, and a shop a few minutes away. Airbnb search terms: Sighnaghi, Bodbe, Velistsikhe, Napareuli, Kvareli.
2. Bakuriani / Borjomi — the forest option. Roughly 2.5–3 hours southwest. Pine forest and mountain slopes at the edge of Borjomi-Kharagauli National Park — this is your most genuinely wilderness-adjacent pick. Plenty of standalone chalets and cabins, cooler alpine air, and mineral-spring towns nearby for supplies. Airbnb search terms: Bakuriani, Tsaghveri, Borjomi, Tsemi.
3. Gudauri — the high-mountain panorama. Roughly 2 hours north on the Military Highway (same road as Kazbegi, but you stop well short of the rough part). It's a ski resort that goes quiet and green in summer, set high up with enormous open mountain views — lots of view-facing apartments and chalets, and it's very car-friendly. One honest caveat: late May is shoulder season up there, so it'll be peaceful but some restaurants may be shut — plan to self-cater a little. Airbnb search term: Gudauri.
Quick gut-check on fit: if "low-key, easy, great view, food handy" is the priority → Kakheti. If you want forest-cabin seclusion → Bakuriani. If you want the biggest mountain view with the least rough driving → Gudauri.
