Neither, in effect — it nets to zero. You don't owe them anything, and you're not getting anything back.
Here's the math from their own computation:
Your NY tax came out to $1,028.
They tacked on a $58 estimated-tax penalty (for underpaying during the year — section 685(c)), bringingtotal amount due to $1,086.
Your total payments were also $1,086.
So: overpayment $0, refund $0.
What actually happened: you'd overpaid by exactly $58 (payments $1,086 vs. tax $1,028), which would normally have come back to you as a $58 refund. The penalty they assessed is also exactly $58, so it ate the refund. That's why the notice keeps saying "you are not entitled to a refund" — your original return apparently claimed that ~$58 back, and the penalty cancelled it out.
Bottom line: balance due is $0. There's nothing to pay, no deadline to worry about. It's essentially a "here's why your small refund became zero" letter.
The only thing you could do is dispute the $58 penalty if you think it was wrongly applied (you'd have grounds if your withholding/estimates met one of the safe-harbor thresholds), but for $58 it's almost certainly not worth the effort.
One note so you don't conflate them: this is NY State personal income tax (your IT-201-X), completely separate from the federal LLC partnership matter.

The one genuine caveat — and it's about your options, not an obligation — is the dispute window. If you ever wanted to challenge the $58 penalty, the notice gives you two years from the date (June 3, 2026) to protest. But that's purely optional, and for $58 it's not worth it. Letting it sit costs you nothing.
So: no action required. File it and move on.
