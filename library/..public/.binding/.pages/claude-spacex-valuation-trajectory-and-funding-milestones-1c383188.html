A few things worth flagging about what I found, since the data quality genuinely varies:
The spine of the trajectory is solid — the priced rounds (2015 Google/Fidelity at ~$12B, 2020 Series J at $46B, 2021 at $74B→$100B, 2022 Mirae at $127B, 2023 a16z at $137B) are well-documented because real money changed hands at stated prices. The 2024–2026 marks are shakier: they're secondary and insider sales, not new capital, and several come from investment-marketing sites that have a structural bias toward emphasizing upward momentum.
The single noisiest data point is the June 2026 IPO valuation, where reputable sources split between ~$1.77T (offer-price, per Wikipedia) and ~$2.3T (post-trading, per Sacra). I kept both rather than picking one, because the gap is real and reporting is still settling.
The two structural observations I'd draw your attention to: the 2023 a16z round was the last primary raise — everything after is repricing of existing shares, which behaves differently from genuine fundraising — and the IPO-era disclosures revealed a GAAP net loss (~$4.9B in 2025) despite Starlink being a real profit center, so a meaningful slice of the trillion-plus valuation rests on narrative (xAI integration, orbital compute, index-inclusion flows) rather than current earnings.
