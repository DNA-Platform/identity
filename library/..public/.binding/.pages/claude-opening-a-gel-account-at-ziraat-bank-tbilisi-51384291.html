Good news — this looks very doable. Here's what I found:
Ziraat Bank Georgia — Opening a GEL Account
Ziraat Bank Georgia offers multi-currency accounts in GEL, USD, EUR, and TRY, and they explicitly advertise the ability to withdraw cash from accounts opened at Ziraat branches in Turkey. Foreign citizens only need to present a passport or Georgian residence card to open an account — no extra procedures required. They also issue debit cards and have a mobile banking app.
So your existing relationship with Ziraat in Turkey could actually be an advantage — the two entities are connected (Ziraat Georgia is a subsidiary of T.C. Ziraat Bankası), and transferring between your Turkish and Georgian Ziraat accounts should involve favorable commission rates compared to interbank transfers.
For local Georgian purchases — a Ziraat-issued debit card would work at any Georgian business that accepts Visa or Mastercard, which is essentially everywhere in Tbilisi. The card processes through Georgia's domestic card systems (Georgian Card / United Financial Corporation), so it'll behave like any local bank card.
For Wildberries — Wildberries launched in Georgia in October 2024 under a franchise model, and as of September 2025 there are over 90 pickup points across Georgia including Tbilisi, Batumi, Kutaisi, and other cities. Their Tbilisi pickup points offer payment upon receipt from a bank card, so a Ziraat Georgia debit card should work. They also operate through wildberries.ge specifically for the Georgian market.
Practical considerations:
Ziraat is a much smaller bank in Georgia (13th by assets, ~0.26% market share) compared to TBC or Bank of Georgia. That means fewer ATMs and branches — one review noted their ATM situation can be limited. Their hours are also 10:00–13:30 and 14:30–18:00. So it's a perfectly fine account to have, especially for the Turkey transfer corridor, but you might still want a TBC or BoG account for day-to-day convenience given their massive ATM networks and broader app ecosystem.
Given your mobility situation, it might be worth calling ahead at (032) 294 37 04 to confirm what documents you need and whether the branch closest to Saburtalo would be easiest to visit.

You're right, that's solid logic. Ziraat Georgia explicitly states that foreign citizens only need a passport or residence card — that "or" is key. TBC and Bank of Georgia have become notoriously picky with non-residents over the past couple years, often requiring proof of income, residency, or a Georgian phone number, and sometimes just declining people outright.
So yes — if you can walk into Ziraat with your US passport and your existing Ziraat Turkey relationship, you're likely looking at the lowest-friction path to a working GEL account and debit card. The Turkey connection might even make them more accommodating since you're already a known customer in their system.
The tradeoffs (smaller ATM network, fewer branches) are real but manageable — you can always withdraw cash at any ATM with the card even if you pay a small fee at non-Ziraat machines, and for most purchases you'll just be tapping the card anyway. And for Wildberries specifically, you just need a working card to link.
Worth calling ahead to confirm nothing has changed in their policy and to ask whether they can pull up your Turkish account history, which might speed things along.
