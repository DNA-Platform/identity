Your intuition is right. Since March 2022 for Form N3, and since April 2024 for Form N2, prescriptions for controlled substances in Georgia are electronic only — paper forms are no longer issued and prescriptions are created exclusively in electronic form. The psychiatrist enters it into the central Ministry of Health system (prescription.moh.gov.ge) tied to your passport/ID, and any licensed pharmacy pulls it up when you arrive. Nothing is called into a specific pharmacy. The Ministry has explicitly confirmed that a patient can visit various pharmacies at different times, in any region of Georgia, and buy the medication at whichever pharmacy they need.
One important classification note: lorazepam/Ativan is Group I, dispensed on Form N2 — not the more common Form N3 used for most benzos. Aversi lists lorazepam 2mg as "Group I, issued with Form №2 prescription". Group I is the stricter tier (alongside narcotics), and fewer pharmacies are licensed to dispense it than Form N3. Worth confirming with the psychiatrist that they can actually issue Form N2 — not every prescriber has that authorization.
Finding the pharmacies:
Google Maps has no "psychotropic license" tag, so you can't filter for it there. Three practical paths:
Yellow Pages Georgia (yp.com.ge) has a dedicated "აფთიაქები - ფსიქოტროპული" category. In Saburtalo specifically, two are listed:
Pharmaguide #5 — Vazha-Pshavela Ave 6, ☎ 238 76 46
Pharmaton — Vazha-Pshavela Ave 27, ☎ 237 38 81
Call the chain hotlines. PSP (☎ 240 20 20) and Aversi (☎ 250 66 77) can tell you which of their Saburtalo branches hold Form N2 authorization — several do, they just don't advertise it. The 24-hour Aversi at Tsintsadze 2-4 and the PSP at Vazha-Pshavela Quarter VII are worth asking about first given proximity.
Ask the psychiatrist directly. They know from experience which nearby pharmacies have reliably filled their Form N2 scripts. This is the highest-signal route.
One more thing worth knowing: new rules took effect April 1, 2026 limiting adult psychotropic prescriptions to no more than 14 days' supply, and a new prescription is only allowed if no more than 5 days of medication remain. So plan refills accordingly.
