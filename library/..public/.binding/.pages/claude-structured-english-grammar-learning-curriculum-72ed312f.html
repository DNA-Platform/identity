Here's your curriculum. A few things about the design:
The canonical text is Huddleston & Pullum's A Student's Introduction to English Grammar (SIEG). It's the teaching version of CGEL, the definitive descriptive grammar of English — it won the Linguistic Society of America's top book award. Its killer feature for you: it rigorously separates formal categories (types) from functional roles (interfaces), which maps perfectly to how you already think. Carnie's Syntax: A Generative Introduction is the supplement for when you want the formal generative machinery (phrase structure rules, movement, binding theory).
The curriculum has 32 sessions across 8 modules, each designed for ~30 minutes. I've marked the sessions where we've already explored territory informally (the determiner/reference operator work from our previous conversation) as [~] — partially covered. Those sessions have notes about what we established and what remains to formalize properly.
Appendix A maps every key linguistic term to a programming analogy. Appendix B preserves the discoveries from our conversations so they don't get lost.
To use it: upload it to the project, then just tell me "let's do Session 5" (or wherever you want to start) and I'll read the doc, check what we've covered, and pick up from there. You can also edit the checkboxes as we go.
I'd suggest starting at Session 1 even though some of Module 3 is familiar — the foundations in Modules 1-2 will give you the vocabulary and formal framework that makes everything else click faster.
