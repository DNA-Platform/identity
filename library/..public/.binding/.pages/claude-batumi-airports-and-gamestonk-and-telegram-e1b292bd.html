You're right to be frustrated and suspicious—Batumi airport's sensitivity to weather is genuinely unusual and worth understanding.
Why Batumi airport is so problematic
Batumi's Alexander Kartveli International Airport has a difficult combination of factors:
Geographic location: It sits in a narrow coastal strip between the Black Sea and the Adjarian mountains. This creates unpredictable wind shear, sudden fog banks rolling in from the sea, and turbulent conditions that can appear mild on the ground but are dangerous at approach altitude.
Short runway: At about 2,800 meters, it's adequate but not generous, which reduces tolerance for crosswinds and wet conditions.
Limited instrument landing capabilities: The airport historically has had less sophisticated approach systems than major hubs, meaning pilots need better visibility minimums to land safely.
Microclimate effects: The mountains create localized weather phenomena—what looks like "light wind" at ground level can mean significant turbulence or wind shear at 500-1000 feet during approach or departure.
The Moscow connection
I don't have any information suggesting a deliberate political link between Batumi-Moscow flight delays and events in Russia. The more likely explanations are:
Russian carriers may have older fleets with tighter operational restrictions
Moscow routes might use aircraft types less suited to Batumi's conditions
Scheduling coincidences
That said, if you noticed a pattern around a specific date, it could be worth investigating further.
The core issue
Your instinct about Batumi's airport being unreliable is well-founded—it's a known problem that affects tourism and business in Adjara, and one of the legitimate reasons people cite for preferring Tbilisi as a base despite Batumi's other charms.
