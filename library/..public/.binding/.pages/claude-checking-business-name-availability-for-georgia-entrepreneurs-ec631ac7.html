Yes — there's a public search, but first the thing that actually matters for your form: for an Individual Entrepreneur the name field is genuinely optional, and "optional" here means something specific. An IE registers under your own personal name; a separate firm/trade name isn't required at all. For sole proprietors, a separate business name is not required — the business is registered under your personal name. So you can leave that field blank and be fully registered. You'd only fill it in if you want to trade under a name different from your own.
If you do want a trade name, it should be unique, and you can check what's already taken at the National Agency of Public Registry's entrepreneurs registry:
https://enreg.reestri.gov.ge/main.php?m=new_index&l=en
I just confirmed the page loads. Click "Search Application" (top of the page) — it opens a search form where you can look up any registered subject (including IEs) by identification code, by name/designation, or by legal form. So you can type a candidate name and see if anything comes back.
Two honest caveats, since this is the registry and I'd rather flag them than have you trust a clean-looking search:
The registered data is stored in Georgian — according to the General Administrative Code, the language of administrative proceedings is Georgian, so name, surname and firm name are recorded in Georgian. An English-spelled search may miss a name that exists only in Georgian transliteration. If a name matters to you, search the Georgian form too.
What I can verify is that the search exists and shows existing registrations. What I can't verify for you is the exact enforcement mechanism for an IE trade name — for LLCs a unique name is mandatory and NAPR blocks duplicates and even lets you reserve one in advance; for an IE trade name the sources say uniqueness applies but are vaguer on how hard the registrar enforces it. If the name is commercially important, that's worth a one-line question to whoever's processing the registration at the Public Service Hall rather than my guess.
Bottom line: if you don't have a specific brand reason, leave it blank — it's not slowing anything down. If you do, the link above is the place to check.

Look at the Legal form column — every one of the 3,460 rows is ინდივიდუალური მეწარმე, "individual entrepreneur," and every entry in Firm name is just ინდივიდუალური მეწარმე [a person's name]: "Individual Entrepreneur Giorgi Sharashenidze," "Individual Entrepreneur Luka Gagnidze," "Individual Entrepreneur Artem Mikhailov," "Individual Entrepreneur Ksenia Melekova," and so on. None of them contain "DNA."
So what you're looking at isn't a "DNA" match — it's a list of sole proprietors filed under their own personal names. The search didn't filter on "DNA" in any meaningful way (a Latin string won't match the Georgian-script name field cleanly, and the firm-name field here doesn't hold brand names to begin with).
And that's actually the concrete confirmation of what I flagged before: for an individual entrepreneur, the "firm name" is the person's own name. This whole screen is the proof — 3,460 people, each registered as "Individual Entrepreneur + their name." Sole proprietors don't get a separate brand slot in this registry the way an LLC does. Which is exactly why that field on your form is optional and why you can leave it blank.
Two things worth separating, since they live in different places:
Company-name uniqueness (the "is this name taken / can I reserve it" mechanic) is an LLC thing in this registry. It doesn't really apply to a sole-proprietor brand, so searching here won't give you a clean yes/no on "DNA."
Brand / trademark protection — if you actually want to operate under "DNA" (signage, invoices, marketing) and have it protected — lives at Sakpatenti, the National Intellectual Property Center (sakpatenti.gov.ge), which runs the trademark register and has an online trademark search. That's the database that would tell you whether "DNA" is already claimed as a mark.
I'll be straight about the edge of what I can verify: I can read what this screen shows, but I haven't confirmed the exact mechanics of how an IE would attach a distinct trade name in Georgia or how Sakpatenti's filing process runs end to end — so if the "DNA" brand matters commercially, that's a question to put to whoever's handling the registration, not something I'd want to improvise. For the IE form itself, none of this blocks you: leave the name blank and you're registered under your own name.
