In 3-4 sentences, what is the P versus NP problem and why does it matter?
Claude
07:56 UTC, 21 June 2026
P versus NP asks whether every problem whose solution can be verified quickly (in polynomial time) can also be solved quickly. NP is the class of problems with efficiently checkable solutions; P is the class solvable efficiently; the open question is whether these two classes are actually the same. It matters because a vast number of important problems—logistics, scheduling, protein folding, cryptography—are in NP, and if P = NP, all of them would have efficient algorithms, while if P ≠ NP (as most researchers suspect), some problems are inherently hard to solve despite being easy to check. The stakes are especially sharp for cryptography, which depends on certain problems being hard: a proof that P = NP could undermine much of modern encryption.
