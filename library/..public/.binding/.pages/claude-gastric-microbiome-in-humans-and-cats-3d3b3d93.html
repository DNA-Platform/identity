Do we humans or cats have any part of our micro biome in our stomachs?
Claude
13:20 UTC, 24 April 2026
Yes — both have gastric microbiomes. The old "sterile stomach" dogma collapsed with Marshall and Warren'sH. pylori work. Density is low compared to the colon (~10¹–10³ vs 10¹¹–10¹² CFU/mL), but it's a real, structured community, not just transient swallowed flora.
Humans: Beyond H. pylori, the dominant residents are Streptococcus, Prevotella, Veillonella, Rothia, and Lactobacillus — a mix of acid-tolerant colonizers and oral-origin taxa that establish some residence along the mucus layer.
Cats: Arguably a richer gastric Helicobacter ecology than humans. Feline stomachs commonly carry multiple non-pylori species — H. felis, H. heilmannii, H. bizzozeronii, H. salomonis — often at high prevalence without overt disease, which is a notable contrast to the human H. pylori pathogenesis story. You also see the usual acid-tolerant Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes/Proteobacteria background.
Mechanistically, gastric acid is a strong selective filter rather than a sterilizer. Survivors tend to be urease-producers (locally neutralize pH), mucus-layer biofilm formers, or bile/acid-tolerant transients riding through from the oropharynx.
