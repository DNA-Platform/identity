Do I need a lawyer to navigate the SSDI application?
Claude
23:50 UTC, 6 November 2024
While you don't legally require a lawyer for Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI) applications, here's what you should consider:
Without a lawyer:
You can complete the initial application yourself online, by phone, or in person
The process is designed to be accessible to individuals
It's free to apply on your own
SSA representatives can answer basic questions
Reasons to consider a lawyer:
Initial applications have a high denial rate (around 65%)
Lawyers understand how to:
Present medical evidence effectively
Frame your condition in terms of SSA's requirements
Meet technical documentation requirements
Navigate appeals if needed
Most disability lawyers work on contingency (they only get paid if you win)
Fees are typically limited to 25% of back pay or $6,000, whichever is less
Common middle ground:
Apply initially on your own
Consider hiring a lawyer if denied and need to appeal
Contact free disability advocates or legal aid organizations for guidance

Yes, banks offer safe deposit boxes for storing valuables like gold and silver. Here's what you should know:
Key benefits:
High security (vault, alarms, surveillance)
Insurance (though coverage varies by bank)
Private access
Often cheaper than private storage facilities

Size restrictions
Access only during bank hours

Annual rental fees ($20-$300+ depending on size)

Home safe (for smaller amounts)
Private vault facilities (often 24/7 access)

Safety deposit box at a credit union
Important considerations:
Document everything you store
Consider additional private insurance
Tell a trusted person how to access in emergencies

Check your homeowner's insurance for coverage limits on precious metals stored at home

Describe any side effects from medications that affect your ability to work
Explain how frequently your symptoms occur and their severity
This question directly relates to the SSA's definition of disability - being unable to perform substantial gainful activity due to a medical condition expected to last at least one year or result in death.
