Pavel Durov reportedly speaks eight languages. According to his official VK page, he can speak Russian, English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Latin and Farsi.
This linguistic ability stems from his multilingual upbringing and education. He spent most of his childhood in Turin, Italy, where his father was employed, and later studied English and Translation at Saint Petersburg State University. He also attended a school where he studied all subjects in depth, including four foreign languages.
There's some variation in sources—one source claims he became fluent in nine languages, including Italian and Persian, after returning to Russia—but the eight-language figure from his own VK profile is the most commonly cited.

Good question—the discrepancy likely comes from a few factors:
One source lists his eight languages as "including Arabic, Ukrainian, German, and Spanish"—which is a different set than the VK profile list (Russian, English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Latin, Farsi). This suggests different sources are counting differently.
Regarding Ukrainian specifically: during his time at VKontakte, Durov was the first among non-native speakers to launch a Ukrainian interface for the site. He spent several months testing the Ukrainian interface himself and learned a bit of the language. So Ukrainian may have been added to his repertoire after the VK profile was created.
The nine-language claim from NamuWiki may also simply be counting differently or including a language he learned later. Given that his father is a professor of philology and he and his brother Nikolai were learning six foreign languages each at school, along with advanced mathematics and chemistry, it wouldn't be surprising if his language count has grown over time.
So the answer is probably some combination of: different sources counting native vs. learned languages differently, varying definitions of "fluency," and Durov potentially picking up additional languages (like Ukrainian) since that VK profile was written.
