Yes, both exist — several Batumi hotels sell day passes to non-guests, and there's one big non-hotel pool.
Best-documented options:
Sheraton Batumi — outdoor Olympic-size pool in garden grounds, probably the best kid option. Hours 8:00–20:00; 95 GEL/day adults, 47.5 GEL kids under 12, free under 6. It also has a children's pool.
Le Méridien Batumi — smaller indoor pool, towels provided, 7:00–23:00, 80 GEL/day. Central location.
Radisson Blu (Anne Semonin Spa) — heated pool in the cold season; day pass includes pool, gym, and sauna. More spa than kid venue, though.
Batumi Plaza (shopping complex, not a hotel) — the city's largest pool, on the top floor, indoor with a retractable roof in summer; jacuzzi and gym on site. Older sources list ~30 GEL per visit, but that price is dated — worth calling to confirm.
Caveats: the Sheraton/Le Méridien prices above are from May 2026 and look current; the Batumi Plaza and other prices come from older articles and I couldn't verify them for right now. Kids' age policies vary — some hotel spa pools restrict young children, so confirm before going. Sheraton explicitly welcomes kids (free under 6), which makes it the safest bet.
If the weather's good, the Sheraton outdoor pool is the obvious pick for a family day (~190 GEL for you plus two kids under 12, less if either is under 6).

The strongest match is SeaView Restaurant Club — it sits directly on the beach at 8 Lech and Maria Kaczynski St, which is right across the street from the Orbi Beach Tower (the tall cylindrical Orbi building on Kaczynski). It's a large deck structure built out over the water.
What I found about it:
Cuisine: Asian, European, and Georgian cuisine, with hookah, karaoke, DJ sets, and a sea view; open 9:30 until 5:00. The menu also has a Lebanese section and a dedicated children's menu, plus pizza, pasta, khachapuri, and seafood.
Reviews are mixed: Google rating 3.7 from ~400 reviews. People consistently praise the location, sunset views, and the Lebanese dishes; the recurring complaints are slow, disorganized service — forgotten orders, long waits, and one reviewer describing a billing dispute. A Tripadvisor reviewer, though, described attentive ownership, very clean facilities, and beach chairs with umbrella and towel for 30 GEL for the whole day — so experiences vary a lot.
It leans club/lounge in the evening (loud music is a complaint in recent reviews), so lunch or early dinner is probably the better family window.
Phone: +995 598 17 17 22; it's also on Wolt if you'd rather just order to the room.
One caveat on identification: "tall circular Orbi" fits Orbi Beach Tower on Kaczynski, and SeaView is the only restaurant structure extending over the water there. But if you're actually at Orbi City on Khimshiashvili (the twin curved towers), the beachfront spots across from that one are Montaña Seaside, Sea Pearl Cafe, and Shore House — none of which extend into the sea the way SeaView does. If the building you're seeing is on pilings over the water, it's SeaView.

Good call — G17 it is, and you're right that it's not just a restaurant. It's a concept venue built on a bridge extending into the sea on Batumi's New Embankment, combining dining, lounge, bar, a stage, and a patisserie, with regular live music performances. The address is 13 Sherif Khimshiashvili St — which puts it directly across from the Orbi towers, matching what you're seeing.
What it actually is:
Their own branding calls it a "multifunctional venue by the glimmering Black Sea" with all-day dining plus Dolci17, an in-house patisserie, and a separate event space called Stage17. It hosts concerts — Boney M. played there, and tickets for events are sold through Georgian ticketing sites.
Hours 10:00–02:00, with a lunch menu, live music, parking, cards accepted; phone (+995) 511 44 17 17.
Food reviews are split. One Tripadvisor visitor praised the stylish, well-maintained premises, the "unsurpassed" location in the sea, and tasty food (though small portions); another called it overpriced diner-quality food with poor management.
Vibe shifts by time of day: daytime dining transforms toward a lively beach-club atmosphere with a resident DJ as the sun sets.
One inconsistency worth flagging: a couple of aggregator pages describe a "G17" as a 17th-floor rooftop sky bar. That doesn't match the pier location or the official address — either it's a second G17-branded space or the aggregator is just wrong. The venue you're looking at, on the bridge over the water at Khimshiashvili 13, is the one described in the sources above.
Practical read: fine for a daytime family lunch or the patisserie with the kids — it turns into a DJ/lounge scene in the evening.
