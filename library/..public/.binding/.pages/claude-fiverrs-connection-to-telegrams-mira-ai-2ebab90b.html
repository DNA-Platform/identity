Here's what the record shows. The Telegram Mira is real and exactly what you described: TOP announced Mira as the first personal AI assistant built by a TOP portfolio team, live in Telegram, and in private mode requests run via Cocoon's decentralized GPU network built on TON with encrypted execution. It launched February 2026, went global in group chats in May, has passed 2 million users and 500K+ MAU, and routes tasks across OpenAI, Anthropic, Minimax, ByteDance and ElevenLabs, with a roadmap of agent-to-agent interactions and payments through a dedicated sub-wallet, under CEO Daria Yakovleva.
On the Fiverr side, I can't find the announcement you received anywhere public — no press release, help-center article, or news coverage ties Fiverr to any "Mira." Fiverr's own AI branding trail runs Fiverr Go (Feb 2025) → AI Video Hub (March 2026), and their existing brief system already uses an unnamed AI assistant that refines client project briefs before matching freelancers. "Mira will be curating job descriptions" reads like Fiverr putting a persona name on that brief-curation layer — and Mira is a heavily collided name in this space right now (Meltwater shipped a "Mira" too).
So my posterior on identity is low, well under 10% — not from base rates alone, but from two specific absences: TOP PRs every milestone loudly, and a Fiverr integration would be a bigger story for them than for Fiverr; and an NYSE-listed Israeli marketplace outsourcing core curation to a TON-ecosystem Telegram agent would be a strange procurement move to do silently. The notification itself should discriminate quickly: any Telegram/@mira branding, a link to mira.tg, or Cocoon/privacy language would flip this; a bare first-name persona with Fiverr's own styling confirms the in-house read.
The live channel between the two worlds runs the other direction, though, and it's the more interesting one for your thesis: Mira connects skills, APIs, and MCP integrations and is built to act — crypto layer included, and the sub-wallet roadmap means Telegram-native agents transacting on external marketplaces within authorized limits. That's an agent reaching into Fiverr-like platforms as a demand channel on TON rails — which slots into the adoption picture regardless of what Fiverr named its curator.
