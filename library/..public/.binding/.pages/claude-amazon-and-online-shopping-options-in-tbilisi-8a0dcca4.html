Short answer: Amazon doesn't operate in Georgia the way Wildberries does — no local warehouse, no pickup points, no fast domestic-style delivery.
Wildberries is actually your best Amazon-like option there. It launched in Georgia in late 2024 and has built out a real local presence — by September 2025 there were over 90 pickup points across Tbilisi, Batumi, Kutaisi, Rustavi and other cities. Clothing, footwear, cosmetics, and books are the most popular categories. So for everyday goods, Wildberries is probably your closest thing to an Amazon experience with fast local delivery.
For Amazon specifically, your options are all forwarding-based:
Freight forwarders like Dnipro LLC, MyUS, or Strelicia will give you a US (or Russian) address, receive your packages, then ship them to Tbilisi. Dnipro charges about $6.14/kg by air with free pickup from their Tbilisi warehouse. But you're looking at 1-3 weeks total, not a few days.
Amazon International Shopping lets you filter for items that ship directly to Georgia, but the selection is limited and delivery is slow.
Services like Strelicia specifically handle Wildberries, Ozon, and other Russian store purchases forwarded to Georgia — shipments from Russia go once a week, from other countries every two weeks.
Other local/regional e-commerce options worth knowing about: Temu, 2 Nabiji (a Georgian app), Joom, SHEIN, and Trendyol (Turkish) are all popular shopping apps in Georgia. Trendyol in particular ships from Turkey and might get things to you relatively quickly given the proximity.
For your situation with limited mobility, Wildberries pickup points + Glovo/Wolt for local stuff is probably your most practical combo. If you need something specific from Amazon, the forwarder route works but plan for ~2 weeks minimum.

Here's the practical setup, comparing your two main options:
Option 1: MyUS (best for multiple/larger orders)
Sign up at myus.com — you instantly get a US address in Sarasota, Florida with a unique suite number
Shop Amazon (or any US store) and use that MyUS address at checkout
Your MyUS address gives you 0% US sales tax, which is a nice bonus
Packages arrive at their warehouse, they notify you by email
You can wait for multiple orders to arrive, then they consolidate everything into one box (saving up to 80% on shipping)
Submit a ship request, pick your carrier (DHL, FedEx, UPS)
Express shipping takes about 2-4 business days once it leaves their facility, economy 5-10 days
Pricing: Premium membership is free for 3 months, then $9.99/month — this gets you free consolidation and repackaging. Shipping cost is weight-based.
Option 2: Dnipro LLC (cheaper for Georgia specifically)
Register at dniprollc.com — get a US address
Same process: shop, ship to their US address
Air shipping to their Tbilisi pickup location is $6.14/kg, home delivery in Tbilisi adds $5, and warehouse pickup is free
Free storage up to 45 days, max parcel weight 30kg
My recommendation for your situation: Given that you're on mobility aids, MyUS with DHL/FedEx door-to-door delivery is probably worth the premium since they deliver right to your address. Dnipro is cheaper per kilo but requires either pickup from their warehouse or paying for local delivery separately.
One heads up: prescription medications (including veterinary) can't be forwarded — so for Penelope's meds, that won't work through these services. But for electronics, household stuff, books, specialty items, etc., this is the way to go.

Yes, you're already set up. Traveling Mailbox can receive packages and forward them anywhere in the world, with customs preparation included for free. They claim to have the lowest shipping rates in the industry.
Here's how it works with what you already have:
Ship Amazon orders to your Traveling Mailbox address — they can receive packages from Amazon, UPS, FedEx, all carriers
Log into your dashboard — you'll see the package show up just like your mail
Request forwarding to your Tbilisi address — you pick USPS, FedEx, or UPS, and they consolidate multiple packages at no extra charge
Cost is postage + $2 per shipment (not per item), so bundling saves a lot
A few practical notes:
First 30 days of package storage is free, then $0.10/lb/day after that — so don't let stuff sit too long
All forwarding requests are completed within 24 hours, then transit time depends on the carrier you pick
You can bundle multiple Amazon orders into one international shipment to save on shipping
So you don't need MyUS or Dnipro at all — just order from Amazon to your existing Traveling Mailbox address and have them forward to Georgia. The only advantage of a dedicated forwarder like MyUS would be if their bulk international rates to Georgia end up cheaper, but honestly, with consolidation and your existing setup, Traveling Mailbox should work fine.
