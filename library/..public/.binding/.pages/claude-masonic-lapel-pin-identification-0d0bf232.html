That's a 1964 Kennedy half dollar embedded in a yellow/amber lucite (acrylic resin) paperweight — a pretty common novelty item from the 1960s-70s.
The coin itself is noteworthy: 1964 was the first year the Kennedy half was minted, and crucially, it's90% silver. Based on current silver prices, the melt value alone is typically around $10-12, though it fluctuates with silver spot prices.
As for the paperweight as a whole, it's a mixed bag. The lucite encasement was a popular souvenir/gift item of the era. Some collectors like them, but embedding the coin permanently reduces its numismatic value since you can't grade or inspect it properly. A loose 1964 Kennedy half in good condition would be worth more to a coin collector than one trapped in resin.
That said, the paperweight itself has some modest vintage/kitsch appeal — you might see them go for $15-25 on eBay depending on condition. If someone were willing to crack the resin to extract the coin, the silver value alone makes it worth holding onto.

That's a great provenance. A jeweler's collection of gemstones and cameos could have some real treasures in it — jewelers tend to accumulate interesting pieces over a career, both for professional stock and personal fascination.
The Masonic pin fits perfectly: Freemasonry had deep roots in the trades and crafts, and jewelers were well-represented in lodges. Your grandfather may well have been a member himself, or it could have been a custom piece he made or was given.
If there are cameos in the collection, those could be the most valuable items depending on the material and craftsmanship. Shell cameos are the most common, but a jeweler might have had hardstone cameos (agate, onyx, sardonyx) which are considerably more valuable, especially antique ones. Hand-carved Italian cameos from the 19th century can fetch serious money.
Are you going through the full collection? If you want to photograph more pieces, I'm happy to help identify what you've got — and it might be worth having a gemologist or antique jewelry appraiser take a look at the better pieces, especially if there are loose stones or unmounted cameos in the mix.
