Searched the web for “giardia litter box cleaning disinfection prevent reinfection”
After removing all the litter, here's what you need to do to properly disinfect the litter box and prevent giardia reinfection:
Cleaning steps:
Scrub with hot soapy water - Empty the box completely and scrub all surfaces thoroughly with hot water and dish soap
Disinfect with bleach solution - Soak the clean box in a dilute bleach solution:
Mix 3/4 cup to 2 cups of bleach per gallon of water
Let the solution stay in contact with all surfaces for the recommended time (check the bleach product label)
Rinse thoroughly - Rinse with clean water until no bleach residue remains
Let it dry completely - Giardia cysts are susceptible to drying, so allow the box to air dry fully before adding fresh litter
Additional important steps:
Scoop twice daily during treatment (not just once)
Do the deep clean on the last day of treatment
Clean the scoop the same way (soak in bleach solution)
Consider steam cleaning if you have a steam cleaner - the high heat is very effective
Alternative disinfectants: If you can't use bleach on your particular litter box material, quaternary ammonium compounds (like some Lysol products) also work against giardia cysts.
The key is that soap alone isn't enough - you need the disinfectant step to kill the hardy giardia cysts that can survive for months in damp environments.

That's actually a smart call. Litter boxes are relatively inexpensive, and replacing them eliminates any risk of missing cysts in scratches or porous areas that are hard to fully disinfect. Many vets actually recommend this approach, especially if you have multiple cats or the boxes are older and scratched up.
Just make sure to thoroughly clean and disinfect the area where the old boxes were sitting before putting down the new ones, and properly dispose of the old boxes (bag them up before throwing away). You'll still want to bathe your cat to remove any cysts from their fur and maintain that twice-daily scooping routine during treatment to prevent reinfection.
