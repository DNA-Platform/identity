The pattern you're describing is fairly reassuring: a lower-GI bleed from an inflamed colon that's starting to recover.
Where the blood is coming from. The blood sits on the surface in one patch rather than being mixed through the stool, so the source is distal, meaning the colon, rectum, or anus. Darkened or clotted red blood on a formed stool usually means it pooled briefly in the distal colon or rectum before she passed it. That's different from melena, which is black, tarry, mixed throughout, and points to the upper GI tract. Nothing in the photo looks like melena.
Why it happens even as things improve. Hematochezia with mucus is a classic feature of Tritrichomonas colitis, and the mucosa heals more slowly than stool form normalizes. A firmer stool moving through still-friable colon can also scrape the lining and leave a small clot. Better consistency with some residual blood is a common mid-treatment picture. The glistening jelly around the clot is likely mucus, which fits the same colitis.
What would change this read:
Blood in more stools over several days, or the amount increasing
Fresh red blood dripping, or blood without stool
Black, tarry stool
Straining with little output, lethargy, vomiting, or reduced appetite
It's worth sending this photo to your vet as a mid-course data point. If the next several stools stay formed and the blood fades out, that's the trajectory you want.
