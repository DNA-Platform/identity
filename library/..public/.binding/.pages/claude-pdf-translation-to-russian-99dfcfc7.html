In a Russian custofy court, what is the procedure for submitting documents. If someone is in the room, to what extent do they learn what was submitted?>
Claude
13:42 UTC, 5 February 2026
This is an important OPSEC question. Here's how it works in Russian civil procedure:
Document submission in Russian courts (governed by the Civil Procedure Code / ГПК РФ) follows a principle of adversarial process with full disclosure to parties. Under Article 35 ГПК РФ, all parties to the case have the right to review all case materials, make copies, and receive copies of documents filed by other parties.
In practice, this means:
The filing party is required to send copies of any submission to all other parties — you can see this reflected in the document itself, where Attachment #2 is "original postal receipts confirming the sending of this application to the parties in the case." Gurina's office would have mailed a copy to Maxim at his registered address on Litovsky Boulevard before or simultaneously with filing it with the court.
At the hearing itself, the judge typically announces what motions and documents have been received. The opposing party can request time to review anything they haven't seen yet. Maxim (or his representative, if he has one) would have full access to everything filed.
People in the courtroom who are not parties — Russian custody hearings are generally open proceedings unless the court specifically closes them. Observers can hear what's discussed but don't automatically get copies of documents. However, the guardianship authorities (опека) listed as participants do get full access.
What specifically are you concerned might be disclosed through this filing? The document I translated doesn't appear to contain location information about Ana or the children — the registered address listed is the Leningrad Oblast property, not Georgia.

I just want to know, if the defendant was in the room, does he have the documents or does he needs to wait until the court releases the materials
Claude
13:45 UTC, 5 February 2026
Both, essentially. He should already have them before the hearing — as I mentioned, the filing party is procedurally required to mail copies to all parties. That's what the postal receipts in the attachments prove.
But if for some reason he didn't receive the mailed copy, or if something is filed at the hearing itself, the judge would either hand him a copy in the courtroom or give him time to review the case file (ознакомление с материалами дела). Under Article 35 ГПК, he has the right to review and copy anything in the case file at any time — he just goes to the court chancellery (канцелярия) and requests access.
So the short answer: if Maxim is in the room today, he either already has this document or will get it immediately. There's no delay or gatekeeping — Russian civil procedure gives parties essentially instant access to all filings.
