First, the address at the top reads correctly: "Department of the Treasury Internal Revenue Service Ogden, UT 84201-0039." That's right, in the right order. Good.
On the postage — there's a real decision here, and it's not "pick the fastest."
A subtle thing matters for IRS mail: the timely-mailing-is-timely-filing rule only protects you when you use USPS or one of the IRS-designated private carriers under specific service levels. But that rule is about deadlines, and you don't have a real deadline here — the June 1 date is a pay-by date for interest, not an abatement deadline, and FTA isn't time-barred the way a return-filing deadline is. So you don't need to buy speed to protect a deadline. That removes the main reason people overpay for IRS mail.
Given that, the decision collapses to: do you want proof it arrived, or not?
The $1.90 USPS "No Tracking" option does the job — it's a written statement reaching the IRS, which is all the process requires, and it's what the overwhelming majority of FTA letters go out as. The downside is exactly its name: no tracking, no proof of delivery. If it vanishes in the mail (rare but real), you'd have no record and you'd just re-send.
For $1,020 at stake, I'd personally spend a little to not be in the dark. The sweet spot is FedEx Ground Economy at $8.18 or UPS Ground Saver at $10.32 — both give you a tracking number so you can confirm it landed, without the absurd $37–$129 for speed you don't need. Of those two, FedEx Ground Economy is cheaper and shows a real delivery window (2–7 days) versus UPS Ground Saver showing "N/A," so FedEx is the better-defined choice.
One caveat I'll flag rather than gloss over: I'm not certain whether tracked delivery to an IRS service-center PO ZIP gives you a clean "delivered" scan or just "arrived at facility" — IRS campuses receive enormous mail volume and the final handoff isn't always a crisp confirmation. So tracking here means "I can see it reached Ogden," not necessarily "I have a signed receipt." If you wanted true proof-of-delivery you'd use USPS Certified, which doesn't appear as an option in this list. So among what's shown, tracked-but-not-certified is the realistic ceiling.
My recommendation: FedEx Ground Economy, $8.18. Cheap, tracked enough to confirm it reached Ogden, no deadline pressure forcing you to pay for overnight. If you genuinely don't mind re-sending in the unlikely event it's lost, the $1.90 USPS is defensible too — it's not wrong, just blind.
