Leonardo da Vinci was known for his distinctive "mirror writing" - he wrote from right to left in a reversed script that could be read normally when viewed in a mirror. While there's some debate about why he wrote this way, it may have been to protect his work from being easily read by others, or because it was more comfortable for him as a left-handed person.
His handwriting was small and precise, with no spaces between words and minimal punctuation. He used both hands for writing but favored his left hand. The text often flowed vertically down the page in columns.
You can see examples of this writing style in his notebooks like the Codex Arundel and Codex Leicester. The script has a flowing, consistent quality despite being backward, showing his remarkable control and precision.
However, it's worth noting that Leonardo didn't exclusively write in mirror script - he could write normally when needed, particularly for documents intended for others to read. He also wrote in a conventional direction when working on materials meant for public consumption.
Since this is quite a specialized historical topic about a specific individual's handwriting style from centuries ago, I should mention that while I aim to be accurate, you may want to verify these specific details against primary sources or academic works about Leonardo da Vinci.
