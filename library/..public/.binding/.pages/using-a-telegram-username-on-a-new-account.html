I bought a telegram username on TON but I don’t know how to open a new account with it. What do I do? I have a windows computer with telegram desktop
Claude
18:00 UTC, 10 August 2026
A username on Fragment doesn't create an account by itself — Telegram accounts are anchored to a number, and the username is an asset you assign to an account afterward. So there are two paths depending on what you want:
If you want the username on a brand-new account:
You need a number for the new account. Either a regular SIM, or — cleaner for this use case — an anonymous +888 number from Fragment (fragment.com/numbers). The +888 numbers receive login codes through Fragment itself, no SIM involved.
Create the account. Telegram Desktop on Windows works for this: log out or add account (hamburger menu → Add Account), enter the number. If it's a +888 number, the login code appears on Fragment (log in there with your TON wallet, the code shows under the number's page).
Assign the username: on Fragment, open your username's page → "Assign to account" → it links via the Telegram login widget. Or from Telegram: Settings → Username → your Fragment usernames appear as "collectibles" you can activate once the account is linked.
If you just want it on your existing account: skip the new-account part — go to the username page on Fragment while logged in with the same TON wallet that holds it, and assign it to your logged-in Telegram account. It becomes selectable in Settings → Edit Profile → Username.
One thing worth knowing: a fresh account created on desktop with no SMS-capable number can occasionally trip Telegram's anti-spam and get limited early. The +888 route is the officially supported path and avoids that better than virtual SIM services do.

I have ageorgian number. Can I make an account out of that?
Claude
18:03 UTC, 10 August 2026
Yes — a Georgian number works exactly like any other SIM number. In Telegram Desktop: hamburger menu → Add Account (or just enter it on the login screen if starting fresh), type the number with +995, and you'll get the code by SMS or a call.
Two caveats:
If that number is already attached to your existing Telegram account (or Ana's), you can't use it for a second account — one number, one account. You'd need a different number.
Once the new account exists, assign the Fragment username to it: fragment.com → your username's page → "Assign to account," logging into Fragment with the TON wallet that holds the username.
After that, the username shows up under Settings → Edit Profile → Username on the new account.

Does the fragment username do anything? Whats the point
Claude
18:04 UTC, 10 August 2026
Functionally, almost nothing beyond what a free username does — people can find you at t.me/yourname, message you without your number, that's it. The point is in the properties around it:
It's yours as property. A free username is a rental from Telegram — go inactive long enough and it can be reclaimed and reassigned. A Fragment username is an NFT on TON; it sits in your wallet, nobody can take it back, and it survives you deleting the account entirely.
It's transferable and sellable. You can move it between your own accounts, or sell it on Fragment. Short and dictionary-word names have a real secondary market — that's essentially the whole reason the auction system exists. Some people treat them purely as speculative assets and never attach them to an account at all.
Scarce names are only available this way. The short, clean, generic ones (@casino, @bank, four-letter words) were reserved out of the free pool; Fragment is the only door to them.
It anchors t.me web presence. The username also gives you the t.me/yourname link and, for channels, a tradeable brand identity that isn't hostage to Telegram's reclamation policy.
So: as a utility, it's a vanity plate. As a holding, it's a small piece of transferable, censorship-resistant namespace on the network — which is more or less a miniature of the broader TON thesis, incidentally: Telegram converting parts of its platform into on-chain property.
