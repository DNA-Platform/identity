The quote is real. Isaacman said it in an interview with Benny Johnson, framing it in the context of Mars sample return missions specifically — ancient microbial life, not little green men.
Now to your actual question, which is more interesting. You're essentially poking at two distinct issues:
Is 90% a defensible number? It's a hype number, not a scientific estimate. The scientific community is genuinely excited about Mars habitability — it had liquid water, a thicker atmosphere, and hydrothermal systems during the Noachian period (~4 billion years ago) — but "90%" is Isaacman doing advocacy for sample return funding, not Bayesian reasoning. No astrobiologist would put a number that high on it publicly without being laughed out of the room.
Your deeper point about the DNA timeline is sharp. You're essentially asking: even if Mars was habitable ~3.5-4 Gya, was that long enough after abiogenesis on Earth for life to have spread there via panspermia? Or conversely, was Mars habitable long enough to have independently generated life before going dead? This is a real constraint that gets underappreciated in popular coverage. Mars dried out relatively fast — the window of surface habitability may have been only a few hundred million years, which is tight for independent abiogenesis but not impossible given that life on Earth appears to have emerged pretty quickly after conditions allowed.
The lithopanspermia angle (your "fossilized something from Earth") is actually taken seriously. Large impacts on early Earth could absolutely have launched material to Mars, and some fraction of that could carry organisms in cryptobiotic states. The Martian meteorite transit time is on the order of millions of years, which is long — but spores in rock interiors can be shielded from radiation. The problem is it's almost unfalsifiable: if we found microbial fossils on Mars with Earth-like biochemistry, we couldn't easily distinguish independent origin from contamination from Earth, ancient or modern.
The most scientifically honest version of Isaacman's claim would be something like: sample return would substantially update our probability estimates — not that the prior is already 90%.
