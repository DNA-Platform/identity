Dynamical Systems Research, Inc. was a software startup co-founded by Nathan Myhrvold in the mid-1980s in Oakland/Berkeley, California. Myhrvold had just left a postdoctoral fellowship at Cambridge where he'd been working under Stephen Hawking on quantum cosmology — he took the summer of 1984 off to work on a software project that grew into the company.
The company's main product was Mondrian, a clone of IBM's TopView multitasking environment for DOS. Essentially, it was building software to enable multitasking on personal computers running DOS — a significant capability at the time when PCs were still single-task machines.
The program caught Steve Ballmer's attention, and Microsoft purchased Dynamical Systems Research in 1986 for $1.5 million in stock. The acquisition was really a talent acquisition as much as a technology play — Myhrvold and his brother Cameron both joined Microsoft as part of the deal.
Once at Microsoft, Myhrvold rose rapidly. He worked at Microsoft for 13 years, culminating in his appointment as the company's first chief technology officer in 1996. His most lasting contribution was arguably founding Microsoft Research in 1991, which he envisioned as comparable to Xerox PARC and Bell Labs. He also played key roles in developing Windows, Windows NT, and Office, and co-authored The Road Ahead with Bill Gates.
