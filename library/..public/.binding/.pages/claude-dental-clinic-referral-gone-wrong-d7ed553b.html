Based on your description - hardware placed at the heel with a lateral (outside) incision - this sounds like a lateralizing calcaneal osteotomy with screw or plate fixation, likely for cavus foot or similar alignment correction.
Reassuring signs at 6-7 weeks:
Incision fully closed, no drainage or opening
Swelling decreasing (though some swelling normal for months)
Pain trending down week-over-week, not up
No sharp pain at the hardware site
Skin color normal, not red or hot
You can wiggle toes without significant pain
Concerning signs that need urgent attention:
Pain getting worse rather than better
New sharp pain at screw/plate site (could indicate hardware loosening)
Redness, warmth, or drainage at incision
Fever
Sudden pain with any movement that was previously tolerable
The hard truth:
At 6-7 weeks, clinical signs tell you about soft tissue healing and infection - they don't tell you about bone. The bone could feel fine and still not be ready for weight-bearing. Conversely, some discomfort is normal even when bone is healing well.
The orthopedist needs X-rays to see the callus formation (new bone bridging the osteotomy site). That's the only way to clear you for weight-bearing.

What you're describing sounds consistent with sural nerve irritation - which is a known, common complication of the lateral approach for calcaneal osteotomy.
The sural nerve:
Runs exactly where your incision is (lateral heel/ankle)
Provides sensation to the outer foot and pinky toe
Gets stretched, compressed, or bruised during surgery in this area
The encouraging part:
Your numbness is improving. That's the key indicator. If the nerve were severed, it wouldn't be getting better. What you're describing - numbness that initially extended to the pinky toe and is now receding - suggests the nerve was stretched or bruised (neuropraxia), not cut.
The tingling and "weirdness":
That's actually what nerve recovery feels like. As the nerve wakes back up, it often produces strange sensations - tingling, pins and needles, odd pressure feelings. It's annoying but it's a sign of healing, not damage.
Timeline reality:
Sural nerve recovery can take 3-12 months. Some residual numbness along the incision line may be permanent but usually isn't functionally significant.
For the orthopedist:
Mention the nerve symptoms. They may want to document the distribution of numbness and track recovery. But "getting better" is exactly what you want to hear at this stage.
The weird bone feeling when weight-bearing is likely just your heel learning to bear load again after 6 weeks off - plus possible sensation from hardware.
