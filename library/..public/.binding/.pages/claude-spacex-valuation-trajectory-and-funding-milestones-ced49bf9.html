A few things I'd flag as the analytically interesting parts, in case they shape how you want to use it:
The trajectory is really two different curves stitched together. Through January 2023 (~$137B, the a16z round), every jump was a primary raise — new capital at a negotiated price, generally anchored to a demonstrated capability. After that, the climb from ~$137B to ~$800B was almost entirely secondary/insider marks: scarcity pricing for pre-IPO shares, not money into the company. A naive valuation-over-time chart hides that the mechanism changed completely. I split the "type" column explicitly so that distinction stays visible.
The last leg is an AI re-rating, not an operations story. ~$137B to $1.77T is roughly 13x in 3.5 years against revenue (~$15–16B in 2025) that grew far less — a 100x+ revenue multiple. The xAI absorption in Feb 2026 is the hinge: it re-cast SpaceX as a vertically integrated orbital-compute play, which is simultaneously the bull thesis and the reason the downside is real.
