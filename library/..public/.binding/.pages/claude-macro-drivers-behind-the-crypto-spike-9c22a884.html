The SpaceX pattern was: pain before, relief on. The stock bled into the event — a seven-day losing streak in July, then a nearly 14% drop on the Wednesday before, its second-worst day on record, closing at $108.27 against a $135 IPO. Then on unlock day, with 911.5M shares eligible — more than doubling the float from 4.9% to 11.8% of shares outstanding — the stock closed up 6.1%. Classic sell-the-rumor: the supply fear got expressed through the price in the weeks prior, and the actual holders mostly didn't sell at a loss. (Side note for your other forward test: on August 14 SpaceX closed the Cursor acquisition, issuing ~389M new Class A shares — about $60B of stock. That signal is already confirmed, eight days after the first unlock.)
Whether Monad rhymes depends on three differences, and they cut against full pricing-in:
Size. SpaceX's unlock roughly doubled float in a stock that had only traded two months. Monad's November cliff is ~4B tokens against ~12B circulating — a 33% float increase — but those are team/investor/treasury tokens with cost bases near zero, not employees underwater. SpaceX insiders were looking at $108 vs a $135 print and chose to wait. Monad seed investors at fractions of a cent have no such restraint.
Bid depth. SPCX had Morgan Stanley talking it up and a passive-index bid coming. MON is a $300M-cap token with $40M daily volume on a good day; the buyer of last resort is the Foundation's own buyback program, if it's still live.
How much is already priced. MON has been drifting at $0.02 for months — below ICO, near all-time lows, with the largest perp short in the name sitting on the calendar date. That's a market that knows the unlock is coming and has been shorting it for nine months. So a lot is priced, but "priced in" for a thin token means the shorts get paid when the flow actually hits, not that the flow is absorbed. The SpaceX analog would have to be: the squeeze now is the pre-event relief, the grind lower resumes into November, and the day itself is a non-event or bounce — which is what you'd want for the exit, since it means the window is now, not then.
