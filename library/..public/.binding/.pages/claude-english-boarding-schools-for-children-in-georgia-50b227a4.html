Short answer: Yes, Russian-passport children can legally attend Cyprus schools in 2026 — Russian families do it routinely — but the operational scenario matters a lot, and the unaccompanied-boarding version is the hard one.
What's still working for Russians in Cyprus
Cyprus is not Schengen yet (still planning to join in 2026), so it runs its own national visa system. Russian Federation citizens are explicitly listed among the most common Pink Slip applicants of the last 5 years, alongside Ukraine, China, Belarus, Israel, Lebanon, UAE, Egypt, India and Pakistan. Visa centres in Nicosia, Limassol and Paphos are operating as of August 2025. Russian documents don't require apostille (bilateral agreement). The Russian-speaking community in Cyprus (especially Limassol) is large and well-served, with dedicated Russian-medium schools.
What changed
Russian arrivals collapsed from 780,000 in 2019 to ~90,000 in 2024 after sanctions and flight bans. More consequentially, on 7 November 2025 the European Commission formally revoked the possibility for Russian nationals to obtain multiple-entry Schengen visas; every Russian applicant must now file a fresh application for each trip. Cyprus — still outside Schengen but aligned with Schengen visa policy — effectively means the same for short-stay visitor visas.
Two viable pathways
Pathway 1 — Family relocates to Cyprus, kids as day students. This is the well-trodden route. Pink Slip / temporary residence permit for the whole family. Requirements: €24,000/year baseline income from abroad transferred to a Cyprus bank account, +20% for spouse (€4,800), +15% per child (€3,600), so a family of four needs roughly €36,000/year demonstrated foreign income; €10,000 in a Cyprus bank account at application; a minimum 1-year rental or property purchase. The Pink Slip explicitly grants "access to local schools for children" and inclusion of spouse and minor children as dependents. Both kids attend ISOP, PASCAL, or another school as day pupils. No boarding visa complication. This is the path Russian families have been using since 2022.
Pathway 2 — Children in Cyprus boarding, parents stay in Tbilisi. This is the operationally fragile one. Cyprus does have a secondary-school student status, but the immigration code I found is framed for pupils aged 12-19 in "recognised pupil exchange programmes" — which isn't quite the same as private-school boarding. ISOP and PASCAL aren't large international-boarding operations (ISOP boards up to 70 total; PASCAL's boarding is similarly small) and most boarders appear to be regional rather than non-EU minors with parents in a third country. Whether either school routinely sponsors residence permits for Russian-passport minors with parents resident in Georgia — I can't confirm from public sources. This needs a direct admissions conversation, ideally followed by a Cyprus immigration lawyer.
The travel friction nobody mentions
Even if the boarding visa is solvable, each Tbilisi → Cyprus → Tbilisi trip is its own Cyprus visa application for the kids. Russian citizens must obtain a visa before travelling to Cyprus; the Sticker visa is valid 3 months and must be used within 90 days of issuance; 9 documents required. Multi-entry is effectively dead for Russians under the Nov 2025 EU change. If the kids hold a Cyprus residence permit, that solves re-entry to Cyprus, but transit through any Schengen airport (Athens, Vienna, Frankfurt) on the way back to Tbilisi means a Schengen transit/short-stay visa per trip. The realistic flight path becomes: direct Larnaca/Paphos ↔ Tbilisi via non-Schengen carriers (Pegasus via Istanbul, FlyDubai via Dubai, Wizz via non-Schengen routing). Workable but rigid, especially for the 5-day flexi-boarding option where weekends home become impossible.
The age constraint compounds this
The earlier finding still holds: neither ISOP nor PASCAL boards under age 11 (ISOP) or 12 (PASCAL Year 1). Your 9-year-old can't board there regardless of visa status. So the boarding question is really only about the 11-year-old, and only if/when they reach Year 7.
Practical recommendation, given Doug's situation
If Cyprus becomes a real option, the cleaner pivot is to relocate the family (or Ana with the kids) and use day schooling — the Pink Slip route is well-paved for Russian families and the kids attend ISOP/PASCAL day, no special boarding visa needed. The boarding-with-parents-in-Tbilisi version asks the Cyprus immigration system to do something it's not really designed for and depends on individual school admissions willingness, which can change with each rotation of UK/Cyprus political climate.
Before going further, two concrete checks worth €0:
Email ISOP admissions (admissions@paphosinternationalschool.com per the school website) and PASCAL admissions, with the exact question: "Do you sponsor residence permits for non-EU boarding students whose parents are resident in a third country (Georgia)? What is the documentary process for Russian-passport minors?" Their answer determines whether Pathway 2 is real or aspirational.
If they say yes, get the specific permit category in writing and run it past a Cyprus immigration lawyer before committing — this is exactly the kind of question where the school admissions office's optimistic answer and the Civil Registry & Migration Department's actual decision can diverge.

Yes — and the risk shape is worth being precise about, because "stranded in Cyprus" can mean a few different things and only some of them are realistic.
The most likely failure modes, ranked by historical precedent:
Re-entry friction, not revocation. The EU norm — including how Estonia, Latvia, Finland, Czechia have handled their Russian-national tightenings since 2022 — has been to grandfather existing residence permit holders and bite at the renewal/re-entry point. So the realistic risk isn't "Cyprus deports your kids mid-term"; it's "Cyprus declines to renew the permit next year" or "Cyprus declines to issue a fresh entry visa after a holiday in Tbilisi." That's still a problem but it's a problem with months of warning, not a sudden lockout.
Transit, not destination. Cyprus joining Schengen (planned 2026, repeatedly delayed) would in theory make Cyprus easier for the kids to enter on a Schengen-aligned permit, but it would also bring Cyprus fully under whatever the EU's posture toward Russian nationals is at that moment — which since Nov 2025 includes no multi-entry visas. If you're flying Larnaca ↔ Tbilisi via Athens/Vienna/Warsaw, the Schengen transit visa is the chokepoint, not the Cyprus permit itself. Direct routes via Istanbul or Dubai are the resilience-buying answer.
The deeper structural risk isn't Cyprus, it's stack fragility. The kids' legal standing rests on a stack: Russia (citizenship, but not a place they should rely on), Georgia (residence tied to your TRP, still in process), and Cyprus (school + permit, hypothetical). Cyprus tightening is one peg failing. The real bad scenario is two pegs failing together — e.g., your Georgian TRP application stalls or gets rejected at the same time as Cyprus tightens — because then the kids have no legal home except Russia. The mitigation is to make sure the Georgian peg is rock-solid before committing them to a Cyprus dependency, not after.
What the precedent doesn't tell us. I can't reliably predict whether Cyprus, specifically, would do something hard like non-renewal of minors' permits. No EU country has done that to enrolled schoolchildren that I'm aware of since 2022, but the absence of precedent isn't a guarantee — it's just data. If the war escalates or there's a specific Cyprus political shift (and Cyprus has had its own Russian-money-related political pressure), the calculus could change quickly.
Honestly, all of this is an argument for keeping the kids where their legal status is most defensible — i.e., wherever the parents are legally resident. The Cyprus boarding option only really makes sense if there's an academic reason that outweighs adding a third jurisdiction's politics to your family's exposure. For ages 9 and 11 specifically, I'm not sure there is one. Happy School + Foxford in Tbilisi keeps the kids inside your TRP umbrella, which is the peg you actually control.
