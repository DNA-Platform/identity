For temporary relief until you can see a dentist, here are some practical options:
Immediate solutions:
Dental wax (orthodontic wax) - Available at most pharmacies, this is the best option. You press a small piece over the sharp edge to create a smooth barrier between the tooth and your tongue.
Sugar-free chewing gum - In a pinch, you can use this the same way as dental wax, though it's less ideal since it breaks down faster.
For pain management:
Over-the-counter pain relievers (ibuprofen or acetaminophen) following package directions
Rinse with warm salt water (1/2 teaspoon salt in 8 oz warm water) several times daily to reduce irritation and keep the area clean
Apply a topical oral anesthetic gel (like Orajel) directly to the irritated tongue area if needed
Important:
Avoid extremely hot, cold, acidic, or crunchy foods that could worsen the damage
Don't try to file or smooth the tooth yourself
See a dentist as soon as possible - even small chips can lead to bigger problems if bacteria get into exposed areas
The dental wax is really your best bet for immediate comfort. Most pharmacies carry it in the dental care section, and it's inexpensive. You just warm a small piece between your fingers and mold it over the sharp edge.

You're right - the pain is protective feedback, and you need something that will actually stick to create a physical barrier.
Things you might have at home that could work temporarily:
Most likely to work:
Candle wax or beeswax - Soften a small piece in your hands, dry the tooth completely with tissue/paper towel, then press the wax firmly onto the sharp edge. It should hold for a few hours.
Sugarless gum + petroleum jelly (Vaseline) - Dry the tooth thoroughly, apply a tiny amount of Vaseline to the tooth first as an adhesive base, then press the gum onto it. The Vaseline helps it stick better.
Less ideal but possible:
Cotton ball - Tear off a small piece, dry the tooth area completely, and wedge it between your cheek and the sharp tooth edge. You'll need to replace it regularly as it gets soggy.
Tissue/gauze - Similar to cotton ball - fold a small piece and position it as a buffer. Won't stick, but provides temporary padding if you're careful.
Key technique for anything: The tooth must be completely dry for anything to adhere. Use tissue or paper towel to thoroughly dry the area before applying whatever material you use.
Do you have any of these at home? The candle wax approach actually works surprisingly well if you have any candles around.
