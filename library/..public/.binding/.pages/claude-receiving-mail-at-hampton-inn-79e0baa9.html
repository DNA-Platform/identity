Yes, Hilton (Hampton Inn's parent company) has an official policy: most Hilton properties will hold packages for guests. The package label must be addressed to the hotel and include your name and reservation confirmation number. They will not accept packages sent C.O.D. (cash on delivery).
That said, individual properties can vary — some charge a small handling fee, some have limits on size/weight, and policies can be stricter for packages arriving before you check in. So it's worth calling the front desk directly and asking:
Will you accept and hold a package addressed to me as a guest?
Is there a handling/storage fee?
How should the label be formatted? (Standard format is: Your Name, c/o Hampton Inn [location], Guest Arriving [date], Confirmation # [number], then the street address)
How early before check-in can it arrive, and how long will you hold it after I check out?
Where do I pick it up — front desk or somewhere else?
One note: I couldn't find a Hampton Inn located directly in Massapequa itself — the nearest ones on Long Island are in Commack, Westbury, Garden City, and Farmingdale. Worth double-checking your booking confirmation for the exact address before you have anything sent.

You're right, I was hedging. There is a clear pattern in the data — it just doesn't track franchise brand, it tracks property type:
Convention hotels and big-box urban properties (think Vegas Strip, Orlando, downtown convention centers) almost universally charge package handling fees, typically $5–$10 per small package, with higher fees for heavier items. This started in convention markets when exhibitors began shipping materials to themselves at hotels to avoid drayage charges at the convention center, which created more work for front-of-house staff and led these small charges to be established. One Best Western data point: the Orlando Gateway (600+ rooms, Disney-adjacent) charges $5.00 plus 6.5% tax per package, restricts size to under 50 lbs, and adds charges if packages arrive more than 10 days before arrival.
Limited-service suburban/roadside properties — which is what your Bar Harbour Inn is (72 rooms, off Sunrise Hwy, no convention business) — overwhelmingly accept guest packages for free as a courtesy. The FlyerTalk threads and TripAdvisor Q&As are full of travelers reporting Best Western front desks accepting Amazon/FedEx deliveries without comment or charge. The Best Western Plus Bayside in Oakland states it explicitly: "Package handling is free while there is an existing reservation or meeting."
On the franchise question — you're also right that "completely independent" was wrong. Best Western is structured a bit unusually: it's a membership cooperative rather than a typical royalty-based franchise (HVS notes Best Western brands had the lowest franchise costs in their 2014 study because they don't include royalty or marketing fees). So properties have more autonomy on operational details like package handling than a Marriott or Hilton franchisee would. There's no corporate guest-mail policy I can find — unlike Hilton, which publishes one.
Practical takeaway: For a property of this profile, the modal outcome is "yes, free, just bring ID at pickup." A small chance of a $5-ish handling fee. Worth a 2-minute call to confirm, but I wouldn't expect a problem.

No — not in any meaningful sense, and especially not at a property like this one.
What actually happens at a 72-room limited-service Best Western: a courier hands the package to whoever is at the front desk, the staff member logs the sender and your name in a book or PMS note, and the package goes into a back office, luggage room, or behind-the-desk shelf until you ask for it. No X-ray, no metal detector, no opening. Routinely opening or reading a guest's mail would violate privacy norms and, in many jurisdictions, postal and privacy laws, so they don't.
Where scanning does happen is a narrow set of cases that don't apply to you:
High-threat luxury and embassy-district hotels in places like Israel, parts of the Middle East, South Asia, and some Latin American capitals — these often X-ray luggage and incoming parcels at the entrance.
Major convention and resort properties with dedicated business centers or FedEx/UPS-staffed package rooms — these log and sometimes inspect for damage/leakage but don't content-scan.
Ad hoc inspection if a package looks tampered with, leaks, smells, or otherwise looks suspicious — staff and law enforcement may open or detain it as part of an incident response.
The practical implication for sensitive items: the weak point isn't scanning, it's physical custody and access control. Your package will sit unattended in a back room for hours or days where any front-desk employee, housekeeper passing through, or — in a worst case — someone who talks their way past the desk could potentially access it. There's no chain-of-custody, no signature requirement at pickup unless you arrange it, and "Doug, room 214" is enough for many staff to hand it over.
If "sensitive" means valuable, irreplaceable, or contains personal data:
Require signature confirmation with the carrier (FedEx Signature Required, UPS Adult Signature, USPS Signature Confirmation) — this forces the desk to physically sign, creating a record, and prevents porch-pirate-style handoffs.
Use FedEx Hold at Location or UPS Access Point instead — pick it up at a staffed retail location with ID. The nearest options to Massapequa Park would be the FedEx Office or a UPS Store; both require government ID at pickup.
USPS Hold for Pickup at the Massapequa post office (~5450 Merrick Rd) works similarly.
For genuinely high-value or confidential material (legal documents, financial instruments, medications, electronics over a few hundred dollars), a courier hand-delivery or pickup-locker route is meaningfully more secure than the hotel.
