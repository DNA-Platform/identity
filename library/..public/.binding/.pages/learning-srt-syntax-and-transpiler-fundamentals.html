Yes, several formalisms circle this, though none does exactly what you're describing. The closest relatives, roughly from nearest to farthest:
Guarded quantification. The guarded fragment of FOL (Andréka, van Benthem, Németi) only permits quantifiers of the form E[y]: R(x,y) & P — every new variable must enter through an atom relating it to variables already in scope. Your restricted binder E(s: x =s> y) is essentially this. But the guard is a single relating, not a pattern of relationships.
Bounded quantification in type theory. System F<: writes ∀α <: T — quantify a new type variable constrained to inherit from an existing one. This is quantification bounded by an inheritance order, which SRT's arrow could play the same role for: something like E(x' -> x).
Freshness quantification. Nominal logic (Gabbay–Pitts) has a dedicated quantifier for "a new name, distinct from everything in scope." This is the equality-side of your observation — it formalizes the E[x,y]-vs-E{x,y} distinction as a quantifier in its own right, but says nothing about relational similarity.
Hilbert's ε-operator. εx.P(x) is a term denoting "some x satisfying P" — witness introduction as syntax. Related to how (x,y) abbreviates the Specification witness.
Counterpart theory and bisimulation. Lewis's counterparts (a thing in another world standing in relevantly similar relations, rather than identity across worlds) and bisimilarity in process calculi (two states equivalent when their relational patterns match step-for-step) are the two places where "similar relational pattern" itself is the formalized notion. But both are defined relations, not quantifier syntax.
Here's the part that seems worth noting for SRT specifically. In standard FOL, "x' bears the same relationships x bears" requires quantifying over relations — it goes second-order, or gets handled by axiom schemas. In SRT it's first-order, because t is a referent: A[t,y]: x =t> y =|> x' =t> y is a well-formed sentence of the theory. So the quantifier you want may be definable sugar over machinery you already have — and the Phase VI nine-condition structure-preserving specialization already is the "similar relationships" pattern, stated as conditions on a given pair rather than as a binder that introduces one.
One question before syntax: by "similar relationships to others," do you mean a new variable bearing thesame relationships to the same referents, or bearing a structure-preserving pattern — the Phase VI map, where the relata may specialize too? The two want different binders.

The idea. To say "couldn't," you need more than the actual facts — you need all the ways things could have gone. Call each complete way things could have gone a scenario. Today's rain-plus-umbrella is one scenario; rain-and-I-forgot-it is another, perfectly coherent one. Then:
P ⥽ Q   :=   in every scenario where P holds, Q holds
Written with scenarios as objects w and P(w) for "P holds in scenario w":
∀w (P(w) → Q(w))
Notice what this formula literally is: the material arrow, applied in every scenario instead of only the actual one. Strict implication doesn't replace material implication — it universalizes it. That single move kills the accident: "rain ⥽ umbrella" now fails, because the forgot-it scenario exists; while "bachelor ⥽ unmarried" holds, because no scenario anywhere contains a married bachelor.

Material implication is a fact about one point — where you're standing, it's not P-and-not-Q there.
Strict implication is containment of regions: the P-region sits inside the Q-region. Every rain-point is a wet-street-point.
Possibility, ⋄P, is non-emptiness: the P-region has at least one point.
Consistency, ⋄(P ∧ Q), is overlap: the regions share a point.

Containment is asymmetric: the rain-region sits inside the wet-street-region, but not conversely, and the proof of "not conversely" is a single point — a scenario with a burst pipe, wet street, no rain. One point with Q and without P. That is your separating case, drawn on a map. Overlap, by contrast, is symmetric — a shared point is shared in no direction. So within this one logic: the universal region-relation (containment) carries direction; the existential one (overlap) cannot.
