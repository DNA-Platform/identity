At week 8 post-lateral calcaneal osteotomy, you're in that tricky zone where it's genuinely hard to parse what's happening. Let me help you think through the differential:
Signs pointing toward nerve recovery (less concerning):
Tingling, burning, or hypersensitivity that wasn't there before
Discomfort that's more "weird" than painful
Symptoms that don't worsen with activity or improve with rest
No progressive changes day-to-day
Signs pointing toward overuse/inflammation (warrants pulling back):
Pain that increases with weight-bearing and improves with rest
Redness that's warm to touch
Swelling that's worse at end of day
Pain that's more mechanical (sharp with certain movements)
Red flags requiring prompt evaluation:
Redness that's spreading or has distinct borders
Warmth significantly greater than the other foot
Fever, chills, or feeling unwell
Drainage from incision
Pain that's worsening despite rest
Given that your doctor noted healing is behind schedule, that's your body telling you to be more conservative, not less. The fact that you're questioning whether you walked too much probably means you did.
Practical suggestion: Can you quantify your walking? If you're doing more than short household distances at week 8 with delayed healing, that's likely too much. The bone doesn't care that you feel capable—it heals on its own timeline.

That's a very plausible culprit, especially as you've increased activity. Boot pressure on an incision site would typically feel like:
Localized irritation pattern:
Tenderness that maps to where the boot edge or strap contacts
Redness in a line or patch corresponding to pressure points
Discomfort that builds during the day while wearing the boot and eases after you take it off
Possible slight swelling localized to that area
Skin might look a bit abraded or shiny, not just red
Key differentiator: Take the boot off and note exactly where the redness/tenderness is. Then look at the boot interior — does the geography match? Is there a seam, edge, or strap that lines up?
What to do if this is it:
Add padding (moleskin, gel pads, folded gauze) to create a buffer between boot and incision
Adjust strap positioning if possible
Consider wearing a thin sock or sleeve underneath if you aren't already
Some people cut a small relief hole in the boot liner over the incision (ask your surgeon first)
The fact that you're walking more now means more cumulative friction and pressure cycles per day — something that was tolerable at lower activity levels can become irritating quickly when you scale up.
