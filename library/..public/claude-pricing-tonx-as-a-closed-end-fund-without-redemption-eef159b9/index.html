Comparing My Analysis to Expert Frameworks
What the Experts Say
1. The mNAV Framework (Industry Standard)
The most common valuation yardstick is the mNAV. It compares a company's enterprise value (EV) — which is a firm's market cap plus debt minus any cash — to the market value of its bitcoin holdings.
If mNAV < 1.0 → The company trades at a discount to the value of its Bitcoin holdings, possibly reflecting concerns about debt, operating model, or other risks.
2. Why Premiums Exist (The Bull Case for DATs)
First, Strategy takes on leverage by issuing equity and debt to invest the proceeds into bitcoin, earning the difference between the return on bitcoin and its cost of capital (ie. "carry")... the first reason MSTR trades at a premium to its BTC holdings is that investors pull future BTC carry to the present.
If they doubled their share count by issuing 10 shares at market prices ($20/share) and used the proceeds to buy a digital assets, suddenly they have $300 of NAV and 20 shares outstanding, driving NAV to $15/share. If the company maintains the same mNAV multiple, the stock will go from $20 to $30/share. Everyone wins!
3. Why Discounts Persist
When a DAT's treasury sits above its average purchase price, the market usually assigns a premium (>1x mNAV). Below cost, the market discounts it. Across the DeFiLlama Digital Asset Treasuries dashboard, profitable treasuries consistently trade higher while underwater ones remain cheap.
If market cap falls below the company's crypto holdings (i.e., trades below 1.0x mNAV), and they are unable to reverse this trend, investor outflows or a funding freeze can trigger a reflexive death spiral.
4. TONX-Specific Expert Take
TON Strategy offers a unique, highly speculative opportunity as the only public vehicle for exposure to Toncoin and its exclusive Telegram integration. In a June 2025 blog post, Greg Cipolaro, the global head of research at NYDIG, offered a sharp critique of mNAV as it's commonly used. He argued the metric is "woefully deficient" for failing to reflect key balance sheet risks — especially assumptions about convertible notes...
