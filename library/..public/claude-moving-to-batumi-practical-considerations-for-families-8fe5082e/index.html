Based on my research, I can give you a pretty comprehensive picture of what you're dealing with. Let me address your concerns.
Batumi Airport Reliability
Your concerns are valid. Batumi airport does experience flight cancellations due to strong winds - there was a recent example just days ago where flights to Turkey and Israel were cancelled due to bad weather and wind. In February 2025, the airport had to suspend air traffic for two days due to severe weather conditions. The airport is small and coastal, making it more vulnerable to weather disruptions than Tbilisi's much larger airport, which handled 4.75 million passengers in 2024 and has over 45 airlines operating from it. If reliable flight access is critical for your work or life, Tbilisi is definitely the safer choice.
Suburban Houses/Townhouses in Tbilisi - Yes, It's Possible
The good news is that suburban family living with a car is absolutely a viable option in Tbilisi, and it's quite affordable by Western standards.
Best suburbs for families with houses:
Village Dighomi / Didi Digomi area: This is a great neighborhood with private houses, green backyards, and a relaxed atmosphere. It's a bit tricky in terms of public transportation, but for those with a car who want to live rurally, but still near city conveniences, it's the perfect option. This area is near the QSI International School and the US Embassy.
Digomi: This represents the "new Tbilisi," where neighborhoods are built to modern planning standards. Streets are wider, and houses have more surrounding space. It's considered one of the most comfortable areas, though farther from the center (20-30 minutes).
Rental Prices:
For apartments, the average price to rent long-term in Didi Dighomi is the cheapest in the district ($7-$10 per sq meter per month). The more desirable Village Dighomi and Tbilisi Mall areas run $10-$12 per sq meter.
For a standalone house, you're looking at roughly 2,000-3,500 GEL/month (around $700-$1,300 USD) depending on size and location. The rental market in these suburban areas with actual houses is less documented online than apartments, so you'd likely need to work with a local agent once you arrive.
Transportation - Different from the US
Tbilisi has buses, a metro, and marshrutkas (small minibuses that run on fixed routes throughout the city). They are a popular and affordable way to get around, but they can be crowded and uncomfortable.
However, for suburban living: Village Dighomi is a bit tricky in terms of public transportation - if you're living in a house in the outer suburbs, you really need a car. Taxis in Georgia are cheap - a trip from the nearby districts to the city centre will cost 5-8 GEL (about $2-3), and apps like Bolt work well.
Public transportation works well for getting around the central city, but won't replace a car for suburban family life the way it might in denser American suburbs with good bus networks.
Schools - Good Options with Bus Service
There are about 16 international schools in Tbilisi. The main one Americans typically use is QSI International School of Tbilisi, which is actually located in the Dighomi area (convenient if you live nearby). The school provides a door-to-door bus service to families, covering most of the city zone and the Dighomi area.
Tuition costs (2023-2024 figures): Annual tuition rates are: 2-4-Year-Old Class: $5,800 and K-grade 12: $24,000. There is an annual capital fund fee of $3,200 for students in kindergarten through grade 12.
Other options include the International School of Georgia (IB curriculum), British-Georgian Academy, and The Guivy Zaldastanishvili American Academy with tuition for non-Georgian citizens at 28,000 GEL (roughly $10,000).
Bottom Line
Tbilisi is a realistic option for your family if you're willing to:
Have a car (essential for suburban house living)
Budget $500-1,300/month for a house rental depending on what you want
Budget $24,000+ annually per child for international school (though some schools are cheaper)
Rely on school buses for getting kids to school
The suburban experience won't feel like American suburbs with sidewalks and school buses picking up at every corner - it's more like living in a semi-rural area with a car and using the school's private transport service. But the cost of living is dramatically lower, and the airport reliability issue you're experiencing in Batumi goes away.

Schools for Anna's Children - Excellent Options
QSI International School of Tbilisi is particularly well-suited for your situation:
QSI schools accept students with no previous experience with English from preschool through 13-year-old class (US Grade 8). Intensive English classes are offered to students that need additional help with English.
So depending on how old her children are, they could potentially enroll with zero English and get intensive support. Older students with little or no English experience may expect to spend additional years enrolled in Secondary in order to graduate with a QSI diploma. For high schoolers, there's more of an English requirement.
What's also nice is that additional language acquisition is provided in Georgian, Russian, German, French, Spanish, and Mandarin - so the kids could maintain their Russian while learning English, rather than losing it entirely.
The school has about 227 students from over 30 nationalities, so the kids would be among other non-native English speakers, which often makes the transition easier socially.
Other options worth considering:
British International School - reportedly cheaper than QSI, also offers language support for students who are non-native English speakers
European School - Students are offered to learn English and Russian languages from 1st grade
Housing - What You Can Get
For that $700-1,300 range you mentioned, here's the realistic picture:
In the Didi Digomi / Village Dighomi area (near QSI school): Didi Dighomi is one of Tbilisi's fastest-growing and most sought-after districts, known for its affordable rental prices and peaceful atmosphere. It's an ideal choice for families with kids - this area has a mix of modern apartment towers and actual houses with yards.
For standalone houses (not apartments), the rental market is less transparent online - most listings I found show houses renting in the $1,200-1,500+ range for something with multiple bedrooms and a yard. At the lower end of your budget ($700-900), you'd more likely get a large apartment (100+ sqm, 3 bedrooms) in a newer building, or possibly a smaller/older house or the upper floor of a house. At $1,000-1,300, you're in range for a modest standalone house with a small yard, particularly if you look in:
Village Dighomi - actual houses with gardens, near Tbilisi Mall
Tskneti - hillside suburb, quieter, cottage-style houses
Kojori - further out, more rural feel, larger properties
Rental prices are generally lower than in central Tbilisi, while the standard of housing remains high.
The honest answer is that you'll need to work with a local agent or use sites like Avezor, SS.ge, or MyHome.ge once you're there - the actual house rental market (as opposed to apartments) moves largely through word-of-mouth and local contacts, especially for long-term rentals where landlords prefer to negotiate directly.
Key question for you: How old are Anna's children? That affects both school options (the intensive English cutoff at grade 8) and what kind of housing space you'd actually need.
