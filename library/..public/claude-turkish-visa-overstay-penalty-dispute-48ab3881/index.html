Searched the web for “Turkey visa overstay penalty ban duration 2024 2025”
Based on the official Turkish immigration guidelines, a 3-month ban for a 10-day overstay doesn't match the standard penalty structure. Here's what the rules actually say:
For overstays under 3 months (which includes 10 days): Decision on entry ban to Turkey is not taken for the foreigners who leave the country pursuant to the terms below, even though they violate the right to legal stay: The foreigners who violate the right to legal stay up to 3 months (does not include 3 months) and come to the border gates by themselves in order to leave Turkey before their situation is detected by the competent authorities and make the payment for administrative fines.
Another source confirms: If an individual has only violated their visa or visa exemption period by a few days (less than 10 days) and voluntarily proceeds to exit, they may, according to the law, not be subject to a deportation order or entry ban. However, the cash fine related to those few days must still be paid.
Entry bans are structured for longer overstays: Staying between 3 months and 6 months without a visa will ban a person from entering for one month. Staying between 6 months and one year without a visa will ban a person from entering for three months.
So what likely happened:
They may have miscalculated her 90/180 day situation — if they're looking at cumulative days in a 180-day rolling window rather than just the 10-day extension past her visa, the math might look different to them
Language barrier / arbitrary application — Sometimes, if your stay is slightly delayed, the Migration Department prefers not to impose an entry ban or restricts visits to Turkey for a short period of no more than 1-3 months. This suggests officials sometimes impose short bans at their discretion even when not strictly required
They may have given 3 months as a generic number without properly calculating
Did she pay the fine at departure? If not, that triggers stricter consequences. But even then, 3 months for 10 days seems like either an error or them not wanting to work through the actual calculation.
You might consider having her request a written explanation of the ban duration and its legal basis, or consulting with a Turkish immigration lawyer about whether this can be contested.

You're raising a really important distinction. There are two completely different things that could be happening here:
Entry ban — a penalty that prohibits entry regardless of visa eligibility
90/180 day reset — simply waiting for her visa-free days to replenish under the rolling window
If she maxed out her 90 days in a 180-day period and then overstayed 10 more days, the "3 months" might just be them telling her when she'll have visa days available again under the normal 90/180 rule — not an actual ban.
Let me check on Russian-specific rules and the ikamet situation:
Searched the web for “Russian citizens Turkey residence permit ikamet denied 2024 2025”
This is really helpful. A few key things:
Russian-specific visa rules: Russian citizens can stay in Turkey for up to 60 days per visit, with a maximum of 90 days within a 180-day period.
Russians are actually EXEMPT from some stricter rules: This new restriction applies to the majority of foreign nationalities. However, a critical exemption exists for citizens of European Union (EU) member states, Russia, the People's Republic of China, and countries belonging to the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD).
So Russians can still get tourist ikamet renewals based on rental agreements when other nationalities can't. That said, the crowded neighborhood restrictions (1,200+ neighborhoods with 20%+ foreign population) and property value requirements could explain ikamet denial.
Your key question though: Is "3 months" an entry ban or just a visa day reset?
This is crucial. If Ana used up her 90 days in the rolling 180-day window and then overstayed 10 more, the woman at the counter may just be telling her "you can't come back for 3 months" meaning when her visa days will replenish — not an actual ban coded into the system.
The way to tell the difference:
Did they put a specific stamp or code in her passport?
Is there a code like Ç-101 or M-54 mentioned?
Did they give her any document about an "entry prohibition"?
If it's just the 90/180 reset, that's completely different from a punitive ban — she'd simply need to wait until enough days "fall off" the rolling window. Can you ask the official whether this is an entry ban (yasak) or just waiting for visa days?
