That's exactly what WiFi calling handles. When you enable it on your iPhone, your US number receives calls and texts over WiFi just as if you were in the US — no cellular needed, no roaming charges. Anyone texting or calling your US number reaches you normally.
The setup is: Settings → Phone → Wi-Fi Calling → toggle on. That's it.
The only edge cases where this breaks down:
You're away from WiFi and need to receive a call or text on cellular (Magti won't carry your US number, obviously)
Some carriers charge for WiFi calling internationally — worth checking your AT&T plan's fine print, but most postpaid plans include it free
If you're mostly home or in cafes with WiFi, this covers essentially all your use cases without any add-on or plan change. You'd only need the Day Pass on days you're somewhere without WiFi and expecting important US calls or texts.
So before doing anything else — try enabling WiFi calling tonight and see if it solves the problem as-is, before switching carriers or buying add-ons.

Your AT&T SIM is still active and connected to a Georgian network in the background. Even without you deliberately using it, background app activity — push notifications, iMessage sync, etc. — is enough to trigger the $12 day pass automatically.
The fix is to turn off Data Roaming specifically for the AT&T line:
Settings → Cellular → tap your AT&T line → Data Roaming → toggle OFF
That stops the AT&T SIM from using any cellular data abroad, so it can never trigger the day pass. WiFi Calling still works independently over WiFi regardless, so your US number stays reachable. Magti handles all your cellular data as usual.
Essentially: AT&T SIM = voice/WiFi calling only, no cellular data. Magti = all cellular data. That combination costs you nothing extra.

The fallback would be pay-per-use rates if you ever did use AT&T cellular, but since MAGTICOM handles all your data and WiFi handles your US number, you'd essentially never hit those.
