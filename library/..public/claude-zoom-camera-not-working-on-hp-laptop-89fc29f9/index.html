Why is Zoom video not working with my laptop’s camera?
Claude
10:02 UTC, 11 February 2026
I can see your Zoom video settings with the "HP True Vision FHD Camera" selected, but the camera icon is crossed out — meaning Zoom detects the camera but can't get a feed from it.
Here are the most common fixes, starting with the most likely:
1. Check if another app is using the camera. Close any other apps that might have the camera open (Teams, Skype, browser tabs with video calls, Windows Camera app, etc.). Only one app can use the camera at a time.
2. Check Windows privacy settings. Go to Settings → Privacy & Security → Camera and make sure both "Camera access" is turned on and Zoom is allowed to use it. This is the most common culprit on HP laptops.
3. Check for a physical camera kill switch. Many HP laptops have a dedicated camera privacy key (often F8 or a key with a camera icon on the function row). Press it to toggle the camera on. Some models also have a physical slider near the camera lens.
4. Restart the camera driver. Open Device Manager → Cameras → HP True Vision FHD Camera, right-click and choose Disable device, wait a few seconds, then Enable device again.
5. Update the camera driver. In Device Manager, right-click the camera and select Update driver. You can also check HP's support site for your specific laptop model.
6. Restart Zoom entirely (not just leave the meeting — fully quit and reopen it).
I'd start with #2 and #3 since those catch the vast majority of cases on HP machines. Let me know what you find.

Your privacy settings all look correct — Zoom even shows "Currently in use." So that's not the issue.
Since Zoom sees the camera and Windows is granting access, here's what to check next:
Most likely: HP camera privacy key. Look at your keyboard's function row for a key with a camera icon (usually F8 on HP laptops). Press it (you may need to hold Fn + F8 depending on your function key settings). There's often a small LED indicator near the camera that shows orange/red when the camera is physically disabled. This hardware-level kill switch overrides everything in software.
If that's not it:
Open Device Manager (right-click Start button → Device Manager), expand Cameras, and check if the HP True Vision camera has a yellow warning triangle. If so, right-click →Uninstall device, then reboot — Windows will reinstall the driver automatically.
Try opening the Windows Camera app directly (search "Camera" in Start). If that also shows a black screen or crossed-out icon, the issue is system-wide, not Zoom-specific — which points back to either the hardware kill switch or a driver problem.
I'd bet on the privacy key being the culprit. Check for that first.
