Going by the syllable count, this is a haiku in the "Refrigerator" meme tradition — 5/7/and a single absurd polysyllable to land it:
Chalmers, Dennett, Hof (5) A consciousness discussing (7) Hofstadterian (5)
The word I'd slot in is Hofstadterian.
It's the strange-loopiest possible choice: line 1 can't fit his whole name (a nice little Gödelian incompleteness — "Hof" is all five syllables will allow), and then line 3 smuggles him back in fully as an adjective. The braid closes on itself, which is about as on-theme for a consciousness haiku as you can get.
If you want the deadpan meme version instead of the self-referential one, the classic move is to end on a totally orthogonal 5-syllable word — "Refrigerator" being the canonical punchline. But for this trio, Hofstadterian is the one that earns the GEB comparison.

Ah — the initials. That's the pattern I fumbled.
Gödel, Escher, Bach → G E B, and the subtitle An Eternal Golden Braid quietly permutes them right back: E G B. The subtitle isn't just describing the book, it's re-weaving the title's own letters into itself. A little strange loop hiding in plain sight. Of course.
So the consciousness trio — Chalmers, Dennett, Hof[stadter] = C D H — needs a subtitle whose three words braid those same initials back together. My pick:
Chalmers, Dennett, Hof A Hard Conscious Delusion
H, C, D — a clean permutation of the three. And it lands the joke three ways at once: Hard (Chalmers' Hard Problem), Conscious (the whole fight), Delusion (very on-brand for Dennett, who'd happily call the other two's qualia exactly that).
If you want to swap the one word you're missing, the noun slot is where the flavor lives:
Delusion — the Dennett dunk (my favorite)
Dilemma — gentler, more neutral
Headache — pure deflation, the anti-"Golden Braid"
