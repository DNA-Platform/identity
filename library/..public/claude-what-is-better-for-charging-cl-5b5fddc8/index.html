What is better for charging client in terms of commission PayPal or Wise? Money is going to a USD account and might be from British pounds in one case otherwise it’s always USD to USD

Searched the web for “PayPal receiving payment fees USD domestic 2025 percentage”
Searched the web for “Wise business receiving USD fee GBP to USD conversion fee 2025”
Here's the breakdown for your two scenarios:
USD → USD (domestic)
PayPal: ~2.99% + $0.49 fixed fee per transaction for goods/services invoicing. On a $1,000 invoice, you'd lose about $30.39.
Wise: Free to receive USD via ACH into your Wise USD account. $6.11 flat fee if received via wire/SWIFT. On that same $1,000, you keep $993.89 at worst (wire), or the full $1,000 (ACH).
Wise wins massively here. It's not even close.
GBP → USD
PayPal: 4.49% + fixed fee for international transactions, PLUS a 3-4% exchange rate markup on the currency conversion. On a £1,000 payment you could easily lose 7-8% total between fees and the unfavorable exchange rate.
Wise: Free to receive GBP into your Wise GBP account (non-SWIFT), then convert GBP→USD at the mid-market rate with a conversion fee starting around 0.57%. On £1,000 you'd pay roughly £5-7 in conversion fees. If the client sends GBP via SWIFT, add a £2.16 receiving fee.
Wise wins again, dramatically — you keep the real exchange rate and pay a fraction of a percent instead of PayPal's hidden 3-4% markup baked into their conversion rate.
Bottom line: For your situation, Wise is cheaper in both cases. The only real advantage PayPal has is ubiquity — clients are more likely to already have a PayPal account. But if you can get clients to send via bank transfer to your Wise account details, you'll save significant money, especially on the GBP→USD route where PayPal's currency conversion markup is brutal. The one-time $31 setup fee for Wise Business account details pays for itself on essentially the first transaction.

If I want to do a fixed monthly payment on 20K at 10% interest for 24 months. Do the math on the amount that would be paid per month
Claude
21:43 UTC, 19 February 2026
Monthly payment: $922.90
Here's the math:
Using the standard amortization formula: M = P × [r(1+r)^n] / [(1+r)^n - 1]
P = $20,000
Annual rate = 10%, so monthly rate r = 0.8333%
n = 24 months
(1.008333)^24 = 1.22039
M = 20,000 × (0.008333 × 1.22039) / (1.22039 - 1) = 20,000 × 0.04614 = $922.90/month
Totals:
Total paid over 24 months: $22,149.60
Total interest paid: $2,149.60
