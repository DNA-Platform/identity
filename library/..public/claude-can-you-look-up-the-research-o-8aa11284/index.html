A quick orientation before the artifact: semantic primes come from the Natural Semantic Metalanguage (NSM), a theory developed by Anna Wierzbicka and Cliff Goddard over more than five decades. The core claim is that every human language shares a small set of indefinable, universal word-meanings — primes — that can be translated literally into any language and cannot be defined using anything simpler. After more than four decades of cross-linguistic research covering roughly thirty languages, the list reached 65 elements, grouped into "onto-syntactic" categories. The 65 figure is current; notably, DON'T WANT was restored to the inventory as a separate prime in Goddard & Wierzbicka (2014), having earlier been folded into WANT + NOT — which is part of why the count settled at 65.
