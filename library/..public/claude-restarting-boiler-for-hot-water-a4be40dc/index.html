Restarting Your Airfel Digifix Duo
Here's what to do based on what I can see on the panel:
1. Check if it's in error/lockout mode. The red light between the display and the dials — if it's solid or flashing, the boiler has locked out and needs a reset.
2. Reset the boiler. There should be a small reset button right next to or behind that red indicator light (often labeled "R"). Press and hold it for about 3–5 seconds, then release. The display should flash or change as it restarts the ignition sequence.
3. Check the right dial (hot water, marked with the tap/faucet icon). Make sure it's turned up from "min" toward "max" — if it's set to off or minimum, you won't get hot water even if the boiler is running.
4. Check the left dial (heating, marked with the radiator icon). This controls central heating. Set it as needed, but for hot water specifically the right dial matters most.
5. Wait 30–60 seconds. After resetting, the boiler should attempt to fire. You should hear the ignition click and the fan start. The display temperature should begin climbing when you open a hot water tap.
If it locks out again after reset:
Check water pressure — look at the pressure gauge (usually on the bottom of the unit or on the pipes below). It should read between 1.0–1.5 bar. If it's below 1.0, you'll need to use the filling loop (the valve assembly on the pipes underneath) to re-pressurize.
Check the gas supply — make sure the yellow gas line valve below is open.
If it locks out repeatedly, you'll need a technician — it could be a flame sensor, ignition, or gas valve issue.

Let me look up what error code 23 and "LP" mean for this specific model.
Searched the web for “Airfel Digifix Duo error code 23 LP”
LP = Low Pressure. Despite the water from taps seeming fine, this is about the closed heating circuit pressure inside the boiler — not your mains water supply. The boiler is refusing to fire because its internal system pressure has dropped below 1 bar.
Here's what to do:
1. Find the blue filling valve. On your Digifix Duo, it's located on the bottom right of the boiler, among those pipes you can see in the photo. Look for a small blue tap/valve.
2. Open it slowly. Turn the blue valve to the left (counterclockwise). You should hear water flowing into the system.
3. Watch the pressure gauge. There should be a small pressure gauge on the bottom left of the unit. Keep filling until it reads 1.5 bar, then immediately close the valve by turning it back to the right. Don't overfill — going above 2 bar can trigger the pressure relief valve.
4. The LP error should clear automatically once pressure reaches the right level. If the wrench icon persists after pressure is at 1.5, press the reset button (R) once more.
The boiler should then fire up and you'll have hot water.
One thing to keep in mind: if you find yourself needing to re-pressurize frequently (every few days/weeks), there's likely a slow leak somewhere in the system — a radiator valve, a pipe joint, or the boiler's expansion tank could be the culprit. But for now, topping up the pressure should get you running.
