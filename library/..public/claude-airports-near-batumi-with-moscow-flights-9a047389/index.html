This is a really important question for Ana's travel planning. The short answer is: it depends on whether she needs to pass through immigration.
Airside Transit (No Immigration): If Ana stays in the international transit zone and doesn't leave the secure area of the airport, she typically wouldn't pass through Turkish immigration. Many airports allow this for connecting flights on the same ticket. Istanbul airports (both IST and SAW) have international transit areas.
However, there are complicating factors:
Turkey's transit visa policy - Turkey generally allows visa-free transit for passengers staying in the international zone, but airlines check passenger eligibility before boarding. If Ana is flagged in the system for an overstay, the airline might refuse to board her even for a transit.
The overstay itself - Turkish immigration records overstays. When she left Turkey, she likely received an entry ban (typically 1-5 years depending on the length of overstay). This ban usually applies to entry, but airlines often check this at departure and may be cautious.
Airline liability - Airlines face fines if they transport passengers who get denied entry, so they tend to be conservative. Even for transit, they might refuse boarding if there's any flag on her record.
Practical recommendations:
Safest option: Book flights that don't transit through Turkey at all. The Batumi-Moscow Red Wings direct flight avoids this entirely.

Yes, exactly. Here's what happened:
2019-2023: Complete ban Russia unilaterally imposed a ban on direct flights by Russian airlines to Georgia in 2019 after a wave of anti-Kremlin protests in Georgia. The protests were sparked when a Russian MP took the Georgian Parliament Speaker's seat during an international conference — a huge symbolic insult given Russia's occupation of Georgian territory.
May 2023: Ban lifted Russian President Vladimir Putin lifted a ban on flights to Georgia and eased visa requirements for its citizens — so direct flights have only been back for about 20 months. The route is still rebuilding.
Why it's still limited:
Only certain airlines can fly it — Since 2022, countries in Europe and North America have closed their airspace to Russian aircraft and vice versa. So the Georgia-Russia route is basically limited to Russian carriers (Red Wings, Azimuth), Georgian Airways, and airlines from non-sanctioning countries.
Political sensitivity — The plane's arrival was met by protesters carrying Georgian and Ukrainian flags, as well as signs condemning "occupant" and "terrorist" Russia. Georgian public opinion is divided on resuming ties with Russia, which still occupies ~20% of Georgian territory.
Infrastructure hasn't caught up — After 4 years of no direct service, the routes are still sparse. Batumi especially — it's a smaller airport that gets seasonal tourist traffic but isn't a major hub.
The practical result: You're stuck with Red Wings twice a week from Batumi, or driving to Tbilisi for daily options. It's a geopolitical bottleneck, not just bad scheduling.
