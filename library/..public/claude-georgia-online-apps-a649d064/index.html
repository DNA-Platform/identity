Here's the breakdown:
Georgia: Tipping is not part of the mainstream culture in Georgia and is not considered mandatory. However, it is now expected in most service industries – especially in Tbilisi (and Batumi). 10% is standard if you're satisfied with service.
The catch: Most restaurants add 18% VAT and/or a 5-10% service charge to the bill. This 'service fee' never goes to the servers, though. So if you want to actually tip your waiter, leave cash - tips left on credit cards are unlikely to make their way into the hands of your server.
Compared to Turkey: You're right that tipping wasn't really a thing there. Georgia is slightly more tip-oriented, but still nothing like the US. It's more "appreciated if you're happy" than "expected."
Compared to US: Night and day. No obligation, no guilt, no 20% baseline. 10% cash for good service is generous in Georgia.
American vs Russian expectations: There's no real differential treatment. Georgians always leave tips at restaurants themselves, so it's normalized for everyone. The large Russian-speaking expat/tourist population means staff won't assume anything based on which language you're speaking. Ana won't be expected to tip less, and you won't be expected to tip American-style just because you're American.
Practical approach: Check if there's already a service charge on the bill (there probably will be). If service was good, leave 10% in cash on top of that, handed directly to the server if possible.

The servers still make low base wages and depend on actual tips that customers now think they've already paid.
It's deceptive labeling, plain and simple. "Service charge" sounds like a gratuity. It's not. It's just a fee.
What to make of it practically:
Ignore the service charge when deciding whether to tip
If you got good service and want to reward it, leave 10% cash on top
Hand it directly to your server if you can, so there's no ambiguity about where it goes
The 2024 law supposedly requiring restaurants to include VAT in menu prices was meant to address some of this transparency issue, but some venues flaunt this rule and continue to add VAT on top. Enforcement seems... loose.
