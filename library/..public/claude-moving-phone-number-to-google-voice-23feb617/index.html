AT&T owns its own cellular network and has bilateral roaming agreements with foreign carriers like Magticom. Those agreements work like this: Magticom lets your AT&T-numbered phone register on their towers, and AT&T pays Magticom a wholesale rate per minute, text, and MB consumed. AT&T then bills you to recover that cost — that's what IDP is. International roaming is enabled by default on AT&T postpaid lines because AT&T wants the roaming revenue. To stop it, you have to optout by asking them to flip the flag, and even then it's not bulletproof because the default state of the system is "let this line roam."
US Mobile is an MVNO. They own zero cellular infrastructure — they lease capacity from T-Mobile or Verizon, and their margins are tiny. They don't have direct foreign roaming agreements, and enabling them on every customer line by default would cost them money they don't have. So international roaming is disabled by default. The way it works on US Mobile: if you want to roam, you explicitly buy a Pay-As-You-Go credit balance and toggle "International Roaming" on in your dashboard. Until you do that, your home carrier (US Mobile) refuses to authorize foreign tower registration when Magticom asks. Your phone shows "No Service" on the US Mobile eSIM, and no charges are possible because no cellular connection is possible.
There's no IDP equivalent on US Mobile because there's no roaming feature to attach it to. The default state of the system is "this line cannot roam." Same architecture on Tello, Red Pocket, Ultra Mobile, basically every MVNO. The reason your AT&T line keeps bleeding money isn't because you've configured something wrong — it's because AT&T's product is structurally designed to bill you for roaming, and you have to actively fight it.
