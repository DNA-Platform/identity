What VPN actually helps with — and the thing that does sometimes affect US users — is Atomic's backend block explorer access. Balances and transaction history are governed by block explorers, and if some are blocked in your country or your IP was banned by CloudFlare, balances will reflect incorrectly, often showing "—". In these cases they suggest using a VPN. CloudFlare bans are random and can hit any IP. So a VPN can help, but only for that specific failure mode (the "—" symbol or stale data), not for "transaction confirmed on-chain but not appearing."
The critical thing about your setup: a VPN running only in your browser does nothing for the Atomic Wallet desktop/mobile app. Atomic is a standalone application, not a web wallet — it makes its own network connections outside your browser. Browser extensions like the built-in VPN in Opera, or extensions like Hola, only proxy browser traffic. To affect Atomic, you need a system-level VPN (NordVPN, Mullvad, ProtonVPN, etc.) running as an app, which routes all your device's traffic.
That said — before you go down the VPN path, do the block-explorer check first. Drop the receiving address into mempool.space. If the BTC is there with confirmations, the wallet is fine and it's just a display sync issue. How long does it take if I sent monero to another wallet to clear? Not monero to Atomic Wallet, but in terms of the block chain.
Claude
23:42 UTC, 29 April 2026
Monero block time is 2 minutes, and the standard convention is 10 confirmations for a transaction to be considered fully settled — so roughly 20 minutes for full settlement on the receiving end.
That said, what counts as "cleared" depends on who's asking:
1 confirmation (~2 min): transaction is in a block, very unlikely to reverse but technically possible.
10 confirmations (~20 min): the standard "spendable" / "fully confirmed" threshold most wallets and exchanges use. This is also when XMR becomes spendable in the receiving wallet — Monero enforces a 10-block lock on outputs at the protocol level, which is unusual (most chains let you spend after 1 confirmation, the wait is just a security convention).
More than 10: mostly relevant for large amounts or exchanges with stricter policies; some require 15–20.
So the floor is 20 minutes for it to be properly usable on the other end, regardless of how the receiving wallet displays it. Some wallets show the incoming transaction immediately as "pending" or "unconfirmed" and then update the spendable balance after 10 confirmations.
Two other Monero-specific things worth knowing if the receive side has display issues:
The receiving wallet has to scan the blockchain with the view key to even see the transaction. Unlike BTC where any explorer can show balances at an address, XMR balances are only visible to someone holding the view key. So if a wallet is slow to sync, it's not just a display lag — it literally hasn't found the incoming output yet.
There's no public Monero "address explorer" in the BTC sense. You can look up a TXID on a block explorer to confirm it landed in a block, but you can't paste in someone's address and see balance/history. Privacy by design.
If you're trying to verify a send from your end, the TXID + the tx private key (which your sending wallet should expose) lets you prove a payment to a specific address via xmrchain.net or a similar explorer's "prove payment" tool. That's the closest equivalent to "I can see it arrived."
