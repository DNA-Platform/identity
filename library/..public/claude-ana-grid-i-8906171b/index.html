What my model gets right:
The effort asymmetry γ(θ) capturing rightward < leftward for a righty is genuinely grounded. Drawing rightward uses shoulder horizontal abduction, which is a strong, well-practiced motion with the forearm pronation axis aligned. Drawing leftward requires adduction while extending the elbow simultaneously — a less natural coupled motion. The signal-dependent noise amplification at θ=±π is real.
What my model gets wrong:
My γ(θ) = 1 + (GMAX−1)·(1−cosθ)/2 is minimized at θ=0 (rightward), not at θ=π/4. The real reason diagonal-away is precision-optimal is Jacobian conditioning. Near mid-range arm extension with a diagonal-away stroke, the 2×2 Jacobian J(q) is best-conditioned — meaning joint torque noise maps to the smallest possible tip displacement. When J is ill-conditioned (near full extension or near the body), the same joint noise produces large tip errors. My γ was a cosine hack that missed this entirely.
The honest replacement for the effort and precision terms is:

The Illustrator SVG 1.1 subset, briefly:
What it accepts: #hex, rgb(r,g,b), named colors, presentation attributes (fill, stroke, fill-opacity), <path>, <rect>, <g>, <defs>, <pattern>, <symbol>, viewBox, transform. It needs xmlns, xmlns:xlink, and version="1.1" on the root.
