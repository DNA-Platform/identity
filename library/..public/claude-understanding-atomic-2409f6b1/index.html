09:21 UTC, 18 January 2026
Those 12 words work for ALL of your wallets within Atomic Wallet - and that's actually the elegant part of how modern crypto wallets work.
Here's what's happening:
That single 12-word seed phrase is a master key that mathematically generates all your different cryptocurrency addresses (Bitcoin, Ethereum, Monero, TON, etc.) through a process called hierarchical deterministic (HD) key derivation. One seed phrase → many addresses across different blockchains.
So when you look at your Atomic Wallet and see separate "wallets" for BTC, ETH, XMR, etc., they're all controlled by those same 12 words. If you ever need to recover your wallet on a new device, you enter those 12 words once and get access to all your cryptocurrency addresses.
Different scenarios:
Same wallet app (Atomic Wallet): One 12-word phrase controls all cryptocurrencies ✓
Different wallet apps: Each app generates its own 12-word phrase. Your Coinbase wallet has different words than your Atomic Wallet, which would have different words than a hardware wallet, etc.
For your transfer from Coinbase:
Just get your Bitcoin receiving address from Atomic Wallet (it'll be a string starting with bc1... or 3... or 1...) and use that as the withdrawal address in Coinbase. The 12-word phrase stays private - you never share it, and Coinbase doesn't need it. They just need the public address to send to.
Does that clear it up?
