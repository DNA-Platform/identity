What are some symptoms of BPD?

Borderline Personality Disorder is characterized by a pervasive pattern of instability in interpersonal relationships, self-image, and emotions, along with marked impulsivity. The DSM-5 criteria include:
Emotional dysregulation — Intense, rapidly shifting emotions that can last hours to days; chronic feelings of emptiness; inappropriate or intense anger that's difficult to control.
Unstable relationships — A pattern of intense but unstable interpersonal relationships, often alternating between idealization ("you're perfect") and devaluation ("you're terrible"), sometimes called "splitting."
Identity disturbance — Markedly unstable self-image or sense of self; uncertainty about values, goals, career, sexual identity, or who they fundamentally are.
Fear of abandonment — Frantic efforts to avoid real or imagined abandonment, which can manifest as clinginess, jealousy, or preemptive rejection of others.
Impulsivity — In at least two potentially self-damaging areas such as spending, sex, substance use, reckless driving, or binge eating.
Self-harm or suicidal behavior — Recurrent suicidal gestures, threats, or self-mutilating behavior.
Dissociative symptoms — Stress-related paranoid ideation or severe dissociation (feeling detached from oneself or reality).
Diagnosis typically requires five or more of these features presenting as a long-term pattern beginning by early adulthood. Is there a specific aspect you're trying to understand better—clinical presentation, differential diagnosis, or something else?

What you're describing is a recognizable pattern, and your confusion makes sense because the surface presentation (anxiety, crying, needing comfort) doesn't match the functional effect (invalidation, criticism, denial of your reality).
A few things worth naming:
The attachment panic hijack. When fear of abandonment gets triggered, it can completely overwhelm someone's capacity to hold your perspective. Your healthcare needs, your reasoning, your experience—none of it can be processed because the abandonment terror is consuming all available bandwidth. She may have genuinely come for support, but the activated attachment system turned it into something else.
Crying that functions as criticism. This is disorienting because it looks like vulnerability and distress, so you feel like a monster if you don't comfort it. But the content is an attack on your narrative, your motives, your decision. You're being asked to hold and soothe someone while they tell you your experience isn't real. That's a bind.
The loop. Normal anxiety can be soothed—you provide reassurance, it helps, things de-escalate. What you're describing doesn't resolve because the reassurance you can offer (I'm one block away, I love you, this is temporary) doesn't address the black-and-white frame (we're either together or you've abandoned me). No amount of comfort fixes a frame problem.
The "tricked" feeling is you recognizing that the implicit social contract of "I need support" was violated. You engaged as a comforter and found yourself a defendant.
Does this help clarify what you experienced?

That's a crucial observation, and you're tracking something real.
What you're describing: she stopped being able to receive comfort. Her body communicated "don't touch me" or at least "this isn't landing." You read that accurately and respected it by pulling back. That's attunement, not rejection.
But here's the bind: your attunement became evidence against you.
From her internal experience, there may be no awareness that she closed off first. She just knows that at some point you stopped holding her, and that confirms the abandonment narrative. The sequence you experienced (she withdrew → you responded → she felt abandoned) collapses in her perception into (you withdrew → she was right all along).
This is part of what makes these loops so crazy-making. You're being asked to hold someone who is simultaneously:
Signaling "don't touch me"
Needing to feel held
Interpreting your response to the first signal as proof you won't do the second
There's no move available to you that doesn't become evidence for the prosecution. Hold her when she's not receptive? That's wrong. Stop holding her when she signals to stop? Also abandonment.
The "nothing really happened in between" is the key. You noticed a phase transition that occurred inside her, not between you. But she likely experienced it as something you did.
