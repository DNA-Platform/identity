Two different clocks, and the patient-facing timelines blur them together.
The osteotomy uniting — the two cut faces of the heel bone knitting back together — is the fast clock. The calcaneus is cancellous bone: spongy, trabecular, huge contact area, excellent blood supply. It behaves nothing like a long-bone shaft. Radiographic union is typically 6–12 weeks, which is why full-activity clearance usually gets quoted at 3–4 months, once a CT or x-ray confirms it. At 6.5 months your osteotomy is almost certainly united.
So what eats the rest of the year isn't union — it's everything downstream of it: the healed bone remodeling to the new loading axis (Wolff's law, trabeculae physically reorganizing over months), residual swelling clearing, the subtalar joint adapting to the realigned mechanics, the sural nerve recovering, scar maturing, endurance returning. "United" is an event around month 2–3. "Recovered" is the slow tail to month 12. One number hides both.
Now the part that matters for your screw. Hardware removal is gated by union, not by full recovery. The only reason you don't pull a screw early is that it's holding the osteotomy stable while the bone knits — remove it before union and you risk displacement. Once the bone is consolidated, that structural reason is gone. So if your osteotomy is already united, which is likely at this point, there's no bone-healing reason you'd have to wait another six months.
Which reframes your prediction. The doctor's "revisit past a year" is probably not "the bone can't take it yet." It's the more conservative version: don't do an elective second surgery until the irritation has had time to settle on its own, because some hardware pain fades as swelling resolves and you adapt. That's a "let's not operate prematurely" judgment — not a "you're not healed enough" constraint. And the difference matters, because the first one is a discretionary call you're allowed to weigh in on, not a biological gate.
So the question to put to him is two-part: is the osteotomy radiographically united — and if yes, is the reason to wait on the screw about bone safety, or just about giving it a chance to quiet down?
