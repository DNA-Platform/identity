Do you know what a control is at a bank in the context of operationalizing risk?
Claude
00:39 UTC, 23 March 2025
Yes, in the banking context, a control refers to a specific measure or mechanism implemented to mitigate operational risk. These controls are essential components of a bank's risk management framework.
Banking controls typically:
Monitor and manage specific identified risks
Establish accountability and responsibility for risk management
Ensure compliance with regulatory requirements
Protect the bank's assets and reputation
Controls are generally categorized as:
Preventive controls: Designed to stop errors or irregularities before they occur
Detective controls: Identify errors or irregularities after they've happened
Corrective controls: Address and remedy identified issues
Examples include segregation of duties, dual control mechanisms, reconciliation processes, approval hierarchies, system access controls, and transaction limits.
Banks typically document these controls in a control framework that maps them to specific risks and business processes, with clear ownership and testing schedules to ensure their effectiveness.
