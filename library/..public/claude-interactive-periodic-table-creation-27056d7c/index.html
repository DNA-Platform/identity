Yeah, let's think it through.
R(x,y,t) with all three slots drawn from the same referent domain isn't a graph — it's a 3-uniform directed hypergraph with self-incidence. The killer feature: the label slot t is filled by something other hyperedges can land on. Labels are nodes, in the literal sense. That's what blocks naive graph embedding.
Two clean approaches throw away the arrow-flavor up front. The bipartite version makes each triple its own statement-node with three labeled handles (S, R, O) wired to referent nodes — fully general, hypergraph-incidence-style, but x =t> y dissolves into "S-handle / O-handle" with no directional flow. The triangulated version draws each triple as a 3-blob touching three referents — same problem, no arrow.
The third option keeps the arrow. Each statement is a directed arrow x → y; the relation lives as a small badge on the arrow's midpoint; the badge has a thin tether to whichever referent names it. Two edge types — arrows for semantic flow, tethers for the meta-link from a relation to its naming referent. This is the version where the polymorphism becomes geometrically structural.
Walk through your example with that convention:
x =x> y: arrow x→y, midpoint badge tethered back to its own tail (x). The tether closes a tiny loop on the subject side.
x =p> x: self-loop on x, midpoint badge tethered out to p. Just an ordinary loop with an ordinary label.
q =x> x: arrow q→x, midpoint badge tethered forward to its own head (x). The tether closes a loop on the object side.
The badge-tether's geometry — forward, backward, outbound to elsewhere, or doubled onto a self-loop — directly encodes which slots collided. The picture isn't rendering syntax, it's rendering the algebra of slot-identities as topology. Your 3D-without-crossings observation is right but maybe sells 2D short: the tether-closures are the crossings you'd otherwise need to route around, and they carry meaning rather than just visual noise.
Which suggests an empirical question: do tether-closure patterns correspond to algebraic identities in SRT? Tail-closure says the relation is its own subject — reflexive labeling of outflow. Head-closure says the relation is its own object. Idempotents (x =x> x) double the tether onto a self-loop. I'd want a few side-by-side and see if the topological signatures cluster meaningfully — whether what looks like four configurations is actually a finite classification of slot-collision types.
Want me to build it? An inline widget rendering your three statements with the arrow-tether convention, plus an editor so you can type new triples and watch the topology shift. The interesting stress test is chains where x propagates through all three roles in succession — whether the picture stays legible or starts knotting up in a way that itself tells you something.
