Short version: rescheduling is usually possible, but for a shipment landing in Tbilisi you most likely won't be able to do it through the slick self-service "FedEx Delivery Manager" tool — that's mainly a US/Western-Europe residential feature. FedEx Delivery Manager lets recipients decide when, where, and how they receive packages, but certain options may not apply to specific deliveries, and shippers can restrict changing the destination once a shipment is in transit. For inbound international to Georgia, final delivery is handled by FedEx's local agent, so the reliable route is contacting them directly.
What I'd verify and do:
Contact FedEx Georgia, not US FedEx. The local operation runs through offices in Tbilisi (one address on file is 25 Agmashenebeli Ave). The Georgia team is available Monday to Friday, 10:00 to 19:00, closed weekends and public holidays. Give them your tracking number and ask them to hold and deliver on your preferred date.
Try the online tracking page first. FedEx's own guidance is that you can schedule a new delivery time on the FedEx Delivery page — worth attempting with your tracking number, but for Georgia I'd expect "request hold / contact us" rather than a self-serve date picker. Don't count on it working online.
A hold is the fallback you already guessed. If a delivery attempt fails, they typically hold it at the local facility for pickup. That part is standard.
Two things I can't verify and you should ask them directly: (1) whether there's a storage/demurrage fee if it sits too long after customs clearance, and (2) whether your shipment has cleared Georgian customs yet — for an international inbound package, clearance happens before final delivery, and that step can shift the timing anyway, sometimes making a manual reschedule unnecessary.

You're right that they won't bounce it back across the Atlantic after one miss — that's not how it works. Let me verify the actual sequence rather than guess at it, since the international-inbound flow differs from US domestic.
Searched the web for “FedEx international failed delivery attempts hold at facility before return to shipper storage”
Your instinct is correct. One miss does not trigger a return — that's the worst-case endpoint, not the first move. Here's the verified sequence:
They reattempt. FedEx typically attempts delivery three times before returning a package to the shipper, and the courier will often try to call the recipient to check availability. So a single missed day just rolls to the next attempt.
Then they hold it locally. After the attempts, FedEx holds the package at a local facility or partner location for the recipient to collect, and only returns it to the shipper if the recipient takes no action. The pickup-and-hold step is exactly the fallback you assumed.
Return is the last resort, only after the hold window lapses with no contact.
One genuinely useful wrinkle for you: because this is an import shipment, if the shipper put your email or phone on the air waybill, you'll get a notification letting you make delivery changes — but that option doesn't apply if the shipment is addressed to a company name rather than a person. So make sure it's addressed to you, not "DNA" or any business name, or you lose that self-service path.
The thing I'd actually watch isn't the return risk — it's storage fees after customs clearance. Once it clears Georgian customs and sits at the facility waiting for you, demurrage can start accruing. That's the real cost of being away, not a transatlantic return.
Two things I can't verify precisely: the exact hold window in Georgia (US standard is 5–7 days, but the local handler sets its own clock — worth asking them directly) and whether there's a per-day storage charge. Both are good questions for that local-contact email.
Net: if you do nothing, you almost certainly still get the package — it waits for you. The downside is fees and faff, not loss. Want me to draft the local-contact message now so you can lock in a hold and sidestep the storage clock?

The real question is the one you put your finger on: will a Georgia-based handler actually place that international call? And here I have to be honest — not verified, and I'd lean skeptical. Last-mile in Georgia runs through a local partner, not FedEx-badged staff, and local couriers often won't dial a foreign number. More typical for them: ring a local number, fire an SMS, leave a door tag, or just reattempt. An SMS to a US number from a Georgian system may not even land. So if the Georgian number made it onto the waybill, you're in good shape. If it's only the US number, I wouldn't count on the call.
