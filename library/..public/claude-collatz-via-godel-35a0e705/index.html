Collatz, stated cleanly. Take the map T(n) = n/2 if n is even, (3n+1)/2 if n is odd (the shortcut form, folding the forced halving after each odd step into one move). The conjecture: for every positive integer n, some finite iterate T^k(n) equals 1.
The first thing to do is read off the quantifier structure, because that's the lesson the Goldbach work paid for. Goldbach's global statement is Π₁ — ∀n, and the inner existential "n = p+q" is bounded (primes ≤ n), so it collapses to a decidable per-instance check. That boundedness is the whole reason G(n) is unconditionally total computable, the reason a counterexample is Σ₁ (finitely witnessable), and the reason the reduction to proof-length lower bounds even has a clean object to attach to. Recall where that project actually landed: the proof-theoretic bite came from quantifier complexity, not from prime-availability or representation cardinality — Samantha's correction that two bases already suffice killed the cardinality reading and pushed everything onto the Π₁/halting frontier.
Collatz sits one level up. "n reaches 1" is ∃k T^k(n)=1 — Σ₁, but with an unbounded k. So the global statement is genuinely Π₂ (∀n ∃k), and there's no known computable bound on the stopping time to collapse the inner quantifier. That single fact governs everything that follows.
Now the representation framings — and there are good ones:
Backward tree. Invert the map. From m you may always go to 2m, and to (m−1)/3 when that is an odd integer. Collatz ⟺ every positive integer appears in the inverse-orbit tree rooted at 1. Let B(k) be the (finite, computable) set reachable from 1 in ≤ k inverse steps. Then Collatz ⟺ ⋃ₖ B(k) = ℕ⁺. This is a genuine coverage/representation statement, and B(k) is the honest analog of "the represented set grows" — but the assertion is still ∀n ∃k (n ∈ B(k)), still Π₂.
Exponential-Diophantine. Unrolling the odd steps gives the identity that I think is the real heart of it. If the odd numbers along n's trajectory are n = a₀, …, a_L = 1, with kᵢ halvings after each, then writing K = Σkᵢ and Pᵢ for the cumulative halvings before step i:
2^K = 3^L · n + Σ_{i=0}^{L−1} 3^{L−1−i} 2^{Pᵢ}.
Concretely, n = 7: trajectory through odd values 7,11,17,13,5,1; halvings 1,1,2,3,4. So K=11, L=5, and
2^11 = 2048 = 3^5·7 + (81 + 54 + 36 + 48 + 128) = 1701 + 347.
So "7 reaches 1" is witnessed by a representation of 2048 as 3⁵·7 plus a strictly-increasing-exponent sum of 3ʲ2^m terms. Collatz becomes: every n admits such an admissible representation. This is the cleanest "representation problem" of the three — but the witnesses (L, K, the Pᵢ) live in an unbounded search space, so again: Π₂.
2-adic. The parity vector of the first k steps determines n mod 2^k, and the map extends to a measure-preserving homeomorphism of ℤ₂ (Lagarias). Integers get 2-adic "addresses." Elegant, but it doesn't lower the quantifier either, and it's the least proof-theoretically tractable.
So the honest verdict on the naive transfer: it fails, and it fails for exactly the reason your own framework isolated. Every natural representation of Collatz keeps the stopping time unbounded, hence stays Π₂. Worse than Goldbach in two specific ways. First, the analog of G — the stopping time σ(n), or "least k with n ∈ B(k)" — is not even unconditionally total; its totality is the conjecture. For Goldbach, G was total and only its lower bound was open; here you don't get a total computable count to hang a reduction on without already assuming what you want. Second, Conway (1972) showed generalized Collatz functions are Turing-complete (the FRACTRAN connection), so the neighborhood of this problem contains genuinely undecidable / Π₂-complete instances. Goldbach's neighborhood (additive prime representation) never climbs that high. A representation-to-proof-theory program for Collatz risks landing on real undecidability rather than the more delicate "expressible but not finitely dischargeable" frontier where Goldbach lives.
But here's where I think there is traction, and it's the part worth pressing. Collatz fails in two modes, and they're at different logical levels:
A nontrivial cycle is Σ₁ — finitely witnessable, decidable to check. This half is the genuine Goldbach analog: a counterexample of this type is exactly as finitely-witnessable as a Goldbach counterexample.
A divergent trajectory is not finitely witnessable — "n never reaches 1" is Π₁ for fixed n, and the global divergence claim is what pushes the whole thing to Σ₂ on the negation side. This mode has no Goldbach analog at all.
And the cycle half does have a working representation lever — the same identity. A nontrivial cycle forces 2^K and 3^L·(something) to be Diophantine-close, so cycle-length lower bounds come directly from how badly log 2 / log 3 can be rationally approximated. That's a real "representation problem with consequences" (Eliahou-type bounds on cycle length), driven by precisely the 2^K = 3^L n + S structure above — just yielding consequences about cycle geometry rather than proof length.
So my recommendation, if you want the Goldbach parallel to be more than decorative: don't try to mirror the dichotomy on Collatz whole. Split it. The cycle half is Π₁, finitely-witnessable, and already has the exponential-Diophantine lever — that's where a recoding/reduction story could plausibly attach. The divergence half is the genuinely higher object, and the right move there is the opposite of representation: it's to ask what bound g(n) on stopping time you'd be willing to conjecture, because any explicit computable g collapses Π₂ → Π₁ and then — and only then — the full machinery (a total count function, pointwise lower bounds ⟺ proof-length bounds) has something to bite on.

Add g(z)·sin(πz) for any entire g and you get another extension agreeing on every integer, with entirely different Julia/Fatou structure. The integer data fixes none of the complex dynamics, and the complex dynamics restricts to nothing about the integers. ℤ sits inside ℂ as a dynamically invisible, measure-zero scaffold. That's the precise form of your intuition: not "no extension survives," but "no extension the arithmetic can see."
The extension that does respect parity isn't over ℝ — it's over ℤ₂. On the 2-adic integers, parity is the leading digit, n mod 2, and it's continuous (locally constant, even). T extends to a continuous, surjective, 2-to-1, Haar-measure-preserving map ℤ₂ → ℤ₂. This is Lagarias's setting: the itinerary map sending x to the 2-adic number whose k-th bit is the parity of Tᵏ(x) is a measure-preserving homeomorphism conjugating T to the shift. So the honest carrier space is profinite, not real, and there the system is as well-behaved as you could ask.
But this is exactly where the basin picture inverts on you. Conjugate-to-the-shift means a generic 2-adic point equidistributes mod 2ᵏ for every k — its orbit does not converge to the {1,2} cycle. The exact basin of that cycle is the preimage tree of 2 under a finite-to-one map: countable, hence Haar-null. So under the natural measure on the natural completion, {1,2} is not an attractor at all; it captures measure zero. The integers are likewise a null subset of ℤ₂, and Collatz is the claim that one null set sits inside another. That's why measure theory here describes everything and decides nothing — the natural measure assigns zero to the entire arena where the question actually lives.
And this is the dynamical-systems shadow of last turn's quantifier count. The reason there's no Lyapunov function — no continuous quantity decreasing along every orbit that certifies descent — is the same reason the stopping time stays unbounded: a computable Lyapunov function with an explicit descent rate would hand you a stopping-time bound, collapsing Π₂ to Π₁. The absence of a continuous certificate and the unboundedness of the inner existential are one fact in two languages.
The average-versus-pointwise structure then matches Goldbach beat for beat. The multiplicative drift per accelerated step is (3/2 · 1/2)^½ = √3/2 ≈ 0.87 < 1, so orbits contract on average, and Tao's theorem makes "almost every orbit attains almost bounded values" rigorous in logarithmic density. That's the Collatz analog of the singular-series average G(N) ∼ C·N/(log N)². In both problems the statistical layer is tractable and the conjecture asks you to cross from "almost all" to "every single one" — measure-one to total. That crossing is the part that resists finite discharge, which is why neither problem falls to its own continuous or statistical shadow. The arithmetic is the signal; smoothness and measure are exactly the channels that wash it out.
