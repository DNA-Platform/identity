Neither has disappeared, but both have notably reduced public profiles compared to Trump's first term, for different reasons.
Melania is present but selective. She limits her interactions with the public, creating a public image heavily influenced by speculation. That said, she has been active — she attended the opening night of "Chicago" at the Kennedy Center just yesterday, March 31. She also produced her own documentary, released in January 2026, and has done advocacy work with children's hospitals and foster care initiatives.
Ivanka has genuinely stepped back. She served as an adviser to her father in his first term but has largely avoided the global spotlight during his second. She and Jared Kushner are based in Miami Beach with their children and appear to have made a deliberate choice to stay out of politics this time around.
There's also a reported rift between the two. Sources claim Melania and Ivanka share little personal connection and operate in entirely "different worlds," with insiders alleging there is "no bond at all." Ivanka notably skipped the premiere of Melania's documentary, and Melania's stepchildren — including Ivanka — have no speaking roles in the film.
Both did appear together at the 2026 State of the Union in February, so they show up for major family/political set pieces, but neither is doing the constant public presence thing you might expect.

These are actually two pretty distinct situations with different causes.
Ivanka — a genuine, deliberate exit
This one is fairly clear-cut. After Trump announced his 2024 run, Ivanka said she was choosing to prioritize her young children and the private life she and Jared Kushner were building as a family, stating she did not plan to be involved in politics. She has explained it repeatedly and consistently. "I love policy and impact. I hate politics... there is a darkness to that world that I don't really want to welcome into mine. It's a very dark, negative business." The core stated reason is the kids: "The main reason I am not going back to serve now is I know the cost, and it's a price that I'm not willing to make my kids bear."
There's also Jared Kushner's health factor — he revealed in his 2022 memoir he'd been diagnosed with thyroid cancer in 2019 and had a second surgery in 2022. That context matters. Friends describe the Miami move as a genuine lifestyle reset, not a feud or estrangement from her father.
Melania — more complicated and contested
The honest answer is nobody fully knows, and the White House disputes the darker interpretations. The documented facts: the New York Times reported she had spent fewer than two weeks at the White House since the inauguration, and sources said she was "deeply spooked" about the assassination attempts on her husband's life and what that meant for her family's safety. She splits time between Trump Tower (where Barron is at NYU) and Mar-a-Lago.
But beyond security concerns, there's a pattern going back years. Biographer Michael Wolff claims Melania keeps "careful distance" from her husband over differences on issues including abortion access and border family separation policies, and has "nontraditional living arrangements." The White House has called those claims fabrications.
A more psychologically interesting read comes from a clinical psychologist who argues the absence is calculated — that by limiting her presence, Melania protects herself from scrutiny, reduces her exposure to the political circus, and preserves her own separate identity rather than being "psychologically swallowed" by Trump's dominance of any room he's in.
The Melania documentary and memoir both fit this pattern — she's actively building her own public legacy, on her own terms, separate from his orbit. As one analyst noted, "with the absence of Ivanka, Melania may feel more empowered to be the female figurehead of this administration." So paradoxically, her stepping back from joint appearances may actually be her stepping forward as an independent figure.
