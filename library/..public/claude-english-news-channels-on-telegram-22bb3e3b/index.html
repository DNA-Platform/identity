Here's a solid rundown, Doug. I'd break these into a few categories depending on what angle you want:
General / Breaking News (US-oriented)
Insider Paper — fast, snappy breaking news with a global scope. One of the more popular English-language news channels on Telegram.
Disclose.tv (~357k subscribers) — covers global events with both mainstream and alternative angles. Good for spotting trends early.
The New York Times — their Telegram channel pushes out the same investigative journalism you'd expect.
Geopolitics & Conflict
Intel Slava Z (~396k subscribers) — specializes in geopolitics, conflicts, and international news aggregation. Heavily focused on Ukraine-Russia and Middle East. Worth noting this leans pro-Russian in its framing, so calibrate accordingly.
Rybar (English version: @ryaboreng) — the most important source of conflict information for analysts and media, publishing detailed reports and highly accurate front-line maps. Originally focused on Middle East military analysis, exploded during the Ukraine war. Pro-Russian but tries to maintain analytical credibility — CNN, Bloomberg, and the Institute for the Study of War regularly cite its data. Fascinating from an OSINT perspective.
BRICS News — tracks emerging economies, international trade, and the shifting global order. Relevant if you're following the multipolar dynamics.
Russian Government / Official
Kremlin News (@news_kremlin_eng) — the official English-language Kremlin channel. Straight from the source, which is useful for reading the official narrative unfiltered and comparing it to other accounts.
Independent Russian / Opposition
Astra — an independent journalist-run channel focusing on delivering accurate information on events in Russia. The Kremlin considers them enemies of the state, which is arguably a quality signal. The gap between an Astra report and an official Kremlin narrative is almost always an excellent measure of how much the state narrative differs from reality. Primarily Russian-language but Telegram's built-in translate function handles it well.
The Bell (thebell.io) — independent Russian business/politics journalism with an English edition. They did the major investigation unmasking who runs Rybar.
Meduza — Russian independent media operating from Latvia, publishes extensively in English.
OSINT / Intelligence
Several OSINT channels aggregate satellite imagery, flight tracking, military convoy movements, and verified visuals. These tend to be more niche but are gold for understanding what's actually happening on the ground versus what's being reported.
Pro tip: Telegram's built-in translation (long-press any message → Translate) is surprisingly good, so don't limit yourself to English-only channels. Some of the most interesting Russian channels like Rybar's main Russian feed or Colonel Cassad have much richer content in the original Russian, and the auto-translate gives you a decent read.
The real value of Telegram for this kind of information diet is reading across the spectrum — official Kremlin, pro-Russian milbloggers, independent Russian journalists, Western OSINT — and triangulating. No single channel gives you the full picture, but the delta between them is where the signal lives.

The major American legacy outlets never really invested in Telegram the way they did Twitter/X. Telegram's news ecosystem is dominated by independent aggregators and non-Western sources, which is kind of the whole point of using it.
If you want solid US/Western news on Telegram, you're better off with the channels that are actually native to the platform — @insiderpaper and @disclosetv will cover the same stories NYT does, just faster and without a paywall. The value-add of Telegram isn't getting the same sources you already have access to through a different pipe — it's getting the sources you can't easily get elsewhere, which is where the Russian and geopolitics channels really shine.

Honestly, the answer is a little anticlimactic. The biggest channels by raw subscriber count are almost entirely crypto/gaming projects:
The giants (tens of millions)

Telegram's own channels
@TelegramTips — ~11M, the biggest "real" channel
@telegram — ~6M, official announcements
@TelegramPremium — ~7M
Russian-language news (where the real action is)
Прямой Эфир (Pryamoy Efir) — ~4.6M, news blog
Топор Live — ~4.8M, private news channel
Ramzan Kadyrov's personal channel — ~2.6M
Rybar (main Russian feed) — ~1.4M
The pattern is pretty telling: Telegram's biggest channels are either crypto gambling, Telegram's own meta-channels, or Russian-language news/politics. English-language news channels are comparatively tiny — Intel Slava at ~396K, Disclose.tv at ~357K, Tucker Carlson at ~249K. Even the NYT channel you couldn't find is modest.
This maps to the adoption numbers: 45% of India uses Telegram, 38% of Brazil, but only 9% of the US and 10% of the UK. Telegram just isn't where Americans go for news — it's where Russians, Indians, Brazilians, and crypto people go. Which is exactly why it's interesting if you want perspectives outside the Western media bubble.
The username @news, incidentally, sold for $5.8 million in Toncoin on Telegram's Fragment auction platform — just the handle itself. The domain name game has nothing on Telegram namespace speculation.
