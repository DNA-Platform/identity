To publish apps on the iOS App Store and receive payments, you'll need an Apple Developer Account, which comes in two main types:
Individual Account
Costs $99/year
For individual developers
Uses your personal information and tax details
You receive payments under your own name/SSN (or equivalent tax ID)
Organization Account
Costs $99/year
For companies/organizations
Requires a D-U-N-S® Number
Uses your company's tax information
Payments go to your organization
For payment setup, you'll need to provide:
Valid bank account information
Tax forms (W-9 for US developers, W-8BEN for non-US)
Contact information
Payment currency preference
Apple pays developers monthly when earnings reach a minimum threshold (usually $150), minus their 30% commission (or 15% if you qualify for their Small Business Program with under $1M in annual revenue).

Correct! You don't need Stripe or any other payment processor for App Store payments. Apple handles all the payment processing directly through their system. You can absolutely use a personal bank account.
The process is quite straightforward:
You provide your bank account details to Apple
Users purchase your app or make in-app purchases
Apple collects the money, takes their commission
Apple deposits your share directly into your bank account
This is actually one of the conveniences of the iOS ecosystem - Apple handles all the payment processing, refunds, and tax calculations. The downside is you're locked into their payment system and must use their in-app purchase system for digital goods (you can't use Stripe or other processors to avoid their fees).
The only time you'd need something like Stripe would be if:
You're selling physical goods in your app (like e-commerce)
You're selling services used outside the app (like ride-sharing)
You want to process payments on your own website
