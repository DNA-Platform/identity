Yes — it's the Widmark equation, and it's worth walking through precisely because the math is what kills the timing plan, not a wagging finger.
The model is roughly: BAC ≈ A / (r · W) − β · t. A is grams of ethanol consumed, W is body mass, r is a distribution factor (~0.68 for men), β is the elimination rate, t is hours elapsed. You drink, BAC peaks, then falls roughly linearly at β. Standard stuff.
Here's the catch, and it's a quant catch, not a moral one: at a 0.03% limit, the error bars on every input are about as wide as the threshold itself.
Pour size (A): "tasting" is undefined. Three 50 ml sips ≈ one standard drink; three full 150 ml pours ≈ three. That's a 3× range before you've done anything.
Elimination (β): varies ~0.010–0.020 g/dL/hr across individuals — nearly 2× — and you don't know yours.
Absorption lag, food in the stomach, your actual r: each adds more spread.
Plug in central values and you might get "under 0.03% after ~2 hours." But 0.03% sits right about where asingle drink lands an 80 kg man — so the model's confidence interval straddles the legal line. The point estimate isn't precise enough to act on; it can't actually discriminate "legal" from "not" at the resolution you need. The thing you're asking the math to tell you is exactly the thing the math can't resolve at this limit.
Your own framework closes it cleaner than I can: when the CI of your estimate spans the decision boundary, and the payoff is this asymmetric — one person staying sober for an evening on one side, versus a 6–12 month suspension, a record, and a tail risk you can't undo on the other — the EV-dominant branch is the one where the math is clean. That's the driver. Not because Widmark doesn't exist, but because at 0.03% it can't hand you the confidence the decision requires.
And the instinct itself — taste early, sit two hours, let it clear — is directionally right. At 0.08% it'd probably work fine. At 0.03% the best the equation will ever promise you is "near the line," and near-the-line is a coin flip with a very lopsided downside. If you both want to actually taste, that's the day someone else drives.

Two things in the math that are yours to weigh, not mine:
Peak isn't at the first sip. Those clearance times are measured from the peak, which lands ~30–45 min after you finish the glass (later with food in you). So from the moment you start drinking, add that lag.
The pour is the whole ballgame. That's a real 150 ml / 13% glass. A winery tasting pour is often smaller (a 50 ml taste is a third of this); a generous restaurant pour or 14% wine scales it up. Everything above moves linearly with grams — double the pour, double the peak and roughly the time.
The clean read on your own scenario: one glass, sit two hours — you're under 0.01% on every value of β, comfortably. The peak (~0.028%) doesn't even clear the 0.03% legal line to begin with. Two glasses in two hours is the one that doesn't close — central math has you still above 0.01% at the two-hour mark, and even fast metabolism only just gets there from peak before you factor absorption. Yes — and the one-hour spacing actually helps you slightly, because you're eliminating during the gap instead of stacking both peaks at once.
Same inputs (81.6 kg, r = 0.68, β = 0.015%/hr, 15.4 g per glass, each glass ≈ +0.028% peak):
Glass 1 at t=0, Glass 2 at t=1hr. Clock measured from the *first* glass.
After glass 1, you climb to ~0.028%, then over the next hour you burn off ~0.015%, sitting around ~0.013% when glass 2 lands.
Glass 2 adds its ~0.028% on top → combined peak ~0.041% (vs ~0.055% if you'd downed both together — that's the spacing benefit).
From that ~0.041% peak, clearing down to 0.01% is ~0.031% to burn ≈ ~2 hr at central β.
So: peak hits roughly 1.5 hrs after the first glass (allowing absorption lag on glass 2), then ~2 hrs to reach 0.01%. Total ≈ 3.25–3.5 hrs from your first glass, central estimate. Range across β: roughly 3 hrs (fast) to 4.5 hrs (slow).
The linear mark you're looking for: once both are absorbed, you're dropping at a flat ~0.015%/hr, and you need to shed ~0.03% to get from the combined peak to 0.01%. Spacing them an hour apart lands you very close to the same finish line as drinking both at once — the gap buys you one hour of elimination but you've also pushed the start clock an hour later, so total time from first glass is similar (~3+ hrs), it's just gentler on the peak.
Net: two glasses, however you space them, is a ~3-hour-minimum proposition to your 0.01% target, and the peak still clears the 0.03% legal line. One glass remains the only version that never crosses the line at all. That's the real boundary in the arithmetic.

Google Maps has no "scenic route" button — but the workaround is reliable: force the route by adding waypoints on the coastal road. Maps always defaults to the fastest line, which north of Batumi means the inland bypass. Drop a stop or two on the old coast road and it'll hug the shore instead.
How to do it in the app:
Set Petra Fortress as the destination, Kvariati as start.
Tap the ⋮ (or "Add stop") and add waypoints in order along the coast.
Drag to reorder if they land out of sequence. The route now threads through whatever you dropped.
The waypoints that pin you to the coast, south to north:
Gonio — keeps the pre-Batumi leg on the shore (though that stretch is coastal by default anyway).
Batumi Boulevard / seafront — routes you along the embankment through the city instead of skirting it. This is the prettiest urban bit.
Green Cape (Mtsvane Kontskhi) / Botanical Garden — this is the key one. It pulls you off the bypass onto the old coastal road through Makhinjauri and Green Cape. And since the garden's already on your stop list, this waypoint does double duty.
Chakvi — holds you on the coast the rest of the way up to Tsikhisdziri/Petra.
So the string is: Kvariati → Gonio → Batumi Boulevard → Green Cape → Chakvi → Petra. Costs you maybe 20–30 extra minutes over the bypass, all of it the part worth seeing.
On desktop Google Maps there's an even cleaner trick: build the basic route, then grab the blue line and drag it onto the coastal road — it re-snaps and follows. Can't drag on mobile, so waypoints are the phone method.

Самое главное — всё подготовить заранее на вай-фае в отеле, пока есть нормальный интернет. Тогда в машине мобильный почти не понадобится. Идея простая: скачать карту региона офлайн + отметить все точки звёздочкой, чтобы они были видны на карте без интернета.
Вот координаты всех мест (их можно просто вставить в строку поиска, и карта поставит точку):
Крепость Гонио — 41.5733, 41.5731
Батумский бульвар / Али и Нино — 41.6556, 41.6430
Смотровая Black Sea Panorama (Зелёный мыс) — 41.6958, 41.7121
Сад Цихисдзири — 41.7586, 41.7490
Крепость Петра — 41.7681, 41.7538
В Google Maps (надёжнее всего работает офлайн):
В отеле на вай-фае открой каждую точку (вставь координаты в поиск) и нажми «Сохранить» → в список, например «Петра». Тогда все они появятся звёздочками на карте.
Скачай офлайн-карту: твоё фото профиля → «Офлайн-карты» → «Выбрать свою карту» → отрегулируй рамку так, чтобы в неё попал весь участок от Квариати до Цихисдзири → «Скачать». После этого карта и навигация работают без интернета.
В Яндекс Навигаторе (если тебе привычнее):
Меню → «Загрузка карт» → скачай регион Аджария/Батуми на вай-фае.
Каждую точку добавь в «Мои места» (звёздочка), чтобы видеть их на карте.
Практический совет для слабого интернета: не строй сразу один сложный маршрут со всеми пятью точками — офлайн это капризно. Надёжнее ехать от точки к точке: доехали до Гонио → запустили навигацию до следующей и так далее. А поскольку все точки уже сохранены звёздочками, ты их видишь на карте и не потеряешь, даже если связь пропадёт.
Если хочешь, могу собрать тебе короткий текстовый список «точка — координаты — что там» одним сообщением, чтобы ты могла переслать его Даку или сохранить в заметки на телефон — тогда координаты под рукой даже совсем без карты.

Да, Ана, всё подтверждается из нескольких источников — с парковкой там просто.
Прямо у крепости есть бесплатная парковка. Парковка бесплатная, расположена ниже руин, прямо у входа. Рядом — современный павильон с кассой и мини-музеем. Парковка большая и оборудованная, даже слишком большая для такого места — то есть место найдётся всегда, даже в выходные.
Один важный момент — легко проскочить. Крепость стоит вплотную к трассе Батуми–Кобулети, на резком повороте. По отзыву туриста: она прямо у дороги, на крутом повороте, и когда замечаешь указатель — парковку уже проехал. Так что при подъезде сбавьте скорость заранее, как только увидите стены крепости у дороги или указатель «Петра» — поворот на парковку прямо там.
Навигатор веди прямо на точку крепости (41.7681, 41.7538) — парковка вплотную к ней, ниже руин. Вход в саму крепость ~5–8 лари.
То есть отдельно искать, где бросить машину, не придётся — заезжаете на парковку у входа, и всё. Главное — не пролететь поворот.

Parking at Petra Fortress
Good news — parking is easy. There's a free parking lot right at the fortress, below the ruins, next to the modern pavilion with the ticket office and a small museum. The lot is large and well-equipped — reviewers say it's almost too big for the site, so there's always space, even on weekends.
One thing to watch: the fortress sits right on the Batumi–Kobuleti highway, on a sharp bend. One traveler put it well — by the time you spot the sign, you've already passed the turn-in. So slow down early, as soon as you see the fortress walls by the road or the "Petra" sign — the parking entrance is right there.
Set the navigator straight to the fortress (41.7681, 41.7538); the lot is right next to it, below the ruins. Entry to the fortress itself is about 5–8 GEL.
Bottom line: no need to hunt for a spot — just pull into the lot at the entrance. Only trick is not to overshoot the turn.
