Good news - Tbilisi has a thriving English-speaking expat community with multiple entry points. Given your mobility situation, here are the most practical options:
Weekly Meetups (Seated, Social, Free)
La Mano (Lado Asatiani 56) hosts Wednesday expat socials from 9 PM. It's a female-owned bar described as one of the most welcoming and inclusive spaces in Tbilisi. They also do Thursday board game nights. The WhatsApp group (chat.whatsapp.com/IUnd6m86Ado8R8FFaeGsdC) is active and would let you connect with people before showing up.
Crossroads Bar hosts Friday meetups at 9 PM - it's described as the "#1 expat bar in Tbilisi." Free entry, just buy a drink. They also do Thursday open mic nights and Sunday karaoke.
Real Talk Tbilisi runs a structured English social club on Thursdays, 7:30-10 PM at 21PM Kitchen. It's organized with rotating one-on-one conversations - 15 GEL participation fee. This format is good because you stay seated the whole time.
Coworking Spaces with Community
If you want daytime social contact while working:
Fabrika/Impact Hub is the central hub of the nomad scene - a converted Soviet factory with cafes, bars, and the Impact Hub coworking space. Day passes around $9-14. You can just hang out in the courtyard cafes without a membership.
D Block at Stamba Hotel has networking events on the last Friday of each month.
Online Communities to Join Now
Instagram: @tbilisi_expat_community posts all English events
Facebook groups: "Expats in Tbilisi" and "Digital Nomads in Georgia"
The La Mano WhatsApp group mentioned above
For your situation specifically, I'd suggest starting with the WhatsApp group - you can connect with people immediately, explain you're recovering from surgery, and someone might even offer to meet you at a cafe near your apartment before you venture to evening events. The expat community here tends to be pretty supportive of newcomers.

You're right to push past the generic expat scene. Here's what I found for more interest-aligned options:
Finance/Investing/Startup Scene
This is probably your best bet for finding people who think similarly. Startup Grind Tbilisi hosts monthly fireside chats with founders and investors - they've had VCs like Bill Reichert, Marvin Liao, and Sheel Mohnot speak. Events are held in English. They describe themselves as focused on "making friends not contacts" and helping others before helping yourself.
Startup Büro runs demo days, pitch sessions, and networking events throughout the city. These would put you in rooms with people thinking about risk, strategy, and building under uncertainty.
Global Tech Weekend is coming up June 19-21, 2026 - a three-day tech festival with investor meetups, workshops, and founder gatherings across the city. That's a few months out but worth having on your radar.
Strategy Games
Tsibakha Game Club at Fabrika has 150+ games including chess and "games where you need not only luck but also logic and smartness." This is a seated activity in an accessible location.
Live Poker
Tbilisi has several poker rooms - PokerHouse is the only one outside casinos, near the city center. Cash games start at 19:00 daily. They have a rule that if there's a foreigner at the table, Georgian speaking is prohibited. Royal Tiflis runs games at stakes from 5/5 GEL up through private tables. This would give you regular access to strategic thinking and conversation without needing to plan around specific events.
Intellectual Discussion
There's a Tbilisi English Language Book Club on Goodreads - worth checking if they're still active. Prospero's bookshop in Vake is described as "a favourite among locals and expats alike" with philosophy and history sections, plus a cafe next door. That's the kind of place where you might organically meet people.
The Honest Assessment
The general expat meetups are probably faster for initial social contact - you can show up Wednesday or Friday, meet 5-10 people, and within a couple weeks identify the handful who share your interests. The finance/startup events happen less frequently but attract a more self-selected crowd.
Given your mobility constraints, poker might actually be the most practical regular social outlet right now - it's seated, it's analytical, it happens nightly, and the English-at-table rule means you won't be isolated. Want me to dig deeper into any of these directions?
