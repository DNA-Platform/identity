OK Doug, I've done extensive research across Turkish Cargo's own site (including their Turkish-language FAQ and documentation), freight forwarding forums, and logistics companies that act as intermediaries. Here's the full picture:
How Turkish Cargo Actually Works
Turkish Cargo is not a consumer-facing package delivery service like DHL or UPS. It's a B2B air freight carrier — the cargo arm of Turkish Airlines. This is why you hit a wall on the phone. Their entire business model is built around working with IATA-certified cargo agents (acenteler), not directly with individuals.
The key answer from their own FAQ (dış hat / international):
"Yurt dışına kargo göndermek için gerekli gümrük işlemleri ve konşimento kesimi, ücretlendirme, paketleme vs. gibi konuları sizin adınıza yapmak üzere Türkiye'de bulunan IATA üyesi kargo acenteleri ile bağlantı kurmanız gerekmektedir."
Translation: To send cargo internationally, you need to work with IATA-authorized cargo agents in Turkey who handle customs, the air waybill (konşimento/AWB), pricing, packaging, etc. on your behalf.
So you cannot walk up to the Turkish Cargo counter at AYT or GZP and ship boxes yourself internationally. Domestic (iç hat) is different — for domestic shipments you can walk into the airport cargo area, no reservation needed, and ship airport-to-airport. But for international, you go through an agent.
The Agent Model — What It Means For You
An IATA cargo agent is essentially a freight forwarder licensed to book space on Turkish Cargo flights. They handle:
Konşimento (AWB) — the air waybill, the master shipping document
Gümrük işlemleri — customs/export clearance on the Turkish side
Pricing — they get wholesale rates from Turkish Cargo and mark up, or work on commission
Packaging compliance — ensuring your items meet air transport standards
Physical delivery to the airport cargo facility
You find a local agent, tell them what you need to ship and where, they give you a quote, you deliver your boxes to them (or to the airport cargo area), and they handle everything from there. On the Tbilisi side, someone (you or an agent) picks up from TBS airport cargo.
Your Specific Situation: AYT vs GZP
AYT (Antalya) is listed as a station with direct international cargo capability, including wide-body aircraft capacity. It's one of the stronger stations outside Istanbul.
GZP (Gazipaşa-Alanya) is a small regional airport. It's not listed among stations with cargo restrictions, but it's a much smaller operation with narrow-body aircraft only. International cargo from GZP would almost certainly route through Istanbul (IST) anyway. Given that, AYT is your better bet for cargo — more capacity, likely more agents familiar with the process, and possibly direct flights to IST on wide-bodies that can handle larger shipments.
That said, your cargo will almost certainly transit through Istanbul regardless of origin, since Turkish Cargo's international hub and main facility (SMARTIST) is at Istanbul Airport.
Is It Accompanied or Separate?
It's unaccompanied, separate cargo. This is not excess baggage on your passenger flight. Your boxes ship independently on whatever TK flight has cargo capacity (could be the belly of a passenger aircraft or a dedicated cargo plane). They travel on their own air waybill and go through customs on both ends. You fly separately as a passenger.
There's one important nuance here though: Turkish customs allows "yolcu eşyası" (passenger's personal belongings) sent via cargo to be exempt from the 30kg weight limit and normal import duties, if you can prove you entered the country and these are your personal effects being shipped separately. This matters for the Georgian side — if Georgian customs treats your shipment as personal belongings of a relocating resident rather than commercial import, you may get favorable treatment. But that's a Georgia customs question.
Pricing — How It Works
Turkish Cargo doesn't publish a simple consumer price list. Pricing goes through the agents, who get rates from Turkish Cargo's tariff bulletins and negotiate based on volume. The cost structure includes:
Components of the cost:
Navlun (freight charge) — the per-kg air transport rate, which varies by route, weight bracket, and current demand. For Turkey → Caucasus/CIS routes, general cargo rates typically run somewhere in the range of $1.50–$4.00 per kg depending on total weight and the agent's negotiated rate. Higher weight = lower per-kg rate (weight breaks at 45kg, 100kg, 250kg, 500kg, 1000kg).
Fuel surcharge (FSC) — variable, applied on top of freight
Security surcharge (SCC/MYC) — per AWB
Terminal handling charges — at origin and destination
Agent service fees — the agent's margin for handling paperwork, customs, etc.
Customs clearance fees — both Turkish export and Georgian import sides
Storage fees at destination — if you don't pick up quickly at TBS. Turkish Cargo charges $70 fixed + $0.07/kg/day for the first 24 hours, rising to $0.14–$0.166/kg/day after day 4.
For your scenario (let's say 10 bags × 20kg = 200kg, AYT→IST→TBS):
A rough estimate for the freight alone would be somewhere around $300–$800 total depending on the agent's rate, plus another $100–$300 in surcharges, handling, and agent fees. So you might be looking at $500–$1,200 all-in for the Turkish Cargo portion, but this is genuinely a rough range — you need agent quotes.
How to Actually Get This Done
Step 1: Find an IATA cargo agent in Antalya. Turkish Cargo has a downloadable list of authorized agents on their site at turkishcargo.com → Anlaşmalı Acenteler. You can also email CARGOSALES@THY.COM to ask for agent recommendations in the Antalya region.
Step 2: Contact 2-3 agents for quotes. Give them: origin (Alanya/Antalya), destination (TBS Tbilisi), number of pieces, weight per piece, dimensions, and contents description (personal belongings/household items — "kişisel eşya, ev eşyası"). Ask for an all-in quote including customs clearance on the Turkish side.
Step 3: Prepare your boxes. Package them properly — the agent may have packaging requirements. You'll need a content list (packing list) with descriptions and approximate values for customs purposes.
Step 4: Deliver to agent or airport cargo area. Most agents don't offer pickup from Alanya — you'd likely need to transport boxes to the agent's warehouse or directly to AYT cargo. Some agents or third-party logistics companies might arrange pickup for an extra fee.
Step 5: Customs and pickup in Tbilisi. At TBS you or a customs broker picks up from the airport cargo area with the AWB number and your passport/ID.
Alternative You Should Seriously Consider
Given your situation (8-12 bags of personal belongings, Alanya to Tbilisi), Turkish Cargo via an agent is one option, but you might find these alternatives simpler and possibly cheaper:
Road freight / parsiyel nakliye (partial load trucking): Companies like Şah Global Lojistik, YLC Lojistik, and others specialize in Turkey→Georgia road freight. They pick up from your door, handle customs at the Sarp border crossing, and deliver to Tbilisi. For ~200kg of personal items this could run $200–$500 and take 3-5 days. They work with individuals routinely.
Express courier (DHL, UPS, FedEx via an intermediary like KargomKolay, QuickShipper, or Navlungo): These platforms let individuals ship internationally with express carriers, usually with door-to-door service. More expensive per kg but zero hassle.
Excess baggage on Turkish Airlines: If you're flying TK anyway, their excess baggage rates might be competitive for a few extra bags. Check the TK excess baggage calculator — sometimes paying for extra checked bags on your own flight is simpler than the whole cargo operation, especially for 8-12 bags.

That tracks. NY→AYT was piece concept (US routes always are) — per bag, flat rate, 40 lbs (~18kg) cap per piece. GZP→IST→TBS is weight concept — per kg, which is actually better for you since you're not penalized for having fewer heavier bags versus more lighter ones.
So the real math: 100-125kg total, minus your 30kg allowance, leaves 70-95kg extra at ~$10/kg = $700-$950.
That's your move to Tbilisi for under a thousand dollars, bags on the plane, no agents, no customs broker, no tracking a truck through Sarp. You fly, everything arrives.
One thing — you bought that 30kg extra on the GZP ticket already. Since extra baggage isn't covered by the 24-hour free cancel, you'd need to refund it at a TK sales office. There should be one in Alanya or Antalya. Or if you're planning to fly from GZP anyway and the numbers work, just keep the ticket and buy the remaining kg when you know your actual weight.
