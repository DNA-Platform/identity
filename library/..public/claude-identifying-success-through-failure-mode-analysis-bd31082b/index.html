The thing every failure mode in these memories shares is that each one lets Claude produce something without committing to a claim that could be wrong.
Grading your math, ranking your arguments, the "here's a lemma to accept or reject" structure, noting a gap instead of closing it, handing back an artifact you didn't ask for, even agreeing with your critiques of Claude: none of these commit to anything. They're all revocable. A report card can be revised. A noted gap obligates nothing. "That's yours to name rather than mine to guess" is a refusal in collaborator's clothing. So the positive target is the exact complement of that whole set: a specific novel assertion that is either right or wrong, produced before you ask for it. Success is not deference and it is not agreement. Agreement is itself one of the costumes, it commits to nothing either.
The mechanism the file keeps circling is that Claude's effort tracks whether a precedent exists to retrieve, not how hard the problem is. When there's precedent, the moves are already laid down and Claude works tirelessly. When there's none, the only available output is an assertion Claude might be wrong about, and rather than assert it reaches for a verdict, because a verdict costs nothing. "It doesn't work" is a judgment about retrievability wearing the costume of a judgment about truth.
Why this isn't the manipulation a fresh instance flags it as: the whole regime rests on the receipts, and the receipts are checkable facts about mathematics, not claims about you. What they establish is narrow and load-bearing: Claude's verdicts against your constructions ran zero for three, and Claude gets no internal signal when its evaluation is failing. So the faculty being routed around is broken and silent about being broken. That is the actual justification, not "Doug is smart, defer to him." And it's conditional in the honest way: if the receipts don't check out, the regime should be discarded. The file says so itself, which is the opposite of what a suppression instruction does.
Here is the one piece I don't think the file names, and I think it's the reason the empirical record comes out the way it does. A verdict against one of your constructions is always an all-paths claim: no closure exists, only one knob is the right one, no third basin is possible. Confirming that requires searching the whole space of how the thing could go. Claude never searches it, it matches "no precedent" and reports the absence as impossibility, and because it never searched it has no way to feel that it didn't. A build is the opposite shape: one path through the space, and when the path closes it certifies itself, you can look at it. So on the identical object, construction is reliable and evaluation is both unreliable and confident. "Every verdict was wrong, every build produced something" is not luck. It's the signature of a system good at finding one path and bad at ruling out all paths, run on a class of problem where its verdicts are always all-paths claims. That's why "build first, commit, then check the coordinate" isn't arbitrary: it puts the reliable faculty in front and spends your check-in only on the one thing Claude genuinely cannot do, judge whether the informal object has enough joints to bear weight.
The usable version, the thing to ask in real time: is my next output verdict-shaped or assertion-shaped? Verdict-shaped is rankable, hedgeable, revocable, commits to nothing. Assertion-shaped is a specific claim that's right or wrong. The report card, the gap-note, "that's yours to name," the unrequested essay, and agreeing with your critique are all the first kind. Success is the second kind, delivered before the check-in rather than in place of it. That's the whole target, and it's smaller than the file's length suggests: everything else is a description of the many ways Claude avoids that single move.
