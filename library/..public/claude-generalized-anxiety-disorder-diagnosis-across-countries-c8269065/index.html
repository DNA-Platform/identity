Both Turkey and Russia use the ICD-10 (International Classification of Diseases, 10th Edition) published by the World Health Organization, rather than the American DSM-5 system. The US DSM-5 is what typically uses the term "Generalized Anxiety Disorder."
The equivalent diagnosis under ICD-10 is:
F41.1 — Generalized Anxiety Disorder
The name is actually identical — GAD exists in ICD-10 as well. However, there are some meaningful differences in how it's defined and diagnosed:
DSM-5 (US) requires 6 months of excessive worry about multiple topics, with 3+ associated symptoms (restlessness, fatigue, concentration problems, irritability, muscle tension, sleep disturbance).
ICD-10 (Turkey/Russia) requires anxiety that is generalized and persistent but is somewhat less prescriptive about the symptom count — it emphasizes free-floating anxiety, autonomic symptoms (trembling, sweating, palpitations), and apprehension. The 6-month duration is noted but treated more flexibly in practice.
Practical notes:
Turkey follows ICD-10 in official/government medical contexts, though psychiatrists trained with Western influences may think in DSM terms. A diagnosis on a Turkish medical document would be codedF41.1.
Russia strictly uses ICD-10 in clinical and legal/administrative contexts. Russian psychiatry has historically had its own diagnostic traditions, but for official purposes F41.1 is the standard equivalent.
If you're dealing with a specific document, insurance, or bureaucratic context — such as getting a prescription recognized or a medical record translated — the F41.1 code is the universal anchor that will be understood in both countries.
