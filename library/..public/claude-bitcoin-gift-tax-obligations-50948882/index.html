From a US tax perspective, giving away appreciated Bitcoin creates two separate tax events:
1. Capital Gains Tax on Your Appreciation
When you give Bitcoin as a gift, the IRS treats this as a disposition that triggers capital gains tax on your appreciation:
You owe capital gains tax on the difference between your cost basis and the fair market value at the time of the gift
Long-term (>1 year holding) = preferential rates (0%, 15%, or 20% depending on income)
Short-term (≤1 year) = ordinary income rates
Example: You bought Bitcoin at $20k, it's now worth $80k, you gift it.
Your taxable gain: $60k
You owe tax on that $60k regardless of the gift
2. Gift Tax Considerations
The gift itself may trigger gift tax obligations:
Annual exclusion (2024): $18,000 per recipient per year - no reporting needed
Above $18k: Must file Form 709 (gift tax return), but you likely won't owe tax due to lifetime exemption (~$13.6M currently)
The recipient takes your original cost basis ($20k in example above), not the $80k FMV
Key Trap
This creates a nasty situation for the recipient: they inherit your low basis but you already paid tax on the appreciation. If they sell at $80k, they owe tax on $60k gain too - double taxation on the same appreciation.
Better Strategies
If charitably inclined: Donate to a qualified charity - you get fair market value deduction, avoid capital gains entirely
Estate planning: Hold until death - recipient gets stepped-up basis to FMV at death, erasing all gains
Spousal transfers: Unlimited marital deduction, no gift tax, and they take your basis (but still triggers your capital gains)
The gift tax system is separate from income tax - don't confuse "no gift tax owed" (due to exemptions) with "no capital gains tax owed" (always triggered on appreciated assets).

The Key Distinction
Gift tax = tax on the transfer of wealth (mostly eliminated by high exemptions)
Capital gains tax = tax on the appreciation you experienced (no exemption for gifts)
The lifetime exclusion only shields you from gift/estate taxes, not from income/capital gains taxes. You're still on the hook for gains when you dispose of appreciated assets, whether by sale OR gift.
This is why holding until death is powerful - stepped-up basis eliminates the capital gains entirely, AND the estate tax exemption shields most estates from estate tax.
