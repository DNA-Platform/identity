Let me help break this down - it's a fascinating development in neuropharmacology!
The muscarinic/cholinergic connection between schizophrenia and smoking is indeed highly relevant here. People with schizophrenia have very high rates of smoking (50-90% compared to ~20% in general population), and many researchers believe this may be a form of self-medication targeting the cholinergic system, specifically nicotinic acetylcholine receptors.
KarXT (xanomeline-trospium) is interesting because it targets muscarinic acetylcholine receptors instead. Here's why this matters:
Cholinergic System & Smoking
Nicotine activates nicotinic acetylcholine receptors (nAChRs)
People with schizophrenia may smoke to normalize cholinergic signaling
This suggests broader cholinergic dysfunction in schizophrenia
KarXT's Mechanism
Targets M1/M4 muscarinic receptors specifically
M1: Important for cognition and memory
M4: Associated with antipsychotic effects
Avoids common side effects of typical antipsychotics (which target dopamine)
Regarding atropine - yes, there's a fascinating connection! Atropine is an anticholinergic that blocks muscarinic receptors, while KarXT activates them selectively. This helps explain why atropine can cause psychosis-like symptoms (the old phrase "mad as a hatter" comes from mercury poisoning, which has anticholinergic effects).
For bipolar disorder - while KarXT hasn't been specifically approved for bipolar, there's potential interest because:
Psychotic symptoms can occur in severe bipolar episodes
Cognitive dysfunction is common in bipolar disorder
The M1 receptor's role in cognition might help with bipolar cognitive symptoms

This is an interesting question about muscarinic pharmacology!
KarXT isn't the only muscarinic agonist, but it's the first approved for schizophrenia. Here are some other notable muscarinic agonists:
Bethanechol (Urecholine) - primarily used for bladder conditions
Pilocarpine - used in glaucoma treatment
Cevimeline - used for dry mouth/Sjögren's syndrome
Carbachol - ophthalmic use
KarXT is unique because it:
Selectively targets M1/M4 receptors
Includes trospium to block peripheral side effects
Was specifically developed for CNS conditions
Regarding smoking cessation and KarXT - there isn't direct clinical data yet, but there are interesting theoretical connections:
Withdrawal Challenge
When people with schizophrenia quit smoking, they lose nicotinic receptor stimulation
This can worsen symptoms and cognition
KarXT might help stabilize cholinergic signaling during withdrawal
Potential Hypothesis
KarXT's M1 activation might help with the cognitive effects of nicotine withdrawal
M4 activation could help with any worsening of psychotic symptoms
However, this is theoretical - we need clinical studies

Let me break down what we know about cholinergic systems and bipolar disorder:
Direct KarXT Evidence:
Currently no published clinical trials of KarXT specifically for bipolar disorder
Most research has focused on schizophrenia and Alzheimer's psychosis
General Cholinergic Evidence in Bipolar:
Theoretical Support:
The "cholinergic-adrenergic hypothesis of mania and depression" suggests:
Depression = cholinergic dominance
Mania = cholinergic deficiency
This fits with observations that anticholinergics can induce mania-like symptoms
Indirect Clinical Evidence:
Many mood stabilizers affect cholinergic signaling indirectly
Lithium has been shown to increase acetylcholine release
Valproate affects cholinergic neurotransmission
Smoking Patterns:
Bipolar patients have higher smoking rates than general population
Similar to schizophrenia but less pronounced
Suggests possible cholinergic involvement
Cognitive Symptoms:
Cholinergic drugs may help with bipolar cognitive deficits
M1 receptor activation (like KarXT provides) could theoretically help
However, it's important to note that most of this is theoretical or indirect evidence. The cholinergic system in bipolar disorder is less well-understood than in schizophrenia.

Yes, KarXT (brand name Cobenfy) was approved by the FDA in September 2023 specifically for schizophrenia in adults. However, it's important to note:
Current Approval Status:
Approved only for schizophrenia in adults in the US
Not yet approved for other conditions like Alzheimer's psychosis
Not yet approved in other countries/regions
Ongoing Development:
Clinical trials are currently underway for:
Alzheimer's disease psychosis (by Bristol Myers Squibb)
Other potential indications are being explored
Market Availability:
Despite FDA approval, it's relatively new to the market
Accessibility and insurance coverage may still be developing
As with any new medication, long-term real-world data is still accumulating
