Let me explain the concept of imeniny. While I aim to be accurate with cultural information like this, I want to note that this is a somewhat specialized topic where you may want to verify details.
An imeniny (именины), also known as a name day, is a traditional celebration in Russian Orthodox culture that commemorates the feast day of the saint after whom a person is named. It's different from a birthday - while everyone has a birthday, imeniny are celebrated by those named after Orthodox saints. Historically, imeniny were often considered more important than birthdays in Russian culture.
To congratulate someone on their imeniny, you could say:
"С именинами!" (S imeninami!) - "Happy name day!"
"Поздравляю с днём ангела!" (Pozdravlyayu s dnyom angela!) - "Congratulations on your angel's day!"
The second phrase refers to the concept that the saint after whom you're named becomes your heavenly patron or "guardian angel."

An ontology and a heirarchy. What type of conceptual structures are these? Are they types of catalogues? Are they categorical? What does a category mean?
Claude
18:41 UTC, 11 November 2024
Let me break this down systematically:
General Classification:
Both ontologies and hierarchies are types of knowledge organization systems (KOS)
They are conceptual structures that help organize and relate information
While they can be used in cataloging, they aren't strictly "catalogues" themselves
Key Distinctions:
Hierarchy: A simple tree-like structure showing relationships of subordination (parent-child)
Example: Animal → Mammal → Dog → Labrador
Only shows "is-a" or "part-of" relationships
Moves from general to specific
Ontology: A more complex network of concepts showing multiple types of relationships
Includes hierarchical relationships BUT ALSO:
Properties of concepts
Rules and axioms
Different types of relationships (e.g., "causes," "influences," "located-in")
Example: A medical ontology might show that "Aspirin treats Headache" AND "Headache is-a Pain" AND "Pain occurs-in Body_Part"
Categories and Categorization:
A category is a group of things sharing common properties
Categories can be:
Classical (strict boundaries, clear membership criteria)
Prototype-based (fuzzy boundaries, degrees of membership)
Goal-derived (based on function or purpose)
Relationship to Categories:
Both hierarchies and ontologies use categorical thinking
Hierarchies: Use categories in a strict nested structure
Ontologies: Use categories but allow for more complex relationships between them
In essence, hierarchies and ontologies are more sophisticated than simple catalogues because they encode relationships, not just lists. They are categorical in that they group things into categories, but go beyond simple categorization by specifying how these categories relate to each other.
