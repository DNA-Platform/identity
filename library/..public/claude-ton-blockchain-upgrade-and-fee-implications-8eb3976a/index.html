Good push — this is a real conceptual thing and the decoupling is genuinely unintuitive. Let me work it through.
You are not paying validators when you stake. That's the crux. When you stake, you're locking capital to earn the right to claim newly-minted TON. The "staking yield" is not fee revenue being paid to you — it's mostly the protocol minting new tokens and handing them to stakers, diluting everyone who isn't staking.
Two separate revenue streams flow to the validator/staker pool:
1. Emission (inflation). The protocol mints ~568,000 TON per day and gives it to validators. This doesn't come from users — it comes from dilution of every TON holder who isn't staking. If you hold unstaked TON, your share of the total supply shrinks daily. If you stake, you get that dilution back (and then some, because you're capturing the dilution of people who didn't stake, minus validator commission). This is ~99.6% of validator revenue right now.
2. Transaction fees. Users transacting on the network pay gas. 50% burns, 50% goes to the validator pool. That's 2,447 TON/day — 0.43% of revenue.
So when Pavel says "cut fees 6x," what literally happens: the 2.2M people transacting each day save some money (users win). Stream #2 shrinks from 2,447 to ~408 TON/day. Stream #1 is unaffected. Validator revenue drops ~0.36%.
Your intuition — "I'm supporting validator work through staking" — is how a fee-driven system like mature Bitcoin would work, where emission is nearly zero and stakers/miners are paid purely from user fees. In that world, cutting fees 6x is the Azure moment and your analogy lands perfectly. But TON is in the emission-subsidized phase: the network is paying validators by printing money and diluting everyone, not by taxing network usage. Fees are basically a rounding error on top.
This also explains why your 18% yield is so much higher than the 3.6% inflation rate. The emission gets concentrated into a narrow base (only ~16% of supply is staked), so 3.6% dilution of everyone becomes ~22% yield to the few who stake (before fees and commissions). The yield is coming from the 84% of holders who are getting diluted, not from network users paying fees.
Which is also why the June vote is the real threat: it directly cuts stream #1, the one that actually matters. Cutting masterchain reward from 1.7 → 0.35 TON/block hits 99.6% of validator revenue, not 0.43%. That's the emission knob. The fee cut is the usage knob and it barely touches staker economics today.
The Azure analogy becomes valid in the state you're long-term betting on — when TON actually is the commerce layer and fees are, say, 50% of validator revenue. At that point, emission will have presumably tapered (it's meant to) and the network will be fee-sustained. Then cutting fees matters. We're many orders of magnitude of transaction volume away from that.

Where TON sits in the PoS landscape:
TON is currently the most emission-dependent major network by a wide margin. The comparison looks roughly like this:
Ethereum runs at ~0.35% inflation with about 28% of supply staked, yielding ~3% — and EIP-1559 burns frequently push it net deflationary. Ethereum is basically the only major network that's actually fee-sustained. Cardano has ~71% of supply staked and yields 2.4-5%. Solana runs ~6.8% APY with about 67-70% of supply staked, inflation tapering from 8% down toward 1.5% long-term. Cosmos is the high-inflation outlier at 21% APY with ~60% staked but has been overhauling its monetary policy over the last two years, voting to reduce max inflation and transition toward a revenue-based model.
TON is the structural oddball: 3.6% inflation but only ~16% of supply staked, which is why yield concentrates to ~18%. Every comparable network has 50-70% stake ratios. The TON low stake ratio exists partly because huge chunks of supply are structurally unable to stake — genesis wallets frozen, Believers Fund still locked from 2023, exchange custody, the Telegram ecosystem users who hold TON for transactional reasons rather than yield. So the 18% is a real artifact of a specific supply structure, not a competitive rate the Foundation is trying to sustain.
Who gets hurt by the June vote:
Math first: 6x block rate × 5x per-block reward cut = ~1.2x net emission vs pre-Catchain. Inflation settles around 0.7%, basically back to where it was. At 0.7% inflation with 16% staked, yield lands around 4-5% net of LST fees.
Concretely hurt:
Everyone currently earning 18% on tsTON — that's a ~75% yield haircut
TONX's TON-per-share accumulation rate compresses to roughly a quarter of its current trajectory
Liquid staking protocols (Tonstakers, Bemo, etc.) lose their main customer-acquisition hook
New stakers entering now at 18% will see yields collapse within weeks of entry
Helped (or at least not hurt):
Unstaked TON holders — dilution drops from 3.6% to 0.7%
The ~45% of supply in frozen/locked contracts benefits from reduced dilution
TON as a monetary asset — cleaner, less inflationary
Long-term TON price thesis — less structural sell pressure from stakers monetizing yield
Validators — their revenue per stake drops, but ~4-5% yield is still profitable infrastructure economics
Is it dangerous?
Three ways to read "dangerous":
For network security: No. 4-5% is the modal yield across serious PoS networks. Ethereum is at ~3%, Cardano ~3%, Solana ~7%. Nobody is claiming ETH's security is in crisis at 3%. TON would still have ~370 validators holding ~$1B+ in stake. The risk of validators exiting en masse is low because validator economics at that yield still cover infrastructure costs.
For the TON narrative: Actually the opposite — it's a maturation signal. This is the same move Cosmos made. It's what Injective did with INJ 3.0. It's what Polkadot just did with its January 2026 supply cap and "Pi Schedule" that reduces issuance rate by 13.14% every two years. Mature PoS networks all converge on: reduce emission, move toward fee sustenance, let the token appreciate on scarcity rather than bribing security via inflation. If the June vote passes, TON is joining that club. That's consistent with the "liberation infrastructure" thesis, not against it — decentralized cloud infrastructure doesn't need 3.6% inflation forever.
For your specific position: This is the honest question. The transient 18% yield is effectively a gift between Catchain 2.0 going live and the June vote closing. TONX's thesis was never supposed to be yield-farming the emission subsidy. The thesis is being the TON-per-share accumulation vehicle during the 10-year adoption curve. At 4-5% yield, TONX still accumulates; just much more slowly. The material damage is to the near-term narrative — "look at this yield" is a powerful retail acquisition story for TONX shares, and that story compresses if June passes.
The actually-dangerous scenario for you isn't the June vote passing. It's the June vote passing and the commerce layer not materializing on schedule. Because then you've lost the emission subsidy without getting the fee-driven revenue that's supposed to replace it. That's the Cosmos failure mode — cut inflation, but the fee economy never showed up to carry the security budget. In TON's case, the billion-user Telegram integration is the hedge against that failure mode, so the timeline to meaningful on-chain commerce is what you should be watching.
Is the vote actually going to pass, do you have a read on that? The validator population gains from it mathematically — less dilution, same absolute share of a healthier monetary base — so there's alignment on the vote side. But the Foundation proposing it this aggressively (5x per-block cut, not 2x or 3x) signals they see the inflation as genuinely problematic.
