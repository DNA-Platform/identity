Confidential Information

Members of the BCM Community are entrusted with the personal, medical and financial information of our students, program applicants, employees, research study participants, and patients. Not only is BCM ethically responsible for protecting this confidential information, but maintaining the security and privacy of confidential information is considered the standard of care for academic and healthcare institutions. 

Texas and Federal laws have made the ethical and professional duty of maintaining information security and privacy a legal requirement.

Distance Education

All programs offering distance education courses must be compliant with FERPA regulations to protect the privacy of student records and rights of the student. 

As outlined in the BCM Distance Education Policy, the College expects each student to 

preserve the academic integrity of distance education courses and maintain the confidentiality of course materials, including any assessments administered during the course and/or outside the classroom setting, and
uphold the privacy of other students enrolled in the course, not sharing personal information divulged by another student outside the virtual classroom, discussion, forum, or assessment setting. 
This means it is never appropriate to share class documents or exam questions, or to discuss personal information you learn about other students. To learn more about FERPA, please review the U.S. Department of Education FERPA site.

Reusing Passwords

As cyber crimes become more prolific, it is important to make sure we as members of the Baylor community are doing all we can to safeguard our access to the BCM network. One area of weakness is in the reuse of passwords for multiple sites. Reuse of passwords opens an employee up to a specific type of threat called credential stuffing. 

Credential stuffing is the name of an automated attack that takes the compromised credentials from one leak/site and uses them to break into user accounts from other services/sites that use the same username and password. Passwords are typically obtained by cybercriminals through data breaches and phishing.

With this in mind BCM will be using enhanced password standards to improve security, provide for flexibility, and to be in alignment with the recently released Federal National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) standards. For additional information, please reference the Password Standard Knowledge Base Article on the IT Portal.

It is important to not reuse passwords, especially any password you use to access the Baylor network
