Confidential Information

Members of the BCM Community are entrusted with the personal, medical and financial information of our students, program applicants, employees, research study participants, and patients. Not only is BCM ethically responsible for protecting this confidential information, but maintaining the security and privacy of confidential information is considered the standard of care for academic and healthcare institutions. 

Texas and Federal laws have made the ethical and professional duty of maintaining information security and privacy a legal requirement.

Confidential Information: that which the Baylor Community is required to keep in strict confidence, including but not limited to

personally identifiable information
student records
protected health information (PHI)
financial information
Privacy and Security is EVERYONE’s responsibility. YOU must safeguard confidential information to respect the privacy of the BCM Community and to prevent misuse, improper use or disclosure of information that may lead to:

Identity Theft
Insurance Fraud
Financial Harm
Discriminatory/Wrongful Use

Genetic Information


The Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act of 2008 (GINA) is a federal law that protects individuals from genetic discrimination in health insurance and employment. Genetic discrimination is the misuse of genetic information. 


Leave of Absence


Do not disclose the reason for FMLA (Family Medical Leave Act) or non-FMLA absences with other employees or supervisors; only address time periods for approval, duration and frequency (if applicable).
Do not disclose that an employee is on FMLA or non-FMLA leave specifically. It may generate more questions especially if the employee is friends with the co-worker that is currently off work.

Being responsible with employee information

Although some information may not be protected by specific laws, courts have granted civil remedies for the inaccurate release of information that has a detrimental effect on a former or current employee’s attempt to gain new employment.

Distance Education

All programs offering distance education courses must be compliant with FERPA regulations to protect the privacy of student records and rights of the student. 

As outlined in the BCM Distance Education Policy, the College expects each student to 

preserve the academic integrity of distance education courses and maintain the confidentiality of course materials, including any assessments administered during the course and/or outside the classroom setting, and
uphold the privacy of other students enrolled in the course, not sharing personal information divulged by another student outside the virtual classroom, discussion, forum, or assessment setting. 
This means it is never appropriate to share class documents or exam questions, or to discuss personal information you learn about other students. To learn more about FERPA, please review the U.S. Department of Education FERPA site.
