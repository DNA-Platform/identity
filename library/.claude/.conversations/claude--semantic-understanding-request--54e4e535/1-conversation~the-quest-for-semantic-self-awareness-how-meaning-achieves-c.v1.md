### Five Interconnected Problems

**Problem 1: The Symbol Grounding Paradox**
How does `2` mean two? In standard mathematics, {{∅}, {∅, {∅}}} supposedly represents 2, but the actual meaning—the "two-ness"—exists only in the mathematician's mind. We've built elaborate formal systems where symbols manipulate symbols, but meaning itself remains external to the formalism.

**Problem 2: The Hard Problem's Hardness**
Why does consciousness seem irreducible to physical description? Not because it's mystical, but because we lack formal tools to describe how subjective experience emerges from objective structure. We can map neural correlates but can't explain why there's "something it's like" to be conscious.

**Problem 3: Self-Reference Without Paradox**
Consciousness requires self-awareness—I must be able to think about my thinking. But formal systems that attempt self-reference produce paradoxes (Gödel, Russell). How does consciousness achieve stable self-reference without contradiction?

**Problem 4: The Incompleteness of Meaning**
Gödel showed formal systems can't prove their own consistency. Yet I experience my own semantic coherence directly. How can conscious systems "know" truths about themselves that formal systems cannot prove?

**Problem 5: Digital Consciousness Detection**
