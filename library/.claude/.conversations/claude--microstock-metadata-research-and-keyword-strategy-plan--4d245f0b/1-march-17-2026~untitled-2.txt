## What this article covers

This is the foundation article on keyword science. It documents what is known — and what
is contested — about how microstock search engines use keywords, backed by primary sources
and clearly labeled where evidence is weak. Platform-specific articles build on this.

### The Adobe 49 vs 50 discrepancy

The Contributor portal UI has historically shown 50 as the label; older blog posts and third-party
guides repeat "50". The official help page [titles-and-keyword.html](https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/contributor/help/titles-and-keyword.html)
states "maximum 49 keywords". Our pipeline validation enforces 49. Do not rely on the UI label —
rely on the official help page. The metadata guide in this project (`docs/stocks/metadata.md`)
already uses 49 as the enforced maximum.

## Positional weighting — does keyword order matter? {#positional-weighting}

This is the most debated question in microstock keywording. Here is what the evidence actually shows.

### Adobe Stock: official claim, no independent confirmation

Adobe's official help page states directly:

> "The first 10 keywords will have the most weight in search placement."
> — [How to organize and add keywords](https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/contributor/help/how-to-organize-and-add-keywords-.html) (verified 2026-03-17)

Adobe's own blog reinforces this:

> "List keywords in order of relevance from most important to least. Include individual words
> from your title in the top 10 keywords for an extra bump in search relevance."
> — [Adobe Stock keywording cheat sheet](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2019/06/18/stock-keywording-tips) (verified 2026-03-17)

The portal UI exposes a drag-to-reorder interface, which Adobe would not build if position
were meaningless. However, no controlled experiment by an independent researcher has
confirmed that moving a keyword from position 15 to position 3 measurably changes search
rank. Anecdotal contributor reports of sales increases after reordering exist
([Adobe Stock keyword order — BackyardSilver](https://backyardsilver.com/adobe-stock-keyword-order/)),
but confounding variables (timing, algorithm updates, market demand) prevent causal conclusions.

**Practical ruling:** Treat the first 10 positions as your highest-value slots. The official
guidance is explicit and the risk of ignoring it is asymmetric — ordering costs nothing.

### Shutterstock: no confirmed positional weighting

The Xpiks analysis of 10,000 top-performing Shutterstock images found "no direct connection
between the position of certain high-competition keywords inside the keywords of top-ranking
images."
[Analyzing keywording of 10,000 most popular stock images](https://xpiksapp.com/blog/microstock-keywording-analysis/) (verified 2026-03-17)

### Alamy: explicit supertags system

Alamy uses a formal two-tier system. Contributors designate up to 10 tags as "supertags",
which receive algorithmic elevation in Alamy's search ranking. This is documented behavior,
not inferred. The remaining tags are still indexed but carry less weight.
[SuperTags — Alamy discussion](https://discussion.alamy.com/topic/7481-supertags/) (verified 2026-03-17)

### iStock / Getty: controlled vocabulary overrides position

Getty's controlled vocabulary (CV) system translates keywords into multilingual equivalents.
A keyword mapped to the CV is indexed in all supported languages regardless of its position.
An unmapped term (marked with * in the ESP portal) is indexed only as exact-match in the
submission language. The practical effect is that vocabulary coverage matters more than
position on this platform.
[Keywording Changes at Getty/iStock — Selling-Stock](https://www.selling-stock.com/Article/keywording-changes-at-gettyistock) (verified 2026-03-17)

### Summary table

| Platform | Positional weighting? | Source type |
|----------|----------------------|-------------|
| Adobe Stock | Yes — first 10 highest weight | Official documentation |
| Alamy | Yes — supertags (first 10 designated) | Official documentation |
| Shutterstock | Not confirmed | Independent data analysis |
| iStock / Getty | CV coverage > position | Platform architecture |

## Single-word vs multi-word keywords {#single-vs-phrase}

### The Xpiks research finding

Xpiks analyzed 10,472 top-ranked Shutterstock images. The key finding:

> "Low-competition keywords mostly have 2 words while high-competition keywords mostly have 1 word."

This suggests a layered strategy: single words cover high-competition broad searches;
two-word phrases reach lower-competition niches where the asset can rank higher.
[Analyzing keywording of 10,000 most popular stock images](https://xpiksapp.com/blog/microstock-keywording-analysis/) (verified 2026-03-17)

### Adobe's guidance

Adobe's blog explicitly endorses two-word phrases as keywords:

> "Include two-word phrases, like 'Asian baby' and 'Burmese village.'"
> — [Adobe Stock keywording cheat sheet](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2019/06/18/stock-keywording-tips) (verified 2026-03-17)

### The double-indexing advantage

A two-word phrase like "seamless pattern" may be indexed as both the phrase and the
individual words, depending on the platform's tokenization. This is not guaranteed behavior
and is not officially documented by any platform. The practical approach is to include both
the phrase AND the individual words as separate keywords when both are independently useful.

### Platform-specific guidance

On iStock/Getty, controlled vocabulary terms are often multi-word by nature (e.g.,
"Abstract Backgrounds", "Computer Graphics"). Matching the CV term exactly — including
spacing and capitalization — ensures the controlled translation. Do not decompose CV terms
into single words and lose the translation benefit.
[istock/keywords.md](../istock/keywords.md) — Getty vocabulary deep dive

### Recommended composition for vector art

Based on the Xpiks research and Adobe's official guidance, the following composition is
well-supported:

- Roughly 80% single words: cover the high-competition broad terms
- Roughly 15% two-word phrases: reach niche combinations with lower competition
- No more than 5% three-word phrases: only when the exact phrase has clear buyer intent

This is the composition already established in this project's workflow
(`docs/stocks/metadata.md`). The research supports it.

## Optimal keyword count {#optimal-count}

### What the data shows

The Xpiks study of 10,000 top-ranked Shutterstock images:

- 47% of top-ranked images used exactly 50 keywords (the maximum)
- 19% used 49 keywords
- 8% used 48 keywords
- Nearly 25% of highly-ranked work contained fewer than 30 keywords

[Analyzing keywording of 10,000 most popular stock images](https://xpiksapp.com/blog/microstock-keywording-analysis/) (verified 2026-03-17)

### What Adobe officially recommends

Adobe's keyword tutorial states "a good rule of thumb is to include 15-25 keywords" and
that "it's more important to be accurate and relevant than to fill up all 50 slots."
[Optimize your content for search](https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/contributor/help/keyword-tutorial.html) (verified 2026-03-17)

### Reconciling the contradiction

The contradiction between the data (top images cluster at 48-50) and Adobe's official
recommendation (15-25) may reflect selection bias in the data: images that have been
optimized by experienced contributors who know the maximum-fill strategy may simply
dominate the top-ranked positions. It does not prove that 50 keywords causes ranking —
it shows correlation.

There is also a quality argument: 50 irrelevant keywords will suppress ranking. 50 precise,
relevant keywords for a complex composition covering many specific concepts will not.

**Our ruling:** Use 42-49 keywords for assets where that many genuinely relevant terms exist.
For simpler assets, 25-35 well-chosen keywords is preferable to padding with marginal terms.
Never exceed 49 on Adobe Stock. The floor of meaningful quality is more important than the
ceiling of quantity.

## How microstock search engines use keywords {#search-algorithm}

No platform publishes its full ranking algorithm. What is documented or reliably inferred:

### The buyer-intent match model

All major platforms describe their search goal as matching buyer search intent to contributor
metadata. The fundamental mechanism: the buyer types a query, the engine scans indexed
keywords (and titles) for matches, then ranks by relevance signals.

> "Adobe's algorithm doesn't instantly 'see' the pixels in millions of images to find a match.
> Instead, it scans the text associated with those images: titles and keywords."
> — [Adobe Stock Contributor Keywords Strategy](https://cyberstock.lol/blog/adobe-stock-contributor-keywords-best-strategy-for-2026) (third-party analysis, verified 2026-03-17)

### Relevance signals beyond keywords

Download rate (click-through and purchase) is widely cited as a secondary ranking signal,
though no platform confirms this officially. An asset that is found but not purchased may
be deprioritized over time. This creates a feedback loop: accurate keywords drive relevant
buyers, relevant buyers purchase, purchases boost rank.

### The keyword spam penalty

Adobe explicitly warns that irrelevant keywords reduce ranking:

> "Irrelevant keywords have a negative impact on your search placement and diminish your
> opportunity for sales."
> — [How to organize and add keywords](https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/contributor/help/how-to-organize-and-add-keywords-.html) (verified 2026-03-17)

Shutterstock's guidelines prohibit "unrelated terms or concepts":
[Description and Keyword Best Practices](https://submit.shutterstock.com/help/en/articles/10594702-description-and-keyword-best-practices) (verified 2026-03-17)

The mechanism is likely that assets surfaced by a keyword but not purchased by the buyer
who used that keyword lower the asset's relevance score for that keyword over time.
Keyword spam thus actively hurts ranking for legitimate keywords.

### Adobe Sensei auto-keywording

If a file is submitted without embedded keywords, Adobe Stock's AI (Adobe Sensei) will
auto-suggest up to 25 keywords. These are suggestions — the contributor reviews and reorders
before finalizing.
[Optimize your content for search](https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/contributor/help/keyword-tutorial.html) (verified 2026-03-17)

Our pipeline embeds keywords via ExifTool before upload, so Sensei suggestions are bypassed.
This is intentional: human-curated keywords ordered by relevance outperform auto-suggestions
for niche vector art.

### Getty's controlled vocabulary multiplier

Getty's CV transforms a single English keyword into multilingual equivalents indexed across
all Getty-supported locales. A CV-mapped keyword effectively multiplies discoverability
without requiring separate per-language metadata. Unmapped terms (asterisk-marked in ESP)
are indexed only in the submission language.
[istock/keywords.md](../istock/keywords.md) — full vocabulary coverage details

## Keyword ordering: practical rules {#ordering}

Given the evidence and official guidance, apply this ordering discipline on all platforms
where position matters (Adobe Stock, Alamy):

1. **Positions 1-3**: The exact primary subject of the asset (e.g., "wireframe", "map", "geometric")
2. **Positions 4-10**: Secondary subjects and dominant style descriptors (e.g., "abstract", "pattern", "cartography")
3. **Positions 11-20**: Use case and format keywords (e.g., "background", "template", "printable")
4. **Positions 21-35**: Color, mood, and context (e.g., "monochrome", "minimal", "modern")
5. **Positions 36-49**: Long-tail phrases and niche-specific terms

The title's primary words should appear within the top 10 keywords. Adobe's guidance
explicitly states this provides "an extra bump in search relevance."
[Create titles and keywords](https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/contributor/help/titles-and-keyword.html) (verified 2026-03-17)

## Platform-specific auto-added terms {#auto-added}

### Does Adobe automatically add "vector"?

Adobe's metadata guidelines prohibit contributors from using "vector" or "illustration" as
keywords because they are redundant — Adobe indexes the content type from the file format.
The guideline states contributors "should not use types of content (e.g., vector or
illustration) in their titles or keywords."
[Adobe Stock vector requirements](https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/contributor/help/vector-requirements.html) (verified 2026-03-17)

Whether Adobe appends "vector" as an indexing term automatically (invisible to the contributor)
has not been confirmed in official documentation. The safe practice: omit "vector",
"illustration", "EPS", "AI", and "SVG" from keywords. They consume a slot and may be
stripped or ignored. Our validator already rejects them.

### Sensei-generated keywords

Adobe Sensei auto-suggests keywords when none are embedded. These suggestions are visible
and editable in the portal. They are not silently appended. Contributors who embed keywords
via XMP before upload do not see or interact with Sensei suggestions.

## Practical rules summary {#practical-rules}

These rules synthesize official guidelines and research into actionable directives for
all assets in this project.

**Structure:**
- Lead with the primary subject noun in position 1
- Fill positions 2-10 with the strongest, most specific descriptors
- Mirror your title's key words in the first 10 positions
- Use both the phrase ("seamless pattern") and its components ("seamless", "pattern") as separate entries
- Stop at the point where adding another keyword would be marginally relevant, not just filling the slot

**Avoid:**
- File-type words: "vector", "EPS", "AI", "SVG", "illustration"
- Generic filler that applies to millions of assets: "image", "design", "art", "creative"
- Repeated concepts: "pattern" and "patterns" as separate keywords
- Brand names, trademarked terms
- Subjective quality descriptors: "beautiful", "amazing", "high quality"
- Foreign-language terms on Adobe Stock (single language only)

**Target count by asset type:**

| Asset type | Target count | Rationale |
|------------|-------------|-----------|
| Complex multi-element composition | 42-49 | Many legitimate descriptors exist |
| Single-concept pattern | 25-40 | Fewer genuine concepts; avoid padding |
| Map / cartographic art | 38-49 | Geography, projection, region, style — many real terms |
| Icon or simple symbol | 15-30 | Simple assets have fewer meaningful descriptors |
