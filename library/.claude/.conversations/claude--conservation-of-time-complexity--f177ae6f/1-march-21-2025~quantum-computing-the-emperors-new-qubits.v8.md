Let's do some math that nobody in the quantum computing industry seems willing to do:

Step 1: Convert our quantum factorization progress to bits
   log₂(15) = 3.91 bits
   log₂(21) = 4.39 bits
   log₂(35) = 5.13 bits

Step 2: Calculate our annual progress rate
   (5.13 - 3.91) ÷ 21 years = 0.059 bits/year

Step 3: Determine time to reach modern encryption standards (3072 bits)
   (3072 - 5.13) ÷ 0.059 = 52,055 years

Step 4: Account for encryption standards growing at 4.06% annually
   By the time quantum computing catches up, encryption will be using numbers with ~10^800 digits

Here's the hilarious part: Post-quantum cryptography is being implemented between 2022-2030.

This means we're solving the quantum threat approximately 52,025 years before it becomes an actual problem!

And we still haven't created a single fault-tolerant qubit. Not one. As we add error correction, the computational advantage may vanish entirely due to the exponential noise scaling problem.
