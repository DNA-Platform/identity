**The 50,000-Year Quantum Computing Myth**

Billions of dollars are being poured into quantum computing with promises that it will "break all encryption" and "revolutionize computing." But what if the entire field is chasing a mathematical impossibility?

I ran the numbers, and the results are startling.

In 2001, quantum computers could factor 15.
In 2012, they reached 21.
In 2022, after another decade and countless billions: 35.

That's not a typo. Thirty-five.

Meanwhile, cracking modern encryption requires factoring numbers with 2048-4096 bits.

Here's the brutal math no one is talking about:

Current quantum progress rate = 0.059 bits/year
Time to break today's 3072-bit RSA = (3072 - 5.13) ÷ 0.059 = 52,055 years

Even worse, encryption standards grow at 4.06% annually, making the gap wider every year.

The fundamental problem? Quantum decoherence scales exponentially with qubit count. Each additional qubit makes the system exponentially more likely to produce errors, requiring exponentially more resources to maintain stability.

This isn't like the early days of classical computing. It's a fundamental physical barrier that may be impossible to overcome.

The field can't even produce a single fault-tolerant qubit, yet we're supposed to believe they'll soon have thousands?

Before investing in quantum startups or worrying about quantum hackers, do the math yourself.

Some bubbles take 50,000 years to pop.
