Shor's algorithm is a quantum algorithm for factorizing large numbers, which runs in polynomial time. RSA encryption is based on factorizing numbers into primes. Numbers are easy to multiply but hard to factorize, leading to the asymmetry that makes for good encryption. We know the numbers we multiplied but no one can figure out which ones we used. 

RSA requires 300+ digit numbers to be factorized. More modern protocols require 100+ digit numbers. 

In 2001, 15 was the largest number factorized by Shor on a quantum computer
In 2012 it was 21
In 2022 it was 35

I personally don't believe in the reality of quantum computing, following the reasoning of mathematician Richard Gil, who notes that noise increases exponentially with the number of qubits in a quantum system. So you get a magical polynomial time algorithm which destroys a certain type of encryption, but you need to solve an exponentially increasing noise problem - which basically means the more "transistors" (qubits) the computer has, you need something like exponentially colder temperature to keep them stable, or they simply return the wrong answer more and more and more frequently

To date, we have never created a fault tolerate quantum computer with even one qubit - meaning if it returns a zero or one, we don't know if it's accurate. There are fault tolerant quantum algorithms being developed, but they are at the expense of speed, so if the error rate goes up exponentially with qubits, we end up with exponentially slower and slower fault tolerant machines. 

We haven't made it to 1 yet and we need to get 3300 of them to crack RSA. And the technology needed to control the entropy of the quantum processor scales exponentially with the number of quits. We are 0% of the way there

And here's the lovely thing about quantum computing: if it were ever to become *practical* how would we know? The test is to run algorithms that classical computers can't practically, so we have no way of verifying the correctness of their output!!! So how can we know that the quantum computer is working? 

There are deep physical and philosophical problems with quantum computing. Quantum computers are vaporware in its purest form
