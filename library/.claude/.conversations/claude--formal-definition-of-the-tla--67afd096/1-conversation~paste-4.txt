Final Refined Proof
Theorem: Computational Equivalence of TLAs and Turing Machines
Transductive Logical Automatons are Turing-equivalent; that is, TLAs can compute exactly the class of Turing-computable functions.
Part 1: TLAs can simulate any Turing Machine (TM → TLA)

Output Representation: By the Representability Theorem (Kleene, 1952), for any Turing-computable function A, there exists a formula φ_A,x(y) in first-order arithmetic such that φ_A,x(y) is true if and only if y = A(x).
Systematic Output Construction: A TLA implements a binary search strategy over the output space by:

Beginning with the conjecture "∃y. φ_A,x(y)"
Recursively refining this through conjectures that constrain y (e.g., "first bit is 0")
For each refinement, proving whether it's consistent with φ_A,x
Continuing until a unique output is determined


Completeness: By the Completeness Theorem (Gödel, 1930), the TLA will eventually prove φ_A,x(A(x)), constructing the correct output through its refinement process.

Part 2: Any TLA computation can be implemented on a Turing Machine (TLA → TM)

Algorithmic Implementation: All core operations of a TLA (conjecture formation, proof checking, transcription, transduction) are algorithmically implementable, as established by the Decidability of Proof Checking (Kleene, 1952).
Recursive Structure: The recursive management of child TLAs follows standard patterns of computation that are implementable on a Turing machine, as guaranteed by the Recursion Theorem (Kleene, 1938).
Simulation: By the Church-Turing Thesis, any effective procedure can be implemented on a Turing machine. Since all aspects of TLA execution are effective procedures, a Turing machine can simulate any TLA computation.

Conclusion
Therefore, TLAs and Turing machines are computationally equivalent.
Remaining Considerations

Efficiency: While equivalence is established, the efficiency of simulation in either direction may vary significantly.
Non-terminating Computations: For non-terminating computations, the TLA will continue its refinement process indefinitely, mirroring the non-halting behavior of the corresponding Turing machine.
Practical Implementation: The systematic binary search strategy could be optimized based on domain-specific knowledge when implementing TLAs for specific applications.
