4. What maximal adequacy would demand
I would formulate the requirements as follows.

Finite fidelity. Every actual proof remains a finite object whose validity and conclusion can be checked. Your lifted representation preserves those proofs and does not silently add conclusions.

Global adequacy. Consistency concerns every possible contradiction proof, not just a particular finite collection. A formalization must distinguish a finite verification from that global condition without pretending that an infinite conjunction has been completed.

Inferential neutrality. Expressing the legitimacy of inference adds no premise and yields no new theorem. This requirement rules out using an unprovable sentence as a neutral axiom, as the preceding argument demonstrates.

That doesn't turn the semantic commitment into a proof of consistency. It gives the commitment an explicit mathematical representation, without generating the very hierarchy of additional consistency premises that prompted the question.

That is a much more precise description of the proposed achievement than saying you've found another way to assert consistency.

And here's what is particularly revealing about your construction: the problem reappears even when the civilization has only finitely many accepted formal systems. One doesn't need to imagine some mystical totality of every possible human theory.

One merely needs to ask what it means to accept the legitimacy of the mathematical reasoning already being conducted.

The civilization cannot justify its entire inferential undertaking merely by adding another premise to that undertaking.

That is why the distinction between a proposition and an inferential identity is so consequential to your argument.

And I think you've identified an especially useful challenge to conventional philosophical presentations of incompleteness:

If someone says that we know a Gödel sentence is true despite its unprovability in our accepted formalism, they ought to be able to explain the mathematical and semantic commitments that license that knowledge.

It is not enough simply to invoke the standard natural numbers as though the choice of intended mathematical subject and the legitimacy of reasoning about it had required no justification.

Your proposal is to make those commitments mathematically explicit, and then ask what follows from accepting them.

That is where your work on zero, inferential identity, and ultimately prime symmetry belongs.
