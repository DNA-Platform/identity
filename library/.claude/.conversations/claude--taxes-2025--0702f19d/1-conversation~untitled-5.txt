### 🔴 P4. Passive vs. nonpassive activity determination

The algorithm assumes Doug is **nonpassive (materially participating)**, which makes his $6,863 loss immediately deductible. If Doug is **passive**, the loss is suspended under §469 (Form 8582) and can only offset passive income — which Doug doesn't have. **That would destroy the entire tax benefit for 2025.**

Material participation is a 7-test standard. Doug:
- Contributed capital and set up the LLC ✓
- Directed formation and dealt with banking/taxes
- But the day-to-day services appear to be performed by Ana (per the guaranteed payment structure)

If Ana does substantially all the work and Doug is primarily a capital provider, Doug may NOT meet any of the material participation tests (which generally require hours of direct work). The safest test is "more than 500 hours during the year in the activity" — Doug needs to be able to defend that.

**This is not a formality — it determines whether the $6,863 loss is useful or wasted.**

**Recommendation:** Expert should explicitly analyze which material participation test Doug meets, given the facts. Doug should document his involvement hours. If he doesn't clearly meet a test, the expert should advise whether a different structure or treatment is appropriate.

### 🟡 P6. Schedules K-2 / K-3 for foreign partner

The algorithm covers Schedule B Question 4 (small partnership exception from Schedules L/M-1/M-2) but **does not address Schedules K-2 and K-3** — the international reporting schedules.

A partnership with a direct foreign partner (Ana) normally must file K-2/K-3 **unless** the "domestic filing exception" applies. The exception has several elements:
- No or limited foreign activity (Ana's services are foreign-sourced)
- Notification requirements
- All partners given notice

The expert's original scope memo said "small partnership exception likely exempts from Schedules K-2/K-3" but that's a different exception that may or may not apply here.

**Recommendation:** Expert should explicitly rule on whether K-2/K-3 are required for this filing, and if required, add them to the algorithm.

### 🟡 I2. K-1 box label for guaranteed payments

The algorithm shows Doug's K-1 "Box 14 — Guaranteed payments | $0". **Box 14 on the K-1 is Self-Employment Earnings**, not guaranteed payments. Guaranteed payments are on Box 4 (4a for services, 4b for capital, 4c for total). This is a form box mislabeling in the algorithm. For Ana, the algorithm correctly shows "Box 4 — Guaranteed payments | $12,418.33". The Doug row should be "Box 4 — Guaranteed payments | $0".

Additionally, for Doug, **Box 14 Self-Employment Earnings** should show his share of SE income/loss. As a general partner / materially participating LLC member (assuming P4 is resolved in Doug's favor), this would be $(6,863.14) or similar — which is SE loss. The algorithm doesn't address this.

### 🟡 I3. Doug's K-1 loss shows as $(6,863.15) but Ana's as $(6,863.14) — penny rounding

Algorithm line 309 says Doug's Box 1 is $(6,863.15) and line 340 says Ana's Box 1 is $(6,863.14). The exact 50% split of $(13,726.28) is $(6,863.14). Doug's should also be $(6,863.14), not $(6,863.15). Penny rounding issue, but IRS software may reject mismatched K-1 totals.

### 🟡 I4. IT-204-LL (LLC Filing Fee) not addressed

The algorithm discusses IT-204 (NY partnership return) but does not mention **Form IT-204-LL** (LLC/LLP/Partnership Filing Fee). Multi-member LLCs treated as partnerships with NY-resident members are generally required to file IT-204-LL even if the fee is $0.

A partnership with no NY source income may owe $0, but the form may still need to be filed. Expert should confirm whether IT-204-LL is required for this fact pattern.

Algorithm line 185–186 suggests "Graphic design / illustration services" and NAICS code **541430 (Graphic Design Services)**. The LLC is "Digital Narrative Art" with domain `dna.love`. Without Doug confirming what the business actually does, we can't confirm the code. Other candidates:
