## Why this matters

The original plan assumed the wage & income transcript would tell us whether a 1099-S existed. It can't, because the entire 2025 information return dataset for Doug is empty.

## Why we believe this is NOT a propagation delay

We researched IRS timing for 1099-S forms appearing in taxpayer accounts:

- Recipient copies due: **February 17, 2026**
- Electronic filing deadline to IRS: **March 31, 2026**
- IRS guidance: Information return documents are added "as the IRS receives them," weekly updates
- General expectation by mid-April 2026: 1099s filed by the March 31 deadline should be visible

Mid-April is **two weeks past** the e-file deadline. If the title company had filed a 1099-S, it should be on the page by now. The complete absence of any 2025 accordion (not just an empty one — no accordion at all) is consistent with no 2025 information returns having been filed for Doug, by anyone, for any reason.

## The most likely explanation: §121 principal residence exemption

Per IRS Form 1099-S instructions, **a title company is NOT required to file a 1099-S** when:

1. The sale price is ≤$250,000 (or ≤$500,000 if married filing jointly), AND
2. The seller provides a written certification, signed under penalties of perjury, that:
   - The property was the seller's principal residence
   - The full gain is excludable under IRC §121

If Doug signed such a certification at the December 2025 closing — which is **standard practice** for residential closings on a principal residence within the exclusion limits — the title company would not have filed a 1099-S, and the absence in the IRS system is exactly what we should expect to see.

This is consistent with:
- The expert's earlier observation that the gain "is close to or possibly at $250k before improvement adjustments"
- The clean, exclusion-eligible nature of the sale (NY primary residence, owned since 2019)
