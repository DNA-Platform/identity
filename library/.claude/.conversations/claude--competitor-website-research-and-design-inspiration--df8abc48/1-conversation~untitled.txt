Interactive Cartographers — Who is Selling Interactive Illustrated Maps?
Mission: Custom Map Providers
Character: Survey
Prior: AgentFire ClickMaps — What is their Business Model?
Started: 2026-02-20
Status: Active

Question
Who is selling interactive illustrated maps — maps that are both hand-crafted or artistically rendered AND clickable, navigable, or otherwise programmatically functional? Not one or the other; both.

Sub-questions held loosely:

Is "interactive illustrated map" a product category that anyone names, or a gap between two separate industries?
What companies exist whose primary business is producing maps that integrate illustration with interactivity?
What freelancers on Fiverr, Upwork, Etsy, and other platforms offer this as a combined service?
What domains buy these maps? Which domains have mature markets, which have no supply at all?
What does the quality spectrum look like, from template-generated icons to genuine hand-painted cartographic art?
Where in this landscape does AgentFire ClickMaps sit?
What is the relationship between the people who illustrate maps and the people who make maps interactive — and how often are they the same person?
Assumptions
The product may exist without a name. Providers may call their work "custom maps," "clickable maps," "illustrated maps," "hand-drawn maps," "interactive site plans," "resort maps," "wayfinding maps," or domain-specific terms. We cannot search for the product category by name because the category may not be named.

Illustration and interactivity are probably sold separately. The prior experiment on AgentFire suggested they occupy an unusual position. This survey tests whether that's typical or anomalous.

Providers span a wide range. From solo freelancers on Fiverr to specialized studios to features embedded within larger platforms (like AgentFire embedding maps into WordPress themes).

Domain matters. A ski resort trail map and a wedding venue map and a hospital wayfinding kiosk are all "maps" but the industries around them may be entirely different ecosystems with different providers, price points, and quality expectations.

The experimentalist has domain expertise. Ana operates a Fiverr Pro digital cartography business. The experimentalist ran her client communications and knows the platform, the pricing dynamics, and the client-side experience. This prior knowledge calibrates what "typical" looks like.

Method
What Claude does
Systematic web search across three axes:

Axis 1 — Provider type. Search for companies, studios, freelancers, and platforms whose business involves producing illustrated maps, interactive maps, or both. Search terms: "custom map company," "illustrated map studio," "interactive map design," "bespoke cartography," "clickable map freelancer."

Axis 2 — Client domain. Search for maps within every business context that plausibly buys them: hotels, resorts, ski areas, theme parks, universities, hospitals, wineries, breweries, agritourism, boat shows, weddings, real estate, festivals, corporate campuses, national parks, etc. The goal is to find who makes them, working backwards from who uses them.

Axis 3 — Platform. Search Fiverr, Upwork, Etsy, Behance for gigs and portfolios. Search both the illustration side ("illustrated map," "custom map illustration," "vector map") and the interactivity side ("interactive map," "clickable map," "Leaflet map," "Mapbox," "WebGL map").

For promising providers, Claude fetches their websites and extracts: services offered, pricing (where visible), client domains, technology used, whether illustration and interactivity are integrated or separate.

What the experimentalist does
Visual assessment of portfolios. Interactive behavior testing. Quality judgment that requires seeing rendered pages, not just reading HTML. Screenshots with annotations where Claude cannot perceive what matters.

What we built first (and should have)
A domain enumeration: a comprehensive list of every business context where maps are purchased. This list structures the search and makes coverage gaps visible. Without it, we searched what came to mind rather than what exists.

Observations
What was perceived, before interpretation. Provenance annotated per the framework.

II. Providers: Companies That Integrate Illustration and Interactivity
The following providers sell maps where illustration and interactivity come from the same vendor. This is the rarest configuration.

Travel Graphics International (TGI Maps) — tgimaps.com . 40+ years in operation. Produces illustrated 3D visitor maps for hotels and tourism. Their model: maintain base illustrated maps of destinations (Hawaii, Boston, Minneapolis, etc.), then customize for individual hotel properties with the hotel's branding, nearby restaurants, attractions. Revenue is ad-supported — local businesses pay to appear on the maps. Maps are delivered as print collateral AND online interactive versions AND a "Best Guest Guides" virtual concierge app. The Twin Cities market alone peaked at $250K+ gross revenue. Major hotel brand clients include Starwood, Hilton, and Marriott. Japanese-language versions produced for Hawaii properties serving Japanese tourists. (via Claude — website fetch and analysis)

North Pole Design — northpoledesign.com . Sole operator named Senan. Produces illustrated resort maps AND operates a proprietary "InterMaps" interactive platform. Primary domain: ski resorts (Steamboat, Wachusett Mountain, Belleayre). Also serves waterparks (H2OBX Waterpark), theme parks, and — notably — hospitals, where InterMaps provides turn-by-turn wayfinding directions, multilingual support, and voice-activated kiosk interfaces. This is one of the very few providers where the same person (or small team) creates the illustration AND builds the interactive layer. (via Claude — website fetch)

HipMaps — hipmaps.com . Wine/vineyard niche exclusively. Produces illustrated vineyard maps for print (tasting room display, marketing materials) AND an interactive app with location awareness, curated tasting notes, directions, and AVA/elevation/vine-age data. Their pitch: "100% customized to your content — vineyards, varietals, AVAs, elevation, vine age." The app layer transforms a static illustrated map into a functional field tool for winery visitors. (via Claude — website fetch)

CartoBlue — cartoblue.com . Resort maps, 3D renderings, virtual tours, drone videography. Serves hotels, resorts, golf courses. Interactive floor plans with clickable icons. Clients include Vail Resorts and St. Joe Club. Bridges illustration and interactivity, though the "illustration" leans toward 3D rendering rather than hand-drawn cartographic art. (via Claude — website fetch)

III. Providers: Illustration Studios (No Interactivity)
These providers produce beautiful illustrated maps — some at the highest level of cartographic art — but deliver static files. The client must find someone else to make them interactive.

Kickstarter book became the highest-supported Art-Illustration project ever (5,000+ backers). Retains the original paintings; licenses digital scans to resorts. $3,500–$15,000 per map, several weeks of work. No interactive component whatsoever. The paintings are art objects. (via Claude — website fetch and multiple articles)

Digital since 1994. 40+ US ski resorts, 10 in Japan, 1 New Zealand, 1 Italy. 70+ amusement parks and zoos (Hersheypark, Columbus Zoo, Houston Zoo, Louisville Zoo, Roger Williams Zoo). The vector format is explicitly more interactivity-ready than Niehues's hand-painted gouache, though Milliken himself does not build the interactive layer. (via Claude — website fetch and interview articles)

XI. Technology Stacks Observed
Illustration tools: Adobe Illustrator (dominant), Photoshop, SketchUp Pro (for 3D source models), Procreate (iPad). InDesign for print layout. Vector output (AI, SVG, PDF) is the professional standard.

Interactive map libraries used by Fiverr freelancers: Leaflet.js (most common — open source, lightweight), Mapbox GL JS (popular, more features, paid at scale), OpenLayers (less common), Google Maps API (used but less customizable), D3.js (for data-driven visualization), Deck.gl (for large datasets).

3D/immersive: Three.js and WebGL (for 3D interactive environments), Spline (3D design tool), Babylon.js. React Three Fiber (R3F) for React integration. GSAP for animation.

Platforms/CMS: WordPress with MapSVG plugin, Webflow, Wix Studio, Framer. Embedding via iframe or JavaScript snippet.

Data connections: Google Sheets (real-time updates without code changes — offered by Geocadder, Newtonnjeri, Wiktorian), CSV/Excel import, GeoJSON, custom APIs, PostGIS/PostgreSQL for serious GIS work.

Findings
What was judged. Epistemic annotations per the framework.

1. "Interactive illustrated map" is not a product category. (fact)
No provider uses this phrase. No platform has this as a category. No search engine returns it as a meaningful cluster. It exists as a description of an outcome that a client might want, but the market is not organized around producing it. The market is organized around illustration on one side and interactivity on the other, with a structural gap between them.

This is the single most important finding of the survey. Everything else follows from it.

2. The illustration/interactivity split is structural, not accidental. (inferred)
The split manifests consistently across every domain observed: ski resorts (Niehues paints, propMAPS adds clicks), campuses (Rabinky illustrates, CampusTours adds interactivity), weddings (Etsy artists illustrate, nobody adds interactivity at all). On Fiverr, the split is empirically visible: interactivity freelancers ($10–$600) convert existing images into clickable HTML/SVG; illustration freelancers ($20–$250+) draw maps as static files. Of approximately 25+ map-related Fiverr gigs examined, only 2 explicitly combine illustration and interactivity in a single offering (Hamja for RV parks, Neonfable for fantasy/RPG maps).

The split is structural because illustration and interactivity require different skills (artistic talent vs. programming), different tools (Illustrator vs. JavaScript), different deliverables (vector files vs. deployed web applications), and are sold through different professional identities. The incentive to integrate is weak because each side has its own market that pays without requiring the other skill.

3. Integrated providers are rare and domain-locked. (inferred)
Only four companies were found that genuinely integrate illustration and interactivity as a unified product: TGI Maps (hotels/tourism, 40+ years), North Pole Design (ski resorts/hospitals, sole operator), HipMaps (wine/vineyards), and AgentFire ClickMaps (real estate, bundled into WordPress theme). Each serves a single vertical. None competes with any other. None markets to "anyone who wants an interactive illustrated map" — each markets to a specific industry.

This suggests that integration is economically viable only when a provider knows a domain deeply enough to build a reusable framework (TGI's base destination maps, NPD's InterMaps platform, HipMaps' vineyard app template, AgentFire's WordPress plugin) that amortizes the development cost across many clients within the domain.

4. The Fiverr interactive map market is deeper and more capable than it first appears. (fact)
The range runs from $10 (Cameronnimmo471, basic Leaflet) to $600 (Zladron, 3D WebGL with hotspots). The mid-tier ($40–$75) contains genuinely capable developers — Geocadder (Fiverr Pro, Google Sheets integration), Wiktorian (cartographer-turned-developer, multi-format output), Gilge_it (Fiverr Pro). These are not amateurs producing toy products. They are software developers who specialize in spatial web applications.

Adjacent to the map-specific freelancers, the Three.js/WebGL/R3F category on Fiverr contains dozens of 3D web developers ($80–$500) whose technical stack could produce interactive illustrated maps at a quality level exceeding any current map-specific provider — if someone gave them the artwork and the map-specific design direction.

5. Illustration quality and interactivity capability are inversely correlated across the market. (inferred)
The highest-quality illustrated maps (Niehues, Milliken, Molly O'Halloran, John Potter) have no interactive component. The most capable interactive platforms (Concept3D, Mappedin, Attractions.io ) use standardized 3D renders or data-driven tiles, not hand-crafted illustration. The providers who integrate both (TGI, NPD) produce competent but not exceptional illustration. This pattern holds across every domain observed.

The reason appears to be economic: extraordinary illustration requires extraordinary time and talent, and the market rewards that talent through print/display products where the full visual quality is preserved. Interactivity, by contrast, rewards speed, scalability, and real-time data — qualities that pull in the opposite direction from patient hand-crafted illustration.

6. Several large domains have no illustrated interactive map supply at all. (fact)
Wedding maps: huge Etsy market, entirely static. Brewery trails: illustrated art prints OR functional GIS maps, never both. Music festivals: in-house or agency-produced, no specialist providers found. Agritourism: one specialist (Bot Roda) for illustration, no interactive component. Aquariums, botanical gardens, casinos, cruise ships, senior living communities, churches — no dedicated illustrated map provider found for any of these.

7. The platform layer captures recurring revenue; illustration captures one-time fees. (inferred)
Concept3D, CampusTours, Mapme, propMAPS, and Mapiddiction all operate on SaaS subscription models. A campus or resort pays annually for interactive map hosting, updates, and features. The illustrator who drew the base map got paid once. This economic structure explains why the platform side is well-capitalized (Concept3D serving Penn State system-wide) while even the best illustrators (Niehues, Milliken) operate as sole practitioners or tiny studios.

8. Vector illustration is the bridge technology. (hypothesis)
Milliken's VistaMap ("the paint never dries") and Rad Smith's hybrid approach (hand-painted backgrounds + GIS/vector trails) suggest that fully vector illustration — where every element is independently selectable and editable — is what makes illustration interactivity-ready. Niehues's hand-painted gouache is magnificent but functionally a flat image. Smith explicitly designs vector overlays "to enable interactive maps to zoom in and maintain crisp resolution." SVG-native illustration (which is what AI tools like Claude naturally produce) may be better positioned for the interactive bridge than traditional hand-painted or raster-based illustration.

9. Domain-specialist companies thrive where generic providers cannot compete. (inferred)
Pelland Advertising (campground site maps), Bot Roda (agritourism/corn maze maps), HipMaps (vineyard maps), propMAPS (ski resort interactive overlays), Farm Web Design (agritourism websites) — each has built a business serving a single domain. Their advantage is not artistic superiority or technical innovation. It is domain knowledge: understanding what a campground owner needs, what a winery visitor wants, what ski patrol requires in a real-time trail status interface. The domain knowledge makes the sales conversation efficient and the deliverable fit-for-purpose on the first attempt.

10. The price spectrum is enormous and domain-dependent. (fact)
Fiverr interactive map development: $10–$600. Fiverr illustrated map illustration: $20–$250+. Ski trail map painting: $3,500–$15,000. Concept3D campus platform: enterprise pricing (likely $10K–$50K+ annually). TGI Maps hotel program: ad-supported, likely $5K–$25K+ per property program. Etsy wedding maps: $100–$250. The same abstract product — "a map" — spans three orders of magnitude in price depending on domain, quality, and whether interactivity is included.
