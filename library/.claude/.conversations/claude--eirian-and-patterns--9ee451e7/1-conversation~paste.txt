✅ What Are Seamless Patterns — Structurally and Semantically?
A seamless pattern is one that tiles without visible edges or breaks, enabling infinite repetition across any surface. But different types of seamless patterns serve different purposes. Here’s what designers really talk about:

🔹 1. Tiling Logic (Structure)
These are the underlying grid mechanics:

Pattern Type	Description
Full Drop (Square/Block Repeat)	Simple XY tiling, all edges align perfectly
Half Drop	Offset every other row, often used in wallpaper and textiles
Brick Repeat	Horizontal offset (like a brick wall), adds dynamism
Mirror Repeat	Elements mirrored on edges to create symmetry
Tossed/Scattered	Random-looking, but still tiles — feels organic and natural
Stripe/Linear	Repeats on only one axis, often vertical or horizontal
Rotated Tile	One tile repeated and rotated, for kaleidoscopic effects

🔹 2. Motif Scale Hierarchy
Expert designers (like Cat Coq and Bonnie Christine) emphasize the importance of three motif types in a successful pattern:

Hero elements – large, eye-catching shapes

Supporting elements – medium-sized, visual bridges

Filler elements – tiny dots, stars, swirls, textures that balance empty space

The balance between these creates visual rhythm, which prevents repetition from feeling too mechanical.

🔹 3. Semantic Pattern Categories

Floral & Botanical	Home décor, fashion, stationery	Tossed or stripe, detailed or minimal
