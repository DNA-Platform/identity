# Initial Data Exploration
![](figures/cover-psychedelic.jpg)

**Matched two-photon V1 recordings before and after DOI — a first descriptive pass**
*Animal 33328 · session 6 (baseline), session 7 (DOI) · 2026-06-23*

---

## What this exploration is for

These are the matched pre/post-DOI recordings of mouse V1 — same stimuli, repeated test images,
cells tracked across both sessions. The data are sound; the open question is how to analyze them.
An initial pass has three jobs: confirm the data supports the comparisons we plan, characterize the
pre/post differences at face value, and find the confounds that decide whether a naive contrast is
interpretable at all. The analyses build from a single image to the population to the same cells,
then close on the behavioural confound that governs every reading upstream of it. The product is a
decision about how DOI must be analyzed — not a claim that it does anything.

## What the design provides

![The two recordings](figures/00_recordings.png)
![Example stimuli](figures/01_stimuli.png)

Two preconditions for what follows. Both sessions carry the repeated test tier — ~100 images shown
~10× each — the only lever for a per-cell noise ceiling; and the sessions are balanced (1,654 cells
/ 5,936 trials baseline; 1,449 / 5,996 under DOI) over the same stimuli, so same-image and
cross-session comparisons are licensed. These confirm the analyses are well-posed; they are not
results.

## A single image, on the cortical surface

![One image, two states](figures/02_single_image.png)

What does one stimulus do to the population, and where? For an image shown in both sessions, each
neuron is drawn at its cortical position and shaded by its response on that trial, on a shared
scale.[^single] The code is sparse: one image drives a scattered minority of cells, with no spatial
clustering or artifact. This fixes the regime — mean rates ride on a few high-responders,
single-image contrasts are underpowered — so the analysis moves to distributions over all cells and
trials, taking with it a first qualitative look at whether the same image recruits cells differently
under DOI.

## Behavioural state, per session

![Behavioural state differs](figures/03_behavior.png)

V1 is strongly modulated by arousal and locomotion, so each session's behavioural state must be
characterized before any neural comparison is trusted. The three covariates — pupil size, its
derivative, running speed[^behchannels] — are shown as per-session distributions. They differ, most
visibly in running: near-stationary at baseline, frequently locomoting under DOI. This puts the
confound on the table at the start, and motivates the final analysis — how much of the pre/post
distinction is nothing but this.

## Per-cell reliability (the noise ceiling)

![Per-cell reliability](figures/04_reliability.png)

Reliability caps how much of a cell's response any model could explain, and is the natural basis for
selecting analyzable cells. Per session it is the split-half correlation over the repeated images:
split each image's repeats in two, average within each half, and correlate the two per-cell
half-means across images.[^splithalf] The ceiling is usable — a broad spread to ~0.9, baseline
median near 0.5 — and it drops under DOI (median 0.51 → 0.38). That is both a data-quality fact (DOI
fits will look worse for non-mechanistic reasons) and a candidate effect — one already shadowed by
the state difference above, since a more variable state inflates apparent noise on its own.

## Population rate structure

![Population code](figures/05_population.png)

The gross population statistics — sparsity, dynamic range, any global shift in scale — inform
normalization and tell whether a difference is global or cell-specific. The panels show each
neuron's trial-averaged mean, sorted, and the density of all response values.[^units] The code is
sharply long-tailed in both states, and the baseline rate profile sits above DOI across the whole
rank range: the reduction is broad, not confined to a subset. That points the matched-cell analysis
at one question — does the shift survive with cell identity held fixed?

## Population co-activity

![Population raster](figures/06_population_raster.png)

Trial-averaging discards the structure that justifies population methods, so it is worth checking
for co-active ensembles and population-wide events directly. Neurons are ordered by a Rastermap
embedding and shown as a grayscale neuron × trial raster.[^raster] Both sessions show clear
structure — horizontal bands of co-active cells, vertical streaks where one trial drives much of the
population — so the activity is far from independent and population-geometry methods are warranted.
A qualitative check by construction: the orderings are per session and not comparable row-for-row.

## The same neurons, before and after

![The same cells](figures/07_matched_cells.png)

The cleanest contrast removes cell identity as a variable, using only cells located in both
sessions. For each of 749 matched pairs, the baseline mean response is plotted against the same
cell's mean under DOI, the diagonal marking no change.[^matching] The population rate drop is not an
artifact of comparing different cells: it holds cell by cell, the cloud lying below the diagonal.
This is the strongest face-value statement the pass can make — and the one the final panel forces us
to qualify.

## Decoding the session from behaviour alone

![State from behaviour alone](figures/08_drug_state.png)

Every difference so far is a candidate DOI effect; this measures the alternative. A random forest
predicts the session from the behavioural features alone — no neural data — scored by
cross-validation.[^classifier] It reaches 97%, on pupil position and running speed. The two sessions
are nearly separable on behaviour, so none of the neural differences can be assigned to the drug
rather than to the animal being in a different state during the DOI session.

---

## What this exploration achieves

Three things, for whoever chooses the analysis:

- **The data is usable and its regime known** — sparse code, long-tailed rates, structured
  co-activity, a workable noise ceiling, 749 matched cells.
- **The differences are characterized and consistent** — reliability down, rates down globally, the
  same cells responding less.
- **The binding constraint is identified** — behaviour alone separates the sessions at 97%, so the
  difference is entangled with arousal and locomotion and cannot, here, be read as a drug effect.

A credible comparison must therefore either condition on behavioural state — matching or regressing
on pupil and running — or move to a stimulus-and-behaviour encoding model that separates the
stimulus-driven response from the state-driven one. The pass finds no DOI effect; it shows the data
is sound, what the naive differences are, and what must be controlled before they can mean anything.

[^single]: A test image present in both sessions (median of the shared ids), one presentation each;
cells at their `cell_motor_coordinates` (x, y); shared colour scale, `vmax` at the 99th percentile
across both trials, white = 0. The cell sets are independently segmented, so each panel is its own
field — a within-session view, not a cell-by-cell overlay.

[^behchannels]: Channel order was verified against the extract, not assumed: channel 0 strictly
positive (pupil size), 1 symmetric about zero (its derivative), 2 non-negative with a stationary
mode (running speed). Pupil-centre (x, y) is stored separately and enters only the classifier.

[^splithalf]: One random split per image (odd repeats dropped); the Pearson correlation of the two
half-mean vectors across images. A single-split, uncorrected estimate — conservative and relative,
adequate for comparing sessions, not a calibrated absolute ceiling.

[^units]: Responses are deconvolved activity in arbitrary units (not ΔF/F); the distribution is
decimated for plotting only. No normalization is applied — the global scale difference is part of
what the panel reports.

[^raster]: Rastermap orders neurons on a 1-D embedding (Stringer & Pachitariu, 2024), computed
independently per session, so row position is not comparable between panels; trials subsampled to
400 columns, display clipped at the 99th percentile.

[^matching]: Reciprocal nearest neighbour in the common functional-stack frame, mutually-nearest
pairs ≤ 10 µm — 749 pairs, median 2.68 µm. The match is geometric, not yet functionally validated.
Per-cell values are means over all trials.

[^classifier]: Random forest, 200 trees, predicting session from the five behavioural features
(pupil size, derivative, running speed, pupil x, y) per trial, pooled; accuracy the mean of 5-fold
stratified cross-validation, bars the Gini importances; no neural data. Per-trial decoding ignores
trial-to-trial autocorrelation, which inflates accuracy somewhat — not enough to change the
conclusion.
