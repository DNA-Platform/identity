MALPRACTICE & LIABILITY CONCERNS FOR US THERAPIST PROVIDING COURT DOCUMENT IN RUSSIAN CUSTODY CASE

1. JURISDICTIONAL LIABILITY: CAN RUSSIA SUE A US THERAPIST?
Short answer: Highly unlikely to succeed, but not impossible to attempt.
Russian courts can claim jurisdiction over foreign professionals only if:
The professional has assets located in Russia, OR
The professional is "directing activities" at Russian consumers (e.g., Russian-language website with prices in rubles)
Source: https://academic.oup.com/grurint/article-abstract/71/11/1118/6614250
However, because Ana is physically located in Russia (Leningrad region), Russian law technically applies to where the client receives services. This creates a gray area.
Source: https://www.katlove.com/can-an-american-therapist-do-therapy-with-american-expats-living-abroad-or-citizens-of-other-countries
Critical point: Even if a Russian court issued a judgment against a US-based therapist, US courts would almost certainly not enforce it. There is no treaty between the US and Russia for mutual enforcement of civil judgments.

2. US MALPRACTICE INSURANCE COVERAGE
Most US malpractice policies cover work if a client sues in a US jurisdiction. They typically do NOT cover if a client sues from a foreign jurisdiction.
Source: https://ethicsdemystified.com/can-i-provide-telemental-health-in-other-countries/
Action item: The therapist should contact her malpractice carrier directly and confirm whether international telehealth practice is covered, and specifically whether submitting a document to a foreign court is covered.

3. THE "COACHING" VS. "CLINICAL" CAPACITY DISTINCTION
When she mentioned submitting in a "coaching capacity," she is likely referring to the distinction between:
FACT WITNESS (Treating Therapist):
Can testify about what the client reported
Can describe clinical observations from sessions
Can state diagnosis and treatment provided
Can describe client's response to treatment
CANNOT offer forensic opinions about custody, parenting fitness, or the other party
EXPERT WITNESS (Forensic Evaluator):
Hired specifically to evaluate and opine on legal questions
Can offer opinions on custody, fitness, etc.
Requires comprehensive assessment of ALL parties
Source: https://memphisdivorce.com/dual-roles-in-custody-litigation-ethical-conflicts-with-therapeutic-and-forensic-roles/
Source: https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/progress-notes/202011/9-tips-for-psychotherapists-providing-court-testimony
The ethical problem: A treating therapist CANNOT ethically serve as both therapist AND forensic expert in the same case. This creates a "dual role" conflict.
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7683260/

4. WHAT A TREATING THERAPIST CAN AND CANNOT SAY
CAN SAY (as fact witness):
Patient's history as reported by the patient directly, without interpretation
Clinical diagnosis following study of patient's symptoms, signs, and findings
Care and type of treatment provided
Patient's response or reaction to care and treatment
Dates and duration of treatment
Clinical observations made during sessions
Source: https://memphisdivorce.com/dual-roles-in-custody-litigation-ethical-conflicts-with-therapeutic-and-forensic-roles/
CANNOT SAY (without becoming expert witness):
Opinions about the other parent's fitness
Recommendations about custody or placement
Conclusions about what happened outside of sessions (can only report what client reported)
Forensic conclusions about the "best interests of the child"
Clinical speculation about what may have happened or could happen in the future
Source: https://www.myfamilylawyer.net/blog/2014/03/tips-for-testifying-lesson-5-lay-testimony-expert-testimony-and-the-mental-health-professional/
Key guidance from Ohio Administrative Code (representative of US standards): "Licensees asked by a judge to make a recommendation in a custody, visitation and/or guardianship hearing, not hired by the court to do so, shall cite their role as a client's therapist and note that any testimony would be an ethics violation of their license and they can only testify to facts that they know."
Source: https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code/rule-4757-6-01

5. ALLEGATIONS ABOUT THE DEFENDANT (MAXIM)
This is where the therapist's concern is most valid.
The issue: Ana confided allegations of rape and violence. The therapist can report that Ana disclosed these allegations. The therapist CANNOT assert that these events occurred as fact, because she did not witness them and has not evaluated the accused.
Defamation risk: In the US, statements made in judicial proceedings generally receive "absolute privilege" or "qualified privilege" - meaning they cannot be the basis for a defamation lawsuit, even if false.
Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/absolute_privilege
Source: https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/privileges-defenses-defamation-cases.html
However: This privilege typically applies to parties, witnesses, and attorneys in the proceeding. Whether it extends to a US therapist submitting a document to a Russian court is legally untested territory.
Safe language approach:
"Client reported that..." ✓
"Client disclosed experiencing..." ✓
"Client presented with symptoms consistent with trauma, including..." ✓
"[Defendant] raped [client]" ✗ (asserts fact therapist did not witness)
"[Defendant] is violent" ✗ (asserts fact about person therapist has not evaluated)
Source: https://www.psychiatrictimes.com/view/legal-duty-therapists-third-parties

6. NASW CODE OF ETHICS CONSIDERATIONS
The NASW Code of Ethics (which governs LCSWs) states:
Social workers should comply with laws of both the jurisdiction where the client is located AND where the social worker is located
All ethical standards apply to interactions via technology
Social workers should base conclusions on appropriate data and avoid inaccuracies
Social workers' primary responsibility is to promote the well-being of clients
Source: https://www.socialworkers.org/About/Ethics/Code-of-Ethics/Code-of-Ethics-English
Source: https://socialwork.sdsu.edu/~resources/files/nasw-code-of-ethics2017.pdf

7. RECOMMENDED APPROACH FOR THE DOCUMENT
The therapist should frame the document as a clinical treatment summary rather than a forensic evaluation. Structure:
Therapist credentials and relationship to client
Period of treatment
Reason client sought treatment / presenting concerns
Client's reported history (using "client reported..." language)
Clinical observations and diagnosis
Treatment provided
Client's progress and current clinical status
Avoid:
Conclusions about the defendant's character or fitness
Recommendations about custody
Assertions of fact about events the therapist did not witness
Forensic opinions
