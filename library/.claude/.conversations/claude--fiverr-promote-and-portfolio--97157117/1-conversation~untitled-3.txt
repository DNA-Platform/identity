Is it worth selling on Amazon especially with their high fees?
Profile photo for Daniel Ford
Daniel Ford
15 + years online, including eBay and AmazonAuthor has 222 answers and 1.2M answer views6y
“Is it worth selling on Amazon especially with their high fees?”

Selling fees is just another cost of doing business. The real question is not whether you should consider selling on Amazon, but whether the products you wish to sell are well-suited to Amazon.

Here are some points to consider:

(1) Selling on Amazon is far less costly than opening a retail store.

First of all, if you’ve ever considered the costs associated with opening a physical store, Amazon fees are cheap! A typical retail store may cost $100,000 or more before your very first sale! Whereas one can probably get started on Amazon for less than a $1000. That’s a bargain.

(2) Selling on Amazon is low-risk (if you follow their rules.)

With a start-up cost of less than $1000 dollars, your financial risk is very low compared to other business models. Odds are that even if your items don’t sell at a profit when all costs are taken into account, then your actual losses may only be a $100 or so. That’s peanuts compared to say, the costs of opening and then closing a brick and mortar store.

(3) Your selling costs on Amazon can be “turned off” at any time.

If your selling fees on a $100 item are $25, you have the option of discontinuing selling on Amazon at any time or relisting your inventory in another online marketplace. That can’t be said for conventional stores where you may be locked into a 12 or 24 month lease.

(4) Selling on Amazon gives you flexibility.

If an item doesn’t sell well or profitably on Amazon, you can move into other products with very little effort, save for perhaps qualifying to sell in different Amazon categories. This is not easily done with a conventional store where the branding, marketing, or even the location make it difficult to shift your product focus. Just note, if you’re new to online selling, don’t fall for the temptation to order a truckload of inventory you’re unsure will sell. Start small, list a few test products, find your niche, then scale-up.

(5) Not all products are suitable for Amazon.

While Amazon is a great marketplace, not all products are suited for Amazon. For example, low-cost items that are bulky or heavy have very high shipping and handling costs. For example, an ordinary $10 case of water may have selling and shipping fees of $30 dollars or more on Amazon. Likewise, a case of paper towels, although light, are bulky and therefore your handling fees will be high.

Suggestions:

(1) Start selling on Ebay before Amazon.

Amazon is a great place to sell your products. But it’s highly regimented and mistakes can be costly up to an including losing your selling privileges. Whereas Ebay is far less regimented and each listing you create is your own and not shared by dozens of other sellers. [1]

Likewise, Ebay is a better platform for selling used, unique, and unbranded products. This is especially important if you’re looking to start off with minimal expense using yard sales and second-hand stores as potential sources of inventory.

(2) Consider Affiliate Sales.

Affiliate sales, whereby you promote others products for a commission, is another low-risk way to ease into online selling. Amazon’s own affiliate program is one of the largest and easiest to join.[2]

Other than perhaps some software and a time investment, there’s no risk and the skills you learn in affiliate marketing will serve you well if you opt to transition to physical products.

Hope you find this information helpful. Good luck in your online selling!

[2] The web's most popular and successful Affiliate Program

Would you recommend selling a product before it is ready? If so, how?
Profile photo for Todd Shimkus
Todd Shimkus
Former Web/Ecommerce Director at Tahoe Mountain Sports (2009–2017)Author has 156 answers and 260.7K answer views8y
Consumers are not likely to pre-order a product unless it already has some buzz and demand (e.g., the next iPhone, Tesla solar roofing, etc.).

If it’s something innovative and unique you might get enough traction and early interest to prompt pre-sales. It would also help if your company already has a strong reputation.

Stories of crowd-funding campaigns that fail to deliver have made people reluctant to back products and companies that are entering the market with no track record.

That said, if you can manage to get people excited about your product before you go to market they will be more likely to invest before delivery. Incentivizing early adoption with a discount or other perks can help. Be sure to keep those pre-order customers up to date on the status of the product and expected delivery date so they don’t lose faith and pull out.
