***Doug's phrase, 2026-09-13:*** **"I would call it conversation as a folder."** *A design worked out in Doug's Library and meant to be handed to the IXP team as a plan. Nothing here is built; every claim about the framework below was read at the line it is cited to, in `@dna-platform/public` as it stands today.*

**The premise.** A conversation is a **book**, and a book is a **folder** — `.book.tsx` beside one file per chapter, which is what every demo in `.wiki` and `.latex` already is. A transcript is the book's reading; a dialogue is a **section** whose inner parts are **exchanges**; and because an exchange is where the writing happens, everything Claude can put in a message must land on kinds the framework already has. The dialogue is not a new document type. It is a kind of section, so it can stand in an article, in an encyclopedia page, or in a book of its own, and be dressed by whichever theme is above it.

**What the design has to survive.** Doug named four demands in one breath: *the dialogue might exist in any document* · *exchanges are its inner sections* · *we support recursion* · *all of the formatting Claude provides*, with a document viewer on the right when a thing is clicked. Three of the four fall out of the framework as built. **One does not, and the chapter below says exactly which, at the line that refuses it** — because the standing rule in this codebase is that a base is never designed from the subclasses in front of it, and the fix is judged as a wart before it is made.

## Chapters

1. [The Model](01-the-model.md) — what a dialogue, an exchange and a turn ARE: the folder, the composition hierarchy, and the admission rule read at [`Composition.parts()`](../../package/src/writing/Composition.tsx). **A composition admits its own kind and everything narrower — so inheritance NARROWS what a kind will hold**, and layered sections are safe because `$Section` is the base everything section-grade descends from. Dialogue, Exchange and Turn as three nesting sections; the participant and the topic as Doug ruled them; and **three proposals withdrawn**, including one that asked `.public` for a seam it did not need.

2. [The Format and the Theme](02-the-format-and-the-theme.md) — how a dialogue DRAWS, and why the same dialogue works in Wikipedia dress and in the paper's. Themes per type, formats per instance; the dialogue theme holds RULES and reads VALUES from the theme above it, which is the whole trick. The rail that already exists in the encyclopedia's three-track grid, and the document viewer that goes in it.

3. [What Claude Writes](03-what-claude-writes.md) — the inventory: every element a Claude conversation can contain, mapped to the kind that already draws it, the four kinds that do not exist yet, and the two features already in the framework that were built for something else and fit this exactly.

4. [The Types](04-the-types.md) — **level types against annotative ones**, the distinction `$Writing.kind` already makes and the whole design runs on: a type declared `extends $Type` says what a writing IS without touching what it HOLDS. The three tiers written out in the type–class–interface pattern, what each specification validates, and Doug's question answered per kind — *what needs a class, and what is only a type.* **Two of six are classes.**
5. [Document Only](05-document-only.md) — SUPERSEDES chapter 1's grades, and carries the design as Doug settled it on 2026-09-16: Conversation is the FOLDER and one day the book's name, a DIALOGUE is the DOCUMENT, an EXCHANGE is a SECTION and is the full thing one person says. Exchange at section grade is what makes the speaker question disappear — a message is one exchange however long it runs, so its participant is named once rather than once per paragraph, and sections take sections so a quoted block inside a message costs nothing. Dialogue at document grade is what makes it placeable: it stands wherever a document stands, including inside another dialogue. The role each plays, which is the question he asked: the conversation knows the cast and is LAYOUT — Book-is-layout one rung down, and how Claude's own chat answers the speaker question, by placing messages rather than labelling them; a dialogue knows what it is about and is what SEARCH returns, since a document is the smallest thing with a name worth handing back; an exchange is somebody's words. The contextual mechanism he reached for is NOT owed — reflection.nearest is the walk up the holders and book(), chapter() and holding() are three one-line uses of it — and the give side is supplies. The cast is declared once and named by key thereafter, the shape Citation and Entry already have, needing the per-document scratchpad he raised in Sprint 59. AND THE POLY THEME WORLD COSTS ONE LINE: scoped registration, nesting context and re-dressing are all built and promised, so a document-level theme is Book.view's two lines moved to the base — but a Theme answers its own theme as ITSELF, so a nested theme cannot read the values of the theme it stands inside, which is the whole trick the encyclopedia already uses and the entire reason to want one. Ends with what the design walks into rather than causes: a sibling section survives only because the shadowed above keeps it, and loose inline writing eats what follows it.
