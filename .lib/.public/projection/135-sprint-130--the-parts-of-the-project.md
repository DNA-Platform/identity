## <a id="spec"></a>The sprint's specification — draft, said back for his corrections

1. The sprint as content
   - 1.1 The sprint is a small project with a specification that evolves; this record carries it, amended in place, never replaced.
   - 1.2 The sprint is a project and is content — his word, fourth round — so its two sides are found by rendering it in the Content format: what belongs on its front and what on its back is answered by the rendering. *(Open: what stands on the front of a sprint.)*
2. The ground
   - 2.1 The working ground is the textbook's first chapter, The Symmetry of Arithmetic — refining it is the sprint's deliverable.
   - 2.2 He writes the other documents with Claude Desktop; the room transcribes them in verbatim through the content's machinery.
   - 2.3 The deliverable at this sprint's close, specified on his challenge that the sprint specify its own work: the parts' forms identified with worked examples in this record; the sketch The Project's Page standing in The Design with its photographs; the content dress amended to the hierarchy ruling; the ratified structure written into Our Reference; and the textbook chapter refined as the model's first ground. *(Added at the sixth round.)*
3. Progress
   - 3.1 Progress is the narrative glue: it narrates how an object has been identified based on the specification. *(His answer, 17 November.)*
   - 3.2 Progress entries are tied to team-member authorship and to time — progress may rule out something from the past — and together they give a sense of how far we've come. *(His answer, 17 November.)*
4. The parts
   - 4.1 The parts, in the order of the work itself: you specify what you are looking for first; you try your best to identify the thing you are talking about justifiably; you build an intuition of what it might look like to find this blurry thing; and you express in narrative what it has meant to make progress. Specification, identification, justification, intuition, progress. *(His answer, 17 November, second round.)*
   - 4.2 A justification is an annotation on the identified formalism that proves the specification is satisfied — relatively, not completely formally — and justifications frequently map to the citations on the project side. The justification is the *justifiably* in the identifying — the manner of the act, carried as annotations, not a step after it. *(His answers, 17 November.)*
   - 4.3 Intuition lives on the project side, below identification and above progress; it covers how the spec relates to existing things, how results will work, what might be relevant. All parts are independently viewable, with references between them. *(His answer, 17 November. Resolved by the second round: identification stands among the parts — the ordering is the order of the work.)*
5. Annotations *(rewritten at his tenth round — the foundation specified first, before more design)*
   - 5.1 Anything on a project page can be annotated: whatever can be mentioned can carry an annotation, because an annotation finds its subject by mention, never by position or a runtime id. Addressability is the only requirement — a clause, an entry, a principle, a progress entry, or a span of writing inside one.
   - 5.2 An annotation is writing said of a thing: it has a sort — Idea, Question, Revision, Archive stand already; the project adds its own, Justification among them — the mention of its subject, and its writing. It is the infrastructure's existing annotation, no second species.
   - 5.3 A relation is a type of annotation between two things: writing said of a pair, finding both of its ends by mention. A justification relates an identification entry to a clause; a connecting principle relates two principles.
   - 5.4 An annotation is itself a thing: it can be mentioned, so it can be annotated and related in turn. This one closure gives everything the walk found — the justification that claims several clauses is a thing fanning out one relation to each; the relationship that is itself an intuition; the graph that tangles.
   - 5.5 A section toggles its own annotations: the toggle is a small square in the section's heading furniture, in the dress the content's flip already wears; its state is a word on the section, and only the annotations whose subjects live under that section answer to it. The page's whole layer is every section's toggle at once.
   - 5.6 Seeing: an annotation draws beside its subject — in the margin where there is one, inline after its subject where there isn't — in the small voice, teal only in its cites, and the writing never moves to make room. A relation draws at both of its ends, each end showing the writing and the cite to the other end; opened by a press, it stands between its two things, and the return undoes it. Nothing draws while the toggle is off.
   - 5.7 Their uses stand from the seed: connecting related things, writing ideas, writing justifications for progress.
   - 5.8 Ratified and built at the eleventh round: the annotations are in the object model and their view is controlled — the sketch renders every mark, the coverage and the Archive strike from data; a relation draws at both of its ends; a section's square toggles its own; the bar's square is every section's at once. The model corrected the hand on its first render: clause 4.2, hand-marked open, is claimed by J3 and the computed coverage says so. *(Model probe 9 checks 0 failures; focus probe 12 checks 0 failures on the rebuilt page.)*
6. The views
   - 6.1 The views are found by play, in his light UI aesthetic: elegant ways to configure what you want to see on the screen. The configuration set so far: annotations on and off; references showing or hiding; annotation color as an advanced maybe. Explored by mockups on one page, never by asking him to imagine. *(His answer, 17 November.)*
   - 6.2 Every part is independently viewable, with references between the parts. *(His answer, 17 November.)*
7. The structure of the parts — from their semantics *(drafted at his word, third round; his corrections rule)*
   - 7.1 The specification is the agreement, so its unit is the clause — the atomic promise, uniquely numbered, citable; its form is the legal document already specified in Our Reference. Satisfaction is judged clause by clause, which is why the clause is the unit. Its numbered tree and the operations on it are section 10's.
   - 7.2 The identification is the deliverable's genome in referential form: its unit is the entry, binding a name the specification introduces to a referent — the place in the deliverable where the thing is actually created — together with the formal statement of what the thing is. It holds the identity of what the specification specifies in generic form: the entry constrains nothing about what its referent is, so a theorem, a component or a chapter are held alike, and an entry whose referent is an interpolated word holds an identity whose expression is still owed. The deliverable remains the only place the thing is created; the identification refers, never duplicates; it states and never narrates, each entry an anchored span a justification can sit on. *(Rewritten at his sixth round; his corrections rule.)*
   - 7.3 The justification is the justifiably, shaped as intuition is: it connects a part of the identification to the specification — possibly to multiple clauses at once — and it is itself a thing, its writing arguing the claim in words, complex enough to be viewed in its own right. Relative proof is words plus citation; an annotation on the identification without a clause cite is an Idea, not a justification; the grain is enforceable. *(Rewritten at his eighth round.)*
   - 7.4 The intuition pictures the blurry thing, and it has semantic structure: its unit is the principle — a stated intuition, comparable with its peers because stated. Principles carry annotated relationships, and a relationship can itself be another principle: any two intuitions can be connected by a third, so the intuitions form a graph whose edges carry content, tangled where it must be, viewed in different ways rather than read in order. Its references still run outward to existing things and across to clauses and entries. *(Rewritten at his seventh round, replacing the essay paragraph.)*
   - 7.5 The progress narrates, so its unit is the entry — byline and date as frontmatter, narrative prose as body, its references to the statements and clauses it narrates being its substance. Entries stand in time order; ruling out the past is an Archive mark that strikes an entry and never deletes it.
   - 7.6 The project's parts share one face — the source face — integrated rather than four stitched registers, though not everything is markdown; only the specification wears the literal marks, and the literal mode is smoother and subtler: partially rendered, a section's title more important than markdown's own headings, which sit at the body's size, a little bold, not much. Said for the sketch and the arithmetic specification alike; the bound dress took it the same day. *(His fifth round.)*
   - 7.7 Worked examples, demanded at the sixth round, from the ground: in the textbook, the symmetry — named by the statement of work, created where the deliverable defines it, its interpolated X's holding identities whose expression is owed; in this sprint, the justification mark — named by clause 7.3, created on the sketch's statements in The Design's third chapter, justified against 7.3 and 4.2 at once; in intuition, the borrowed form — the principle that connects the contract's ancestry to the talk page, an edge that is itself a node.
8. The tools — checkers that report, never rewrite
   - 8.1 The coverage checker reads the specification against the justifications and reports each clause satisfied or open — the sprint's own progress meter, since progress narrates what coverage shows.
   - 8.2 The resolution checker proves every reference in every part lands on a clause, a statement, or an entry — the binder's link proof extended to the parts' language.
   - 8.3 The anatomy checker holds each unit to its structure: a justification carries its two references, an entry its author and date, a statement its anchor, a defined term its single definition.
   - 8.4 The tools live beside the chapter that specifies the parts, as the library keeps a thing's code beside its chapter.
9. The views — chosen by relation *(extends 6, drafted at his word, third round)*
   - 9.1 A view shows one part alone, or a pair joined by its relation with the references lit: the identification with the justification layer on; the specification with its coverage marks; the progress beside what it narrates; the intuition beside what it pictures.
   - 9.2 A reference navigates both ways: a Cite lands on the clause as it does today, and a clause's coverage view answers with the justifications that cite it.
   - 9.3 The exploration ground is the sprint's own record: the first mockups are built from this chapter's real material — its specification, its walk as progress — on one page with a switch. *(His suggestion, third round: "Might it be associated with the sprint chapter??")*
   - 9.4 The exploration uses the Content format itself: this sprint's project rendered with the parts in their drafted forms — the specification in the legal register, the identification as anchored statements of the forms the parts take, the justifications as marks citing clauses, the intuition from his named relatives, the progress as the walk's bylined entries — the relational presentation being the thing explored. *(His direction, fourth round.)*
   - 9.5 The project is one structure, not five documents: a typed graph whose nodes are clauses, entries, principles and progress entries, and whose edges — justifications and connecting principles — are nodes themselves. The documents are projections of the graph into pages; the views are projections chosen by relation; and the motion between projections preserves identity — the same node travels to its place in the next view, by the platform's dissolve. *(The team's hearing of his ninth round, built rather than said back; his corrections rule.)*
   - 9.6 The primitive interaction is opening a connection: a press on a justification turns the page into that justification in its own right — its clauses, possibly several, and its entry standing face to face with the argument between them; a press on a connecting principle stands the edge between its ends. *(Built into the sketch at the ninth round, each state driven and photographed.)*

10. The specification's tree — numbered, nested, operable *(put in at his twelfth round and built the same hour)*
   - 10.1 A specification always has numbers or codes, always has nesting, and the numbers nest — a clause's code carries its ancestry, 4.2 standing under 4. It does not have to be only a list of lists, but it can be imagined as one. *(His twelfth round.)*
   - 10.2 A part with sub-parts collapses: a press on its heading folds the children beneath it, the parent standing with a quiet count of what folded; a second press unfolds.
   - 10.3 A part can be hidden entirely, and show-all brings every hidden part back. The team reads hiding as a view act, never an edit — the tree beneath is unchanged; his correction rules.
   - 10.4 Parts can be moved around to compare: a part dragged beside another keeps its code and its marks, because the code carries identity — 4.2 standing beside 7.3 is still 4.2, and the move is an act on the projection, never a renumbering. *(The team's reading of the comparing; his corrections rule.)*
   - 10.5 The annotations toggle on what is visible: an annotation draws only where its subject stands on the page — a folded or hidden clause draws nothing, and its marks return with it — while a relation's visible end still draws, citing across. An opened connection overrides the tree: its things stand while it is open, because the connection is a projection of its own.
   - 10.6 Lifted at his thirteenth round — "the reference manual should have this code": The Specification in Our Reference gained section 9, the tree, with the tree's code as its appendix, the specification held as an object and the appendix page its rendering run on that specification itself; The Content gained the five parts in clause 2.1 and the annotation foundation as its fifth section, and its "nothing collapses" fell to the twelfth round. *(Manual tree probe 11 checks 0 failures; tree probe 15 checks 0 failures on the sketch.)*
11. The implementation *(opened at his fourteenth round — "We have to implement this and it needs to be interactive and have great examples")*
   - 11.1 The tree is implemented where the specification renders: the Content family in Our Reference's The Content, whose renderer already speaks the specification's citation language. A second pass nests what the first identified — each heading gathering its section, each block of a section a part — and the operations ride the bound page: fold on the heading's press with its count, hide from the gutter with show-all, move by drag with code and marks travelling, the annotation layer a square per section and one for the page.
   - 11.2 The annotations' first real sort is computed, not authored: the deliverable already cites the specification through Cite, so coverage is read from the page's own citations — clause 4.2 made mechanism, justifications mapping to the citations on the project side. A claimed clause wears its mark and the mark leads back to the claim; an unclaimed clause reads Open.
   - 11.3 The great example is the real ground: the statement of work of The Symmetry of Arithmetic, bound and interactive on the textbook's page, with the manual's own appendix standing as the second.
   - 11.4 The grain of the first build: paragraph clauses and section headings wear margin marks; a table-row clause wears its mark inline in the small voice; finer grains follow when the margin learns collision and the phone.
   - 11.5 "Nothing collapses" is struck in the code as it was in the manual: the Project's comment records the supersession by the twelfth round rather than deleting the history — the earlier ruling was against the page deciding for the reader, and the tree's fold is the reader deciding.
   - 11.6 Built and verified the same hour: the tree rides Markdown as a second pass behind a tree switch the specification sets, the operations land in the one dispatcher, and the coverage is computed at the first flip. *(Bind exit 0; implementation probe 18 checks 0 failures on the bound textbook page — ten sections wrapped, the numbers nested, 1.1 Cited and 1.5 Open and K2 cited twice from the chapter's real citations, the fold with its count, hide and show-all, 4.1 seated beside 1.1 across sections with its mark travelling, the master square stilling every mark, a mark turning the page back to its claim.)*
   - 11.7 The family stands complete at the library's top, his fifteenth round: Identification and Intuition join Specification and Progress as the project's named parts in Our Reference's Content, each a section naming itself, the order of the work their order in the code. The justification is deliberately not a section — by 4.2 and 7.3 it is the manner of the identifying, carried as annotations, the computed coverage its first sort. Mathematics re-exports the whole family, so the textbook keeps one door.

## <a id="walk"></a>The walk — his answers, verbatim with spelling cleaned

The first round, 17 November. On progress: *"Progress narrates how an object has been identified based on the specification. If we have identification, it can be understood as the narrative glue, tied to team member authorship, time as progress might be ruling out something from the past, that gives us a sense of how far we've come"*. On justification: *"Well if you identify something specified, don't you need to like prove that it satisfies the specification?? So it is like annotations on the identified formalism that prove the spec is satisfied relatively not but completely formally. Justification might frequently map to the citations on the project side"*. On intuition: *"On the project side, below identification, above progress, but all should be independently viewable with references between"*. On the views: *"We need to play with this. You can see my light UI aesthetic. Let's find elegant ways to configure what you want to see on the screen. Annotations on and off, maybe references showing or hiding, annotation color? Maybe that's advanced"*.

The second round, 17 November, on the order of the parts: *"You specify what you are looking for first, you try your best to identify the thing you are talking about justifiably, you build an intuition what it might look like to find this blurry thing, and you try to express in narrative what it has meant to make progress"*.

The third round, 17 November, handing the team the structural specifying: *"Why don't you try and specify the structure of the different sections of the project based on the semantics of what they mean. You will end up writing these things in the reference manual too, but that's okay. What do these sections look like? How do we make library tools that help enforce them? How do we navigate these parts in different views so we can see relations? Where might you explore this? Might it be associated with the sprint chapter??"*.

The fourth round, 17 November, directing the exploration: *"That is a project right? It is content? Maybe you can use that format to explore"* — and, on the work itself: *"We are specifying this project structure with parts, and need to identify the form they will take and how they can be presented relationally"*.

The fifth round, 17 November, on the dress: *"I recommend everything in the project part at least having the same font, though not necessarily in markdown form? I would consider something more integrated. I still think our markdown literal mode can be smoother and more subtle"* — and on hierarchy: *"The title of the section needs to be more important and the markdown sections — here and in arithmetic, shouldn't be much bigger if at all. Maybe they can be a little bold, not much. Partially rendered"*.

The sixth round, 17 November, on the theory of identification: *"What theory are you using to formalize the object of an identification? How is it structured? It needs to be a pretty flexible thing — that which can hold the identity of a specification in generic form. Are you sure you have specified your work this sprint? You need to capture the meaning of the semantics of these parts of the project. Give examples! How do you say what you have identified, remembering that the other side of the content is where it is actually created — you are doing something like giving its genome in a more referential form with justification to the spec"*.

The seventh round, 17 November, on intuition's structure: *"Your intuition is paragraphs? You don't think there's any semantic structure to intuition? Maybe parts of this can be reordered as a principle so they can be compared. Do we have annotated relationships between them? Can a relationship be another intuition? How about that? Any two intuitions can be connected by another, and that graph can be tangled. And you can view it in different ways?"*.

The eighth round, 17 November, on justification's structure: *"Justification can be similar — a part of the identification is connected to the specification — possibly multiple parts, and that justification could be a complex thing to view in its own right"*.

The ninth round, 17 November, the challenge to hear rather than transcribe: *"What did you hear. Are you hearing interactive data structures with relationships and nodes on them, moving, visualizing a justification that connects the two as a new thing to see! What have you heard me specify, or try to? Can you build on the thing I am trying to specify based on its reach and aim and aesthetics and not just saying back to me what I said?"*.

The tenth round, 17 November, the call to specify the foundation first: *"You need to specify what these sections are right? It's not just a design. Do you see the interactive and relational thing? Why don't we start simple. How can you add the idea of annotating anything on a project page. How about a relation is a type of annotation between two things. How? How can we make it so that a section can toggle their annotations? How does it even work to see them!! How about you specify that first"*.

The eleventh round, 17 November, ratifying and opening the build: *"Let's build that. And yes, I think we want these to be annotations, and they are in the object model and we can control their view?"*.

The twelfth round, 17 November, specifying the specification itself: *"Good so here's more. Specifications should always have numbers or codes, always have nesting, and their numbers should nest.
