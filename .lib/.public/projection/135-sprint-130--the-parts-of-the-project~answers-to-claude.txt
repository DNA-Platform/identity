**2. "As we discussed in IXP."** IXP is Inexplicable Phenomena, Doug's library. **3. The figure.** Your reading is right, and it is now built. A figure is an **annotation, not a part of the contract**: it names an object already in the specification by its code — the axiom table via 1.1, the kinds via 4.1, a whole section — and carries a title and a caption. Its mark rides the annotation rail; pressed, its object becomes the only thing visible, the title above, the caption beneath — and the caption is not shown until the title is pressed. A figure without a title shows just the writing, alone. Two direct answers: a figure has **no code in the document's numbering** (in the annotation layer's own series it may carry an id, F1, as revisions carry R1), and its content **does not bind** — an annotation refers, it never adds contract content, so the boundary stands.

**4. Requirements carrying opens (C7, C8).** You have caught a real tension, and here is the room's resolution. The open determinations live in the progress; a requirement may **cite** one, and the citation marks exactly where the specification is not yet specific enough — the progress says this of itself. The general form's answer is its interpolation clause: what is specified and not yet found stands as a placeholder naming the open determination it waits on. So a minimal contract carries the requirement's *form* — the commitment, stated so it can fail — with its precise statement interpolated against O2/O3/O5; the content of the open stays in progress, and the work closes when the last placeholder is replaced. If your essence-spec can state this seam more sharply than ours does, do.

**5. C3 against the boundary.** The binding force is C3's, and C3 is inside the boundary; the identification is the referent, not a binder. What C3 imports is a **tracing obligation**: a solution must show each clause of X traces to some entry I1–I20. The entries themselves remain comprehension — revising an entry does not breach the contract; it changes what a trace can land on. We admit the seam is loose: whether each entry should tie to a specific place in the deliverable is an open we have named. Your essence-spec may rule: either incorporate the identification by reference explicitly for tracing only, or forbid a contract clause from reaching outside and make C3 carry its own anchor list.

**6. The candidate of 16 Nov.** Correct: it stays out of the contract. The contract identifies; candidates are judged against it and never named in it — the deliverable section says "a solution states which kind it is." Candidate figures and their trials belong to the progress and to the earlier specification's archive, which the schedules cite as kept.

**7. Minimal against "nothing disappears."** Exactly your reading. Permanence says a specification is updated, never thrown away; minimality is achieved by moving content to its right document with citations following — which is what the reorganization did: meaning went to the identification, opens to the progress, reference to the schedules, and the boundary clause says where everything stands. So the essence is the minimal contract **plus** an account of where everything else went; "recommend where context might go" in your assignment is this rule applied.

## Appendix — The Specification, the general chapter, current text

*Preamble: The specification of a specification, written in its own form. The form of a legal document was chosen on 16 November 2026, its anatomy the contract's — recitals, articles, clauses, defined terms, provisos, schedules — evolved for a library that specifies deliverables. The form is the library's; a subject may redress it to fit its style. The first full instance is the statement of work of The Symmetry of Arithmetic.*

**1. Purpose.** 1.1 A specification is the formal identification of a deliverable — the thing agreed between buyer and seller in a service contract. It fixes what the deliverable must be, uniquely, so that candidates can be judged against it and the right one recognized when it is found. The specification is the legal document; the deliverable is not. 1.2 The specification is the anchor for the work. The work meets the specification part by part, and the deliverable cites each part as it is met. 1.3 A demand that cannot be checked has specified nothing, and a demand is stated so that it can fail.

**2. The parts.** 2.1 The preamble — the contract's recitals: what the document is and the choice it records, standing before the numbered sections and carrying no number. 2.2 The sections — the contract's articles: each bears a number and a name, and the sections run in one sequence. 2.3 The clauses: a section nests numbered parts; clause 2.3 is the third clause of section 2; a clause nests further where its matter requires it. 2.4 The labels — the contract's defined terms: a recurring kind of clause carries a letter and a number, defined where the clause is stated; in the first instance, the known results K, the constraints C, and the open determinations O. 2.5 The provisos: where a demand is motivated, the motivation stands beside it and says so. 2.6 The tables: a table states what a table states best — axioms, results, tests — and its rows are clauses carrying their labels.

**3. Citation.** 3.1 The specification references itself: any clause cites any other by its number or its label, in free-form citation language, and every citation is a link to the clause it names. 3.2 A specification is highly connected: where a clause depends on another, motivates it, or constrains it, it says so by citation. 3.3 Citation reaches across the project's documents: the specification cites the progress by its labels, and the progress cites the specification by its numbers.

**4. Boundary.** 4.1 The specification states where it ends; nothing outside the boundary binds the work. 4.2 What stands after the end is the schedules — the contract's annexes: reference bearing on the work and binding none of it.

**5. Interpolation.** 5.1 What is specified and not yet found stands as a placeholder for itself, in a brace naming the open determination it waits on. 5.2 The work closes when the last placeholder is replaced by the thing it stands for.

**6. Permanence.** 6.1 A specification is not thrown away; it is updated when its parts are found inconsistent, since an inconsistent specification has specified nothing. 6.2 A change is made by changing the specification, in the context of all its other parts.

**7. The implementation.** 7.1 The implementation meets the specification part by part, and cites each part as it is met, by its number or its label. 7.2 A citation from the implementation leads to the part it names, and the specification stands beside what implements it: the two forms are each reachable from the other. 7.3 A justification is the annotation form of this relationship: it says how the implementation satisfies a part, citing the part by number.

**8. Format.** 8.1 The format chosen here is the legal document: a preamble, then numbered, named sections of numbered clauses. 8.2 A subject may change the format to fit its style; the form of sections 1 to 7 is kept, and the tree of section 9 with it; the dress is the subject's.

**9. The tree** (the operable form — the rendering contract, included for completeness; your essence may treat it as the presentation of the thing rather than the thing). 9.1 A specification always has numbers or codes, always has nesting, and the numbers nest. 9.2 The tree is a projection of the document; every operation is an act on the projection, never on the document — a moved part keeps its code and its annotations, because the code carries identity. 9.3 Unique showability: at any moment every part is on the page for exactly one reason or off it for exactly one reason — shown; folded away by an ancestor; hidden by the reader; or standing aside while one part shows alone — each reason with exactly one control and one inverse. 9.4 The operations, each with an inverse, at every level: fold (9.4.1), hide/show-all (9.4.2), only/everything (9.4.3 — a figure's mark is a second door to this state), move to compare (9.4.4), reset, the strongest inverse (9.4.5). 9.5 The bar carries the whole tree's verbs. 9.6 The annotations are consistent: one-line marks in a single rail, stacked, never colliding, the writing never moving to make room; no square starts checked — the layer stands dark until the bar's square lights every mark. 9.7 The code of the tree stands in the appendix, run on this specification itself. 9.8 The figure — an annotation naming an object of the specification by its code, with a title and a caption; its press is 9.4.3 performed on its object, dressed; the caption unshown until the title is pressed; a figure without a title is just the writing, alone.
