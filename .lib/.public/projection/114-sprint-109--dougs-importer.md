## <a id="architecture"></a>The architecture, in the library's words

**One Claude book**, filed under Dougs Library and a subject by its About, which catalogues two kinds of book: **the conversations book**, and **one book per project**.

**The conversations book** is filed under the Claude book and is a subject by its About; **every conversation is a book filed under it**, so a conversation has one home whatever the mapping says, the 152 the mapping never files included. Its table answers for every one, and its chapters are their synopses, imported, the catalogue pattern [his library already uses](../library/01-books-in-annotations.md#a-catalogues-chapter-is-another-books-synopsis). *"It will be a big book"*: its page is the list he decided, by recency and by size, and is a later sprint's to design.

**A project book** is filed under the Claude book and is two things at once. A **reference manual of the project's resources**: its description and instructions as a chapter, and each knowledge file a chapter with the file beside it, as the manual shows a chapter beside its file. And a **secondary catalogue of its conversations**: a chapter per conversation the mapping files under it, importing that conversation's synopsis and answering for it in the table with the topic form, while the conversation's cover names the project as a topic. *"We can end up cataloguing them in many ways"*: a topic may hold many ways, so a second reading over the same conversations, by month or by whom, is another book of the same shape.

**The way back** he asked for is the topic itself: a conversation's cover says which projects catalogue it, the importer writes it from the mapping, the binder checks both halves, and the conversation's page draws it as it draws the subject line.

**What is idempotent, and how.** The importer only ever adds. A book whose folder exists is never written again; a chapter or a row absent from a catalogue is added; nothing the importer wrote is rewritten. So a conversation, once in, is his to refine; a project book gains conversations as the mapping gains them; a run repeated changes nothing.

**One reading set aside.** If *"one book with all conversations"* meant the conversations as chapters of one book, that page would carry every message of every conversation, 19,704, which one page per book cannot hold. He confirmed the book-per-conversation reading twice.

## <a id="process"></a>The process, file to conversation, in eight stages

*His ask, 2026-10-13: "well what is our process for the import. How do we go from file to conversation? There is a lot of planning to be done here and I am not seeing a whole lot of ideas. If it were up to me, I would start from the raw export file, along with the project export, trying to pair them. I would export from each of the different places. I wouldn't try to use the files in dna-library, but I would use the code there as a beginning and I would use the library there as a check. We do need all of the project files, the conversation artifacts etc... to be in our version."* Each stage is a chapter of the importer book with its code beside it. Where a stage has alternatives they are named, the record's reading marked as the record's.

1. **Export, from each place, read where it lands.** Claude.ai's export is requested there and arrives as a manifest whose four zips the browser must fetch; they stay in the dated folder the sister repo keeps, with the app's capture of the project lists beside them, made by its own command on his go-ahead. Claude Code's transcripts are on disk under the user's `.claude/projects`, one file per session, read in place. The sister repo's markdown is never read: its code is the beginning, its archive the check of stage 7.
2. **Read, one reader per packaging, one record for all.** A reader streams the big array as the old importer did and reads the small zips by their directory, and every reader yields the same record: a conversation with its name, its times and its messages as a tree, each message who, when and its blocks; a project with its name, description, instructions, its documents with their content, and its memory. That record is the seam: the stages after it never see a source's words, and the second importer, Claude Code, is a reader of about a hundred lines whose project is the working folder and whose title is the line the app wrote.
3. **Pair, by evidence; the machine proposes and he disposes.** Four kinds of evidence, each scored and said by name: a knowledge search quoting the project's own document, certain, 24 today; the app's capture by title, with its relative date and its position to tell the ten *A Continuation...* apart, 272 once today; a project's memory prose naming the conversation, unmeasured; for Claude Code the working folder, certain. A conversation is paired where the evidence agrees and left unpaired where it conflicts or is silent, reported by name; it has its home in the conversations book either way. The pairing stands as a file beside the pairing chapter, one line per conversation with its project and its evidence, which he may edit, and the importer reads his lines first. That is refine-by-hand at the point where judgement lives.
4. **Thread.** The path on the screen, the latest leaf and its parents to the root, the count on branches said. For Claude Code the side chains a session spawned are counted and set aside.
5. **Cut.** Three ways: by day, mechanical and dated, *the record's reading*; by gap, a chapter opening after some hours of silence, nearer to sittings but a threshold to argue over; by hand, the old way, a cut as the opening words of the message it starts at. By day first and by hand after: the journal's words already carry a date, a conversation's files are his once written, and a cut by hand is the refinement he named.
6. **Write the writing, once, on the way in.** Text blocks into the notation, paragraphs, lists, headings, code, bold, italics, links, tables and mathematics, the last two already the package's; html escaped as the text it is. An artifact a file beside the chapter that made it, its updates and rewrites replayed, the final version named for it and the earlier ones numbered. An attachment's extracted text a file beside the chapter. An uploaded file by name. A tool use one line saying what was done, its result folded. Thinking left out and counted. *Which of these come in was his, 2026-10-13: artifacts every version, attachments' text, tool uses one line each; thinking not.*
7. **Write the books, and check.** The three levels as approved, every file a first draft, add only. Then the check against the sister repo's archive: our counts against its statistics, our pairing against its scaffold's with every difference listed by name, our message count per conversation against its transcript's; then the bind; then the promise reading every written conversation back against the export.
8. **Run and report.** Bare it says; with the write flag it writes; repeated it changes nothing; the report names what was found, paired, unpaired, cut, written and left out, with the numbers.

## <a id="format"></a>The format of a conversation book, the import target, as designed in U5

*His ask, 2026-10-13: "I'm still feeling we're a bit low on things to implement. What is the format for a book that is a conversation? Doesn't that have to be designed? Isn't that our import target?" So U5 went first, and the format was designed in the book's own medium: the type and its words in his manual's chapter* The Conversation *(proxy), and a sample hand-written from the real first ten exchanges of* Semantics of Types & More, *bound in a galley and seen on 4244. What stands, file by file, is what the importer writes.*

- **The folder** is the name's slug under Our Conversations; it exists or it does not.
- **`.book.tsx`**, one line: the class extends the manual's conversation type.
- **`.cover.tsx`**: the package's Cover; a Scheme of six colours, the project's; the Title; the Author, Doug; the Subject, Our Conversations, with the two-star form; each project that catalogues it with the three-star form; one paragraph saying between whom, each a word *said*, from which day to which, each a Date, and the counts: exchanges on the screen, messages in all, left on other paths.
- **`.synopsis.tsx`**: the export's summary where one stands, else the opening words; refined by hand after.

## <a id="decisions"></a>Decisions, each with what it was chosen over

- **D1 · One record, many readers.** Every source is read into one record, a conversation with its messages as a tree of blocks and a project with its documents and memory, and every stage after the reading sees only the record. R2.
- **D2 · No dependency.** The zips are read with Node's own inflate, the big array streamed by the old importer's scan and the small ones by their central directory, as the probes did in under eighteen seconds for everything. - **D3 · The importer is TypeScript run by `tsx`**, which the binding already carries, every stage a file beside its chapter, `N-<chapter>~<id>.ts`, the run's entry the first chapter's file, as the old importer was. - **D4 · The folders.** The importer book in a folder of its own beside his three; the Claude book in a folder of its own, holding the conversations book's folder and one folder per project, each conversation a folder under the conversations book's.
