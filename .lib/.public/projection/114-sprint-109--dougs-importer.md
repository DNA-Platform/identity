## <a id="architecture"></a>The architecture, in the library's words

**One Claude book**, filed under Dougs Library and a subject by its About, which catalogues two kinds of book: **the conversations book**, and **one book per project**.

**The conversations book** is filed under the Claude book and is a subject by its About; **every conversation is a book filed under it**, so a conversation has one home whatever the mapping says, the 152 the mapping never files included. Its table answers for every one, and its chapters are their synopses, imported, the catalogue pattern [his library already uses](../library/01-books-in-annotations.md#a-catalogues-chapter-is-another-books-synopsis). *"It will be a big book"*: its page is the list he decided, by recency and by size, and is a later sprint's to design.

**A project book** is filed under the Claude book and is two things at once. A **reference manual of the project's resources**: its description and instructions as a chapter, and each knowledge file a chapter with the file beside it, as the manual shows a chapter beside its file. And a **secondary catalogue of its conversations**: a chapter per conversation the mapping files under it, importing that conversation's synopsis and answering for it in the table with the topic form, while the conversation's cover names the project as a topic. *"We can end up cataloguing them in many ways"*: a topic may hold many ways, so a second reading over the same conversations, by month or by whom, is another book of the same shape.

**The way back** he asked for is the topic itself: a conversation's cover says which projects catalogue it, the importer writes it from the mapping, the binder checks both halves, and the conversation's page draws it as it draws the subject line.

**What is idempotent, and how.** The importer only ever adds. A book whose folder exists is never written again; a chapter or a row absent from a catalogue is added; nothing the importer wrote is rewritten. So a conversation, once in, is his to refine; a project book gains conversations as the mapping gains them; a run repeated changes nothing.

**One reading set aside.** If *"one book with all conversations"* meant the conversations as chapters of one book, that page would carry every message of every conversation, 19,704, which one page per book cannot hold. He confirmed the book-per-conversation reading twice.

## <a id="process"></a>The process, file to conversation, in eight stages

*His ask, 2026-10-13: "well what is our process for the import. How do we go from file to conversation? There is a lot of planning to be done here and I am not seeing a whole lot of ideas. If it were up to me, I would start from the raw export file, along with the project export, trying to pair them. I would export from each of the different places. I wouldn't try to use the files in dna-library, but I would use the code there as a beginning and I would use the library there as a check. We do need all of the project files, the conversation artifacts etc... to be in our version."* Each stage is a chapter of the importer book with its code beside it. Where a stage has alternatives they are named, the record's reading marked as the record's.

1. **Export, from each place, read where it lands.** Claude.ai's export is requested there and arrives as a manifest whose four zips the browser must fetch; they stay in the dated folder the sister repo keeps, with the app's capture of the project lists beside them, made by its own command on his go-ahead. Claude Code's transcripts are on disk under the user's `.claude/projects`, one file per session, read in place. The sister repo's markdown is never read: its code is the beginning, its archive the check of stage 7.
2. **Read, one reader per packaging, one record for all.** A reader streams the big array as the old importer did and reads the small zips by their directory, and every reader yields the same record: a conversation with its name, its times and its messages as a tree, each message who, when and its blocks; a project with its name, description, instructions, its documents with their content, and its memory. That record is the seam: the stages after it never see a source's words, and the second importer, Claude Code, is a reader of about a hundred lines whose project is the working folder and whose title is the line the app wrote.
3. **Pair, by evidence; the machine proposes and he disposes.** Four kinds of evidence, each scored and said by name: a knowledge search quoting the project's own document, certain, 24 today; the app's capture by title, with its relative date and its position to tell the ten *A Continuation...* apart, 272 once today; a project's memory prose naming the conversation, unmeasured; for Claude Code the working folder, certain. A conversation is paired where the evidence agrees and left unpaired where it conflicts or is silent, reported by name; it has its home in the conversations book either way. The pairing stands as a file beside the pairing chapter, one line per conversation with its project and its evidence, which he may edit, and the importer reads his lines first. That is refine-by-hand at the point where judgement lives.
4. **Thread.** The path on the screen, the latest leaf and its parents to the root, the count on branches said. For Claude Code the side chains a session spawned are counted and set aside.
5. **Cut.** Three ways: by day, mechanical and dated, *the record's reading*; by gap, a chapter opening after some hours of silence, nearer to sittings but a threshold to argue over; by hand, the old way, a cut as the opening words of the message it starts at. By day first and by hand after: the journal's words already carry a date, a conversation's files are his once written, and a cut by hand is the refinement he named.
6. **Write the writing, once, on the way in.** Text blocks into the notation, paragraphs, lists, headings, code, bold, italics, links, tables and mathematics, the last two already the package's; html escaped as the text it is. An artifact a file beside the chapter that made it, its updates and rewrites replayed, the final version named for it and the earlier ones numbered. An attachment's extracted text a file beside the chapter. An uploaded file by name. A tool use one line saying what was done, its result folded. Thinking left out and counted. *Which of these come in was his, 2026-10-13: artifacts every version, attachments' text, tool uses one line each; thinking not.*
7. **Write the books, and check.** The three levels as approved, every file a first draft, add only. Then the check against the sister repo's archive: our counts against its statistics, our pairing against its scaffold's with every difference listed by name, our message count per conversation against its transcript's; then the bind; then the promise reading every written conversation back against the export.
8. **Run and report.** Bare it says; with the write flag it writes; repeated it changes nothing; the report names what was found, paired, unpaired, cut, written and left out, with the numbers.

## <a id="decisions"></a>Decisions, each with what it was chosen over

- **D1 · One record, many readers.** Every source is read into one record, a conversation with its messages as a tree of blocks and a project with its documents and memory, and every stage after the reading sees only the record. Chosen over a pipeline per source, since the second source is already on disk and differs only in its packaging ([what the exploration found](#what-the-exploration-found-each-a-claim-with-its-source)). R2.
- **D2 · No dependency.** The zips are read with Node's own inflate, the big array streamed by the old importer's scan and the small ones by their central directory, as the probes did in under eighteen seconds for everything. Chosen over the sister repo's `adm-zip` and its child process, since his library's binding carries no dependency for a tool and the scan is forty lines. R3.
- **D3 · The importer is TypeScript run by `tsx`**, which the binding already carries, every stage a file beside its chapter, `N-<chapter>~<id>.ts`, the run's entry the first chapter's file, as the old importer was. Chosen over one script, on Closure's rule and his: *"every piece of code is a resource to a chapter."* R1.
- **D4 · The folders.** The importer book in a folder of its own beside his three; the Claude book in a folder of its own, holding the conversations book's folder and one folder per project, each conversation a folder under the conversations book's. The binder walks beneath a book and every address is flat, so the nesting costs nothing and makes a project's and a conversation's folder the unit that exists or does not. Folder names are the plan's and proxies. R6 to R8.
- **D5 · The types the generated books wear.** Our Conversations is *"a very simple book cataloguing all of them"*: a new type in the manual, *the register* (proxy), a journal of synopses, extending the Journal so that every entry is a chapter carrying another book's synopsis, dated with that conversation's date, in the part of its month, all on one page newest first with the way into each book, which is the list by recency he decided; Dougs Claude wears the same type this sprint, its entries the conversations book and each project, and takes the Bookshelf when its page is designed, *"bookshelf for projects, when we get to that"*. A project book extends the Manual, since it is a reference manual of its resources, and so every chapter it is given opens with a brief the importer writes. A conversation extends a new type under the base book, *the conversation* (proxy), with the journal's two rules, every chapter dated and in the part of its month, read oldest first under the library's layout, one chapter at a time. Chosen over the Bookshelf's Catalogue for the two catalogues, whose class carries the desk, the shelf and the jackets a shelf needs and he set aside; and over the base book bare for a conversation, since the rules are what make the generated chapters a conversation and not a pile. R19, R23.
- **D6 · The words a conversation needs, in the manual, one chapter.** An *exchange*, said of a section, one message; *said by*, a word naming who spoke, Doug or Claude; *at*, a word for the time; a *tool use*, said of a paragraph and folded, which the manual's Folded already gives; an artifact or an attachment an Append, which the package already gives. Names proxies. R19.
- **D7 · A name is the title, made unique by a count, and the address keeps every alphabet.** His rule: an untitled conversation is *Untitled 1*, *Untitled 2*, by a count the importer keeps; a repeated title the same way, *A Continuation 1* to *10*; no day in a name, since a conversation happens across time. And the package's slug keeps the letters and digits of every script, so *Понимание основ СРТ* stands at its own address: a one-line change under the package, his yes given, its diff shown before it lands. Chosen over the day, which he refused, and over transliteration. R11, R33.
- **D8 · A file beside a chapter wears one of the fifteen types the binder knows**; an artifact or attachment of another type is written as `.txt` with its own name said in the chapter. Chosen over widening the binder's list, which is the package's. R17, R18.
- **D9 · No drawings this sprint.** The two catalogues are registers and draw no jackets, so a generated cover is plain: its title, its author, its subject, its topics, and a scheme of six colours so a theme can take its pen from the cover, a project's chosen from a small palette by its position and its conversations saying the same; no window and no drawing. The Claude book's and the conversations book's covers are written once by hand, in his voice, run past him. Monograms are struck; the drawings come with the Bookshelf for projects when its page is designed. R20, R22.
- **D12 · Exclusion, proposed by the machine and disposed by him.** A conversation that is Ana's is not his and is not imported. The importer proposes exclusions from evidence it names, a conversation filed under Ana's projects first, and writes them into the pairing file as lines he may keep or strike; a struck line imports, a kept line excludes and counts. Nothing is excluded by language, since he writes Russian too. His standing doubt stands beside it: *"maybe we put them all in."* R32.
- **D13 · Non-canonical cataloguing, the one pattern this sprint speaks.** A conversation is filed under Our Conversations, its canonical subject, with the two-star form; each project that catalogues it is named on its cover with the three-star form and answered in the project's table with the same, both halves written by the importer and checked by the binder; the project's chapter for it imports its synopsis, as a canonical catalogue's chapter does. R9, R10.
- **D10 · Overwrite while the importer is made, never after, by the ledger.** *Amended on his ruling of page 038.* ***Amended again 2026-10-14, on his idea: "We can use resource files associated with the conversations book to store metadata about the import, so we can keep track of what has been done even if we decide to make modifications to conversations etc.. We can use resource files to keep track. Integrate this idea into the importer as a way of holding state."*** *So the ledger is no longer one file beside the importer's chapter but a record beside each entry of Our Conversations, named for the entry and listed by it, holding the conversation's id, its book's name and folder, the export it came from, the export's last change and message count at the import, and the digest of the files written; the entry's Append makes the binder account for it and, since a resource is never read, interpret none of it. Built the same day: the records, the four states read from them, the entry carrying its record, the importer keeping state by them, three promises; the one-file ledger removed.* While the importer is being worked on it overwrites what it wrote, on a flag said at the run; once it is done it never overwrites, since a conversation once imported is edited by hand. What tells the two apart, and tells a conversation already in from one that has grown since the export it came from and from one that is new, is **the ledger** (proxy), the importer's own record beside its chapter: one line per conversation with its id, its folder, the export's time of last change and its count of messages at import, and the digest of the files the importer wrote. - **D15 · The data about a conversation are annotations the cover carries, and the page reports them.** When it was held, from which instant to which; how many exchanges shown and how many messages in all; how many left on other paths; which projects catalogue it; and whatever else the record holds that a catalogue could index by. - **D18 · The incremental bind is built this sprint, not pitched on a measurement.** *"We need a way of compiling all these conversations. If the binder doesn't work on ones most recently changed, how will it ever possibly work?"* U10 stands regardless of U9's numbers; U9 sizes it. R30 is amended to say so.
- **D16 · The Claude app's view is the target, and the words are its.** An exchange stands as the app shows it; a web search or a run of code Claude made stands as the app shows it, a row saying what was done that opens on what came back, and the annotations are named for that, a search and its results, never a tool or a fold; the hour is shown in the reader's zone and the date is what matters. *"Just focus on how the Claude app views a conversation for now and we can grow it from there."* R38.
