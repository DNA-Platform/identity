## His words, verbatim, which are the requirements

Asked what the catalogue's reading view should show where its part now stands as a tree: *"You shouldn't see the manual's tree. You should have to click to go to the manual. And when in the context of the manual, you would click to get out. And you only see the chapters that are in the part of the book you are in. That would allow the appendix to feel like its own reference manual inside another book."*

Asked which of three tightenings to make: *"$View is a terrible name for something, considering view is the name of the method of every chemical... I can't answer these. I own .public. You own the abstraction in my library. You can make these decisions. I am talking about things like $View being terrible, that you look at the code and say 'Yes this is a great framework for generalizing reference manuals that can be applied to whole books or just to parts of one and it is implemented naturally' - that is what I meant by the abstraction. That is what you are solving. The goal is not to have to implement a whole second reference manual just to allow appendices to look like one. The two must share code and evolve together."*

- <a id="r1"></a>**R1** Reading a book, a part read as a manual is not seen as a tree: its section of the table stands as the book's theme always drew it, a heading and its rows. *Seen: the catalogue's reading view as before Sprint 104, the plain appendix at the foot.*
- <a id="r2"></a>**R2** A press on one of those rows opens the chapter and the book is in the manual's context. *Seen: the built class on the book, the tree.*
- <a id="r3"></a>**R3** In the manual's context only the part's chapters are seen in the table, the other sections gone, not dimmed. *Seen: the catalogue's Contents section absent while The Catalogue is open.*
- <a id="r4"></a>**R4** In the manual's context one press leads out, back to the book as it reads. *Seen: a row at the head of the tree, the book's own name, leading to the book's address.*
- <a id="r5"></a>**R5** The reference manual changes in nothing a reader sees: its tree is always its tree and it has no way out, since it is the book. *Measured: the page against the pre-sprint galley, the known differences only.*
- <a id="r6"></a>**R6** `$View` is gone; the abstraction is one Format, Manual, one Folder, one all-in-one book, and reads naturally as a framework for manuals over a whole book or a part of one. *Seen: the code, read whole, by him.*
- <a id="r7"></a>**R7** The three tightenings: the bind refuses a part read as a manual that no section of the table is headed for; the base theme's rule for a second word in a row moves beside the catalogue's arrow it serves; the placeholder body goes with the class.

## Decisions

- <a id="d1"></a>**D1. The base class is folded into the manual.** There is one way a part is read in this library; a base for a second was built ahead of its need, which [the coding style](../the-coding-style/03-the-coding-style.md#as-needed) forbids, and carried a name that collides with every chemical's `view()`. `$Manual` is the Format, with the context class and the row's words as its own; a second way of reading a part extracts a base when it arrives, named for two.
- <a id="d2"></a>**D2. The manual's Format and the all-in-one book stand in two files.** The base book must import the Format, the manual's book extends the base book, and a class never extends across a module cycle; so `10-the-manual~code.tsx` holds the Format and `10-the-manual~book.tsx` the book, its specification and its registrations, both appended to The Manual.
- <a id="d3"></a>**D3. The manual gives its rows their words at bind, and the entry reaches for nothing.** The row's twist and file presses are words the manual adds to each paragraph of the table that leads to its chapter, as the base book adds a Turn to each page, so the entry's file no longer imports the manual's and the two do not cycle. The drawn order of a row is unchanged, the Folder's rules placing its words by order.
- <a id="d4"></a>**D4. A folder is open when the reader is in its part, and outside that it draws nothing of its own.** *As built, simpler than first planned:* the folder decides for itself, by parts. Its part is the part of the first chapter its section leads to, or none; it is open when the open chapter's part is the same, both none counting as the same, read in its `defines` so a press redraws it; the open class goes on the section and the layer wears it too. So in the reference manual every folder is always open, since no chapter says a part; in the catalogue only the appendix's folder is, and only once one of its chapters is open; on the front, with nothing open, none is. Every rule of the tree says the book first and the folder open, and the twist, the mark and the file presses are hidden otherwise, so a closed folder's section reads as the theme draws any section; and in the book's context a folder that is not open is not drawn at all, so a book with two parts shows one. Chosen over a context class handed by the book, which could not tell two parts apart, and over a theme undoing the folder's look rule by rule, which every book would pay.
- <a id="d5"></a>**D5. The way out is a row the base book adds to the table at bind: the book's own name, leading to the book's address, drawn only in the context.** A paragraph of the library's own, as the byline and the turn are, placed first by the theme in the context and hidden outside it; the manual's book adds none. *The name is a PROXY: `Root`, the top of a tree in common usage, said of a paragraph.* Chosen over the bar's name alone, which leads out already but is not the press he described, and over a word on the folder's heading, which a book with two parts would draw twice.
- <a id="d6"></a>**D6. In the context, the other sections of the table are not drawn.** The base theme's rules under the built class say `display: none` of every section that is not a folder, where they dimmed them and hid their rows. The catalogue's theme keeps the folder at the foot while reading and first in the context.
- <a id="d7"></a>**D7. The rule, on his library's book specification, in a sentence:** *a part read as a manual is listed under a section headed with its name, and "Name" has none.* Read off the table's parts and the chapters that say Manual; nothing under the package.
