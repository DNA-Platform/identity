## <a id="parts"></a>The parts, as the brainstorm left them

**The paper, The Subject of Arithmetic, in three parts, named by him on 12 November: The Subject of Logic, The Subject of Specification, The Object of Study.**

**Part One, The Subject of Logic, closed 11 November, five sections.** The semantics expressed algebraically: the formal system as an abstract algebra, deduction an operation, identities accrued, first the Boolean algebra with the logical identity adjoined, the inferential identity the proof of the formal identity which is consistency, whose truth value is the logical identity, something whose truth is outside logic. The arithmetization, agreeing with Gödel on what is provable and what is undecidable, differing in the interpretation of the unprovable. Gödel's and Rosser's sentences as unformalizable, each with its negation meaning the same, the empty proof, 1 the empty proof's code and 0 the formal identity. The resolution: the 0 of the coding, the non-positive proof length and the number every prime power divides are one zero, the length of the inferential identity's proof. Objective and subjective inconsistency, to formalize a subject is to believe it consistent, undecidability the formal way of saying something is not formalizable in the subject, the subject complete as a subject. The close: two readings of Gödel, the reading subjective, the interpretation special as the one that expresses subjectivity or objectivity in mathematics. What Part One formalizes is in The Subject of Logic's Toward PA; the sketch is that chapter's.

**Part Two, The Subject of Specification, in four movements, ending on symmetry.**

*The historical and philosophical account, ending at referential inconsistency.* Justify staying in arithmetic: Part One went back to before model theory was born, to Gödel's fixed point, arithmetic interpreting arithmetic; model theory is a reflection of incompleteness, set theory the object representing mathematics and interpreting all the others, the perspective that holds an ω-complete witness as something one can refer to. An account of set theory as the subject that holds the totality as an object, the thing one believes in if one believes mathematics can express its own incompleteness; arithmetic recurring in set theory to show it too cannot prove everything. Gödel's arithmetic an object representing language, set theory the truth of the totality of language, his arithmetic a subject that believes itself expressive enough to specify itself, Gödel numbering one technique carrying that belief out. Sympathy for Hilbert, who wanted Cantor's paradise complete and required proof finite. The rational number: every base-10 digit ever written, in order, modulo simultaneity, is a rational number, and mathematics imagines things far beyond it. God in the examples: Cantor named the absolute infinite God; the point that referential inconsistency about infinity is not unlike concluding things about God from the hypothesis that one could refer to him; what can be referred to sensibly is subjective. The end: the expression of referential inconsistency inside PA, Gödel's own hypothetical, suppose G were provable, as the place his argument refers to what it cannot. Sketch: a definition of referential inconsistency in the subject's terms, the syllogism form; its application to G, to the ω-complete witness, to PA plus not-Con; the subject's referential integrity stated, open whether as a belief of the foundation or a property proved. References: Cantor 1883 and 1891, Hilbert 1926, Frege 1892, Russell 1905, Tarski, Kronecker, Gödel 1947, Cohen 1963, Poincaré against Hilbert 1904, Feferman 1960 held.

*The essential fact and its derivation.* Infinity is not something language can sensibly refer to; the universal quantifier expresses generalization over all, not that it can be removed and replaced with "for none"; to refer to the totality is to express only what can be reflected in zero, and this symmetry is fundamental to the subject. In arithmetic's terms: every number has a successor, totality expressed in the absence; the absence of quantity is referred to as an undifferentiated identity, zero; to refer to the object directly is to refer to something indistinct from zero. Sketch: the fact stated as the subject's one belief, with the "for none" reading of the quantifier left open for his word; its derivation from the successor axiom and zero's place. References: Dedekind 1888 held, the simply infinite system and the base element with no predecessor.

*The completion.* Adding quantity and quantifying multiplicity are complete in the subject; studied in the modular arithmetics where zero is both the smallest number and the successor of the largest; the subject's completion related to modularity in the context of an unrepeated totality, said by "there is no number whose successor is zero"; specified reflectively in PA. Sketch: addition and multiplication complete as theorems; the modular arithmetics as the completions; the unrepeated totality as PA's own axiom read as the subject's commitment. References: Gauss 1801 held.

*The factorial interpretation and the rule.* The number divided by everything up to N is the factorial-like thing, the product of the primes below N; the one divided by everything is zero, and PA proves it is the only one. Under the hypothesis that the factorial-like thing at the top is a finite number, two simple theorems: it is a Goldbach counterexample, since every prime in the list divides it, and its neighbours are a twin pair, since none does; the hypothesis refutes itself, the number plus one bringing another prime, so in PA they are theorems about nothing, the hypothesis referring to the completion as a number it cannot be. A form of induction that never closes in the finite, since the assumption of a completion proves another one, and closes only at zero, which has the completion as its divisors. The rule, proof by symmetry: what is proved of the thing at the top under the hypothesis that it is a number is read at the hypothesis's only referent, zero; the counterexample is pushed off to the totality, and if zero is not a counterexample neither is that; the pairs extend out to the totality. Its one commitment: the totality at the top is indistinct from zero insofar as arithmetic can refer to it. Not the standard model, not Cartesian, not a rule to import into that picture; justified by the subjective choice between subjectivity and incompleteness. One belief where the old version held one per conjecture; the conjectures theorems, not axioms. Sketch: the rule as a schema beside induction, the hypothesis a Π₁ condition PA refutes for every nonzero number, the licence a reading at zero, the conclusions Π₁ and refutable, the refutation clause "should PA find a counter example, we commit to the idea that our specification is inconsistent" standing; safety through the frontier results, each conclusion consistent with PA by H and true under Σ₁-soundness by G; the parity problem as the precise statement that no premise proves the conclusion in PA, so the rule is a licence across exactly that barrier. The factorial returns from the notebook's refused axiom forms in a new role, the interpretation the rule is derived from, which is the difference between an axiom and a rule. References: Hardy and Littlewood 1923 held, Chen 1973, Selberg's parity problem, Euclid.

**Part Three, The Object of Study.** A description of a thing where limits seem to go off, and the thing out there conceptually the torus with no hole: on the Riemann sphere every direction to infinity loops back to one point, zero's mirror under inversion, and the field refers to that point, adds it as an object, the Cartesian act; the torus whose hole has shrunk to a point is the sphere with its two poles glued into one, so positive and negative infinity meet at zero itself, every circle through the pinch a modular circle and all of them sharing only that point. The subject asserts less than the drawing: all you know is the symmetry; you do not know where it loops, and it may not; the object out there cannot be distinguished from zero in any direction. Given as a potential resolution, not claimed, of the object of study in analytic number theory, whose object is the behaviour at the limit, the primes at infinity, the zeta function on the sphere, the circle method's passage from the counts in every completion to the integers: that object is zero insofar as arithmetic refers to it, the counts in the completions are the whole of what can be said of it, the passage to the limit is the rule. Then the proofs by the rule, basic arithmetical proofs: Goldbach, twin primes with the shift by two as its reflection, Riemann's beginning on the sphere where the field keeps the poles apart, the functional equation the reflection about the critical line and the hypothesis that every zero is its own mirror. Goldbach the ending, as the preface says. Sketch: the description in the subject's terms with the sphere and the torus as the field's pictures, the ruling of 29 October standing that negatives make no sense in the PA imagined; the two proofs; the beginning of the third. References: the sphere's topology, a pinched torus a sphere with two points identified, citation to be found; Riemann 1859; Vinogradov 1937 and Helfgott 2013; Zhang 2013, Maynard 2013, Polymath8; Bhowmik and Halupczok on the Goldbach count and RH.
