- <a id="r1"></a>**R1** The thing the book draws per chapter is named for what it is in the framework's own words, or is not there. *Seen: the code, read whole, with no word that means something other than the thing.*
- <a id="r2"></a>**R2** A component that stands in a chapter's apparatus consumes the data model: it reads the chapter, its files and the manual through the tree, and is handed an object of the model where it must be handed anything. *Seen: no interface standing in for a class, no state threaded by a prop.*
- <a id="r3"></a>**R3** The manual is a composition of parts each of which is one thing with its own element and its own rules, and the Format that is the manual holds only what is the manual's: that the chapter is read beside its files, which file, and the arrangement. *Seen: the manual's file read whole; each region's file read whole.*
- <a id="r4"></a>**R4** The pattern is reusable and named: a chapter's presentation in a book is a Format said of the chapter, the book places chapters and nothing else, and a second presentation is built the same way. *Seen: the catalogue's card around a book's synopsis built as the second one.*
- <a id="r5"></a>**R5** Nothing a reader sees changes but what is named here, and every press costs what it cost. *Measured: each book against the galley bound at the previous commit, pressed in and out, folded, the three readings; the presses timed.*

## <a id="decisions"></a>Decisions

- <a id="d1"></a>**D1. The chapter is the page, and there is no wrapper.** The framework's own [Paginated](../../package/src/libraries/Paginated.tsx) says what a page is: a book that is Paginated marks each of its pages `pa-page` at the bind, marks the open one `pa-open` in its `defines`, and hides every page that is not open by that class, on the chapter's own element. The library's layout extends Paginated and then drew a `div` around every chapter and marked that instead, which is the same thing said twice in two words. The wrapper goes: the book draws each chapter in its region and the Paginated's classes and rule do the rest. The region keeps the framework's word, `pd-pages`, since it holds exactly the Paginated's `pages`. The layout's own hiding rule goes with the wrapper. *Chosen over* drawing only the open chapter, which would be lighter at load and cost a mount at every turn, because the framework decided it and the library builds on what it gives.
- <a id="d2"></a>**D2. The pages are everything the book draws in the region, and the body is the reading order.** The layout's `pages` are the synopsis and every chapter the book draws there, the appendix among them, so that Paginated hides what is not open; the book's `body` is the chapters in reading order with the appendix left out, which is what the turn walks and the numbered entry counts. `pages` meant the second thing and is renamed, since the framework already uses the word for the first.
- <a id="d3"></a>**D3. The front is the synopsis as a page.** When nothing is bookmarked the layout's `open` is the book's synopsis, which Paginated then marks open; the base book draws nothing of its own for a front. The manual's book keeps opening on its first chapter of the body.
- <a id="d4"></a>**D4. A chapter's presentation in the region is a Format said of the chapter; the book places chapters and nothing else.** `Manual` is one: its layer is the spread, the chapter's element beside its files. `Desk` is the second, in the catalogue: the card around a book's synopsis with its jacket, its line, its *read on* and its way to the book, and around the catalogue's own synopsis as its front. The base book's `page()`, `front()` and `opening()` and the catalogue's `desk()` go; the catalogue says `Desk` of its books' chapters and of its synopsis at the bind, as the index says `Entry` of each row. *This is the reusable pattern, and it is the framework's: what a chapter is, an annotation says; how it is shown, a Format says; where it stands, the book says.*
- <a id="d5"></a>**D5. A layer around a page hides itself with the page.** Paginated's rule hides the chapter's element, and a Format's layer stands outside it; so the spread and the desk read their chapter's `pa-open` and wear it on their own element, as the folder reads its section's, and hide themselves otherwise.
- <a id="d6"></a>**D6. Components consume the model and reach up the tree.** A file press and a listing are given the `$Append` they stand for, an object of the model, and read the chapter, the name, the type, the lines and the colour off it; the manual they belong to is the chapter's, `append.chapter.annotations.expressed($Manual)`. The interface that stood in for the manual and the prop that carried it go. The manual's state is `shown`, the `$Append` in front, the first when none has been pressed. The module cycle between the listing's file and the manual's is used inside methods only, which is how the package itself declares its cycles.
- <a id="d7"></a>**D7. The regions of the spread are nouns with their own element and rules.** `Panel`, the file panel, writes the tab bar and the listings; `Tabs`, the bar, writes a file tab per append, the words tab, the dock and the options; `Rail` writes a file press per append; `Grip` is the tab that pulls the split back, with its skeleton. Each is a writing with a `write()` of its own and a styled container carrying its rules; the spread keeps only the grid and the words' typography. Each is fetched through `$()` so a book may swap one. They are documented in two chapters of the manual beside their code: The Panel, and The Rail. *Chosen over* annotations whose notes draw the regions, because a note is drawn inside its writing's element after its text, which is the words column, and a region stands beside the words.
- <a id="d8"></a>**D8. The table's files are the entry's.** The entry's layer draws the chevron and a file press per append for a row whose chapter is a manual, reading `leads.is($Manual)` and `leads.annotations.find($Append)`; the manual no longer reaches into the table at the bind, and its `rows` go. A consumer filtering by checking is the rule of [Sprint 104](109-sprint-104--parts-and-the-manual.md).
- <a id="d9"></a>**D9. The context is the book's own class.** The layout wears `pa-manual` on the book while the open chapter is a manual, read in its `defines` as it reads `pa-turned`; the manual's `context` goes. `pa-manual` on a chapter says the chapter is a manual and on a book says the book is in one, and every rule names the level beside it. `Root` stays and is no longer a stand-in: it is the root of the tree, which is the computer science word for the row at its head, and here it is the book.
- <a id="d10"></a>**D10. A chapter with files that is not a manual shows its words.** Its files are documents beside it, cited or shown as figures by the chapter itself; the base no longer draws a file panel for every chapter. The design book's two chapters that append sketches are the case, and what they lose is a panel of listings nobody designed.

## <a id="pitches"></a>What `.public` might grow, written for his word and not built

- **A note drawn by its owner.** Today a chapter draws the note of every annotation it carries, after its text. An annotation that could say *draw me only where my owner draws me* would let a Format's layer compose other annotations' notes beside the words rather than after them, which is the literal form of *"one annotation calls or runs other ones"*. Measured once this week: drawing a note in the layer as well doubles the document.
- **Paginated's front.** Paginated opens on the cover when nothing is bookmarked; every book of his library opens on its synopsis. The layout overrides `open` for it. A `front` on Paginated would say which page is open when none is, and the override would go.
- **Paginated's write across.** Paginated marks the open page by writing `pa-open` into the chapter's classes from the book's `defines`, a cross-chemical write that the branch's own rule says costs a second draw, and that React in development reports on every press as a component updated while another renders. A page annotation on each chapter that reads the book's open in its own `defines`, as the entry reads the bookmark for its row, would mark the same class with no write across and no second draw.
- **The package's noun sentences.** `Cover`, `Part`, `Append`, `Synopsis`, `TableOfContents`, `Author`, `Subject` each specify *a cover is said of a chapter*, *a part is said of a chapter*, *an author is said of a cover*: a noun where *said of* takes a predicate, which is the sentence he refused in his library, *"does one say a manual?"* His library's are rewritten as English about the noun on his *"rewrite both"*, 2026-10-10; the package's would read *a cover is a chapter at the front of a book*, *a part is a chapter's grouping*, *an author is a line of a cover*, and are his to word. The `@specify` sentence and the `$check` message change; nothing else does.

## <a id="the-names"></a>The names, audited, and renamed on his sign-off

**Doug, 2026-10-10, on `appends`, `shown` and `readings` and on the sentence *a manual is said of a chapter*:** *"This code makes no sense. I want you to be far more thoughtful about member names, and if you need to, choose longer member names. When choosing names, I want you to write down ALL related member names that I have come up with in .public, and really ask yourself what makes crystal clear sense in this metaphor, and what is non-semantic jargon that is drawn from other metaphors… if you aren't choosing member names in the CORE library, they don't need to be quite so succinct… tend to nouns, not be cute… does one *say* a manual? **Two faults he found in the first table, 2026-10-10, and the rule that corrects both.** *"Does a book place something? See the anthropomorphism?"* and *"What is the panel? What is the arrangement? That is very very very general. You don't think you will have name collisions when you want to arrange something on another UI element? You are reaching for my one-word names everywhere… that doesn't scale. It can work for the base library because that is the core grammar… Unless you are truly defining the meaning of Arrangement in this library, you are doing no one a favor with that name."* So: **a book does nothing a book cannot do** — chapters have a place in the layout, a chapter is at a place, the layout lays out — **and a one-word noun is the core's privilege.** The package's `parts`, `pages`, `rows` define the grammar; a library's class is a compound that says which thing of which, `FilePanel`, `TreeFolder`, `CatalogueDesk`, and is defined by the pair, so the next panel or desk collides with nothing. A getter may stay one word where its owner qualifies it, *the manual's files*, *the book's body*. *"The rest of it is a step forward."*
