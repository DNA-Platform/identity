***How the look of a page is debugged through the layers that draw it, as fast as it can be — written from one day, 2026-09-20, in which the Wikipedia look of Doug's library was restored, every address became the compiler's, and most of the hours went to finding where a rule lived rather than to changing it.*** **Doug, at the close: "How can we make it better? … Did you maintain a single connection to the browser and refresh? Did you work on the compiled output to prototype when hot reload fails and then backpropagate? Did hot reload allow you to work in front of the compiled output?"** *The answers were no, no and only for one layer, and this book is what those three answers teach.*

**Read it in order the first time.** *[The loop as it stands](01-the-loop-as-it-stands.md) is the facts — what reaches the page how, and what each step cost, measured. [The probes](02-the-probes.md) is what was written to see, and the rule every probe obeys. [One browser, kept open](03-one-browser-kept-open.md) is the fastest loop there is and why the day did not have it. [Working on the compiled output](04-working-on-the-compiled-output.md) is the prototype-and-carry-back Doug asked about. [The traps](05-the-traps.md) is what cost an hour each, so it costs the next reader a minute.*

## Chapters

1. [The loop as it stands](01-the-loop-as-it-stands.md) — The four layers that draw a page and how each reaches it — the sheet hot at 1.4s, a theme through a 4.7s build and a bind, the markup only through src — with every step's cost measured on 2026-09-20 and where the hours actually went.
2. [The probes](02-the-probes.md) — The four probes written to see a page — the rendered tree with its computed boxes, where things stand, one hot-reload round trip, and the photographs — the rule every probe obeys (print how you exited, match on the thing), and where they belong: design/, beside qa.
3. [One browser, kept open](03-one-browser-kept-open.md) — Doug's three questions answered honestly — no single connection, hot for one layer and blind, no prototyping on the output — and the tool the day was missing: one browser kept open, connected to in milliseconds, asked one question at a time, waiting for readiness rather than sleeping.
4. [Working on the compiled output](04-working-on-the-compiled-output.md) — Prototype where a change is cheapest to see, carry it back to where it is true, delete the prototype — a table of which questions the browser answers for free, the one time the day did it right, and the two things never prototyped in the output: an address and a class.
5. [The traps](05-the-traps.md) — Eight traps that each cost between ten minutes and an hour — the stale dist, the dev server's stale compiler, a class grepped in a bundle, a page from a failed bind, the heredoc, the reference that died, the eaten non-breaking space, the fixed sleep — each with its rule, and the seven ideas that would retire them.
