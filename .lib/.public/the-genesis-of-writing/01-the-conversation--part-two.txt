Book as the closure condition — not a container you may use but the condition under which writing exists at all. Nothing is outside a book, including the things that would ordinarily stand outside: the catalogue of books, the architecture of the library, the logs of its construction, the librarian herself. Each of those, on being brought in, arrives as a book. The catalogue is a book. The record replaces the thing recorded. The librarian is her autobiography.

So the metalevel isn't excluded and isn't privileged — it's admitted on the same terms as everything else, which is what makes the closure hold rather than leak. A system that described itself from outside would have an outside. This one doesn't.

And Chapter falls out of that rather than being declared. Once Section exists and everything lives in a book, the thing that holds sections is a chapter — that's what it's called in a book, and there's no other context for it to be in. Which follows how Section arrived too: Section didn't need a level so much as a Heading, the canonical that means it. Chapter gets its identity from position in the closure.

So the API doesn't check anything — add pushes, replace swaps, ensure does its three cases, remove and find scan by type — and the annotation's own specifically(writing) is where uniqueness and placement get tested. That's the genome: each annotation brings its own piece of the host's specification.

It also supersedes three earlier things, which I've recorded: E26's front-by-default and its four modes (add now goes to the end; the modes become named methods), and E22's throw (ensure replaces a parent class instead — which resolves the same-class question, since "a subclass is there" includes the class itself).

Reading Composition again with that focus, here's what I see.

What it adds. A grade (level), two admission modes on it, an openness to non-writing, a body (parts), a position within its own kind (depth), parenthetical fixed false — and, from E34, canonicals: the part whose subject proxies all the others, with the literal levels 0–3 having none.

How it builds on Writing. It continues the same sort one step further. Writing sorts source into contents and annotations — body and tail. Composition sorts contents into parts and the rest — the writing and what isn't. Everything it computes is a further filter of what Writing already computed. Nothing stored twice.

The pattern. This is the part I'd want you to look at. Writing has, on the tail: a declared collection (annotationTypes) built at bond, a computed list, five operations by type, and annotation.specifically(writing). Composition looks like it has the same four things on the body:

canonicalTypes — declared, must come first in order. I've added this one to the model (marked as mine) because it directly replaces Section's and Book's hand-written canonical tests with one inherited test against the declaration. That's the same relation annotationTypes has to "declared annotations were built."
parts — computed, as annotations is.
the five operations on parts — not stated, but ensure(TableOfContents) in Book's bond constructor would be the first real use: the one canonical nobody authors.
canonical.specifically(composition) — not stated either, but the canonical means its composition (E7) and its subject is its parent (E34); Heading's "I am first and my parent is a Section" already has exactly this shape.

What it must specify. The two pairs are opposites; closed ⇒ source is all writing; every writing in the body admitted by level under the current mode; depth agrees with the parent; not parenthetical; canonicals first, in order.

Two things the API itself raised. Protecting source immediately caught Referent reading its host's source for the key — it now reads contents, which is also the right thing. And Types differ by name, not class: find(Type) returns every Type, and ensure(new Type("Cover")) is a no-op if any Type is present, so Cover uses add. What "type" means in the five operations for a Type is the one question the API leaves.
