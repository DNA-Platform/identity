**The record of the redesign of `.public`, which is another draft of it, and the documents that redesign is built from.** Doug, 2026-09-20: *"Listen we are starting over. The key is to build these, and then build outward."* And: *"I need you guys to understand that this is another draft of .public. We want to preserve features or ask how they translate. But we have to build the core, get the compiler working, and THEN build the rest of the components onto this foundation."*

**The source is a conversation Doug held with Claude Desktop, *The Genesis of Writing*, in which he gives birth to the library the way he would if he were coding: one message at a time, each a specification, corrected forward.** Claude Desktop holds that conversation as three documents — an event stream of Doug's messages, a compiled object model in TypeScript, and implementation notes — and this book carries the conversation and all three ***verbatim, as resources beside short chapters that say only whose they are and how they are replaced.*** They will be replaced: Claude Desktop writes newer versions as the work progresses, and a resource is overwritten whole while the chapters that comment on it stand. *Doug: "You will get better if you write it down verbatim in a branch and then add chapters that comment on it."*

**The order this branch builds in is fixed:** *the core from the stream; then the compiler working over it; then the rest of the components translated onto that foundation, each of the first library's features preserved or Doug asked how it translates.* The package beside this library, `library/.public/package`, starts with an empty `src` on the same ruling — *"we clear out a section. We should start with empty src and decide what to pull back in."* Chapters after the four documents are the team's commentary: what we need to understand better, and the rulings Doug gives as we read.

## Chapters

1. [The Conversation](01-the-conversation.md) — Doug's conversation with Claude Desktop, verbatim in two parts as he pasted it on 2026-09-20: part one from the text level, through Text becoming Writing, Letter, Composition and Annotation, sections and headings, to "a chapter is born"; part two from Book as the closure condition through Claude Desktop's reading of E65 and the three documents it maintains. The parts are text resources beside this chapter and are replaced whole.
2. [The Event Stream](02-the-event-stream.md) — Claude Desktop's event stream of the conversation, verbatim as the markdown resource beside the chapter: one entry per message from Doug, E1 to E65 in this copy, each a specification with his corrections marked, then the audit of the compiled model against the stream and the vocabulary sorted into what is in the model, what is from $Chemistry, what is about the model, and what is Claude Desktop's own. Replaced whole when he sends a newer one.
3. [The Compiled Object Model](03-the-compiled-object-model.md) — Claude Desktop's compiled object model, verbatim as the TypeScript resource beside the chapter: every member cited to its event, his readings marked as inferences and his additions as inserted without a genesis, the open items marked; written to two principles, that the bond constructor is unsafe and the guarantees are in specify, and that each annotation applies its specification to its writing. A document, not yet the code.
4. [The Implementation Notes](04-the-implementation-notes.md) — Claude Desktop's implementation notes, verbatim as far as they reached us: the class tree and levels, the cross-cutting rules, which property each specify test guarantees and which open item threatens it, the classes member by member with why each test exists, the inventory of his inserted terms, the twenty open items, and the double-check log. Cut by the fifty-thousand-character limit of a queued message inside the log, at the fifth pass; the remainder is owed.
5. [What We Need to Understand Better](05-what-we-need-to-understand-better.md) — The commentary the documents are read against, kept apart from them: Doug's rulings as they land, dated and verbatim — specification wherever safety can be compiled out, no companion types, the compiler emitting only the link, a test mode with a scaffolded suite, the Genesis as canon where it speaks and a sketch where silent — then what is answered by reading, what remains his, the nine questions from studying the Genesis again with his answers, and what goes to Claude Desktop. Edited as answers arrive.
6. [Answering Claude Desktop from the Source](06-answering-claude-desktop-from-the-source.md) — Claude Desktop's forty-five questions answered from the Genesis and the first library's source under Doug's rule that the Genesis is canon and a divergence in the source is a change he intended or a gap the source solved. Opens with the corrections his reply forced the next day — four of seven departures were the first library carried as if it were the Genesis, each owned and ruled: the cover first and carrying its annotation, the canonical a heading and first, the Canonical a Letter, specifications run under a formal flag, no Referent — then the table of divergences, the answers by number, and the four things we did not understand with Doug's answers.
7. [What to Tell Claude Desktop](07-what-to-tell-claude-desktop.md) — The message that carried what this branch learned to Claude Desktop, sent 2026-09-21, with Doug's rulings marked as his and the team's readings as readings, numbered against his open items; and his reply, verbatim as the resource beside it, with Doug's rulings folded in. What has been sent is marked sent, never deleted; what arrives is written down before it is discussed.
