## What a Theme is, and when

**A Theme is a Format said of a book that holds the values everything drawn in the book is set in: eight reactive properties, `font`, `size`, `leading`, `measure`, `space`, `ink`, `paper`, `link`, and one sheet of rules for the marks.** A book always has one: `$Book` stands the framework's Theme in its `$Define`, so `book.theme` answers the expressed Theme and never nothing, and *a book has one theme* is a rule of its specification beside one cover, one synopsis and one table. Uniqueness is expression's: every theme takes the themes behind it out of expression, so exactly one is ever expressed, the front's. A library's theme is a subclass setting its eight, `ink = 'white'`, and extending the sheet; a book class stands it in its own `$Define` after `super.$Define()`, so it lands in front of the framework's, and the class types `theme` as its own theme's class. A theme written on a book, `<Dark />`, stands in front of both the same way.

**When another theme:** a theme is said of a book and of nothing else, by its specification. A subtree in other values — a dark chapter in a light book — goes through a Format that provides, [below](#formats), reaching another theme through its `theme`; the declarations that would make that theme's values take effect beneath it are owed, so today its subtree reads that theme's values as the fallbacks of its variables.

## How the values flow

**Set on the theme, handed down by its provider as variables, declared once on the theme's element.** The theme's bond makes a *contract* from its eight with styled-components' `createTheme`, prefix `pd`: the same eight, each a `var(--pd-ink, black)` reference with the class's value as fallback. Its provider, a chemical of its own, hands that contract to ThemeProvider, made once so its identity never changes, and draws the theme's sheet wrapped once with the declarations of the eight from the raw fields, `--pd-ink: black;`, on the sheet's own element. So a rule anywhere beneath that interpolates `theme.space` reads the variable, a class is generated once per component and never per value, and a value written on the held instance after mount, `book.theme.ink = '…'`, moves one declaration and regenerates no consumer's class. **Measured 2026-09-29 and pinned in a promise:** the context is one object across the write, every class the same, and every writing beneath the book still redraws once, chemistry's diffusion of a write on an annotation to the writing it annotates — chemistry's to change, pitched.

**Three ways to read a value, and which to use.** A styled component reads through the provider's props, `${({ theme }) => theme.space}`, styled-components' own form; the theme's type is declared once in `Theme.tsx` as the eight, each optional beneath a Format that provides. Never through a closure over the theme, which the promises forbid. Code reads through the book, `this.theme.space` on a Format or `this.$book.theme.space` anywhere, typed, for a computation at draw, a mark to put on a writing, or a check. A control writes through the same path, `book.theme.ink = …`, and the write is reactive. A rule never contains a theme's literal: no colour and no length that is not a variable or a structural constant, and a promise reads `src` for one.

## <a id="formats"></a>What a Format is, and which kind

**A Format is an annotation that is, or adds, the element its writing draws as, and every Format has a theme.** It is an annotation first — it marks the writing, `writing.classes.add(this, 'pa-…')`, takes others out of expression, specifies — its `style` is the element, and its `theme` is its book's, typed as the base Theme and thrown where there is no book; a Format specialised for a library's theme narrows the type with a cast, the cast Doug allows, or overrides the property to reach another theme. Three kinds, chosen by what the element needs to be:

| the element needs | the Format's `style` is | examples |
|---|---|---|
| **only its semantics** — the tag says what it is, the sheet dresses it by mark | a plain tag | Bold `b`, Emphasis `em`, Underline `u`, Cover `header`, TableOfContents `nav` |
| **rules of its own to be what it is** — a grid, a sheet, a panel | a styled component declared inside the class, made once per instance in the bond when it needs the instance, its structure and every layout property its own, its values read from the theme | Table's grid, Theme's sheet |
| **to provide its theme to its subtree** | `themeProvider = true`: the bond wraps the style in a provider handing `this.theme`'s contract | Theme, and a Format reaching another theme |

**A Format exposes its own properties the way Table hands its grid `$columns`: as transient props on a component made once in the bond,** `this.style = props => <Grid $rule={this.rule} {...props} />`, or as marks on its writing that the sheet dresses. It never hands itself as a theme. A Format that adds a container wraps the writing's own element, and the container wears `pd-container`; a Format that replaces the writing's own container, as Table does through `containers.replace`, is the element itself, wearing the writing's id and marks, and takes the writing's Inline or Block out of expression as a pair does. A Format with no `style`, Synopsis, is a Format for its power over the writing's text.

## Marks, the sheet, and where meaning lives

**Three layers, and one law.** The theme's sheet opens with the order statement, `@layer pd.invariants, pd.theme;`, keeps its own element's declarations unlayered, and lays its rules for the marks in `@layer pd.theme`; a Format's styled component is unlayered, so a Format's rule wins over the sheet's regardless of specificity and no rule anywhere repeats a class to win. Beneath both, first, `@layer pd.invariants` holds the rules that are an annotation's *meaning* — a parenthetical present to a reader and not to the eye, a blank keeping its place, a closed page gone, a self-reference without an underline — each `!important`, each in the note of the annotation that means it, so a theme that never mentions the mark changes nothing about it. The head is ordered by the order styled components were defined, so the invariants, from `Writing.tsx`, print first and the sheet's statement appends `pd.theme` after them; the binder writes nothing for it, and the regression reads every bound page's head for that order. **The law across the three: no property on one element is written by two authors,** and a promise reads the served sheet against every Format's rules to hold it. Layout is the Format's — Table's gaps, its cells' padding, its paragraphs' margin; skin — weight, colour, rule — is the sheet's, by mark, from the theme's values; meaning is the invariant's.

**The three tools, and the rule for choosing.** A styled component, local, when an element must have rules to be what it is. The sheet, global and layered, when a mark should look a certain way in this library. And React adding classes, the cheapest and most dynamic, when what varies is a state or a trait of an element that already exists: a page open, a tab selected, a kind labelled — a class toggled costs nothing and the sheet does the rest, which is why Paginated marks `pa-open` rather than styling it. *Structure is a Format, look is the sheet, state is a mark, quantity is a theme value.* The kinds of unseen, and what the meaning requires of each, are [the development story's rows](02-theming-and-formatting.md#where-a-look-lives).

## Sprinkling Formats, and staying themeable

Doug: *"One could probably make many format annotations and sprinkle them around for application-like user experiences. But perhaps then things are hard to theme."* They are hard to theme the moment a Format carries a literal. Kept to the rule above — structure its own, every quantity from the theme, skin left to the sheet by mark — fifty Formats recolour under one theme change, and the guarantee that a book has a theme is what lets a Format be written assuming the values are there. An app-like surface, a tree, tabs, a card, is Formats for its structure, marks for its states, and the theme's values for everything that is a quantity.
