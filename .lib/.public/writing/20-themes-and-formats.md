## What a Theme is, and when

**A Theme is a Format said of a book that holds the values everything drawn in the book is set in: eight reactive properties, `font`, `size`, `leading`, `measure`, `space`, `ink`, `paper`, `link`, and one sheet of rules for the marks.** A book always has one: `$Book` stands the framework's Theme in its `$Define`, so `book.theme` answers the expressed Theme and never nothing, and *a book has one theme* is a rule of its specification beside one cover, one synopsis and one table. Uniqueness is expression's: every theme takes the themes behind it out of expression, so exactly one is ever expressed, the front's. A library's theme is a subclass setting its eight, `ink = 'white'`, and extending the sheet; a book class stands it in its own `$Define` after `super.$Define()`, so it lands in front of the framework's, and the class types `theme` as its own theme's class. A theme written on a book, `<Dark />`, stands in front of both the same way.

**When another theme:** a theme is said of a book and of nothing else, by its specification. A subtree in other values — a dark chapter in a light book — goes through a Format that provides, [below](#formats), reaching another theme through its `theme`; the declarations that would make that theme's values take effect beneath it are owed, so today its subtree reads that theme's values as the fallbacks of its variables.

## How the values flow

**Set on the theme, handed down by its provider as variables, declared once on the theme's element.** The theme's bond makes a *contract* from its eight with styled-components' `createTheme`, prefix `pd`: the same eight, each a `var(--pd-ink, black)` reference with the class's value as fallback. Its provider, a chemical of its own, hands that contract to ThemeProvider, made once so its identity never changes, and draws the theme's sheet with the declarations of the eight inline on the sheet's own element, `style="--pd-ink:black;…"`, so the sheet itself is the class's own and a book's pages share one file of it ([Sprint 95, U8](../projection/100-sprint-95--pages-formats-and-words.md#u8)). So a rule anywhere beneath that interpolates `theme.space` reads the variable, a class is generated once per component and never per value, and a value written on the held instance after mount, `book.theme.ink = '…'`, moves one declaration and regenerates no consumer's class. **Measured 2026-09-29 and pinned in a promise:** the context is one object across the write, every class the same, and every writing beneath the book still redraws once, chemistry's diffusion of a write on an annotation to the writing it annotates — chemistry's to change, [pitched](../../../chemistry/.lib/projection/00-planning.md#pitch-cascade) as a second case of the cascade Sprint 85 pitched.

**Three ways to read a value, and which to use.** A styled component reads through the provider's props, `${({ theme }) => theme.space}`, styled-components' own form; the theme's type is declared once in `Theme.tsx` as the eight, each optional beneath a Format that provides. Never through a closure over the theme, which the promises forbid. Code reads through the book, `this.theme.space` on a Format or `this.book.theme.space` anywhere, typed, for a computation at draw, a mark to put on a writing, or a check. A control writes through the same path, `book.theme.ink = …`, and the write is reactive. A rule never contains a theme's literal: no colour and no length that is not a variable or a structural constant, and a promise reads `src` for one.

## <a id="formats"></a>What a Format is, and which kind

**A Format is an annotation that is, or adds, the element its writing draws as, and every Format has a theme.** It is an annotation first — it marks the writing, `writing.classes.add(this, 'pa-…')`, takes others out of expression, specifies — its `style` is the element, a styled component, which may also wear a class of its own through styled-components' `attrs`, `selection.b.attrs({ className: 'pa-bold' })`, the writer's choice and needed only when another sheet must address that box, and its `theme` is its book's, typed as the base Theme and thrown where there is no book; a Format specialised for a library's theme narrows the type with a cast, the cast Doug allows, or overrides the property to reach another theme. Three kinds, chosen by what the element needs to be:

| the element needs | the Format's `style` is | examples |
|---|---|---|
| **only its semantics** — the tag says what it is and the browser draws it | a styled tag with an empty template, wearing its class | Bold `b`, Emphasis `em`, Underline `u` |
| **a semantic wrapper** — a header, a nav | a plain tag, the Format marking its chapter instead, so a library dresses the chapter by the mark and the wrapper stays a wrapper | Cover `header`, TableOfContents `nav` |
| **rules of its own to be what it is** — a sheet, a panel | a styled component declared inside the class, once per class in a field and never in a bond — the one statement, [Theming and Formatting](02-theming-and-formatting.md) — its structure and every layout property its own, its values read from the theme; when the look needs the instance, a plain component made in the bond closes over it and may hand its values to the styled one as transient props | Theme's sheet; a library's panel; Libby's explorer tree, which draws its leaves in a plain component |
| **to provide its theme to its subtree** | `themeProvider = true`: the bond wraps the style in a provider handing `this.theme`'s contract | Theme, and a Format reaching another theme |

**A Format exposes its own properties as transient props, through a plain component its bond makes around the styled one the class declares,** `this.style = props => <Panel $rule={this.rule} {...props} />` with `Panel` a field of the class, or as marks on its writing that the sheet dresses; a Table, which is no Format since 2026-09-30, does the second, and the sheet is its grid. It never hands itself as a theme. A Format that adds a container wraps the writing's own element, and the container wears `pd-container`; one that replaced the writing's own container through `containers.replace` would be the element itself, wearing the writing's id and marks, and none in `src` does. A Format with no `style`, Synopsis, is a Format for its power over the writing's text.

## <a id="the-rule"></a>The rule, in one line

**A kind of writing is dressed by the sheet, and a Format dresses itself.** Ruled 2026-09-30, ending a long back and forth — Doug: *"Don't we want to use real styled components?… it feels like this should just be a normal application of styled components."* A Paragraph, a Section, a Title draws a plain element with a class and has no template of its own, so its look is the Theme's sheet, the book's typography, the one global sheet every styled-components application keeps. A Format is a styled component, so the look of its own box is its own template, reading the theme's values. A title or a paragraph standing inside a cover is still a kind of writing, so the sheet dresses it, by the cover's class: `.pa-cover .pd-title`. A library changes a Format in the three ordinary ways: a theme value, an extension of its styled component, or a replacement — his pattern, a different `$Cover` exported as `Cover` and used in its place. A Format's own rules beat the sheet's, so a property a Format owns is changed by a value or a replacement and never from a sheet; and a class on a Format's element is part of its styled component, so a subclass that writes its own `style` decides for itself whether to wear one. **And the mark stays on the writing's own element**, which no format in front can move: a library says `.pa-cover` for a cover's card and `.pd-canonical.pd-chapter` for the chapters it counts, and never reaches a wrapper through what it holds, since a format standing in front puts its own layer between and the wrapper is then not adjacent — [the trials of Sprint 95](../projection/100-sprint-95--pages-formats-and-words.md#trials).

## Marks, the sheet, and where meaning lives

**Three layers, and one law.** The theme's sheet opens with the order statement, `@layer pd.invariants, pd.theme;`, keeps its own element's declarations unlayered, and lays its rules for the marks in `@layer pd.theme`; a Format's styled component is unlayered, so a Format's rule wins over the sheet's regardless of specificity and no rule anywhere repeats a class to win. Beneath both, first, `@layer pd.invariants` holds the rules that are an annotation's *meaning* — a parenthetical present to a reader and not to the eye, a blank keeping its place, a closed page gone, a self-reference without an underline — each `!important`, and since [Sprint 95, U5](../projection/100-sprint-95--pages-formats-and-words.md#u5) all four in the Theme's own sheet, the one global sheet, so a theme that extends the default keeps them and a theme that never mentions the mark changes nothing about it; until then each stood in the note of the annotation that meant it, as a global style. The binder writes nothing for it, and the regression reads every bound page's head for that order. Layout that is a mark's, the Table's grid, is the sheet's too, by the mark. **The law across the three: no property on one element is written by two authors,** and a promise reads the served sheet against every Format's rules to hold it. Layout is a Format's where a Format is the element, a header's box, a panel's grid; the grid of a Table, an annotation, is the sheet's by its marks; skin — weight, colour, rule — is the sheet's, by mark, from the theme's values; meaning is the invariant's.

**The three tools, and the rule for choosing.** A styled component, local, when an element must have rules to be what it is. The sheet, global and layered, when a mark should look a certain way in this library. And React adding classes, the cheapest and most dynamic, when what varies is a state or a trait of an element that already exists: a page open, a tab selected, a kind labelled — a class toggled costs nothing and the sheet does the rest, which is why Paginated marks `pa-open` rather than styling it. *Structure is a Format, look is the sheet, state is a mark, quantity is a theme value.* The kinds of unseen, and what the meaning requires of each, are [the development story's rows](02-theming-and-formatting.md#where-a-look-lives).

## Sprinkling Formats, and staying themeable

Doug: *"One could probably make many format annotations and sprinkle them around for application-like user experiences. But perhaps then things are hard to theme."* They are hard to theme the moment a Format carries a literal. Kept to the rule above — structure its own, every quantity from the theme, skin left to the sheet by mark — fifty Formats recolour under one theme change, and the guarantee that a book has a theme is what lets a Format be written assuming the values are there. An app-like surface, a tree, tabs, a card, is Formats for its structure, marks for its states, and the theme's values for everything that is a quantity.
