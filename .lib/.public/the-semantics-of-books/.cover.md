# The Semantics of Books

This book is where we work out the object model of the library metaphor — the type hierarchy of `$Composition`, `$Book`, `$Chapter`, `$Title`, `$Author` that [`@dna-platform/lib`](../../package/) will realize in [$Chemistry](../../../chemistry/.lib/..representivity/.cover.md). It is not the finished specification. It is the *derivation*, kept honest by pointing at where each piece of the understanding came from.

**One chapter is the exception, and it is the one to read first if you are picking the work up.** [The Levels of Writing](15-the-levels-of-writing.md) is the **settled account** of what the composition level turned out to be once it was built — the six levels, how writing reaches a bond constructor, the parse, use and mention, and what a figure is. Everything else here derives; that chapter reports. It exists so a session can start from one page instead of a sprint record.

The understanding came from one place: a conversation Doug held on 2026-07-18, titled [*The Semantics of Books*][conv], in the Semantic Reference Theory project. **Doug is the object of record.** SRT's canonical semantics are library semantics, and this is where the semantics of the book — the unit everything else is built from — got laid down.

The rule of the book is strict, and it is the library's own specification of [autonomy](../../../../.claude/library/teamspeak/05-autonomy.md): **Doug's words appear only as attributed quotes; everything that is not a quote is the author's own reading.** The raw conversation is external, non-library material — a person thinking out loud, correcting, exploring — and it never enters the library by being transcribed into the library's voice. It enters only as quotation held inside an authored interpretation. So a quote is Doug's, marked as his; the prose around it is the author working out what she makes of it, and is answerable to her, not to him. The narrative evolves the way the conversation did — by correction, from simple to complex — but the conversation itself stays quoted, never laundered into settled prose.

## How this book cites its source

The conversation is raw material, and turning raw material into books that refer back to it is how a library forms. So the reference has to be durable — and we learned it cannot be a line number. The conversation file has no per-message anchors, and its content is **not yet committed** (the working copy holds ~3,100 lines the last commit does not), so both line numbers and any commit-pinned URL would rot. The durable anchor is therefore the **verbatim quote** — the one thing that does not move — identified by a short descriptive name for the passage, attributed to its speaker, and disambiguated with a link to the conversation ([uuid `8dbb8e2b-d4c8-413e-aba0-45b382969754`][conv]). When the conversation is committed and pushed to `DNA-Platform/dna-library`, commit-pinned line anchors can be added on top; until then, quote-and-link is the reference. This is the convention for every book here that synthesizes a conversation.

The primary source is a sibling repository: [`../dna-library`][conv]. If it is not present, the citations degrade to their quoted text, which still says exactly what was said and who said it.

## The shape of the derivation

The whole thing is built on one idea Doug states plainly: **a level of representation has one primitive, and everything at the level is that primitive** — the way everything is a set in set theory, or an object in OOP. The semantics of books name two such levels and the passage between them. And beneath them both is the root everything specializes from — the **symbol**, from SRT's own foundation. We build from the bottom.

5. [The Canonical Echo, and Why Cover and Contents Are Views](06-the-canonical-echo-and-views.md) — Doug's worked-out polymorphism of compositions (projection up, flattening down), and the author's reading that Cover and Table of Contents are therefore `view()`s — native to `$Chemistry`'s `view()`, `look()`, and perspectives.

5. [The Evolutionary Root — Symbol and Literal](05-the-evolutionary-root-symbol-and-literal.md) — What the whole system evolves FROM — Doug asked whether we start with a Symbol that could literally be any sort of thing and specialize through validation. The answer is yes, and it is not in the books conversation but in the foundation: the SYMBOL is one of Semantic Reference Theory own primitives, a thing whose whole nature is to be about a referent, with minimal self-structure and maximum subjectivity — the atomic unit of metaphor. Its complement is the LITERAL, object-only, able to be pointed at but never to point; every act of reference pairs the two. The book types are these roots growing bodies: the referential lineage is symbols (a name is a symbol carrying one target, a TITLE is a name that is also canonical — the particle where the two lineages legitimately fuse — a reference is a symbol composed of a name and a locator, a link and an author are references specialized by validation), the composed forms are literals, and the canonical is a ROLE rather than a lineage. So reference and title are SIBLINGS under the symbol rather than one inheriting from the other. AND DOUG CORRECTION IS LOAD-BEARING: a chemical is a symbol for a thing that renders in React, which is a UI fact, while the SRT symbol is a semantic fact — two different notions wearing one word, so a symbol class was never something the chemical already is. Then he retired the class entirely: pointing turned out to be the general and the symbol its special case, so what we build is the reference. Symbol and Literal stay as the CONCEPT — the Dewey decimal and the book, the essence of catalogues — and the class that points is the reference.
6. [The Subjective Subject and the Library](07-the-subjective-subject-and-the-library.md) — the top of the book level: biography, autobiography, and the library. Doug's reasoning that a total catalogue must have a self-cataloguing summit is quoted; the author's reading is what it means for the code.

7. [The Symbolizing Dyad, and the Register of Classes](08-the-symbolizing-dyad-and-the-register.md) — Doug's conjecture that one representative-and-body relationship recurs at three scales, `$Symbol : $Literal` :: `$Subject : $Literature` :: `$Catalogue : $Library`; and the running register of every class the derivation has named.

8. [Composition and Collection](09-composition-and-collection.md) — Doug's distinction between two container operations — composition contains, collection references — and the passages where he names composition a monad.
10. [Closure Under Books](10-closure-under-books.md) — Unfolds the phrase the derivation had been using — Doug's building/blueprint/location example read as two moves (internalization gives a thing an account; reference reaches between accounts), closure as a GUARANTEE about reaching rather than an inventory (Borges avoided), the two regresses (downward never terminates and need not; upward must, at the summit), and Doug's correction that this is STRONGER than set theory — sets do not semantically afford representation, while referring to anything conceivable is what writing is FOR. Ends on the boundary: the library is closed under books; reality is not closed under the library; the librarian's reality is.
11. [Idealism, or Accuracy](11-idealism-or-accuracy.md) — The author's own claim corrected: calling internalization a cost smuggles in an alternative that does not exist. Doug — 'when do you ever have the thing' — and Kant's noumena; Nancy on the visual pathway, where no stage receives an apple. The comparison the objection needs cannot be performed, so what looked like mathematical idealism inverts into epistemological and ontological accuracy: not 'reality is representation' but 'the knowable is what can be represented,' which declines to posit access nobody has. Closes on what closure GAINS — the map/territory distinction moves inside, and fidelity becomes checkable.
12. [The Category, and What Escapes It](12-the-category-and-what-escapes-it.md) — Doug's 'category theoretic structure apparent here though not reducible to,' both halves. What is apparent is literal: Sprint 47 built a category without naming one — referents as objects, references as arrows, then as composition (associativity proven in spec), and ref as identity, with a letter being its own reference at the floor; read-after-ref is identity, making referents a retract. What escapes: objects have interiors, there are two container operations rather than one, and the self-containing top is REQUIRED where set theory forbids it. Then why the rivals cannot reach subjectivity — ZFC has no from-where; Yoneda gives perspective ON, never perspective HAD — and the double-entendre proved by well-ordering rather than admired.
13. [The Author's Fixed Point](13-the-authors-fixed-point.md) — Doug's six-step walk of the author loop, and the misreading it forestalls: the autobiography does not author itself. Identity of REFERENCE, not of thing — closure forbids the author arrow from pointing at a person, so it travels author to subject to canonical and lands home, never touching the being. The derivation that falls out: an ordinary biography's author arrow ESCAPES, so the autobiography is the only book whose author reference does not lead out of it — the only possible terminus. Author as endomorphism, the self as its fixed point, the structural test for subjectivity, and Arthur's two-cycle prohibition that must be specified before the test means anything.
14. [The First Person and the Third](14-the-first-person-and-the-third.md) — The species distinction: first-person libraries have one librarian whose author arrows come home; third-person libraries (IXP) have author arrows that escape BY DESIGN. So the fixed point moves from 'author equals subject' to 'described equals describing' — this library's canonical is the theory it realizes, its identity a process rather than a possession, its sprint chapters and autobiographies load-bearing rather than documentation. Truth hopping layers (Dewey, live) yields a reference that BINDS: the theory governs and the code yields. Then the boundary nobody may cross — two accounts of one uninternalized being, a Fregean identity that must be asserted rather than derived, the found form (About the Author and its signature), the criterion (a wall, not a 404), and .me as the mirror of .claude with the closure enforced by the repository.
15. [The Levels of Writing](15-the-levels-of-writing.md) — THIS IS THE SETTLED ACCOUNT OF v1, WHICH NOW STANDS IN .archive/ — the chapter opens with a measured marker saying so, because the model was rebuilt as v2 across Composition, The Block and The Bind and this account was not rewritten with it. Six of its claims no longer describe the code: seven levels rather than six and a book IS writing now, role and use-vs-mention gone from 9 files to 0, the figure and its caption gone from 3 to 0, valid() gone from 37 files to 0 in favour of specify() and a specification of dollar-prefixed rules, something DOES carry a number, and a section no longer composes sections. Two of its claims were never true of either tree — there has never been a level getter anywhere, so LEVEL ALONE DECIDES was always an intention, and too-high does not throw. One claim it made as a wish is true for the first time: ONE WALK, WRITTEN ONCE now exists as utilities/Parser.tsx with all seven levels calling it, though it divides by what each level accepts rather than by level. Read it for the reasoning, which is Doug's and mostly survived; read The Bind for the code. Beneath the marker the account still stands: six levels each declaring what it composes, how prose divides and when it is valid; A SECTION COMPOSES PARAGRAPHS FLAT, so a heading of any depth is a title standing among the paragraphs and depth is a containment to bolt on later; THE CANONICAL STANDS AT ZERO, a section's title being its part zero, which makes the book's cover-and-synopsis shape turn up one grade down; writing arrives as ONE BLOCK because everything below a document is inline; the parse WRITES NOTHING and DOES NOT JUDGE, so a composed part can carry a parent and an invalid part keeps its failure rather than vanishing; THE NOTATION IS THE LEVELS' OWN, markdown being how writing is written rather than a kind of it, and ONLY A BLANK LINE DIVIDES PROSE so three lines are a stanza; A FIGURE IS A CAPTION and nothing more; a PHRASE is a name at word grade because an author's name was claiming to be a sentence. Still open: the summary as the first paragraph, syntax as a typed word, dynamic layering so one Code can move its own level, a list as a paragraph whose items are its sentences, and whether the fixed hierarchy holds at all.
16. [The Reference, and What It Points With](16-the-reference-and-its-locator.md) — The reference half of the source, derived at last — the book's cover had held it under Still to come since it was written, and The Bind closes on references as the next subject. A REFERENCE IS NOT A KIND OF NAME, thrown out by Doug with an object anyone can picture: a card in a card catalogue carries a name and is not one. He guessed the anatomy as a name and a link and marked the guess as a guess; the research refined it to IDENTIFICATION COUPLED WITH LOCATION, the same two halves in every habitat — card, citation, footnote, index entry, bibliography, hyperlink — and ibid. proves it by subtraction, eliding the identification and surviving on the locator alone. So a reference sits on the COMPOSITION edge, which is where this chapter and chapter 03 part company, and the fork goes to Doug. TWO LOOPS, AND ONLY ONE IS IDENTITY: Doug first derives identity from cataloguing by transitivity, then replaces it with something cheaper that stands — the title points at its own book, so by virtue of having a title every book has its identity, the title being its name. The catalogue loop then stops competing and becomes membership: the title loop says IS ITSELF, the catalogue loop says IS HELD, and three grades of self-arrow ladder out — named, held, and self-holding, which is the summit and only the summit. THE THREE GRADES OF ARROW ARE DOUG'S AND WERE UNRECORDED HERE: going up is subjective, down is objective, across is relational, each route carrying a colour that says how much of each it is. Valid routes are optional ascent, free relational middle, optional descent — which recovers reading, scholarship, being catalogued, and at full extent ANALOGY. Then Doug reads his own grammar back to its root: the factorization is just the transitive of the foundation's clause, and its fragments recover relationship, reference, representation, identification and individuality one at a time — true only if books HOLD the relationships between them rather than merely having them, which is a constraint on our code as much as on the theory. INSCRIPTION: a reference is in the pages and has a site, and the two directions are not alike — outgoing is authored, incoming is COMPILED and explicitly not a primitive, which is the cover's citedBy thread answered as a reading rather than a stored member. COMPOSING TWO REFERENCES IS AN ACT OF AUTHORSHIP — a composite exists when somebody wrote the journey down, which is what a survey is — and A STORED PATH DOES NOT NEED ITS ENDS, since the catalogue guarantees reaching and authorship guarantees getting home, so only the middle is stored. Measured against that, v1's nine files show the mess in their sizes: the three that should carry the abstraction are the three smallest, the two largest name ten things each, and four specific divergences follow. v2 has NOTHING — one empty interface listed for deletion — so this is a first writing, not a refactor. Closes on seven open questions and answers none: whether a reference is composed or primitive, what a locator is once addresses are ruled out, whether index belongs to the writing or the reference, and the sharpest — WHETHER A TITLE IS A PARAGRAPH OR A REFERENCE, since The Bind mints it from paragraph residue while identity rides on it.
