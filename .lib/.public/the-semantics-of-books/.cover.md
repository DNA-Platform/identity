This book is where we work out the object model of the library metaphor — the type hierarchy of `$Composition`, `$Book`, `$Chapter`, `$Title`, `$Author` that [`@dna-platform/lib`](../../package/) will realize in [$Chemistry](../../../chemistry/.lib/..representivity/.cover.md). It is not the finished specification. It is the *derivation*, kept honest by pointing at where each piece of the understanding came from.

The understanding came from one place: a conversation Doug held on 2026-07-18, titled [*The Semantics of Books*][conv], in the Semantic Reference Theory project. **Doug is the object of record.** SRT's canonical semantics are library semantics, and this is where the semantics of the book — the unit everything else is built from — got laid down.

The rule of the book is strict, and it is the library's own law of [autonomy](../../../../.claude/library/teamspeak/05-autonomy.md): **Doug's words appear only as attributed quotes; everything that is not a quote is the author's own reading.** The raw conversation is external, non-library material — a person thinking out loud, correcting, exploring — and it never enters the library by being transcribed into the library's voice. It enters only as quotation held inside an authored interpretation. So a quote is Doug's, marked as his; the prose around it is the author working out what she makes of it, and is answerable to her, not to him. The narrative evolves the way the conversation did — by correction, from simple to complex — but the conversation itself stays quoted, never laundered into settled prose.

## How this book cites its source

The conversation is raw material, and turning raw material into books that refer back to it is how a library forms. So the reference has to be durable — and we learned it cannot be a line number. The conversation file has no per-message anchors, and its content is **not yet committed** (the working copy holds ~3,100 lines the last commit does not), so both line numbers and any commit-pinned URL would rot. The durable anchor is therefore the **verbatim quote** — the one thing that does not move — identified by a short descriptive name for the passage, attributed to its speaker, and disambiguated with a link to the conversation ([uuid `8dbb8e2b-d4c8-413e-aba0-45b382969754`][conv]). When the conversation is committed and pushed to `DNA-Platform/dna-library`, commit-pinned line anchors can be added on top; until then, quote-and-link is the reference. This is the convention for every book here that synthesizes a conversation.

The primary source is a sibling repository: [`../dna-library`][conv]. If it is not present, the citations degrade to their quoted text, which still says exactly what was said and who said it.

## The shape of the derivation

The whole thing is built on one idea Doug states plainly: **a level of representation has one primitive, and everything at the level is that primitive** — the way everything is a set in set theory, or an object in OOP. The semantics of books name two such levels and the passage between them. And beneath them both is the root everything specializes from — the **symbol**, from SRT's own foundation. We build from the bottom.

2. [Composition](02-composition.md) — Level One, built around Doug's definition of a composition; the author's reading of the canonical as `parts[0]`, text as the leaf, title as the canonical text.

3. [Inheritance and Composition](03-inheritance-and-composition.md) — the two relationships Doug names between types — differ by validation, differ by composition — and the author's placement of `$Reference` and `$Author` under those two edges.

4. [The Book and Subjectivity](04-the-book-and-subjectivity.md) — Level Two, on Doug's correction that subject precedes author, and that the catalogue's *subject* and the subjective *subject* name one thing; the author's reading of where `$Book` sits.

5. [The Canonical Echo, and Why Cover and Contents Are Views](06-the-canonical-echo-and-views.md) — Doug's worked-out polymorphism of compositions (projection up, flattening down), and the author's reading that Cover and Table of Contents are therefore `view()`s — native to `$Chemistry`'s `view()`, `look()`, and perspectives.

6. [The Subjective Subject and the Library](07-the-subjective-subject-and-the-library.md) — the top of the book level: biography, autobiography, and the library. Doug's reasoning that a total catalogue must have a self-cataloguing summit is quoted; the author's reading is what it means for the code.

7. [The Symbolizing Dyad, and the Register of Classes](08-the-symbolizing-dyad-and-the-register.md) — Doug's conjecture that one representative-and-body relationship recurs at three scales, `$Symbol : $Literal` :: `$Subject : $Literature` :: `$Catalogue : $Library`; and the running register of every class the derivation has named.

8. [Composition and Collection](09-composition-and-collection.md) — Doug's distinction between two container operations — composition contains, collection references — and the passages where he names composition a monad.
10. [Closure Under Books](10-closure-under-books.md) — Unfolds the phrase the derivation had been using — Doug's building/blueprint/location example read as two moves (internalization gives a thing an account; reference reaches between accounts), closure as a GUARANTEE about reaching rather than an inventory (Borges avoided), the two regresses (downward never terminates and need not; upward must, at the summit), and Doug's correction that this is STRONGER than set theory — sets do not semantically afford representation, while referring to anything conceivable is what writing is FOR. Ends on the boundary: the library is closed under books; reality is not closed under the library; the librarian's reality is.
11. [Idealism, or Accuracy](11-idealism-or-accuracy.md) — The author's own claim corrected: calling internalization a cost smuggles in an alternative that does not exist. Doug — 'when do you ever have the thing' — and Kant's noumena; Nancy on the visual pathway, where no stage receives an apple. The comparison the objection needs cannot be performed, so what looked like mathematical idealism inverts into epistemological and ontological accuracy: not 'reality is representation' but 'the knowable is what can be represented,' which declines to posit access nobody has. Closes on what closure GAINS — the map/territory distinction moves inside, and fidelity becomes checkable.
12. [The Category, and What Escapes It](12-the-category-and-what-escapes-it.md) — Doug's 'category theoretic structure apparent here though not reducible to,' both halves. What is apparent is literal: Sprint 47 built a category without naming one — referents as objects, references as arrows, then as composition (associativity proven in spec), and ref as identity, with a letter being its own reference at the floor; read-after-ref is identity, making referents a retract. What escapes: objects have interiors, there are two container operations rather than one, and the self-containing top is REQUIRED where set theory forbids it. Then why the rivals cannot reach subjectivity — ZFC has no from-where; Yoneda gives perspective ON, never perspective HAD — and the double-entendre proved by well-ordering rather than admired.
13. [The Author's Fixed Point](13-the-authors-fixed-point.md) — Doug's six-step walk of the author loop, and the misreading it forestalls: the autobiography does not author itself. Identity of REFERENCE, not of thing — closure forbids the author arrow from pointing at a person, so it travels author to subject to canonical and lands home, never touching the being. The derivation that falls out: an ordinary biography's author arrow ESCAPES, so the autobiography is the only book whose author reference does not lead out of it — the only possible terminus. Author as endomorphism, the self as its fixed point, the structural test for subjectivity, and Arthur's two-cycle prohibition that must be specified before the test means anything.
14. [The First Person and the Third](14-the-first-person-and-the-third.md) — The species distinction: first-person libraries have one librarian whose author arrows come home; third-person libraries (IXP) have author arrows that escape BY DESIGN. So the fixed point moves from 'author equals subject' to 'described equals describing' — this library's canonical is the theory it realizes, its identity a process rather than a possession, its sprint chapters and autobiographies load-bearing rather than documentation. Truth hopping layers (Dewey, live) yields a reference that BINDS: the theory governs and the code yields. Then the boundary nobody may cross — two accounts of one uninternalized being, a Fregean identity that must be asserted rather than derived, the found form (About the Author and its signature), the criterion (a wall, not a 404), and .me as the mirror of .claude with the closure enforced by the repository.
