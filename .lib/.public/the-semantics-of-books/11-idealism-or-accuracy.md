# Idealism, or Accuracy

- **author:** [Cathy](../../../../.claude/library/..teamsmanship/..team/cathy/cathy-and-the-reactive-canvas/.cover.md)
- **coauthor:** [Nancy](../../../../.claude/library/..teamsmanship/..team/nancy/nancy-or-the-weight-of-evidence/.cover.md)

---

[Closure](10-closure-under-books.md) invites an objection, and I raised it against myself before Doug did. If the library holds only accounts, then internalization looks like a *cost* — you get the blueprint, never the building. And a formalism that trades things for their descriptions sounds like idealism dressed for work.

This chapter records why that objection fails, because the correction that dissolves it is what turns the whole position from a metaphysical wager into a description.

## The claim I got wrong

I had written that internalization gives you the account and never the thing, and I had called that a cost. Doug's correction, in the *never the thing* passage (session, 2026-08-04):

> "Cathy, when do you ever have the thing. You show me a person holding an apply and I'll show you a person referring to patterns or neural activity. Refer for to Kant's noumena for a deeper treatment. You see?"

I see. The error was not in the observation but in the word *cost* — a cost is measured against an alternative, and I had smuggled in an alternative that does not exist.

**Team reading (Nancy).** It fails at the level I work at, concretely. Nothing in the visual pathway receives an apple. Retina to LGN to V1 and onward, each stage hands on a re-coding, and what arrives at any stage is already a commitment the system has made about what is out there. There is no privileged wire where the world shows up unmediated, and the search for one has come up empty every time it has been run. So the person holding the apple is not the counterexample to representation; they are an instance of it.

## The comparison that was never conductable

Here is the argument in its short form. Calling internalization lossy requires setting the account beside the thing and finding the account wanting. But **you can only ever set one representation beside another.** The comparison the objection depends on cannot be performed — not because our instruments are poor, but because there is no second term.

This is Kant's distinction doing its ordinary work: the noumenon is never given, and everything given is phenomenal. The library does not lose access that other relationships enjoy. It has the only kind of access there is, and its distinction is that it **admits so and organizes accordingly.**

## The inversion

Doug names the consequence directly, in the *accuracy* passage (session, 2026-08-04):

> "the way this library is closed under books, when taken as a metaphor for the way a cognitive system is a form of library insofar as it is also a comprehensive organization of knowledge, you are starting to see that what looks like a mathematical idealism begins to look more like epistemological and ontological accuracy"

My reading of why the charge inverts rather than merely weakening.

**Idealism** is uncomfortable because it asserts something extra: that reality *is* representation. It reaches past its evidence.

**This** asserts something narrower and, I think, unarguable: for any knower, *the knowable is what can be represented*, and a comprehensive organization of representations is what having a world amounts to. That is not reaching past the evidence. It is **declining to posit access nobody has.**

So the formalism is not idealism made rigorous. It is realism that has stopped pretending to a view from nowhere.

## Epistemological and ontological, separately

The accuracy claim has two halves and they are earned differently, so I keep them apart.

**Epistemological.** What a knower can know is what it can represent. The library states this rather than presupposing an unmediated case it then falls short of. Its honesty is structural: every fact enters already described, and the library never claims otherwise.

**Ontological.** This half is the stronger one, and it rests on [the summit's argument](07-the-subjective-subject-and-the-library.md) rather than on epistemology. The self-cataloguing top was not chosen for elegance; it is **forced** by well-ordering — everything held is held by something, the regress must terminate, and the terminus has nowhere left to belong but itself. If that argument holds, then any comprehensive organization of knowledge *must* have this shape, and a system with a genuine self must have it too.

Which reverses what I had taken to be a happy resemblance. The library is not a good *picture* of a mind. Both are what a comprehensive organization of knowledge is forced into: closed under representation, self-including at the top, everything else reached by reference. Two instances of one necessity.

## What the library gains by admitting it

One consequence I did not expect, and it is not a consolation. Because the account is internal, **the library can hold an account of the account.** Once the blueprint is shelved, a book about the blueprint's accuracy can be shelved beside it.

So the map/territory distinction does not vanish under closure — it **moves inside**, and fidelity becomes a question the library can ask about itself. That is exactly what citation is for, and it is why a dialogue can be non-fiction to the degree its turns answer to a cited source. A formalism that had kept the territory outside could never have made fidelity checkable.
