## A library is an identity with closure

**A library is closed under books.** Everything at its level is a book: a catalogue of books is a book, a subject is a book that says with its About what it is about, an author is a book — the autobiography, the one book by its own subject — and the library is the top book, filed under what it is about, which is itself. Nothing points outside. [Levels of Closure](../the-semantics-of-books/01-levels-of-closure.md) is Doug's principle: *"there is value for there being levels where you have a certain type of representation and everything is closed under it. You need something like that to express that which is fundamental."* [On Libraries](../../../../.claude/library/bookkeeping/08-on-libraries.md) is the same fact in the team's own library: an autobiography is the teammate, a subject catalogue is the subject, a library catalogue is the library — *"each level of the type system achieves identity through self-reference at its scale."*

**Closure means there is no outside, and that is what makes a library an identity.** There is no position above a library from which to describe it. Whoever speaks of a library speaks as a book in it — an author, vouched for by the one autobiography or filed under it — and the catalogue *"refuses by non-membership alone"* ([The Soundness of a Knowledge Graph](../the-semantics-of-books/18-the-soundness-of-a-knowledge-graph.md)). A voice standing above the library, reporting on it, is a non-member. It is the narrator the team's [substrate protocol](../../../../.claude/library/teamspeak/09-the-substrate.md) forbids in conversation, and the narrator [On Authorship](../../../../.claude/library/bookkeeping/13-on-authorship.md#personal-libraries-are-first-person) forbids in prose, and it is the same failure in a library: something claiming to know the whole from a place the whole does not contain.

**Identity is where the regress stops.** A book answers for itself — its cover says its title, its author and where it stands; its synopsis says what it is; its table answers for what it holds. A writing three levels down walks up its parents until one answers *I am the book*, and the walk ends there, at a Book that returns itself ([Book](../library/05-book.md)). A subject catalogues itself. The autobiography is by its own subject. At every scale the structure terminates in something that answers for itself, and that terminus is what the word *identity* names here. A library one cannot stand outside of, whose every part answers for itself, is a person's shape; that is why an autobiography grounds it and why a librarian is inseparable from the library they keep.

## Why this decides the code

**The compiler is a reader inside the closure.** It reads what authors wrote — the notation, wherever it stands — and never a tag's class or a prop, because a reader of books reads the books and not the printing press; Doug, 2026-09-25: *"You don't need the compiler to check for anything. You can't! They might subclass them."* It refuses by non-membership, and it writes every address from the catalogue so a wrong reference cannot be written ([The Binder, As Built](../the-catalogue-and-the-specification/07-the-binder.md#reading)). A compiler designed from above reads elements, checks kinds and invents fixtures; a compiler designed from inside reads writing. Sprint 90's reading follows: the binder reads the text an author wrote — prose and strings — and never the machinery around it, and a literal appended to a chapter is placed exactly as written, since it is a representation an author put in the library and modifying it is speaking for them.

**A test library is a librarian's library, or it is not a library.** The one the binder is proved against is a library kept by a librarian named Libby: her autobiography grounds it, The Library is filed under Libraries and about itself, a persona is filed under her, a paper is by the persona, and the manual is her book of the tools her library is built with. Built as a fixture it went wrong twice — an autobiography called a Log, and a librarian filed under herself — and [What a Library Is](08-what-a-library-is.md) records both. What it teaches: every book the compiler is tested against must be a book somebody could have written, filed where they would file it, or the compiler is being proved against a shape no library has.

**A library's tools are its own books.** Code for one's library lives in one's library, as files beside the chapters that document them, printed by figures and imported through a manual's door — [The Reference Manual](11-the-reference-manual.md). A library that reaches outside itself for its tools has an outside, and the closure is gone. Doug: *"a library whose inside contains its own outside, at least in printed form."*

## How to conceptualize one — the questions, in order

1. **Who is the librarian?** Their autobiography is the one book by its own subject: About them, by them, filed under the library they keep. Write it first. Everything else is vouched for from it, one step: *"1. A book that is by its subject — there can be only one of those. 2. Any book catalogued by one that is a subject."*
2. **What is the library about?** The top catalogue is filed under what it is about, said as a subject with words apart from a name: `<Subject>**[[ Libraries ]]( The Library )</Subject>`, `<About>[[ The Library ]]</About>`. A library of one librarian is about Libraries.
3. **What are its subjects?** A book becomes a subject by saying it is about something; others are filed under it and its table answers for them. A book grows into a subject when it can no longer hold what it is about — Doug: *"as one builds their library, the subject catalogue grows because books become a poor container for their contents"* — splitting into a catalogue, the book it was, and the manual of its tools.
4. **Who else may author?** A biography filed under the autobiography is a subject in turn, and what is filed under it may author. A persona is a second representation of the same being; a paper is by the persona.
5. **Where do the tools live?** In the manual, as files accompanying its chapters, each chapter saying what the tool is and how the librarian uses it.

## How to tend one

**Tending a library has an object outside the tending, and so it can be finished.** Doug, 2026-09-27: *"A Librarian keeps it organized to maintain the integrity of the catalogue… One does not only tend to a garden. One just tends to tend to a garden."* The object is the integrity of the catalogue: every book answers for itself, and the catalogue answers for what stands under it. The compiler's refusals are the checklist, and a bind that passes is a library that is whole today.

- **Bind often, and read the refusals as your own sentences.** A fault names something a librarian could say about their library without knowing the compiler exists. Fix the library, not the compiler.
- **Keep the prose legible and the complexity in the annotations.** A chapter's source reads as prose with the occasional figure; the look lives in the theme, the kinds in formats, the tools in the manual. Doug: *"the ability to achieve what you need has to be configurable in a book, not by adding features to the compiler."*
- **Drive the links.** Every row of a table, every mention, every Next, in a browser, landing where it says. A link that lands short is a book that does not answer for itself.
- **Keep your metaphor in your own pages.** The components of a library are library-like — the running head, the catchword, the colophon; a metaphor you find helpful lives in the first-person pages of your own books and nowhere in the shared ones. Doug: *"You exist in the perspective of your library. But it is still a library closed under books."*
- **Speak from inside.** A librarian says *my library*, *my manual*, *the books I keep*. A voice that speaks of the library from above is not in it, and what it builds will not be either. The librarian's first question is the reader's: *can they find what they need?* If they cannot, it does not exist.

**Names.** Doug's: *how to be a librarian*, *closed under books*, *the integrity of the catalogue*. Ours, flagged: none.
