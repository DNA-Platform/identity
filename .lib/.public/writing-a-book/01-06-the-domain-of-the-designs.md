## The rule

**The HTML is the design.** Every sketch in his design book is a page written in HTML, with its regions, its values, its breakpoint and, in the frames, its comments saying what each region is. The page is built from that file, exactly — its values into the theme's fields, its regions into the book's `write()`, its breakpoint into the narrow rule — and the photographs are only the check that the built page matches. Doug, 2026-10-06: *"If you have been working from photos and not html, you have been trying to create something that could have been created far more accurately because we literally designed in HTML."* The sentence that had stood in the sprint chapter — *the sketch's HTML is a rough guide to the look* — was wrong and is struck.

**And nothing is named that is not on a design or in the library.** `HomeTheme` took *home* from a sketch's `h1`; a theme named `LibraryTheme` claimed a library has a look. Doug: *"What is the type of design that you are using to decorate the library page? Is that really what a library looks like? Maybe think about what these designs are, and name them accordingly."*

## What the designs are — read off their files

His design book groups its twenty-five concepts five ways, and the grouping is the first reading. **1 to 10 and 24 to 25 are pages** — *"a different layout, with different tools in different places"*: the shelf after Apple Books, the sources after NotebookLM, the wall after Pinterest, the command line, the front page; side by side after Stripe, the notebook, the workbench; the database after Notion, the map; the title page, the reading view. Each has its own palette and its own markup. **11 to 23 are frames** — *"the same pages inside a different frame… each once darker and once lighter"* — and the thirteen are **one file**, `3-every-concept~011.html` to `~023.html` differing in twelve lines, with four attributes on its `html` element:

| attribute | its values | what it is in the library |
|---|---|---|
| `data-layout` | `side`, `header`, `both`, `two`, `rail`, `cards` | **where the library's bar goes** — a Format said of the book: a side bar, a top bar, both, two top bars, a narrow rail, cards on a ground |
| `data-tone` | `dark`, `light` | **the theme** — a set of values: `--bar`, `--bar-fg`, `--bar-dim`, `--bar-on`, `--bar-line`, `--mark`, `--mark-fg` |
| `data-at` | `library`, `subject`, `book`, `chat`, `author` | **which page of the library is open** — the library's own catalogue, a subject's, a book, a chapter of a conversation, the author |
| `data-view` | `shelf`, `list` | **the rank the books are shown at** — covers, or names in rows |

**A concept is one setting of the four.** 11 is `side` + `dark`, 12 `side` + `light`, 13 `header` + `dark`, 14 `header` + `light`, 15 `both` + `dark`, 16 `both` + `light`, 17 `rail` + `dark`, 18 `rail` + `light`, 19 `two` + `dark`, 20 `two` + `light`, 21 `cards` + `light`, 22 `cards` + `dark`, 23 `side` + `dark` at a `chat`. The concept's name is the tone on the layout — *A Black Side Bar*, *Two Top Bars: White, then Opal*. **So a theme in his library is a tone, and there are two; a layout is one of six; and neither is named for a book.** The three papers of the reading view, 25, are the same thing on that page — `book`, `night`, `white` — and the two modes of the design book are 11 and 12.

**One palette under all thirteen**, in the file's own comment: *"The frame is soft black, the blue between it and white, and white. The soft black `#0c1b1f` and the opal `#c8f4fb` are the coming-soon page's own; the blues are the hue between them walked from dark to light. The CONTENT keeps its own colors: every book a cover of its own, the light accents the opal's wave."* — `night`, `deep`, `blue`, `sea`, `sky`, `opal`, `pale`, `mist`, `white`, `ink`, `soft`, `line`, `me`, `wash`, Cormorant Garamond and Inter. That palette is the library's; the teal of 6 is the manual's own, and the base theme today carries the teal with the library's palette added on, which is backwards and is corrected when the designs are decided.

## What the map decides about names

**A theme is named for its tone, in the file's word — `Dark`, `Light`, or on the reading view `BookPaper`, `NightPaper`, `WhitePaper` — never for the book it dresses.** A layout is named for where the bar goes — the six above. A page of the library is named for what it is at — the catalogue, a subject, a book, a chapter, the author — which are the library's own words already. A view is named for its rank — shelf, list, table. **The library's classes today** — `LibraryBookTheme` as the base, `LibraryTheme` on the catalogue, `DesignTheme` with `GalleryMode` and `LibraryMode`, `Bars`, `Frame` — are renamed to this once the design per book is decided, which is the next step and his: *"We need the design book functional enough that we can associate the designs with the books again."*
