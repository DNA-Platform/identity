## What they are

We can make it its own construct, and it can be at the Sentence level… I would say we want a similar relationship, so the code will be similar, between Title and Heading. Title gets the compiler syntax, so it also has the Reference annotation right?"* And of how it is found: *"They can do a by type canonical (no need to abstract), and Chapter type checks."*

**Canonical is a relationship, and the word always carries its second term.** Doug, 2026-09-30, asked whether one word meant two things once a book's `canonical` was its cover and the cover the one chapter without `pd-canonical`: *"Okay, well yes that is the book's canonical chapter. But it's not the canonical type of chapter. Canonical is a relationship - the canonical chapter of a book. The canonical type of chapter."* The canonical *of* a composition is the part that means it, a chapter's its title, a book's its cover, a section's its heading; the canonical *type of* a family is the type its special types depart from, `pd-canonical.pd-chapter` and never the class alone. A cover is the book's canonical chapter and not the canonical type of chapter, and the two point opposite ways on one element by design, since the part that means a whole is nearly always a special type of its family.

**So a title is a link to itself and the place a table lands.** `<Title>[[ The Argument ]]</Title>` in `1-the-argument.tsx` is compiled to `<Title>[The Argument](/a-paper/#the-argument)</Title>` — a fragment of its book's one page since [Sprint 95](../projection/100-sprint-95--pages-formats-and-words.md#d1), a route of its book with a page of its own from [Sprint 85](../projection/91-sprint-85--headings-and-routes.md#u3) until then; the Title draws "The Argument" inside the anchor its Reference lends it, and its own element wears `id="the-argument"`, its name's slug, which its Referent holds. A cover's title, `[A Paper](/a-paper/)`, wears its book's slug the same way, `id="a-paper"`, on the book's page. The canonical goes both ways — `chapter.canonical` is the title and `title.chapter` the chapter — and neither is stored.

### In use

```tsx
export default () => (
    <Chapter>
        <Title>[[ The Evidence ]]</Title>
        <Section>
            <Heading>What was found</Heading>
            <Paragraph>A chapter that is referred to from elsewhere and refers to nothing itself.</Paragraph>
        </Section>
    </Chapter>
);
```

**A chapter whose title a reader need not see declares it and hides it**, which is how every synopsis and table of the test library is titled: `<Title><Parenthetical />[[ Synopsis ]]</Title>`. The title still stands its Reference and wears its id, so `$[[ The Library / Synopsis ]]` lands on it, and the compiler still names the chapter by it, since it reads the form and not whether it prints.

## How they are extended

- **A library's own title** is a class under Title, or under Sentence with a `chapter` of its own; the chapter finds it by class, so a subclass is its title. **The compiler never sees the class**: it names a chapter by the title form in its file, whatever element holds it — a library may write `<MyTitle>[[ The Work ]]</MyTitle>` and it compiles unchanged, [promised in the compiler's suite](../../package/.binding/catalogue/wellformed.test.ts).
- **A type of chapter** is a class under Chapter; it keeps the canonical by class and stands what it is as annotations — a cover, a synopsis and a table are annotations a chapter carries, [not types of chapter](03-cover-synopsis-and-table-of-contents.md).
- **A chapter file is a function**, default-exported, taking nothing and returning its `<Chapter>`; the book calls it and never renders it, so **it holds no hooks** — [the front matter](01-books-in-annotations.md#a-whole-book-written-out).
- **The title is the location, however it looks.** Doug, 2026-09-27. Its id comes from its name, a reference to the chapter lands on it, and the book turns to a chapter by scrolling what wears that id — never by an element handle, which nothing holds. So a title that does not print must still stand on the page: Parenthetical is to quieten a title and not remove it, a change owed in `.public` ([the audit](../projection/93-sprint-88--the-theme-the-element-and-the-blank.md#the-audit-of-the-code-for-the-next-sprint)); until then `turn()` climbs from the title to its chapter's element.
- **One title form to a file.** A title form names the writing its file is, and a second one naming anything else is `TITLED-TWICE` at compile time — *the one exception is a cover's About, [which names its own book](04-author-subject-and-about.md)*.
