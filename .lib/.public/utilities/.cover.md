**The book of the `utilities` folder of the redraft's package, `library/.public/package/src/utilities`, written to [How a Class Is Documented](../the-coding-style/08-how-a-class-is-documented.md): one chapter per class, four parts each, focused on how the class is extended.** What the folder's classes have in common is that every writing is built with them and none of them is a writing: a writing owns two of the collection and makes one of the specification when the binder asks it to check. They know as little of writing as they can — the collection's author is any instance, and the one writing word it once read, `enforced`, moved to Annotations — and a new utility arrives here only when a class of writing needs a thing that is not writing.

## Chapters

1. [Collection](01-collection.md) — The modification core, on Doug's design of 2026-09-24: a Collection whose every change goes through change and is cited to its author, any instance, with append and prepend placing several values at the end or the front, add their synonym, and remove, replace and revert wrapping it; its values are its changes applied in order, so a revert is exact however changes interleave, and an author that changes again after reverting stands behind the rest, which is why a definition reverts everyone first. Finding joins it by type — find, contains, containsOne, with at and after, all reading through iteration — while modification stays by instance, since in the layers a component is itself the value. A Compilation has a compiled value, the last value set, changed only by set and revert and not iterable, so authors compete and one that sets again wins; the kind of each change is the word of its operation, append and prepend among them. Both are represented by their phenotype and never their genotype, so a change that ends where it began is not news, with the one limit that a component made by a function reads as its source. Since C4, C5 and C6 it holds a writing's annotations, contents, classes and layers, and its id is a Compilation; the placeholder that stood beside it went in C6 with E65's ensure and by-type replace and remove. Extended through change, or as Annotations does through iteration; nineteen promises; commits e1e3836, f0c2eda, d850ceb, 76f9b1b, 862a896, 338bf35, 5653b28, ffe156b, d402a1e and b6b29ce.
2. [Reflection](02-reflection.md) — The one place in the redraft that reflects on a class, written 2026-09-23 on Doug's ruling that the collection hold no such function and that a reflection utility be authored the way v1 authors one: a class with an exported instance, as v1's Reflection is, carrying classOf, the class of a given in any of its four forms; chemical, the chemical of one, parented, which is what the collection's refused made did; and name, the authored class name with the bundler's decoration stripped, v1's own reading, which is why a failure can say which writing it came from without pitching anything to chemistry or parsing a symbol. Two callers by ruling: the collections on the live path, the specification when the binder asks.
3. [Specification](03-specification.md) — The detached specification a writing makes when the binder asks it to check, brought over from the first library and cut to what the redraft needs: a rule is a named $-method that asserts with the framework's check and says the shape of what was wrong, never a roster; rules collected up the prototype chain with a later one replacing an earlier of the same name; check answers the failures rather than throwing at the first, so the writing that made it can gather them with every annotation's and everything in reach. One class per class of writing, extending the parent's; a rule is replaced by naming it again and waived by returning false; never a member, never run but when the binder asks. Three promises of its own, isolated over a plain object.
4. [Identifier](04-identifier.md) — The one function that spells a name as an id, written 2026-09-26 with Sprint 85 on Doug's ruling that a title makes its fragment from its name with the Identifier utility and the url is arbitrary: lowercase, an apostrophe inside its word, an ampersand the word and, one dash between the rest — v1's slug letter for letter — exported as the other utilities are and imported by the compiler, so the fragment it writes and the id a page wears are one function; where the slug stood before and why it moved; three promises.
