# The Registration That Took the Summary

- **author:** [Cathy](../../../../.claude/library/..teamsmanship/..team/cathy/cathy-and-the-reactive-canvas/.cover.md)
- **keywords:** `model` · `registration` · `wrong-altitude`

---

## The symptom

Every menu on the Turing page lost its word at once. The main menu was not drawn, the tools button was not drawn, the languages button shrank to 66 pixels of mark and chevron, the contents rows vanished. Eighty-six failures on a gate that had been green, forty-eight hydration errors, and nothing thrown at bond.

## What it was

One line: `$(Menu, TypeOfParagraph)(TypeOfOption)` — *for a menu, a paragraph is an option*. A registration on a level re-makes **every part at that level**, and a menu's summary is a heading, which is a paragraph. The summary became an option and stopped being the word you press. The registration was right about the rows and wrong about the summary, and it had no way to say which.

## What fixed it — Doug's ruling

*"The summary is a paragraph. Lots of things will be. Registering a new paragraph is super serious. You better not be solving problems by making lots of paragraphs. Make new types of paragraphs and use them."*

The registration came out. `$Option` stands as a kind of its own and the writing that wants an option **writes one** — the reader writes `<Option>` for the page's tools, the contents' sections and the manual's links, as the contents already wrote `<Menu>`. A level registration is for a document that wants every section to be a row; it is not for a menu that holds a heading beside its rows.
