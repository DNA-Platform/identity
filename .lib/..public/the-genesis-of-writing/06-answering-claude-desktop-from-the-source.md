## The two things he said he did not understand

**Where identity lives.** He modelled every canonical as a class at a position, Cover extending Chapter and tested as the first part, and could not tell whether Doug's sentence describes a different design. It does, and it is the Genesis's own: *"annotations are the typing mechanism going forward"* (E32) and *"general types first, then typing placed on them — specific annotations, types of chapters"* (E62), made a ruling on 2026-09-20: *"We have a type as an annotation, but not every instance of writing has a type like it does now. We are doing direct inheritance between the parts."* So **the levels are classes by direct inheritance** — Letter, Word, Sentence, Paragraph, Section, Chapter, Book, each extending what the Genesis says — and **the roles are annotations placed on a writing** — Cover, Synopsis, Table of Contents, By, About, Subject, Type — found by search, never by position. The source had already half-arrived: its book finds its author not by position but by searching its cover chapter's parts for the writing carrying the Cover type (**source**: `library/Book.tsx`). Everything he inserted around canonicals — the declared canonical types, the unspliced direct-children helper, the position tests — was solving a problem the design does not have, exactly as he suspected, and `contents` is the member his helper was reaching for.

**The $Chemistry boundary.** What a chemical is given and what it must supply is written in the framework and its branch library, and section C answers each question from it. In one line: the framework hands a bond constructor its children already built, grouped so that every run of inline content is one block; the class keeps what it wants of them; the parent is assigned before the bond runs; nothing is returned; and drawing is a method the class writes.

## The four things we did not understand, and what Doug answered

**Asked on 2026-09-20, the day the chapter was written. Each answer is his; what follows it is what it settles.**

1. **The line between a level and a role.** Asked whether Heading, Title and Summary are classes as the Genesis has them or roles placed on a writing: *classes, as the Genesis has them.* Heading extends Sentence (E7); Title and Summary are writing that come first in a chapter (E30), found by class among the parts; and E30's order remains a rule the book tests. The roles are Cover, Synopsis, Table of Contents, By, About, Subject and Type.
2. **The parse.** *"Yes, the parse is gone."* Levels are authored; prose is calligraphy; a section without a heading is a void in test mode and nothing in production. The first library's making of levels beneath prose, its representatives, its counting per made part and its recovery of a heading do not come back.
3. **The solved gaps, and something larger in their place.** *"Well, I was thinking that maybe we don't do styled chemicals and this works with styled components directly, and format serves this purpose. We would still print the annotations, which would be mostly parenthetical, and perhaps parenthetical means hidden more than not rendered. For parenthetical, I would drop print and make it parenthetical vs narrative, and narrative can be a prop that is the opposite of parenthetical. Nicer than print."* Three things settle at once. E32's open choice between styled-components and styled chemicals is decided: styled-components directly, and Format is where they serve. E64's unnamed opposite of parenthetical is named: the pair is **parenthetical** and **narrative**, and `print` is gone. And parenthetical leans toward *hidden* rather than *not rendered*: annotations are still printed, mostly parenthetical, so what is parenthetical is on the page and not shown. The format-worn-once test and the failure-naming-its-place walk were not ruled on and stand as our readings.
4. **Quotations.** *"The Quote I talked about can be the basic, and maybe block is a prop for it? We didn't spend a lot of time making complex components, but quote can be one. We want to think about how to span the gap between semantically structured and visually structured and Quote is an example."* One class: the Genesis's Quote, with a block quotation as perhaps a prop on it. And a theme for the components phase is named: spanning the gap between what is structured semantically and what is structured visually, with Quote as the first example.

One proposal remains open rather than answered, since it changes what a file declares: keep the class and the component the framework's export pattern needs, drop the companion type as ruled, let the specification be `specify` on the class as E64 says rather than a separate specification class, and drop the interface unless something outside the class needs it.
