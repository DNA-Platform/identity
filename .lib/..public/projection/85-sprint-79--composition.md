## What Composition adds, in the draft's terms

**A Composition is a Writing that structures the contents of a book: it has a level, it reads its parts out of its contents, and it knows its depth.** Doug, 2026-09-22: *"$Composition will be a type of writing that will structure the contents of a book in a .public library, whereas Annotations will be used to customize them."*

- **`level`** — the grade, 1 for Word and Letter, 2 for Sentence, 3 for Paragraph, 4 for Section, 5 for Chapter, 6 for Book. Whether it is a field each level class declares or a prop the generic Composition also takes, as E12's `<Composition open level=4>` has it, is his to rule.
- **`parts`** — the body: the writing among the contents, one level down, a child of the same class not returned but its parts flattened in, annotations and what is not writing skipped. Computed over `contents`, which the collection's `find` by class makes one lookup and a splice.
- **`depth`** — computed against the parent: one more than a parent of the same class, else 0; Heading takes its Section's and Part its Book's plus one, each stating its own rule.
- **The pairs as annotations** — Strict and Permissive on what the level admits, Open and Closed on what is not writing, each a class whose `specifies` checks its constraint and whose `defines` does nothing; a level stands its defaults in `$Define`, Word, Sentence and Paragraph permissive and open, Section closed, Book closed and strict; the written or given pair overrides as Narrative overrides Parenthetical.
- **The canonical** — from Section upward the first part means the composition: Section's Heading, Chapter's Title then Summary, Book's Cover, Synopsis and Table of Contents. Tested in `specify` by order, per class, since the declared list is Claude Desktop's inference; a missing canonical is a void.
- **What it must specify** — closed means every content is writing; every part at or below the level, or at or one below under Strict; depth agrees with the parent; the canonicals first, in order.

## The liberties, on 2026-09-23

Asked after the review — *"assess the sophistication of the way I decoupled pieces of this system through traits. It is clean. How much of this is mess. classOf? Hmm. Where do you think you took liberties? In all of them, present the thing invented, the problem being solved and why your solution might not be durable"* — this is the register, each as invented, solved, and why it may not last.

1. **`classOf` and `Representation`.** Invented so `is(Strict)` takes the lifted component as well as the class; it reads `component.$chemical.constructor`, an undocumented link from a component to its template. Not durable if chemistry changes how a component knows its class. Durable form: a reading chemistry exports, or `is` taking classes only.
2. **The collection's index**, class-keyed buckets with `chain`, `file`, `forget` and `swap`. Invented for *"think about performance aggressively"*, one lookup per `find`. It duplicates the list's order in every bucket and `swap` is the package's most intricate code; `find` measured a third of a microsecond either way, and E26 said *optimization later*. A filter by `instanceof` would be a fifth of the code.
3. **Overriding by inactivation is lasting.** Narrative flips a Parenthetical's `enforced` and a leaver's act lasts, accepted as a feature. The dynamic case does not round-trip: Narrative given through `$is` and taken away leaves the Parenthetical off. Durable form: uniqueness per pass, jotted; the first to fix.
4. **`applied` and `edits`.** A change of `$is` is noticed by reference and re-makes the edits; identity does not survive, ruled fine, but it leans on chemistry's setter comparing by content before storing: `annotations.is` assigned a fresh equal array re-makes the annotations and loses a flipped `enforced`.
5. **Level's number from the first block's elements joined.** Invented for arguments as content; assumes prose arrives as one block of strings, so `<Level><b>5</b></Level>` is not a number. Durable form: a reading on Writing for what was written as text.
6. **`specify` answering strings.** A failure carries its sentence and not its writing, so the binder cannot say where. Durable form: a failure naming its place.
7. **Rules found by a `$` prefix and replaced by naming again**, carried from the first library. Any `$`-named method runs as a rule and a typo adds one instead of replacing.
8. **`Given` and `made`**: a class and a component are both `function`, so a plain function child would be treated as a constructor; and `made` re-parents what it is handed, so one annotation given to two writings belongs to the last.
9. **The genome's string**, symbol, `[enforced]`, comma: punted, knows only `enforced`; a coupling to a format rather than a fault, since an annotation's own fields still redraw the writing.
10. **Small ones**: the note gated by `enforced` while the body renders regardless; the `(error as Error)` casts; `pd-annotation` added in Annotation's bond after Writing's, which depends on a subclass calling `$Writing` first.
