## The method

**QDA and LDA as the Bayes rule for Gaussian classes.** log P(y = k | x) = −½ log|Σ_k| − ½ (x − μ_k)ᵀ Σ_k⁻¹ (x − μ_k) +
log P(y = k) + Cst; LDA is Σ_k = Σ for all k
([scikit-learn user guide 1.2](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/lda_qda.html)). Two classes: P(Y = C | X = x) =
s(Q_C(x) − Q_D(x)), one half on the boundary ([Shewchuk, lecture 7](../../papers/shewchuk-gaussian-discriminant-analysis/.cover.md)).
In the textbook the class parameters are the maximum-likelihood estimates from the class's own samples.

## The quadratic form, and what each modification does to it

Doug, 2026-11-20: *"I thought we were deforming within the constraint of a quadratic form of some sort. Can you tell
me what I think I am talking about or what might be true? Please be familiar with the normalcies and even
technicalities of modifying this technique."*

**One Gaussian class is one quadratic form.** log N(x; μ, Σ) = −½ (x − μ)ᵀ Σ⁻¹ (x − μ) − ½ log|Σ| + Cst. The mean places
it; Σ deforms the space around it: Σ^(−½) is a linear map under which the class is a round ball, so the only
deformations allowed are stretching and rotating (ellipsoids), never bending. It is the maximum-entropy law for a
given mean and covariance (the third deck, slide 3), so the quadratic form is what assuming only two moments costs.
Two classes: the score is xᵀAx + bᵀx + c with A = ½ (Σ₀⁻¹ − Σ₁⁻¹); LDA is A = 0. *Exact.* Unregularized LDA and QDA
are unchanged by any invertible linear change of axes ((Mx)ᵀ(MΣMᵀ)⁻¹(Mx) = xᵀΣ⁻¹x; log|MΣMᵀ| moves every class by
the same constant), so axes need not be orthogonal; shrinkage toward a sphere breaks this, because the sphere
depends on the axes.

**The standard modifications, as operations on the quadratic form:**
- *Pool* one covariance for every class: A = 0, LDA. Ours: C_E shared by every image class (Santhanam's FAcmb).
- *Shrink* toward a sphere, (1 − γ) Σ + γ (tr Σ / d) I (Friedman's γ, via klaR): rounds the ellipsoid.
- *Interpolate* between a class's own and the pooled covariance, (1 − λ) Σ_k + λ Σ (Friedman's λ): every step is a
  convex combination of positive-definite matrices, so still a legal quadratic form. Ours: the classification's
  Σ_c = (1 − c) C_S + c C_E, with λ tied to the pattern's distance instead of chosen by validation.
- *Constrain its structure*: factor analysis W Wᵀ + Ψ (Santhanam et al. 2009; ours, rank 8); low rank in a class
  subspace plus one variance outside it (Bouveyron et al. 2007); volume, shape and orientation each shared or free
  (Bensmail and Celeux 1996, named, not read).
- *Place the centre by a model* (van Bergen and Jehee 2018): the family is unchanged; only where the form centres.
- *Leave the single quadratic form*:
  - Mixtures (mclust eq. 4.1): a class is many forms joined by log Σ exp, a soft maximum, so the score is piecewise
    quadratic. Ours, twice: each description over its literals, and each feature over four descriptions a side.
  - Bayesian QDA (Srivastava et al. 2007, Theorem 1, eq. 13): with the parameters integrated out, a class's score
    depends on x through |S_h + n_h/(n_h + 1) (x − x̄_h)(x − x̄_h)ᵀ + B_h|. By the matrix determinant lemma (my
    step) that is a constant times (1 + k uᵀ(S_h + B_h)⁻¹u), u = x − x̄_h: the log-density falls with the log of
    the quadratic form, not with the form itself. It keeps the same ellipsoids but gives a far sample fewer bits.
    The paper's motivating regime is few samples against the dimension; ours is 548 neurons against 992 and 2,423
    samples.
  - Calibration (Platt; the third deck): rescales the evidence and leaves the level sets alone.

**Where ours sits, measured (2026-11-20, three_qdas.npz).** Each feature is within 1.53 bits (median at most 0.12) of
the difference between the most probable description on each side (the largest possible difference with four a side
is 2 bits). So each feature is, to within a bit or two, one QDA score between two descriptions, and which two depends
on where the sample lies. Most probable of the eight: pre-DOI spontaneous, not literal, not natural, featureless (90%);
image-evoked, literal, natural, featureful (98%); post-DOI spontaneous, literal, natural, featureful (75%) or literal,
not natural, featureful (25%). Inside a description the literals are a soft maximum of ellipsoids of one shape; how
many literals carry it is not measured.

## The step with no source found

**A class composed of separately estimated parts**: the twin's literal, an offset (0, or pre-DOI spontaneous
activity's displacement d) and a covariance (C_S or C_E), each estimated on its own group, so that a class need not be
the law of any recorded group.

- *Exact*, inside the model: if a sample comes from one of the eight descriptions, each equally likely beforehand, a
  feature is the posterior log-odds of one part (Penny et al., equation 21).
- *Not shown*: outside the model, which is where recorded activity is, that comparing such classes isolates one part.
- *Searched* (2026-11-19, one search): representational models and pattern component modelling (Diedrichsen and
  Kriegeskorte 2017, *PLoS Computational Biology*; Diedrichsen et al. 2011) compare models of activity built from
  components by marginal likelihood and treat factorial designs. A candidate precedent; **not yet read**.
