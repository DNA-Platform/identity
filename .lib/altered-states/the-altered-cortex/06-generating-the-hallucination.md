The direction after the delivery: for each presented image, synthesize the most plausible hallucination the post-DOI network could be experiencing, endogenous to the twins, no external image prior. The scratch figures and scripts live in `src/analyses/most-exciting-image/results/examples/hallucination/`, each figure beside the script that made it.

## The construction, as it converged

One image in, one image out. Keep the stimulus's amplitude envelope, reorganize its **phase** in the band the drug released, and let that reorganization be driven by the network's own activity. The release is graded per frequency by the pre→post filter; the fill is the trial's residual (measured activity minus expected) inverted through the **intact (pre)** encoder, so the coefficients are network-derived. Amplitude conserved, phase decohered in proportion to what was lost, coherent not white — a phase distortion of the scene, not noise added to it.

## Where composition lives, and why image-space attempts fail

The readout is **rank-one separable per cell**: cell `i`'s drive is `u_i = Σ_p m_i(p)·⟨w_i, Φ(:,p)⟩ + b_i` — a spatial mask `m_i` (its RF location) times a feature weight vector `w_i` (its feature bar code), read off the shared core feature map `Φ = Core(x)`; then `r_i = ELU(u_i)+1`, which is **exactly invertible** (monotone). So the population Gram **factorizes**: `(R Rᵀ)_ij = ⟨w_i,w_j⟩ · ⟨m_i,m_j⟩` — feature-similarity times spatial-overlap. Two cells share credit only if they are co-located **and** read similar features. That is the inclusion-exclusion rule, exact and cheap, already in the trained readout with no fitting.

But composition does **not** run by depositing feature vectors and inverting. Forming `Φ̂ = Σ_i c_i · m_i · w_i` and inverting it fails: `Φ̂` lives in a ≤749-dim subspace (energy in ~2 of 64 channels per position) that no image's `Core(x)` produces — it is figure 16's category error one level up, in feature coordinates. The correct composition never forms `Φ̂`; it solves for the **image whose features project correctly**, `R·Core(x) = g⁻¹(r) − b`, as a Gauss-Newton step: with `A = R·J` (readout times the core Jacobian at the stimulus), `δ = Aᵀ (A Aᵀ + λI)⁻¹ ε_u`, `ε_u = g⁻¹(r_t) − g⁻¹(r̄)`, solved by conjugate gradient (~20 matvecs, one VJP + one JVP each, nothing materialized).

**This is the correction the fill was missing.** The current fill `d = ∇_x⟨ε, f⟩ = Aᵀ ε` is that same solve with the overlap-inverse **dropped** — one gradient step. `A Aᵀ` has a huge dynamic range across cells, so `Aᵀ` alone is dominated by whichever directions the core happens to amplify, not by what the residual asks for, which is a plausible mechanism for a fill that came out faint and isotropic. The `(A Aᵀ + λI)⁻¹` is the missing composition term; swapping one backward pass for ~20 CG steps is a one-loop change, same tools, no new dependency. (CD's catch, and correct — it saved a doomed feature-space `Φ̂` build.)

## What activity means in features: exact, and the size of the freedom — MEASURED

`figure 17` / `feature_recoverability.py`. Because `ELU+1` is invertible and the readout is linear in features, **each cell is exactly one linear measurement of the 64-dim feature vector at its own location**: `⟨w_i, Φ(:,μ_i)⟩ = g⁻¹(r_i) − b_i`. So activity determines the projection of the local feature vector onto `span{w_i}` of the cells sampling there, and nothing else. Measured on the trained pre twin over 20 natural images:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| feature map | 64 ch × 28 × 56 = 1568 positions |
| positions measured by ≥1 cell | **167 / 1568 (10.7%)** — the RF strip |
| cells per covered position | median **3** (max 29) |
| **local rank: dims of 64 determined** | median **3** (max 29) |
| local feature **energy** determined by activity | **6.5%** |
| **corr(recovered, true) inside the determined subspace** | **0.98** ← the proof (residual is the 5.7% nearest-vs-bilinear approximation) |
| corr(recovered, true) vs the *full* feature vector | 0.20 |
| population `W` rank / effective (participation) rank | 64 / **51** of 64 |

Read it as: **the inversion is solved and exact**, closed-form, no fitting. Locally the activity pins only ~3 of 64 feature dimensions, and the population vocabulary is *richer* than predicted (effective rank 51, not ~30), so what limits us is measurements per location, not vocabulary.

**Correction (from `figure 18`): do not read that 93% feature null space as "the freedom the hallucination gets to fill."** It is mostly *unreachable* directions, not genuine freedom: the feature map is 64×28×56 = 100,352 numbers describing a 2304-pixel image, so it is wildly overcomplete and almost all of its directions are produced by no image at all. (That is the same fact that kills the `Φ̂`-then-invert route.) The count that matters is at the **image** level, and it runs the other way: a trial supplies **749 independent constraints** against **~246 pixels in the RF strip** — over-determined about 3:1. So within the strip the activity is, by counting, more than enough to pin the image; the real freedom lives (a) **outside the strip**, ~89% of the frame, where nothing constrains anything, (b) in the **nonlinear degeneracy** (the metamer set), which is empirical rather than countable, and (c) in the **released frequency band**. Counting alone cannot settle (b), which is exactly why the metamer sets are the instrument for it.

## The measured constraints

- **The filter is a product, not a coherence.** `H = sqrt(S_post/S_pre)` is `gain × coherence`. Measuring coherence `A` directly (phase-scramble a radial ring, watch each twin's response move, normalize so gain cancels) shows the high-power low band (below ~0.18 cyc/px, 97% of the power) is **coherent and retained** (`A ≈ 1`). The decoherence-with-amplification that *is* the hallucination signature is real but confined to **0.24–0.35 cyc/px** (`A` falls to ~0.1, gain rises), a low-power band. `figure 11`.
- **The resolution wall at native size.** At 36×64 that decohered band holds ~2–3% of the image energy, so a visible hallucination is not generatable at native resolution — the effect is real but starved of bandwidth. The filter tail is a **plateau near 0.43** (the same floor seen at the project's start), reaching 0 only near ~3.6 cyc/px. `figures 12, 15`.

## Upsampling: the image *and* the network, and the canvas principle made rigorous

Upsampling the image gives frequency space, and the filter re-expresses trivially in **content frequency** (fixed under upsampling, not cyc/px, which upsampling divides; extrapolate its plateau tail, not the steep mid-slope — `figures 14, 15`). But to use the canvas you must upsample the **network** too, and not by feeding a big image to the small-kernel core: a kernel fit to a 3-pixel feature still spans 3 pixels, a quarter of the visual angle on a 4× canvas, so every feature shifts 4× in cyc/deg into a band the mouse cannot resolve and the network goes **silently blind** to the band it was actually fit to. The correct rescale **expands the kernels** by the same factor (bilinearly upsample each conv kernel, renormalize, scale strides/pooling; kernel interpolation, not à-trous whose gridding would add its own checkerboard), validated by a one-line unit test: the rescaled core on the upsampled stimulus reproduces the original responses to interpolation error. The core is fully convolutional and `μ_i` are normalized grid coordinates, so positions transfer for free.

Part of Doug's canvas principle is discharged here: content at 0.25 cyc/px on 36×64 is one cycle per four pixels — forced blocky and axis-aligned, which *is* the checkerboard, a rendering constraint rather than a fact about the network. The same content in cyc/deg on a 4× canvas is one cycle per sixteen pixels: smoothly positionable at any phase and orientation, so blocks become oriented blobs and gratings several cycles across a receptive field. The rescaled core's cyc/deg cutoff is unchanged, so nothing between the old and new Nyquist can be network-*determined*.

**But "not network-determined" is not a reason to leave it empty — that was a real error of mine** (I wrote "keep the synthesis band strictly below the cutoff," which throws away the null space instead of sampling it). The fine structure of the animal's percept is not determined by V1 either; it is supplied by everything we did not record. The disciplined move is not to refuse to paint there, it is to paint there **from a prior**, and to be explicit that this is a posterior sample rather than a measurement. That is the next section.

## The generative prior — the right frame, and the published machinery for it

**A prior is not contamination; it is the only thing that produces coherent form.** Dreams and hallucinations are the same family of phenomenon, and a diffusion model painting dreams *bound by the per-trial network activity and by what we know the network represents* is a fair description of the target. The scientific discipline is not "refuse the prior," it is: constrain everything the data constrains, sample the rest, and label the output a **posterior sample, not a measurement**. Four published pieces compose into exactly the construction we want, and the fit is unusually clean:

- **Range-null decomposition ([DDNM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00490)-style).** Split the image into the **range space** of a forward operator (fixed by the measurement) and its **null space** (unconstrained, filled by the prior). **Our measured filter `H`/`A` is that forward operator**, and the released band *is* the null space. This is the exact formalization of "reduce the amplitude of what is there, fill in what is missing" — and it names my error above: the released band is a null space to be *sampled*, not a region to leave blank.
- **[ILVR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02938) (Choi et al. 2021)** — training-free conditioning that locks the **low-frequency** components of the generation to a reference image while the model supplies the high-frequency detail, yielding **diverse** samples that stay semantically aligned. That is our algorithm's shape almost verbatim (retained band from the stimulus, released band from the prior), it needs no retraining, and its diversity across seeds is exactly the per-trial family.
- **[DPS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14687) / BrainDiVE-style encoder guidance.** DPS handles **nonlinear** forward operators directly, and BrainDiVE already perturbs the denoising process with **encoder gradients** to steer generation toward a target neural response. **Our twin is that nonlinear operator**: guiding the sampler with `‖f_pre(x) − r_t‖` binds each sample to *that trial's actual recorded activity*, which is Doug's central requirement and the thing that makes this a hallucination *of this animal at this moment* rather than a pretty dream.
- **[Sensorium-Viz](https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/advs.202520220) (Deng et al., Advanced Science 2026)** — a Diffusion Transformer with a spatial neuron-embedding module that reconstructs **high-resolution images from mouse V1 single-neuron calcium responses**, same Sensorium data lineage as our twins. This is the strongest candidate for the prior itself: it is mouse-V1-native rather than a generic natural-image model, and it dissolves the resolution wall on the *rendering* side without waiting for a retrain.

**What this buys.** The prior stands in for everything downstream of V1 that we never recorded, which is precisely what supplies fine percept structure in the animal. The twin constrains what it can constrain (its band, its per-trial activity); the prior fills the rest coherently. Multiple seeds per trial give the **posterior**, and its spread is not a nuisance — the **dimensionality and image-locking of that spread is the measurement** (low-dimensional and structured ⇒ representational content; isotropic and full-rank ⇒ degradation).

## Working at high resolution: no network surgery needed — the operator is `D`

`figure 19` / `highres_operator.py`. **This is the structural answer to "how do we work on larger images consistently with the network," and it needs no kernel rescaling, no feature composition, and no new theory.** A high-res canvas `X` touches the network only through a downsampling operator `D`, so the partition is exact and computable at any resolution, outside the network:

- **row space of `D` = constrained.** The native band. *All* the data lives here.
- **null space of `D` = free.** The network is provably blind here. Project any content with `v − up(D(v))` and it lands exactly in it.

Verified: adding a **clearly visible** hexagonal lattice (0.9× the image's own contrast) to a 144×256 canvas, confined to `null(D)`, moved the population response by **1.6 × 10⁻⁷ relative** — nothing, to float precision. And gradients flow the other way too: optimizing the 144×256 pixels through `D` drives the native response toward a target (28% MSE reduction in 120 Adam steps).

**This also corrects the pixel-counting framing above, and CD is right that bands are the better unit.** The worry that upsampling dilutes the 749 constraints (pixels grow as `s²`) is unfounded: everything the network can see stays in the native band, so the constrained subspace is **exactly as over-determined at any canvas size as it was at 36×64, forever**. Upsampling adds only free directions. Three bands, then: *determined* (below cutoff, `A≈1`, copy the stimulus), *released* (below cutoff, `A<1`, the measurement, and it lives at native resolution regardless of render size), and *free* (`null(D)`, zero data content, filled by a declared rule and labeled). That maps exactly onto the three epistemic tiers.

**The payoff for band 3.** Because any band-3 rule operates inside `null(D)`, it **cannot** break the native measurement — that is a guarantee, not a hope, and the check is free (downsample the synthesis, confirm the response is unchanged). CD's proposal to fill it by **running the same feature vocabulary at a finer scale** (the readout weights `w_i` are channel-space and carry no scale information; scale lives only in kernel width, which was never measured) is therefore safe by construction, self-similar across octaves, and far more endogenous than an ImageNet prior. The declared assumption is scale-invariance of the vocabulary, which is stated rather than hidden.

## DeepDream with the animal's vocabulary — the working proof of concept

`figure 20` / `deepdream_poc.py`. **This is the first construction in this chapter that actually produces a hallucination**, and it works because it finally uses the regularizers rather than a bare gradient ascent. DeepDream's method is: start from the image (not noise), gradient-**ascend** the image on an in-network objective, under three regularizers — **octaves** (multi-scale, run here as `D_s(X)`, so it reuses the partition above and needs no network surgery), **jitter**, and **gradient smoothing + mean-absolute normalization**. Those three are the entire difference between a dream and the checkerboard we produced earlier; our own `synthesis.py` walker ops are the same family, which we had in hand and had not used.

What we have that DeepDream does not: its objective is arbitrary and its features are ImageNet's (hence dogs in clouds). Ours can be **measured** — the animal's own core features and its own recorded cells. Two variants run on a 4× canvas:

- **(a) core dream** (amplify core features everywhere, whole frame): coherent flowing organic form, **no checkerboard**, but at the settings used it is badly **over-driven** (image change RMS 1.60 vs the image's own ≈0.86 std) and saturates the scene away. Needs a much smaller step/energy budget; it is not a failure of the method.
- **(b) population dream** (amplify the 200 best-recorded cells): **the result.** The scene stays fully recognizable while intricate, fibrous, oriented structure grows out of it **inside the RF strip**, and it drives the recorded population **×1.66**. That is Doug's target stated back in images: same scene, same statistics, imaginary pattern-based structure that rewards staring.

Character is as predicted and should not be oversold: mouse V1 carries no semantics, so the forms are oriented, bandpass and lattice/fibre-like, never objects. Confinement to the strip in (b) is honest, not a bug.

## The per-trial, band-confined hallucination — IT WORKS

`figure 21` / `per_trial_dream.py`. **The construction the chapter has been building toward, assembled and working.** Three theories compose: DeepDream supplies the *mechanics* (ascend the image, octaves/jitter/gradient-smoothing, plus an energy budget so the scene survives); the **per-trial residual** supplies the *objective* — maximize `⟨Ω⁻¹·P_struct·(r_t − r̄), f_pre(·)⟩`, i.e. "what would the scene have to look like for the **intact** encoder to produce the deviation this animal actually produced on **this** presentation"; and the measured **coherence `A(f)`** supplies the *constraint*, projecting the accumulated change onto `released(f) = sqrt(1−min(A,1)²)` in content frequency.

Result on one image, three repeats: every trial drives its own objective **positive** (`+0.84, +3.76, +2.26`; raw residual projections `+0.61, +3.50, +1.97`), retained-band leakage is **6.8 × 10⁻⁸** of the painted energy, and the three dreams differ from each other (rms `0.37–0.46`). Visually: the scene is untouched at coarse scale while oriented, wavelet-like structure grows out of the image's own edges — **same photograph, same released band, a different hallucination per presentation.** That spread is the claim.

**Two bugs cost two runs, and both are worth remembering.**
1. *Project the gradient, not the iterate.* Ascending and then projecting the accumulated image discards the part of the step the gradient wanted, so steps stop being ascent directions. Project the gradient into the feasible set, then step.
2. *Do not mix octaves with a band-confined objective — and this follows from our own partition.* The released band (0.24–0.5 cyc/px content) lies **entirely inside the native band**, i.e. in the **row space of `D`**. So the data-driven hallucination is a **native-octave object**. Finer octaves move content the recorded population never saw, yet still change `D(X)`, so they push the native response around at random and fight the objective — which is exactly what the drives showed (2 of 3 negative) until octaves were dropped. Finer scales belong to the **free band** (`null(D)`), a separate declared rendering layer that provably cannot disturb the measurement.

(Also fixed: clamping pixels after gating leaks broadband energy back into the retained band — re-project after the clamp; and score the objective you actually optimize, not a differently-weighted proxy.)

## Partial replacement, done right: the amplitude ledger — the construction closes

`figure 22` / `per_trial_ledger.py`. Doug's catch on `figure 21`: gating *where* the paint lands still leaves the stimulus underneath at **full** strength, so that construction is **addition, not substitution** — band power grows, and the stimulus keeps 100% of its contribution where the measurement says it keeps only `A(f)`. The filter does not attenuate entirely, so each frequency must be **split**, not replaced:

`X(f) = A(f)·a(f)·e^{iφ}` (coherent: the share that still drives) `+ sqrt(1−A²)·a(f)·e^{iψ}` (endogenous: the share the drug released).

The two are uncorrelated, so their **powers** add: `A²a² + (1−A²)a² = a²` **exactly**. Consequences, all verified: amplitude per frequency is conserved and pinned to the stimulus (**ledger 1.000000**; per-ring hallucination/stimulus power **median 0.990**, range 0.958–1.065); the stimulus is attenuated by exactly the measured coherence rather than left intact; and where `A ≈ 1` the endogenous term vanishes on its own, so **the retained band needs no mask at all** — the gate stops being something we impose and becomes a consequence of the split. The arbitrary energy budget also disappears, because the measurement now sets the amplitude.

**The optimization must act on the free variable.** Stepping on the image and then hard-projecting back onto fixed magnitude largely undoes the step (classic alternating-projection failure): drives came out `−0.30, +1.46, +0.55`. Parametrizing by the **endogenous phase directly** — MACO's formulation, magnitude fixed by construction so nothing undoes the gradient — gives `+1.36, +1.87, +1.68`: all positive, tight, one trial per repeat.

**The ledger's own prediction holds.** Endogenous phases vary per trial and average out, so the mean over trials must converge to the attenuated stimulus `A·a`. Measured **corr 0.9932**. Every trial has full contrast; only the average is blurred. That is the cloth-and-glass picture confirmed on the real construction, and it is a check that could have failed.

Honest trade: with amplitude pinned to the stimulus's own spectrum the structure is subtler and more textured than `figure 21`'s free-amplitude gratings. That is correct physics costing visual drama, not a regression.

## The slider, and why cranking it to 11 fails — a NEGATIVE result worth keeping

`figures 23–25`. Three further dials were built and swept: **`λ`** (release the stimulus's phase, `A_λ = A·(1−λ)`), **`μ`** (exaggerate this trial's activation: target `= r̄ + μ·P_struct(r_t − r̄)`), and **recursion** (feed the output back as the new stimulus).

**What worked.** Matching the *whole* response instead of ascending one direction nearly doubles the match to the actual recording (`0.22→0.40`, `0.25→0.40`, `0.16→0.37`), and consumes essentially all the phase freedom (phase moves ~1.50 rad, ceiling ~1.57). Putting the trial specificity **into the target** finally produces real per-trial divergence: at `λ=0.9, μ=4` the three repeats differ by **0.98–1.21 rms against an image std of 0.92** — the hallucinations differ from each other by more than the picture's own contrast. Doug's expectation that each cell's own contribution should make trials genuinely different is confirmed. Radial power spectra of the extremes lie exactly on the photograph's.

**What failed, and this is the finding.** Turning any of the three dials up does **not** intensify the hallucination — it **dissolves the scene into 1/f cloud**. By `λ≈0.7` the lamp is gone; `μ` from 1 to 64 gives different clouds, not more structure; four rounds of recursion wander without organizing. The cause is arithmetic, not aesthetic: **phase carries scene structure, amplitude carries almost none**, so once phase is released the only thing holding the image together is the network — and it supplies just 749 constraints confined to a ~10% receptive-field strip. Outside that strip nothing is constrained, so the image relaxes to the maximum-entropy field consistent with the amplitude spectrum, which is cloud. **The room is real; the constraint runs out long before the room does.**

**Consequences.** (a) The scientifically interesting regime is **low `λ`** — the measured state, where the stimulus holds the scene and structure appears in the released band. (b) To bend reality further *and* still read as a scene requires something to constrain the unmeasured 90%: a natural-image/diffusion prior, which this result quantitatively justifies (it stands in for coverage we never recorded), or far more cells with full-field coverage. (c) Correction to an over-claim: the ledger conserves the **radial** spectrum exactly (the log-log plots superimpose), but taking `real(ifft(...))` of a freely-parametrized phase field is a projection that redistributes energy across *individual* coefficients (per-coefficient drift up to 0.9). Parametrizing by a real field — so Hermitian symmetry, and hence exactness, holds by construction — is the fix.

## The LOCAL ledger, and the published literature that explains everything

`figure 26` / `local_ledger.py`. Doug's requirement: blank walls stay blank, the detailed television transforms, and **squinting recovers the original**. The global radial gate cannot do this — it injects cloud into regions that had no detail to release. Two constraints fix it, and both are enforced *inside* the forward pass so gradients flow through them:

1. **The low band (< 0.08 cyc/px content) is never released, at any setting.** Verified: correlation between the blurred hallucination and the blurred stimulus is **1.000 / 1.000 / 0.999** at `λ = 0 / 0.5 / 1`. Squint-invariance is exact, not approximate. (Releasing the low band is precisely what dissolved the scene in `figures 24–25`.)
2. **Local energy gating.** Within a released octave the permitted change carries the stimulus's *own* local envelope, `allowed(x,y) = release(band)·blur(|band_b(x,y)|)`. Flat patch → envelope ≈ 0 → untouched, with no hand-drawn mask. Textured patch → free. Detail can only be hallucinated where detail already exists, which is also the honest reading of decoherence: there is no phase to decohere in a flat region.

**It works spatially.** In the lighthouse row the sky and the white tower (both flat) are untouched at every setting while the textured dark base transforms progressively; the detail map lights up exactly the edges and textured regions.

**It does not yet work structurally, and the literature says why.** The painted content reads as fine *grain*, not restructured *form*. Three published lines converge on the reason and the remedy:

- **[Phase congruency / local energy model](https://www.cs.rochester.edu/u/brown/Videre/001/articles/v1n3001.pdf)** (Morrone, Burr & Owens; Kovesi). Perceived features sit where Fourier components across scales are **maximally in phase**; edges are broadband events held together by coherent cross-scale phase alignment. Our optimizer treats each frequency's phase as independent, so it **destroys phase congruency** — and an image without congruency has no edges. *This is the mathematical reason the crank produced cloud in `figures 24–25`.*
- **[Portilla–Simoncelli](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1026553619983)** texture model. The constraint set for "change it but keep it the same stuff": marginal statistics, local autocorrelation, magnitude cross-correlations across scale and orientation, and **cross-scale phase statistics** (relative phase between each subband and the next larger). Enforced by iterative projection onto the constraint subspace — structurally what our local ledger does, so ours is a crude special case **missing exactly the cross-scale couplings**.
- **[Eidolons](https://jov.arvojournals.org/article.aspx?articleid=2603227)** (Koenderink, Valsecchi, van Doorn, Wagemans & Gegenfurtner, JoV 2017). Doug's slider already exists: a parametrized distortion family with **reach** (strength), **grain** (spatial scale) and **coherence** — the last defined as retaining "local image structure even when the global image structure is destroyed", i.e. Doug's requirement in their words, with features remaining traceable to the original rather than randomly corrupted.

**The indicated next construction: a network-driven eidolon.** Optimize a smooth **local displacement field** instead of free per-frequency phase. Displacing a patch by `d` shifts every band's phase by `2π f·d` — consistent across scales **by construction** — so phase congruency is preserved automatically. Consequences match the specification for free: a warped blank wall is still a blank wall (no masking needed); a warped television is a genuinely different scene; local amplitude statistics are preserved because warping neither creates nor destroys local energy; and sub-coarse-wavelength displacements keep squint-invariance. Reach/grain/coherence become the dials, with the **trial's population residual driving the displacement** in place of Koenderink's random field.

## The driving principle: coherence is both the signature of structure and the mechanism that makes it

A research pass across signal processing, image processing and visual neuroscience converges on one idea, and the convergence is the elegant part: **the same quantity — local phase coherence — defines what a feature *is*, explains what blur *is*, and is generated by the very circuitry that produces hallucinations.**

**Signal processing: a feature IS cross-scale phase coherence.** The local energy model (Morrone, Burr & Owens; [Kovesi](https://www.cs.rochester.edu/u/brown/Videre/001/articles/v1n3001.pdf)) places perceived features where Fourier components across scales are maximally in phase. [Wang & Simoncelli, NIPS 2003](https://www.cns.nyu.edu/pub/lcv/wang03a-orig.pdf) then show the striking converse: **blur is loss of local phase coherence, not loss of high-frequency energy** — "a blurred image that has its local phase corrected appears much sharper than one with its local amplitude corrected." And in natural images fine-scale phase is **predictable from coarse scale** (coarse-to-fine phase prediction in the complex wavelet transform), because both are consequences of the same underlying edge.

> **This reframes our headline result.** We measured DOI's effect as an *amplitude* filter on MEIs and metamers. The Wang-Simoncelli result says an amplitude-defined blur is the wrong description of what blur is: the perceptually and structurally correct description is decoherence. That is Doug's original hypothesis, now with a signal-processing formalism, an established measurement, and a reason our `A(f)` (coherence) is the more fundamental object than `H(f)` (the gain×coherence product).

**Neuroscience: V1 has explicit machinery for imposing coherence.** The **association field** (Field, Hayes & Hess) groups elements that are co-oriented and co-axial within roughly six carrier wavelengths and 30°, and its substrate is the plexus of **long-range horizontal connections** linking non-overlapping receptive fields of similar orientation (Gilbert, Bosking); their "extent and orientation dependence match the properties of salient contours and the geometry of natural scene contours." **Gamma synchrony** supplies the dynamic version: gamma in two separated V1 sites **phase-locks when one continuous object spans both receptive fields** — coherence across space as the signal that this is one thing. And [travelling waves](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn.2018.20) (Muller, Chavane & Sejnowski) both spontaneous and evoked propagate far beyond the feedforward footprint, which is a mechanism for structure to *spread*.

**The unification, and it is Cowan's own sentence.** [Bressloff & Cowan](https://www.math.utah.edu/~bresslof/publications/01-3.pdf): the lateral connectivity's symmetry (Euclidean, with a shift-twist action on orientation) determines which spontaneous planforms appear when the uniform state destabilises, and these map to the Klüver form constants — while noting that **"the cortical mechanisms that generate geometric visual hallucinations are closely related to those used to process edges, contours, surfaces, and textures."** The same wiring that integrates contours generates form constants once released from stimulus control. Normally the stimulus dictates which elements cohere; under a 5-HT2A agonist the circuit dictates it instead.

**Tested (`figure 27` / `congruent_growth.py`).** With amplitude pinned, adding a local-energy reward raises measured cross-scale alignment from ×1.07 to **×1.45** while squint-invariance holds (r = 0.997–0.9997), and the freed structure does migrate into the flat sky as `λ` rises. So the lever is real. **But the features it makes are punctate — a quasi-regular array of small dark blobs, not contours.** The reason is diagnostic: summing local energy over *all* orientations rewards points where every orientation aligns, and that is a corner/blob, not an edge (Kovesi: phase congruency detects corners *and* edges). Isotropic energy → dots; **co-axial, per-orientation continuation → filaments.** Amusingly the dot lattice is itself a Klüver form-constant class, but it is not what an association field would produce.

**Condensation works (`figure 28` / `condensation.py`).** Doug's ask: make the diffuse haze around an object *contract* onto the grain, "snow with the grain as seeds". Mechanism, Olshausen-Field used as a generator: hold **total power per band fixed** (energy can only move), add **sparsity** (minimise L1/L2, equivalently raise kurtosis — a Portilla-Simoncelli marginal) so haze is expensive and spikes are cheap, and add a **distance-weighted spread penalty** so inward is the only affordable direction. Measured: kurtosis **25 → 142 → 220**, fraction of added energy sitting on the seeds **0.20 → 0.31 → 0.93**. The haze visibly collapses into discrete specks and then pulls in onto the object. *Note the sweet spot is the middle setting* (kurtosis ~142, on-seed 0.31) — discrete specks distributed around the object, which is what "snow" looks like; full condensation over-concentrates into a single tight cluster. `figure 27`'s regression (dots in the empty sky) is also explained: interpolating the envelope toward *uniform* deliberately undid `figure 26`'s spatial gating. **The interesting dial is concentration, not diffusion.**

⚠ **Do not quote the "lattice constant" from `figure 28`.** It reports the MEIs' median spectral peak at 0.028 cyc/px (period ≈ 36 px = the whole frame). That is the MEI **envelope**, not its **carrier** grating: for a blob-dominated MEI the power peak sits near DC (consistent with 97% of MEI power below 0.18 cyc/px). A real carrier estimate needs the low-frequency envelope excluded or a Gabor fit. The orientation histogram does show a strong **cardinal bias** (horizontal dominant, vertical second), which is worth checking against rectangular-sampling artifact before being believed.

## FIRST PRINCIPLES: a spectral account of the lattice generator — and why our crank made cloud

`figure 31` / `lattice_first_principles.py`. Replicated from scratch, no network needed. **This is the diagnosis the whole chapter was missing.**

**The linear step fixes the amplitude and nothing else.** For a neural field `∂u/∂t = −u + w * f(u)`, linearising about the uniform state and Fourier-transforming turns the convolution into a multiplication, so **every mode evolves independently**: `∂û(k)/∂t = [−1 + μ·ŵ(k)]·û(k)`, with `μ = f'(0)` the gain. Instability begins where `μ·ŵ(k) > 1`, and the first mode to go is at **`k_c = argmax ŵ(k)` — the peak of the Fourier transform of the lateral connectivity. That single number *is* the lattice constant.** A Mexican-hat kernel (local excitation, broader inhibition) peaks at nonzero `k`, which is why the pattern has a finite wavelength; measured here `k_c = 0.036 cyc/px` → wavelength 28 px, critical gain `μ_c = 2.01`. If the kernel is isotropic, `λ` depends only on `|k|`, so the **entire ring** `|k| = k_c` destabilises at once, all orientations equally. **The linear theory is completely degenerate in orientation and in phase.**

**The nonlinear step fixes the phases, and that is the entire content of "which lattice".** The quadratic term in the amplitude equations is resonant for wavevector **triads that close**, `k₁ + k₂ + k₃ = 0`, and its contribution depends on the **phase sum** `Φ = φ₁ + φ₂ + φ₃` (the *biphase*). Minimising the Lyapunov function pins `Φ` to `0` or `π` — selecting spots versus honeycomb. Individual phases stay free (that is just translation invariance: sliding the pattern shifts all phases), **only their sum is constrained.** In signal-processing terms this is exactly quadratic phase coupling, and the **bispectrum** is its instrument.

> **A lattice = energy on a ring (from the connectivity) + phase closure on closing triads (from the nonlinearity).** The power spectrum *cannot* distinguish a lattice from isotropic noise — they can have identical spectra. The difference is **entirely bispectral**.

**Replicated, and the numbers are unambiguous.** Four fields built on the same ring at `k_c`:

| field | ring power | biphase closure |
|---|---|---|
| random independent phases | 3.38e7 | **0.010** |
| triad closed, `Φ=0` (spots) | 3.62e7 | **0.840** |
| triad closed, `Φ=π` (honeycomb) | 3.62e7 | **0.690** |
| one wavevector (stripes) | 3.50e7 | 0.068 |

Identical power spectra; wildly different appearance; the only quantity that separates them is triad phase closure. (Stripes score low correctly — a single mode pair forms no closing triad.)

**⇒ This explains every cloud in this chapter.** We placed energy in an annulus with **independent random phases**, which has zero bispectrum, and zero bispectrum on a ring *is* isotropic cloud **by construction**. No amount of gain, recursion, or `λ` was ever going to make it a lattice. The random-phase panel in `figure 31` is visually identical to what `figures 24–25` produced.

**⇒ The missing constraint is therefore concrete and implementable:** impose **biphase closure on closing triads** at the network's preferred `|k|`, letting the residual choose the triad orientation and the sign of `Φ`. That is one new constraint, it is the published mechanism rather than an invention, and it is the thing to build next.

### Flat patches and busy boundaries are standard lattice behaviour (Doug's question)

Both of Doug's guesses are canonical phenomena, with names:

- **Flat here means clustered there.** With a conserved amplitude budget (our ledger), suppressing the pattern in one region *forces* it to concentrate elsewhere. Redistribution, not removal.
- **Localized states.** Systems bistable between a flat background and a patterned state support **patches of pattern surrounded by flat**, as stable solutions. The flat/pattern fronts **pin** to the lattice (Pomeau's *pinning region*, around the Maxwell point) rather than sliding, so patches have definite sizes; under **homoclinic snaking** the patch grows one cell at a time in discrete folds. So "the lattice contained in a boundary" is a real solution branch, not a fudge.
- **Busy boundaries.** Where lattice domains of different orientation meet you get **grain boundaries** built from **penta–hepta defects** (bound dislocation pairs) — and PHD motion is itself a wavevector-*selection* mechanism. The interesting things at the seams are these defects.

**Why this matters for us.** If the image's flat regions sit below threshold and its detailed regions above, localized states give Doug's walls-versus-television requirement *from the physics*, with no hand-drawn mask: lattice in the busy regions, flat in the empty ones, pinned fronts between, defects at the seams. It also re-derives `figure 30`'s seams as domain walls.

## Everything together: the productive contradiction that makes patterns epiphenomenal

`figure 30` / `seams.py`, on the camera. Three requirements in one loop, with **no term anywhere that rewards structure** (the lesson of `figure 27`): (1) each released band must **carry its budget** — an *equality*, because the energy the filter released has to exist somewhere; (2) every patch wants **local smoothness** (total variation, the tractable relaxation of Mumford-Shah: smooth regions + an edge set); (3) the network's response must follow the trial's residual. **(1) and (2) cannot both hold everywhere**, and the only resolution is to concentrate the fixed high-frequency energy into thin **seams** between smooth regions. The seam arrangement is therefore the *residue* of satisfying all three — epiphenomenal by construction. A sharp seam is also maximal cross-scale phase alignment, so the phase relationships at the seams come free rather than being rewarded.

**It produced structure, and it is contours rather than grain.** At `α = 2–6` the added content stops being speckle and becomes **outlines** — closed contours tracing the camera's own boundaries — and the seam maps (`|∇|`) show a clean thin-line network. Network drive rises from the stimulus's +21.5 to **+158…+177**, and squint-invariance holds at 1.000. So the contradiction does what it was supposed to: the energy organises onto boundaries instead of spreading.

**Two honest failures.**
- **These are not fixed points.** Movement per step plateaus around 0.06–0.10 and never decays, so the loop *orbits* the constraint manifold rather than settling. Cause: constant step size on a normalised gradient, with the band-power equality confining the state to a sphere-like set — nothing makes the step shrink. A decaying step size (or convergence measured in the objective, not the state) is required before "fixed point" may be claimed.
- **TV applied to the whole image blurs the stimulus.** At `α = 6` the camera itself softens: total variation is eating the original's mid-band detail, because the ledger only governs the released bands while TV acts on everything. It should act on the *added* content (or carry a fidelity term protecting the stimulus), which is the actual Mumford-Shah form.

## Dynamics from the loop (Doug's insight), and what it is *not*

`figure 29` / `loop_dynamics.py`. The twin is feedforward, so it has no instability of its own — but **iterating it supplies the dynamics the architecture lacks**: `x_{t+1} = Ledger[x_t + η·∇⟨w_residual, f_pre(x_t)⟩]`. That is a discrete dynamical system whose map is the network, and it is also closer to how perception plausibly works (a recurrent / predictive-coding loop, not one feedforward sweep).

**What it does.** From a perturbation of amplitude 1e-4 the loop grows structure by three orders of magnitude and then **saturates** cleanly against the ledger's cap, at every gain tested, with squint-invariance holding (r ≈ 0.9997). The grown pattern is oriented and sits on the image's own structure. Growth-then-saturation is real.

**What it is NOT: a bifurcation — and the growth-rate curve is the tell.** Measured early growth *falls* with gain (+0.171, +0.129, +0.040, +0.016 per step for η = 0.002 → 0.12), which is backwards for an instability. Two reasons, one measurement artefact and one structural:
- the measurement window (steps 1–9) is already inside saturation at high gain, so it under-reports;
- **structurally, the system is *driven*, not spontaneously unstable.** The drive weights `w` are fixed, so `∇⟨w, f_pre(x)⟩ ≠ 0` at the stimulus: **the stimulus is not actually a fixed point**, and the loop always moves. There is therefore no threshold to cross and no sub-threshold regime — growth at all gains, which is exactly what we see.

**What a genuine bifurcation would require:** a *self-referential* drive that vanishes at the stimulus (e.g. driving on `f_pre(x) − f_pre(x_stim)`), so the stimulus is a true fixed point and any growth comes from the loop Jacobian's own eigenvalues. Then gain becomes a real bifurcation parameter (the dose), the threshold is where the leading eigenvalue crosses 1, and the emergent mode is selected by the network's own coupling — the Ermentrout-Cowan structure properly instantiated.

**Also fixed here, and it is the same error twice.** The first run gave growth rate identically 0 at every gain because I wrote the saturation as an *equality* (force the local envelope to equal the seed envelope), which rescales any infinitesimal perturbation to full amplitude at step 1 and erases the small-signal regime. Saturation must be an **upper bound** (shrink only, never amplify). This is the same mistake as projecting the *iterate* rather than the *gradient*: **a projection that is too strong does not constrain the answer, it removes the degrees of freedom the process needs to move.**

**Attractors as reinterpretations** (Doug's framing) become well-posed once this is a dynamical system: fixed points are images the network has nothing further to say about (a metamer is a fixed point of the response-matching loop); attractors are stable self-consistent images the system keeps re-asserting, i.e. *percepts*; multistability from one stimulus is bistable perception. All measurable: run many trajectories from small random perturbations, cluster the endpoints, count distinct interpretations, and use the per-trial spread of endpoints as the uniqueness measure. Caveat to keep: these are attractors of *our synthesis loop plus the twin*, so they are the self-consistent images of this animal's measured encoding function — not a claim about the animal's own dynamics.

**Bressloff-Cowan in this framework — the mapping, and the honest gap.** Their generator needs (i) a field of orientation hypercolumns, (ii) anisotropic lateral connections coupling co-oriented *and co-axial* sites, (iii) an instability letting a spontaneous mode grow. We have measured analogues of (i) and (ii): the readout gives every cell a feature vector `w_i` at a location `μ_i`, and the **factorized Gram** `⟨w_i,w_j⟩·⟨m_i,m_j⟩` is already exactly an association-field form (couple when co-located **and** feature-similar) — so the lateral connectivity is *measurable from the trained readout* rather than assumed, and extends to co-axial by conditioning on offset direction relative to preferred orientation. **We do not have (iii): the twin is feedforward, so there is no Turing instability and nothing grows spontaneously.** The well-posed substitute is static: treat the measured Gram as an effective coupling operator, take its leading eigenmodes at the population's preferred wavevector magnitude, and expand the hallucination in that basis with the trial residual selecting mode and phase. That yields lattice-structured output from measured coupling and a measured period, while being explicit that it is a static analogue, not a dynamical instability.

**The algorithmic consequence, replacing the ad-hoc reward.** Structure should not be coaxed by a generic "maximise local energy" term — `figure 27` shows exactly what that generic term buys and what it misses. Two principled couplings do it by construction:
1. **Coarse-to-fine phase propagation** — predict fine-scale phase from coarse, the natural-image regularity. Cross-scale congruency then holds by construction rather than by penalty, and it is the published generative form of "edges survive".
2. **Association-field phase coupling across space** — couple phase between co-oriented, co-axial neighbours, i.e. V1's own horizontal connectivity, which is also exactly the Bressloff-Cowan lattice generator. This yields Doug's predicted behaviour directly: coherent structure **aggregates** along contours and **extends** into low-detail regions, because that is what the association field and travelling waves do.

## The open gap: features → image, *outside* the network

Working outside the network at any canvas size needs an explicit **image-space dictionary**: a template per feature channel plus a composition rule, because templates are images and therefore **upsample trivially** — that is the whole reason this is the right object for large-canvas rendering. The network would be run once to extract the dictionary, never again at synthesis time. The obstacle is that the core is nonlinear (4 depth-separable conv layers with ELUs), so a channel has no single linear template. What is settled and what is not:

**Do not invent a "linearization validity test."** (Doug's correction, and it is the standing rule: run published methods, verify our *understanding* of what research has already proven, rather than inventing probes.) A first-order Jacobian at a point is an approximation of unknown range, which is exactly the wrong object to build on. Two published frameworks already solve the two halves, and each is **exact or established by proof**, so what we owe is a correctness check of our implementation, not an experiment on whether it works:

- **Image-space attribution: use the path integral, which is exact by theorem.** [Integrated Gradients](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.01365) (Sundararajan, Taly, Yan 2017) rests on the **completeness axiom**, a consequence of the fundamental theorem of calculus: for any differentiable `f`, `f(x) − f(x′)` equals the sum of attributions **exactly**. Equivalently the **path-averaged Jacobian** `Ā = ∫₀¹ J(x′ + t(x−x′)) dt` satisfies `Ā·(x−x′) = f(x) − f(x′)` exactly over the *whole finite displacement*, with no validity range to probe. It is axiomatically characterized (completeness, sensitivity, implementation invariance, linearity, symmetry). **Verified** (`figure 18` / `completeness_check.py`): the attribution sum reproduces the response difference, relative error falling `135.9% → 5.7% → 0.81% → 0.012%` at `m = 1 → 4 → 16 → 128` midpoint steps. Two readings: convergence to zero confirms the implementation, and **`m = 1` *is* the single-point linearization, so its 135.9% error is the honest verdict on linearizing** — obtained as a convergence property against a known target rather than from an invented probe. Use `m ≥ 32` for ~0.1%.
  *(Note why the easy route is closed: ReLU networks are exactly piecewise-affine inside a linear region, so linearization there is exact by proof — but our core uses smooth **ELU**, so that exactness does not transfer, and the path integral, which needs only differentiability, is the correct instrument.)*
- **A linearly-composing dictionary: use sparse dictionary learning, the vision-native proven case.** [Olshausen & Field](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0042698997001697) established that natural images decompose as **sparse linear combinations of an overcomplete dictionary** of localized, oriented, bandpass atoms — the original and best-matched precedent for us, since it is vision, not language. Its modern form is sparse-autoencoder dictionary learning under the **linear representation / superposition hypotheses** (activations as sparse weighted sums of near-orthogonal feature directions). Applied to the core's 64-channel feature map this yields the atom set and the composition rule; the atoms are images, so they upsample.
  *Honest caveat:* superposition arguments assume `M ≫ d`; our measured effective rank is **51 of 64**, so there is little room for superposition here and the dictionary will be close to a rotation, not a large overcomplete set. Sparse coding still applies; the dramatic disentangling claims do not.
- **Channel MEIs are canonical but do not compose** (argmax objects; `figure 16`'s lesson). The [combinatorial-code decomposition](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.02.10.479884v1.full) (Ding, Tolias, Sinz) is the published decomposition of *this* model class into a small dictionary of nonlinear features, and is the paper to reproduce before building anything bespoke.

## The real unblock

Two independent paths, and they are not substitutes. **Higher-resolution stimuli and retrained twins** (the frames exist pre-downsample at Reimer's) is what buys network-*determined* fine detail; the cyc/deg number decides whether it buys resolution the animal actually resolved. **The generative prior** is what buys coherent fine *form* now, at any canvas size, without a retrain — honestly labeled as a posterior sample. Pursue both: the retrain widens what the data can constrain, the prior renders what it cannot.
