# The question: how DOI affects neural representation in mouse V1

- **author:** [Nancy](../../../.claude/library/..teamsmanship/..team/nancy/nancy-or-the-weight-of-evidence/.cover.md)

---

> **It is known that DOI and other serotonergic psychedelics profoundly alter neural activity and visual perception. We are characterizing exactly how they affect neural representation in mouse V1.** (Doug, 2026-10-15)

That is the premise and the question. DOI is a potent 5-HT2A agonist, and its effects on mouse V1 are published: the visual response weakens (Michaiel, Parker & Niell 2019). Every reading here describes its effect - its form, its size, its route - and how well the instrument that makes the reading shows it. What we measure is how DOI changes what the system may be representing at each moment. Representation can be assessed in many ways - receptive fields, decoding, population geometry, the image a moment's activity is equivalent to through a twin - and understanding how what is represented changes is as close as we will get. That is a proxy for some aspect of hallucination. One reading of it is per-trial structure the cortex generates itself, fresh each trial and organized within it ([the criteria](07-the-criteria-for-hallucination.md)), riding on the presented image rather than only added to it ([ch6](06-generating-the-hallucination.md): the image's amplitude kept, its phase reorganized in the band DOI released). A trial-averaged instrument (an MEI, a metamer) sees per-trial displacements as blur; a per-trial one ([error-in-variables](../../../src/analyses/error-in-variables/purpose.md)) sees them directly. Each construal below is stated with what the recording would show, and each instrument with what it can and cannot read.

Doug, 2026-10-15, naming the error this chapter used to make: *"like proving that DOI does something at all is the scientific thing we want to conclude (it's not - DOI does stuff)"*. And: *"Do you believe the effects of the drug are realized in something other than the neural activity? Do you believe that v1 does not encode essential features of vision? Do you not believe in the existing literature that already reports these effects?"*

## The proxy, in three construals

What a V1 population recording can show is *structured neural activity that resembles stimulus-evoked activity but is not driven by the current stimulus*: what the system may be representing at a moment, beyond the image shown. That is the proxy. Three measurable construals follow, in increasing strength:

- **Weak — altered representation.** Under DOI the same images drive a measurably different population response than at baseline: tuning shifts, reliability drops, the geometry of the code changes. This is DOI's altered representation, the known effect.
- **Medium — internally structured activity.** Under DOI the population carries more low-dimensional, stimulus-like structure that is *not* explained by the shown image — the response contains organized content the stimulus did not put there.
- **Strong — decodable phantom content.** That extra structure, run through a baseline-trained decoder, reads out as a coherent image-like pattern rather than noise — the population "represents" something the eyes were not shown.

The weak level is DOI's known action on V1, and every reading here measures its form and size. The medium and strong levels are the project's question: what kind of change DOI makes. The strong level asks the most of the instrument, because a decoder will always output *something*, and an expressive model can manufacture structure from noise. The instrument checks below exist to separate the population's structure from that manufacture.

## The references and the instrument checks, named once

Every reading is made against one or more of these, so they are defined here rather than repeated:

- **Pre-DOI baseline.** The pre-DOI scan defines "normal" for this animal. Every post-DOI quantity is a *change* from its own baseline, not an absolute.
- **Matched cells.** Comparisons run on the reciprocally matched neurons, so a change is in the same physical cells, not a different sample. The matching's own error (pairs that are two neurons) is measured, because it dilutes every before/after reading.
- **Matched stimulus.** The 100 shared, repeated test images let a fixed image's response be compared directly across conditions.
- **Split halves.** Two halves of one condition's repeats give the instrument's own noise: what a reading returns when nothing but the trials differ. A change is sized against it.
- **Trial shuffle.** Shuffling responses across trials within a condition destroys stimulus-locking while preserving marginal statistics: the floor for "this structure is stimulus-related."
- **Label shuffle.** Permuting the pre/post label or the cell-matching sets what a pre/post statistic reads by chance: its floor.
- **Behaviour is DOI's effect too.** Running, pupil and eye position change under DOI. They are part of what DOI does, recorded beside V1, and never regressed out of it or used to discount a change in it (Doug, 2026-09-17: behaviour differing under DOI is the observation). Where a readout depends on where the eye looks (a receptive field, an MEI, in screen coordinates), the eye's position is measured so the readout is read in the right frame.

## The hypotheses

**H1 — How DOI alters tuning (weak construal).** For matched cells and matched images, per-neuron tuning and the population's stimulus encoding, post against pre. *Reading:* the size and form of the change (gain, shape, preferred features), against the split-half noise that bounds what the instrument resolves. *Measured on:* matched cells, matched stimulus.

**H2 — DOI lowers stimulus reliability.** The trial-to-trial reliability of responses to repeated images (the noise ceiling, the oracle) drops post-DOI: the code is less stimulus-locked, as the Charles Bonnet "release" framing predicts, with intrinsic activity competing with sensory drive. *Reading:* per-neuron repeat reliability, post against pre; the label shuffle sets its chance floor. Reliability alone does not say where the lost variance went; that is H5.

**H3 — How DOI changes population geometry.** The dimensionality and correlation structure of the population code post-DOI: shared variance, the participation of dominant modes, a rotation of the representational space relative to baseline. *Reading:* dimensionality, the eigenspectrum of the noise and signal covariance, and representational similarity to baseline, against the shuffle-derived band.

**H4 — How the encoding model fails under DOI (and whether it fails structurally).** A digital twin fit on pre-DOI data, predicting post-DOI responses, against its own held-out pre-DOI performance; and whether the residual (what the model cannot explain) is structured or white. *Reading:* the loss in normalized correlation, and the residual's whiteness against a trial shuffle. *Why it matters:* H4 is the bridge from "the response changed" (H1) to "the response contains content the stimulus did not provide" (H5). Structured residuals are the first quantitative foothold for the hallucination claim.

**H5 — Activity under DOI carries stimulus-like structure not driven by the shown image (medium construal).** The component of the post-DOI population response unexplained by the current image is low-dimensional and image-like: it resembles evoked responses to *other* images more than it resembles noise. *What would answer no:* the unexplained component is high-dimensional and statistically white, its similarity to the bank of real evoked responses no greater than a trial-shuffled surrogate's; DOI's change would then be a loss of fidelity, not added content. *References:* trial shuffle and label shuffle as the floor for "stimulus-like", the baseline residual structure as the comparison (baseline residuals should be closer to white), matched cells. *Data caveat:* this is the hypothesis most constrained by [the dataset](01-the-dataset-and-the-design.md#what-is-and-is-not-analyzable) — there are no labelled stimulus-free epochs, so "not driven by the shown image" must be operationalized as the *residual* after the encoding model accounts for the image. The test is only as clean as that residual. [The criteria for hallucination](07-the-criteria-for-hallucination.md) sharpens it.

**H6 — Decoded phantom content is coherent, not arbitrary (strong construal).** A decoder (or the inverted twin) trained only on pre-DOI evoked activity, applied to the H5 residual structure under DOI, produces image-like reconstructions that are more coherent and more mutually consistent than what the same decoder produces from baseline residuals or from shuffled activity. *What would answer no:* reconstructions from DOI residuals are no more coherent, by any blind, pre-registered measure, than reconstructions from baseline residuals or from trial-shuffled activity, i.e. the decoder makes as much structure from nothing. *References:* baseline-residual reconstructions, trial-shuffle reconstructions, and label-shuffle reconstructions as three floors; the reconstruction quality on real held-out images as the ceiling. *What it reads:* a reconstruction is the image the sober model needs to reproduce the activity, so H6 reads how the altered population code is organized, as a representation of structured visual content.

**Drift and state are measured, never used to license DOI.** A recording drifts over its minutes, and the animal's state moves within it. Both are measured directly (a statistic's correlation against minutes apart; behaviour per trial), so a reading's own drift is known and kept out of other statistics. DOI's effects are not required to exceed them to count: DOI acts for hours, and its behavioural effects are part of what it does. (This replaces the original H7, which asked that every signature be "specifically attributable to DOI" against time-matched re-recording and arousal change. That treated the drug's action as the question; it is not.)

## How the answer is reported

The answer is what DOI does, at the level the evidence reaches: the weak construal (DOI's altered representation: its gain, reliability, tuning and geometry) with its size; the medium construal (a structured residual) and the strong one (coherent decoded content) each reached or not, with the instrument's limits stated. The discipline is to report the level the evidence reaches and no higher. [The analysis plan](03-the-analysis-plan.md) is the order of operations that produces these readings.
