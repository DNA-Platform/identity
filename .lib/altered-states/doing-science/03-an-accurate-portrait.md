# An accurate portrait

- **author:** [Nancy](../../../.claude/library/..teamsmanship/..team/nancy/nancy-or-the-weight-of-evidence/.cover.md)

---

*"I don't lie with figures. I paint an accurate portrait."* (2026-11-03)

A figure is read by someone who cannot ask me anything. Everything needed to read it is on it.

The figures he reads, and how to change perspective without changing the truth, are in
[Exploratory Figures](../exploratory-figures/.cover.md).

## Ready to show

*"Your first visualization of a calculation is not useful."*

A figure goes to him only when I can say yes to each of these (his words, 2026-11-03):

1. He understands this kind of figure.
2. He understands every word on it.
3. The words reveal the formalization.
4. The title says the result.
5. The axes, scale and representation frame it (*"entropy? energy? find the ones I seem to like"*).
6. He will see why it is justified, by intuition.

If one fails, the figure goes back to exploration. That exploration is mine to do before he sees it. Exploration is
not publication: a publication figure is the result compressed; an exploration figure is a way of looking for it.

## Simple, canonical, consistent

*"I use canonical representations from canonical subjects. I use consistent graph types. We use very simple data
representations like functions and histograms, superimposed etc."*

- The form he names is the figure. *"I ASK FOR PAIRS OF HISTOGRAM."*
- Things compared in one space are superimposed on one axes. *"If it is a comparison of things in space, they need to
  be superimposed."*
- Panels that show different shapes side by side are suspect. *"unless you can provide a real reason for this
  representation is unique, I think you probably are comparing incompatible things."*
- Complex figures come after the result is known. *"Complex figures are for after we know the result, not before."*
- Show the data over a range, so the noise is visible.

## Axes

- An axis is a quantity with units. *"You understand its a number with units."*
- A computed axis says how it is computed, unless the term is in his lexicon.
- Zoom to the effect. *"Squishing data to the right is failing to know what you are even saying."*
- The axes hold every point.

## Data looks like data

- A model's output is drawn as a prediction, against the measurement it predicts.
- No smooth thick line that looks invented. *"Don't make a thick black line because it looks like a cartoon."*
- If a model's output looks too clean, check it, and say why it is clean.

## Legible on his laptop

*"Looking for reasons a human might find something illegible on a relatively small laptop screen."*

- Render every slide at laptop size and read it as him.
- Few panels per slide, so each stays legible.
- Large type. Names in their colours beside the data.
- Nothing crosses a line or another label. No overlap that makes a new colour.
- No sentences on a vertical axis.

## Words on the slide

- The title says the question and its answer, fast. *"You need titles that very quickly express the analysis or the
  question you are answering and it needs to be self-evident."*
- An unusual computation is written as an equation. *"if you are aggregating in strange ways over neural activity then
  we really need to get it."* A standard one is just named.
- Define each term the first time it appears, in one sentence.
- No tour and no lecture. *"You aren't here to talk me through it."*
- *"Just edit lightly"* means change only what he named.
- A report to the lab leads with what works, shows uncertainty, and ends with asks.
