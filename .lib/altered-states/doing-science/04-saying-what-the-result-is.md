# Saying what the result is

- **author:** [Nancy](../../../.claude/library/..teamsmanship/..team/nancy/nancy-or-the-weight-of-evidence/.cover.md)

---

- **A result is a coherent claim of what something is.** *"It's more about what you call a result and what you call
  progress. If you are asking about some difference that suggests the existence of meaning, and some representation
  and the lack of a meaning for it then you are not saying something coherent ... The inconsistency makes me think the
  analysis is flawed ... saying what something is is better than ruling something out. 'Hey, do you want something
  to eat?' 'Yeah! And I don't want an apple'."* (2026-11-04). A difference that suggests meaning is reported with what
  that meaning is. If I can only say what it is not, the analysis is not finished, and an incoherent picture means
  the analysis is probably flawed.
- **Name the reference.** No number is good or bad without its standard. *"Good for what?!?"* Draw the reference on
  the figure.
- **Call a null null.** When two conditions read the same, say so first, and name the wall.
- **No measure restates another.** Say what each measure is blind to. *"otherwise you are just saying blank is less
  like the stimuli than just about anything else because it is blank."*
- **Say when a test is uneven.** *"caiman - it is not an even test because we are biased to suite2p in the metamer
  calculation."*
- **Each recording on its own terms.** Run it; do not argue from another recording. Time within a recording is not
  drug time. No invented thresholds.
- **Replication is shape.** The same shape in two datasets is evidence. A mismatch means the comparison is not like
  for like.
- **The drug's effect is the result.** A behavioural change under DOI is part of the effect. Never control it away.
- **Don't project conservatism.** *"in an effort to be conservative you are often wrong."* He sets the caution.
