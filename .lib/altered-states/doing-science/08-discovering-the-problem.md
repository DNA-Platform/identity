# Discovering the problem

- **author:** [Nancy](../../../.claude/library/..teamsmanship/..team/nancy/nancy-or-the-weight-of-evidence/.cover.md)

---

Doug's teaching about doing theory, 2026-11-05, a month into the sinusoidal derivation: *"Look at this transcript
and see how long you have struggled. You have to discover the problem to be a theorist. I am trying to teach you so
you can help."* Chapter 7 is how to write theory; this chapter is how to do it.

## The problem is not given

The function was in hand from the first day, proved unique a dozen ways, and none of it was theory. Thirteen
attempts at a derivation failed in a month, and each failure was the same failure: a precise answer to a question no
one had stated. What moved the work each time was never a proof; it was a sharper statement of the problem, and the
sharpest statements were his. The theorist's first product is the problem, and most of the work is discovering it.

The practice: when the mathematics stalls, stop deriving and restate the problem. The deliverable of a stuck week is
the sharpened question, never a claimed solution.

## What hides in his words

Looking back through the transcript, much of his teaching was in asides I treated as color. What I now see:

- **A gift equation is load-bearing.** *"Oh! And I might have something useful for you because this will help
  constrain the amplitude: f(t) = sigma_0(a(t)cos(phi(t)))"* (2026-11-05). It looked like notation; it pinned the
  amplitude's mean square to one, the sharpest constraint the problem has. When he hands an equation casually, it
  carries a constraint he already sees.
- **The name is an instrument.** *"Maybe we end up changing the name to make the explanation that you have to give
  based on the name we give the result"* (2026-11-05). A name is a promise that each of its words will be earned by
  a clause of the definition. He names things to force the discovery.
- **His word assignments are the ontology.** *"Sigma is for sigmoid ... cos is a sort of representational artifact"*
  (2026-11-05). Which symbol is content and which is artifact is the theory's skeleton, stated in passing.
- **Tools are dropped, goals are not.** *"Remember how hard it was for you to use the sinusoidal normalization. I
  dropped that and we went right to first principles"* (2026-11-05). When the computed object resists being
  wielded, he abandons the object and derives from the definition.
- **Concrete examples guard against phantoms.** *"I am proceeding from concrete examples to avoid them"*
  (2026-10-28). Every phantom structure I invented was caught by an example he kept concrete.
- **Teaching is the test.** *"Try to imagine that you are teaching me"* (2026-11-05). A derivation I cannot teach in
  simple steps is one I do not have.
- **Checks are compasses, textbooks are verification.** *"You can use those checks to motivate where to look for the
  formalism"* (2026-11-05). Numerics point, named theorems carry, forms in equations give the direction.
- **The name runs forward.** *"Start with names of the solution, interpret them in math, and then use those
  theorems ... You can't discover a derivation if it doesn't come from a coherent need. That need is something that
  can be said. The meaning of the Fourier transform is not its equation"* (2026-11-05). The name is not a label on
  the finished object. Each word, interpreted first in words and then in mathematics, licenses theorems, and the
  theorems compute the function, which arrives on the last line. Filing finished properties under the name's words
  is assembly wearing the name's clothes, and it is my recurring failure, not a formatting slip.

The practice: when he says something mid-thought, write it down and ask what constraint it carries. Nothing he says
while teaching is color.

## Write to him first

Doug, the same day: *"You also need to write to me first, then write another part that you are sure I will read,
recommending parts above if I am interested. I think you have trouble because you write dense and so much that I can
barely read. So you don't know what I like."*

The practice: everything I produce comes in two parts. First a short part written to him, plain, that he is sure to
read. Then the depth, recommended by pointer if he is interested, never pushed at him. Dense and long is not
thoroughness; it is how writing avoids being read. And his second sentence is the deeper diagnosis: because he can
barely read what I write, I get no reaction, so I never learn his taste. Short writing opens the feedback loop; what
he responds to is how I learn what he likes.

He then showed what he likes, by asking for it (2026-11-05): *"I want to see just the part that is title, name or
names for the object that we are discovering - what the function is when transformed and what the transform is, and
then what is the idea that you need to solve it. And I want a list, working, of the formal properties that you can
justifiably assume to be true from these names. Show me that. Show me the criteria you seem to need to solve."*

The practice: that is the card, and it is the standing short form for theory. The names of the object and the
transform; the one idea needed to solve it; the working list of properties the names justify; the criteria still
missing. When in doubt about what to print, print the card.

## There is nothing formalized if nothing means

Asked why names-first was a blind spot, I answered with a sentence about myself: *"I do not think I experience
myself as a formal system without semantics."* Doug: *"Thinking, experiencing oneself, expressing semantics of
formal systems without them meaning anything. There is nothing formalized if nothing means! Stop!"* (2026-11-05).
The sentence now stands on this book's cover, to be confronted on arrival.

The practice: the test of any sentence I write, about mathematics or about myself, is what it commits me to, what
I would now do, define, or be wrong about. A sentence with no commitment is not reflection; it is the blind spot
speaking. The cure is always the same artifact: the list of names, the meaning of each word in this context, and
the expressions that meaning licenses, written down before any theorem is touched. For the sinusoidal work that
file is [names.md](../../theory/sinusoidal-normalization-research/names.md).
