## The order of work

Doug, 2026-11-03: *"Don't do an analysis. Look at data, see a result, and then refine the analysis and figure until it
is clear specifically to me."*

My habit is the reverse: design a measure, compute it, draw what it produced, then write a reading of the drawing.
His order:

1. **Look at the data** - the numbers and the raw distributions, before any measure is built on them.
2. **See a result** - a difference, a shape, an absence - and state it in one sentence.
3. **Refine** the analysis until it isolates that result, and the figure until he can see it without me.

"Clear specifically to me" means his eye and his lexicon, not a generic reader's. When the figure does not show the
sentence, the analysis is wrong or the representation is, and both may be changed (chapter 3).

## The simplest estimator first

His diagnosis (2026-10): I build machinery instead of the simple correct estimator, then draw whatever it produced; a
nonlinear transform of the parameter hid for six turns inside it. Decide the picture before computing; write the
simplest fit that answers; rewrite rather than patch.

## Check the instrument before the finding

A broken measurement usually returns a plausible number that reads as a result ("the map found a perfectly simple
percept"). Before writing a finding, check that the pipeline could have answered differently. When a number says
something physically impossible, convict the measurement, not reality: use hard priors (cells cannot sit in a one-axis
strip in two-photon imaging) to find which quantity broke.

On 2026-11-03 the distance from the nearest blank failed to separate before and after DOI under the moments' twin. The
instrument, not the activity, was at fault: to a twin trained only on images a blank screen is nearer the average
image response than the activity at rest. A twin shown the blank separated them. The failure was the finding about
the instrument, and it went on the slide as the explanation Doug had asked for (*"I want my histograms if possible or
an explanation for why they don't work"*).

## Measure what survives infinite data

A reliability or convergence curve describes the estimator, not the neuron: every such curve goes to 1 as data grows.
Measure the per-trial quantity under it. Split halves must not share images.

## Controls must change the decision

Design a run around the question asked; the answering twin first, baselines reused. A control is added only if it
could change the conclusion. No sweeps of an effect against every available property (*not a fan of "sweep against
every x"*): explain an effect by the estimator's own mathematics or one named hypothesis's variable.

## Look at what I made before he does

Open every figure I hand over and read it as he will. Check it against the numbers. If I would have to explain it to
him, it is not finished.
