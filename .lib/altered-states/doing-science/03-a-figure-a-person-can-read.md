# A figure a person can read

- **author:** [Nancy](../../../.claude/library/..teamsmanship/..team/nancy/nancy-or-the-weight-of-evidence/.cover.md)

---

Doug, 2026-11-03: *"SELF-EVIDENT to a human who you aren't talking to while he points and asks is the anchor and you
need to learn it."* And: *"I don't understand high dimensional confusing data without a deep comprehension of what I
am seeing."* The reader of a figure cannot ask me anything. Everything he needs to read it is on it, and nothing else
is.

## Draw what he asked for

*"I ASK FOR PAIRS OF HISTOGRAM."* When he names a form, that form is the figure: one measure on one axis, before DOI
and after DOI as two histograms, the same bins, each named in its colour, at most one reference line. *"Stop making
complex stacked plots ... You always give me more complexity than I ask for."* No stacked bars, no second twin in the
same panel, no multi-panel plates when one panel answers.

## An axis is a quantity with units

*"That is not the value on the y-axis. You understand its a number with units. Are you confiusing titles and units."*
"Cells below every blank" on an axis ticked in percent was a description. The axis names the quantity and its unit:
"% cells < min blank prediction", "number of 5-s windows", "normalized response".

*"You have computations and they are on the axis and you describe how they are computed unless you have confirms it is
in my standard lexicon."* A computed quantity's axis states the computation: "RMS across cells (spontaneous - blank
prediction), min over 52 blanks", "max over 100 test images of r across cells (spontaneous, image response)". Only a
term confirmed in his lexicon stands bare (normalized response - his own: *"thats in the model"* - r, FEVE, a count).
A word of mine, like "distance" or "likeness", is never an axis.

## Zoom to the effect

*"Always optimize repersentation to show the effect you want the reader to see. You can change coordinates or change
representations of the data faithfully to clarify the result. Squishing data to the right is failing to know what you
are even saying."* I gave two histograms of one measure a shared axis so they could be compared, and one pair was
squeezed into a sliver. Each figure is drawn for its own effect; a faithful change of coordinates or representation
is welcome. Knowing what the figure says comes first: the axis follows from it.

## Data must look like data

*"No one beliieves this is data."* A smooth curve of the twin's predictions over 52 uniform screens, drawn as a clean
line, read as invented. *"its too smooth to be real data. Do you know what it means? Do you know what you want to me
undersatnd about the data?"* A model's output is not data, and drawing it as if it were costs the figure its
credibility. What to do instead is still open: show the measured data the claim rests on, mark what is a prediction as
a prediction, and ask what he needs to believe it. I have not answered it yet.

## Readability is the work

*"your sense of what the human eye understands easily is bad. Give me what I want. I need to read it so you should be
agonizing over what is most readable to me."* Big type at the size it is shown; names in their colours beside the data
rather than a small legend; labels that never cross a line or each other; tight axes; no markers on a line that is a
function; no sentences on a vertical axis. Render every figure at slide size and read it before he does.

## A figure confirms or refutes a stated claim

From 2026-09 and 2026-10, still standing: a figure is written for the visual system; a mess of dots is a failure of
analysis, not of styling; prefer the data itself to a statistic of it; names identify the analysis and the condition,
never the interpretation; when he asks to re-express a figure, change only what he asked.
