The sprint-by-sprint record of the team's effort on altered-states — a psychedelic exploration into altered states of consciousness using mouse visual cortex as our lens. A projection maps one space onto another: sprints live in the team's identity, and Projection maps them onto this branch, recording what the team actually did here in the project's own terms. Each chapter is one sprint — what it aimed to do, what was built, and what was learned — and together they form the branch's autobiography. The last sprint chapter is always the current state. This table of contents is empty at creation and grows as sprints are written. Every sprint gets an entry — created when the plan is known (goal, tasks, owners) and completed at the retro with what was built and learned, per [Sprints](../../../.claude/library/library-tree/03-sprints.md).

## Sprints

1. [Sprint 1 — Does the Mouse Hallucinate?](01-sprint-1--does-the-mouse-hallucinate.md) — set up and retro'd: find evidence for or against DOI-induced hallucination in mouse V1 (thousands of cells, controlled stimuli before/after the psychedelic), and if found, decode what the mouse might see. Built: Nancy onboarded (neuroscientist/philosopher/engineer), the branch scaffolded, [The Altered Cortex](../the-altered-cortex/.cover.md) (dataset verified, seven falsifiable hypotheses, digital-twin/inversion plan), and the analysis **environment** (Python 3.11 + torch/Sensorium + a scan loader, data extracted — task 8 done). The setup and environment phase is complete; the DOI analysis (baseline → altered → evidence → decode, tasks 4–7) awaits execution.
2. [Sprint 2 — Equipping the Team](02-sprint-2--equipping-the-team.md) — the infrastructure that made the team ready to run the analysis, recorded at close: the resumable voice/brain operating model and its documentation, the thinking conventions (think-in-links, bold-only nametags, the brain-as-back-of-mind terminology), a cleaned project library (the neutralized overview deck, a Datasets book, de-voiced papers), the repaired link-checker with links green, and refreshed code assignments / `/responsible`. Enablement, not results — the DOI analysis itself is next.
3. [Sprint 3 — Model-Free First](03-sprint-3--model-free-first.md) — **closed at the descriptive slice**: model-free, but a descriptive first pass before the controlled comparison the plan aimed at — and the pass that proved why that comparison is mandatory. Built: a nine-panel exploration of both sessions and the 749 matched cells (reliability, population rate and co-activity, matched-cell pre/post, behaviour-only drug-state decoding), a shared figure grammar + primitives with a compute cache, and the woven [Initial Data Exploration](../../reports/2026-06-23-exploration/initial-data-exploration.md) with its method book [The Exploration Report](../the-exploration-report/.cover.md). Learned: behaviour alone decodes the session at 97%, so the confound is the precondition for any pre/post claim, not a later phase. Resolved at close: the standard per-scan normalization makes the two scans **comparable** (matched cells on the diagonal, profiles overlapping) — the raw amplitude drop is a per-neuron gain scalar normalization removes, and since the published DOI effect *is* a V1 gain reduction (Michaiel 2019) the magnitude question is deferred to within-scan scale-invariant analysis; the paper was rebuilt accordingly, and the throughline lesson is **research before research**. Rolls forward: the controlled comparison (covariates regressed out), tuning and geometry adapters, the `stats/` module and output tests, and the twin (now **Sprint 4**).
4. [Sprint 4 — The Twin and MEI](04-sprint-4--the-twin-and-mei.md) — **planned**, in a dated experiment (`src/experiments/2026-06-23-twin-mei/`): build aligned Sensorium-baseline twins on **both** scans (post warm-started from pre, so the networks compare), **save the checkpoints durably so a good twin is never lost**, and make the pictures — an MEI grid, a **matched cell's MEI pre vs post**, and **pre-vs-post network visualizations** (first-layer filters, feature maps, the readout receptive-field map). Realizes Step 0–1 of [The Altered Cortex plan](../the-altered-cortex/03-the-analysis-plan.md) and the MEI tool. **Owners: Nancy + Adam build it, Libby catalogues.** Built with the real packages and trained properly — the instrument for Step 0–1, not the controlled H1–H6 tests: a twin that fits is not a mechanism.
5. [Sprint 5 — The publication-grade analysis](05-sprint-5--the-publication-grade-analysis.md) — **closed.** Built the modular `src/analysis/` instrument (data, model, validation, the `verify_twin` harness, `mei_recipe`) done **as the literature does it**, and **resolved the gammas** (6.3831/0.0076 confirmed canonical Lurz/Sensorium — copied, not re-fit). Its **rigorous closing audit — the real worth — caught three bugs before any long run**: the CC_norm ceiling mismatch, the **FEVE survivorship artifact**, and cross-condition cell mis-alignment (now the canonical `matched_pair_index`). Retro: we worked within the library *and* fought it — standing lesson, **convert discipline into structure**. The four-twin run + the analysis carry to Sprint 6.
6. The archive is the inspection record; **Doug drives and inspects — the gate is his call.**
7. [Sprint 7 — The four twins, one at a time](07-sprint-7--the-four-twins-one-at-a-time.md) — ACTIVE — the execution sprint. The gated one-twin loop run via the run_twin launch command: four cases one at a time, starting with behaviour+control (pre/Arm-A, the most standard), as a watchable background process. First twin launched; the by-eye gate caught a y-retinotopy collapse traced to an unregularized shifter (gamma_shifter=0) — a dropped Franke config, sourced from CD before any re-train. Carries Sprint 6 gated rule.
8. [Sprint 8 — The pipeline owns its runtime](08-sprint-8--the-pipeline-owns-its-runtime.md) — ACTIVE — a structural sprint: make the most-exciting-image pipeline self-contained by moving its runtime dirs (`_cache`/`_checkpoints`/`_logs`) from the analysis root **into** the `pipeline/` package and re-anchoring every path (the `parents[2]`→`parents[1]` reach-arounds), while `results/` stays at the analysis root as the deliverable. Owner Adam on the code, gated by a **non-destructive dry run** of `run.py` (load-if-exists, nothing overwritten). Principle: a package owns its own runtime artifacts.
9. [Sprint 9 — The raw instruments, handed over](09-sprint-9--the-raw-instruments-handed-over.md) — PLANNED — the handoff sprint. With the four twins trained, generate every raw instrument — one MEI per matched cell + four metamer sets (within-pre / within-post Cobos, cross pre→post / post→pre with the scale-invariant cross loss, Open Q13) — into one fast-to-explore indexed **HDF5** archive, bundled with the code and a Jupyter notebook so the Reimer Lab (Erin, Jake) can explore it **without our pipeline**. **Lean:** the pre/post comparison is de-emphasized into computational characterization — best-one metrics, exemplars, per-matched-cell difference (MEI + metamer). Owners: Adam (archive / MEIs / bundle), Adam+Nancy (metamers + cross loss), Nancy (metrics / exemplars / deliverable + spec rewrite), Libby (catalogue). Spine: generate the raw instruments and hand them over.
10. [Sprint 10 — The y-collapse regression: find it, verify it, rebuild only what's broken](10-sprint-10--the-y-collapse-regression-and-rebuild.md) — CLOSED (2026-07-13) — the diagnosis sprint. Sprint 9's instruments were built on a readout whose elevation had collapsed; this sprint found it, verified it against evidence that did not grade itself, and rebuilt only the affected twins rather than everything. Its lasting result is the standing rule now in [The Altered Cortex](../the-altered-cortex/03-the-analysis-plan.md): **never validate a model's retinotopy against the coordinates it was fit from** — the μy-R² gate was a tautology, and it selected the wrong readout for three sprints. Owners: Adam (the rebuild), Nancy (the evidence).
11. [Sprint 11 — The delivery: a rebuildable deliverable for the Reimer Lab](11-sprint-11--the-delivery-to-the-reimer-lab.md) — ACTIVE (2026-07-16) — the sprint that *delivers*, under Doug's one requirement: **the deliverable is a pipeline that rebuilds, not a snapshot** — with MEI at ~310/749 and growing, "more MEI computed" must be a one-command re-run. Deliberately thin: the method is **not** restated (it lives in comparison.md and The Altered Cortex; a copy would drift, and once did) — the chapter holds only the arc, the owners, and the rebuild spine. **Adam owns the rebuild chain + handoff; Nancy owns the science**, each finding labelled stable-at-n or still-moving. Done = one command yields a consistent deliverable at any cell count, verified at two counts.
12. [Sprint 12 — The fidelity of the percept](12-sprint-12--the-fidelity-of-the-percept.md) — ACTIVE (2026-08-15), `requirements-only` — a new direction on the same data, prompted by a collaborator's email. Every instrument we have built assumes the presented image is what the population encodes; [error-in-variables regression](../../papers/garon-error-in-variables-2026/.cover.md) (Garon, Keeley & Williams 2026) makes that assumption a *parameter* — κ, the coupling between the represented and the presented — and shows that a population with entirely unchanged tuning will look degraded under trial-averaging if that coupling loosens. That is the one rival explanation our delivered resolution result cannot exclude, and our own amplitude ledger already measured its signature (full contrast per trial, blur only in the average, r=0.9932) without naming it. Their method must infer the tuning function and so is confined to 1–2 dimensions; **we supply it — the twins are `f`** — which is the collaborator's "combine it with DNNs" and a constructor argument in their released code. The analysis moves out of most-exciting-image to `src/analyses/error-in-variables/`. Open by design: what the latent is, and JAX versus PyTorch.
13. [Sprint 13 — Reading the machine we built](13-sprint-13--reading-the-machine-we-built.md) — ACTIVE (2026-08-21), documentation complete — prompted by David's second pair of links: [plenoptic](https://docs.plenoptic.org) and [Shin et al. 2025](../../papers/shin-recurrent-pattern-completion-2025/.cover.md) on recurrent pattern completion. Doug wants to be **taught** this rather than handed it, and to read every line we write — so the sprint began by reading the delivered pipeline and writing it down: **7,862 lines across 56 files, thirteen per-file per-function pages plus nine chapters**, in a new analysis `most-exciting-image-replication/`. The correction that shaped it: **plenoptic cannot train a twin and is not meant to** — its models must have no learnable parameters, because synthesis optimises the *input*. What it does supply is a published ladder (Berardino 2017) whose rungs each add exactly one idea, so the lessons climb theirs rather than inventing toys. The reading answered four open questions by opening files nobody had opened — the environment was pinned all along in `direct_url.json`, the gamma search was run and vindicates the inherited hyperparameters, Open Q13 was resolved, and the delivered headline is blur-σ **0.55 px, 80% of cells, 0.6% at the ceiling** — and found five risks, three of them about things that may never have been computed: the seed-null, a seed for metamer synthesis, and a scripted fix for the hand-edited scan metadata that makes "regenerable from git" conditional. Nothing built by decision. **Next: the lesson sprints**, fifteen checkpoints whose keystone is a toy twin trained with the real architecture against receptive fields we chose.
